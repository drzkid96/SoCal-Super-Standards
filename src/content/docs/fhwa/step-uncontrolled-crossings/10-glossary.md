<p>Glossary</p>
<h4>Glossary</h4>
<p>Annual Average Daily Traffic (AADT)</p>
<p>The total volume of traffic passing a point or segment of a highway facility in both directions for one year divided by the number of days in the year.</p>
<p>Average Daily Traffic (ADT)</p>
<p>The average 24-hour volume of traffic passing a point or segment of a highway in both directions.</p>
<p>Complete Streets</p>
<p>Complete Streets are designed and operated to enable safe access for all users, including pedestrians, bicyclists, motorists, and transit riders of all ages and abilities. (Smart Growth America, National Complete Streets Coalition.)</p>
<p>Controlled pedestrian crossing</p>
<p>A pedestrian crossing where motorists are required to stop by either a STOP sign, traffic signal, or other traffic control device.</p>
<p>Crash modification factor (CMF)</p>
<p>A multiplicative factor used to compute the expected number of crashes after implementing a given countermeasure. If available, calibrated or locally developed State estimates may provide a better estimate of effects for the State. (Crash Modification Factors Clearinghouse.)</p>
<p>Crash reduction factor (CRF)</p>
<p>The percentage crash reduction that might be expected after implementing a given countermeasure at a specific site.</p>
<p>Curb extensions</p>
<p>A roadway edge treatment where a curb line is bulbed out toward the middle of the roadway to narrow the width of the street. Curb extensions are sometimes called “neckdowns.”</p>
<p>Highway Safety Improvement Program (HSIP)</p>
<p>A Federal-aid program with the purpose to achieve a significant reduction in traffic fatalities and serious injuries on all public roads, including non-State-owned roads and roads on tribal land. The HSIP requires a data-driven, strategic approach to improving highway safety on all public roads with a focus on performance. (FHWA.)</p>
<p>High visibility crosswalk</p>
<p>A pedestrian crossing location marked by patterns such as zebra, ladder, or continental markings as described by the MUTCD.</p>
<p>Marked crosswalk</p>
<p>A pedestrian crossing that is delineated by white crosswalk pavement markings.</p>
<p>Parking restriction</p>
<p>Parking restriction can include the removal of parking space markings, installation of new “parking prohibition” pavement markings or curb paint, and signs.</p></div>

### Page 35

<div class="pmeta">Topics: crosswalks, signals, speed-calming, signs, intersections</div>
<div class="dtext"><p>28</p>
<p>Guide for Improving Pedestrian Safety at Uncontrolled Crossing Locations</p>
<p>Glossary</p>
<p>Pedestrian Hybrid Beacon (PHB)</p>
<p>A traffic control device with a face that consists of two red lenses above a single yellow lens. Unlike a traffic signal, the PHB rests in dark until a pedestrian activates it via pushbutton or other form of detection.</p>
<p>Raised crosswalk</p>
<p>Raised crosswalks are ramped speed tables spanning the entire width of the roadway, often placed at midblock crossing locations.</p>
<p>Rectangular Rapid-Flashing Beacon (RRFB)</p>
<p>RRFBs are pedestrian-actuated conspicuity enhancements used in combination with a pedestrian, school, or trail crossing warning sign to improve safety at uncontrolled, marked crosswalks. The device includes two rectangular-shaped yellow indications, each with an LED-array-based light source, that flash with high frequency when activated. RRFBs are placed on both ends of a crosswalk. If the crosswalk contains a pedestrian refuge island or other type of median, an RRFB should be placed to the right of the crosswalk and on the median (instead of the left side of the crosswalk). The flashing pattern is pedestrian-activated by pushbuttons or automated detection and is unlit when not activated.</p>
<p>Refuge island</p>
<p>A median with a refuge area that is intended to help protect pedestrians who are crossing the road. This countermeasure is sometimes referred to as a crossing island or pedestrian island.</p>
<p>Road Diet</p>
<p>A roadway reconfiguration resulting in a reduction in the number of travel lanes. The space gained by eliminating lanes is typically used for other uses and travel modes. (FHWA.)</p>
<p>Road Safety Audit (RSA)</p>
<p>A formal examination of an existing or future road or intersection by a multidisciplinary team. It qualitatively estimates and reports on potential road safety issues and identifies opportunities for improvements in safety for all road users. (FHWA.)</p>
<p>Toward Zero Deaths (TZD)</p>
<p>TZD is a traffic safety framework that seeks to eliminate highway fatalities by engaging diverse safety partners and technology to address traffic safety culture. (See also: Vision Zero.)</p>
<p>Uncontrolled pedestrian crossing</p>
<p>An established pedestrian crossing that does not include a traffic signal, beacon, or STOP sign to require that motor vehicles stop before entering the crosswalk.</p>
<p>Vehicle queue</p>
<p>A line of stopped vehicles in a single travel lane, commonly caused by traffic control at an intersection.</p>
<p>Vision Zero (VZ)</p>
<p>Similar to TZD, Vision Zero is a vision to eliminate traffic fatalities and serious injuries within the transportation system. VZ employs comprehensive strategies to address roadway design, traffic behavior, and law enforcement.</p></div>
