### Page 68 - Inductive Loop Installation (Standard Drawing S-70.1A, Sheet 2 of 2)

<div class="pmeta">Topics: transit, signals, parking-curb</div>
<figure class="dfig"><a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ladot/red-book/p0068.webp" target="_blank"><img loading="lazy" src="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ladot/red-book/p0068.webp" alt="Red Book page 68 "></a><figcaption>Red Book, page 68.</figcaption></figure>
<details class="ocr"><summary>Sheet notes</summary><h5>Notes: Perform loop installation in the following order:</h5>
<p>1. Open the pavement at least 42&quot; from curb face and at least 18&quot; from edge of gutter using a star drill or a jack hammer (asphalt concrete only). Open no more than a 12&quot; x 16&quot; area. In cases where there is a concrete bus pad adjacent to the gutter, install the stubout beyond the edge of the bus pad.</p>
<p>2. Install 2&quot; PVC (SC80) or PVC flex (SC40) from the pullbox pit with a 45 degree elbow at the stub-out as shown. Depth of the conduit shall be at least 18&quot; below the street grade.</p>
<p>3. Patch street using hot patch asphalt concrete and sand as shown.</p>
<p>4. Install duct seal where wires enter 2&quot; PVC or 2&quot; PVC flex.</p>
<p>5. Fill sawcut slot with Caltrans approved hot-melt rubberized asphalt sealant.</p>
<p>* No more than 8 loops or 16 wires per stub-out.</p></details>

### Page 69 - Bicycle Detector (S-70.1D)

<div class="pmeta">Drawings: <strong>S-70.0</strong> &middot; Topics: bikeways, signals, parking-curb</div>
<figure class="dfig"><a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ladot/red-book/p0069.webp" target="_blank"><img loading="lazy" src="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ladot/red-book/p0069.webp" alt="Red Book page 69 MT 3-07-17 Title BICYCLE DETECTOR (S-70.0)"></a><figcaption>Red Book, page 69.</figcaption></figure>
<details class="ocr"><summary>Sheet notes</summary><h5>Notes:</h5>
<p>1. Install four (4) complete alternating turns of loop wires, unless otherwise specified.</p>
<p>2. For stub-out and conduit details refer to page 2 of S-70.1A.</p>
<p>3. See LADOT Material Specification No. 92-081-05 or latest revision.</p>
<p>4. Use Case I loops for bike lane installations. Use Case II loops when vehicular loops are intended to detect bicycles.</p>
<p>5. An octagonal shaped loop or other non-round shaped loop may be used instead of the round-loop with prior LADOT approval.</p>
<p>6. Same winding pattern to be used in both loops for Case 1 with 1 pair from each loop leading to the pullbox.</p>
<p>7. Loops installed in bike lanes with parking shall be placed with the near edge of the loop 1&#x27; to the right of the bike lane delineation line, unless otherwise specified.</p>
<p>8. Loops installed in bike lanes adjacent to the roadside without parking shall be centered in the bike lane, or between the gutter and the bike delineation line, unless otherwise specified.</p>
<p>9. Any LADOT approved non-round shaped loops must conform to all other specifications shown on this standard drawing.</p>
<p>* Depth of slot not to exceed depth of pavement.</p>
<p>Labels: Winding detail (not to scale); Loop 1; Loop 2; twisted clockwise (at least 2 turns per ft.) into a pair; to pullbox; curb face; pullbox; 3&#x27;-0&quot; maximum (MAX.); direction of traffic flow; Case I (bicycle only); Case II (bicycle and vehicle).</p></details>

### Page 70 - Preformed Loop Installation (Standard Drawing S-70.1E)

<div class="pmeta">Topics: signals, parking-curb, accessibility</div>
<figure class="dfig"><a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ladot/red-book/p0070.webp" target="_blank"><img loading="lazy" src="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ladot/red-book/p0070.webp" alt="Red Book page 70 "></a><figcaption>Red Book, page 70.</figcaption></figure>
<details class="ocr"><summary>Sheet notes</summary><p>1. Pre-formed loop shall be constructed from 1/2&quot; I.D. polypropylene, with an O.D. of 3/4&quot; inch.</p>
<p>2. Loop is to have one continuous #16 TFFN wire through the loop head and lead-in to prevent loop malfunction due to splicing. Loop shall have four (4) turns of wire.</p>
<p>3. The conduit shall be completely filled with hot, rubberized asphalt which will allow the loop to retain flexibility once cooled, prevent incursion of moisture and set the wire turns firmly in place.</p>
<p>4. Each loop head shall be provided with a 5&quot; contraction/expansion joint to allow for movement of pavement and to prevent breakage of the wire and/or conduit. The joint is to have a 9&quot; long by 3/4&quot; Schedule 80 PVC cover slide to be placed over the joint for protection from the elements.</p>
<p>5. Encase lead-in wires in a non-conductive 250 psi flex hose seamless fiber braid reinforcement and a non-conductive seamless extruded urethane non-perforated jacket. Fill lead-in hose completely with hot rubberized asphalt. Twist wires at least two times per foot for entire run. Attach lead-in to loop head with a Schedule 80 CVPC tee and a CVPC adapter bushing.</p>
<p>6. Any variation to the installation as described above shall be approved by the design engineer prior to installation.</p>
<p>7. The 3/8&quot; O.D. lead-in hose shall be installed in 2&quot; PVC conduit between pullbox and entry to pavement slab. Loop shall be attached to top of reinforcing bars when used in the pavement slab.</p>
<p>Callouts: Expansion joint. CPVC tee. CPVC cross. 3/4&quot; O.D. asphalt rubber filled hydraulic hose. 3/8&quot; O.D. asphalt rubber filled hydraulic hose. Twisted tail. Twisted clockwise (at least 2 turn per ft.) into a pair. To pullbox. Detector loop conductors. Schedule 80 PVC 2&quot; conduit (see note 7). Pavement. 4&quot; min.</p></details>
