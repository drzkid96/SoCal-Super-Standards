<div class="pclean"><p class="psum"><b>Channelized Right Turns.</b> Five cases of channelized right turn control. Stop sign (Case 1) is used where the curb return radius is under 20 ft, pedestrian traffic is high, or visibility is restricted. Yield (Cases 2 and 3), merge (Case 4) and added lane (Case 5) operation depend on the radius and the available acceleration and merging distance. Tables 1 to 3 give S1, D1 and D2 for the merge case, and the added lane case needs an all-day receiving lane of 1/2 mile or more.</p><details class="ocr" open><summary>Notes on this sheet (cleaned from the PDF text)</summary><p>Case 1: Stop sign control (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r1-1/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R1-1.svg">R1-1</a>)</p>
<p>Note: Use stop sign operation where the curb return radius is less than 20 feet (10 miles per hour design speed), pedestrian traffic across the channelized right turn is high, cross-traffic visibility is restricted, or where the use of a yield sign has been shown to be inadequate.</p>
<p>Case 2: Yield sign control (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r1-2/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R1-2.svg">R1-2</a>)</p>
<p>Note: Use yield sign operation where the curb return radius is 20 feet (10 miles per hour design speed) or greater and pedestrian traffic across the channelized right turn is low.</p></details></div>
