<p>Shared mobility parking: on-street</p>
<p>1. Parking meter heads, posts and signs shall be removed or adjusted as needed if shared mobility parking (SMP) is installed in a parking meter zone.</p>
<p>2. Rubberized wheel stops shall be used at the ends of the SMP zones if located next to on-street parking.</p>
<p>3. Minimum clearance from the top of driveway shall be 5&#x27;.</p>
<p>4. Minimum clearance from a fire hydrant shall be 15&#x27;.</p>
<p>5. SMP zones shall remain clear of bus zones.</p>
<p>6. SMP zones shall not be installed in peak hour lanes, red curb zones, or in parking restricted areas (i.e. Tow-Away No Stopping Any Time &quot;T.A.N.S.A.T.&quot;, No Parking Any Time &quot;N.P.A.T.&quot;, etc.)</p>
<p>7. Minimum clearance from two or more consecutive parking spaces shall be 5&#x27;.</p>
<p>8. SMP zones shall not be installed within 30&#x27; of a limit line at a traffic signal or within 25&#x27; of a &quot;Stop&quot; or &quot;Yield&quot; sign.</p>
<p>Shared mobility parking: sidewalk</p>
<p>9. A minimum of 5&#x27; of unobstructed sidewalk must be maintained for a pedestrian pathway.</p>
<p>10. Where there is no parking on the adjacent street, provide:</p>
<p>A. Minimum clearance of 1&#x27;-6&quot; from the face of curb to the edge of the SMP zone.</p>
<p>11. Where there is parking on the adjacent street, provide:</p>
<p>A. Minimum clearance of 3&#x27; from the face of curb to the edge of the SMP zone.</p>
<p>B. Minimum clearance of 4&#x27; from the face of curb to the edge of the SMP zone if the adjacent parking is a loading zone or disabled parking space.</p>
<p>C. Minimum clearance of 4&#x27; of unobstructed pathway at the ends of the SMP zone.</p>
<p>12. Minimum clearance of 5&#x27; from the marked crosswalk line or top of curb ramp.</p>
<p>13. Minimum clearance of 5&#x27; from the top of driveway.</p>
<p>14. Minimum clearance from a fire hydrant shall be 15&#x27;.</p>
<p>15. SMP zones shall remain clear of bus loading and unloading zones.</p>
<p>16. SMP zones shall not:</p>
<p>A. Block access to building entrances or exits.</p>
<p>B. Obstruct utility accesses such as manholes, pullboxes, street light poles, power poles, etc.</p>
<p>C. Obstruct the use of sidewalk furniture such as benches, newsstands, mailboxes, bus shelters, etc.</p>
<p>Miscellaneous</p>
<p>17. All pavement markings shall be skid-resistant.</p></details></div>

### Page 52 - Intersection Striping

<div class="pmeta">Drawings: <strong>S-405.0</strong> &middot; Topics: crosswalks, signals, parking-curb, striping, intersections</div>
<figure class="dfig"><a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ladot/mpp-531/p0052.webp" target="_blank"><img loading="lazy" src="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ladot/mpp-531/p0052.webp" alt="MPP 531 page 52 S-405.0 Intersection Striping"></a><figcaption>MPP 531, page 52 (scanned, cleaned and sharpened). Click to enlarge.</figcaption></figure>
<div class="pclean"><p class="psum"><b>Intersection Striping.</b> Plan layout and policy for dotted intersection striping (cat tracks) through intersections, including multiple left and right turns, trap right-turn lanes, added right-turn lanes and a special double turn detail. Cat tracks are 4 inch wide, about 1 ft long and 7 ft center to center, and match the color of the stripe they extend. The policy sheet sets where cat tracks begin and end, when the line may be offset 1 ft (with 20 ft or 12 ft minimum curb-side width), crosswalk widths at the receiving end, and the conditions that require longitudinal cat tracks.</p><details class="ocr" open><summary>Notes on this sheet (cleaned from the scan)</summary><p>Policy and criteria</p>
<p>General</p>
<p>1. Dotted intersection striping, more commonly known as &quot;cat tracks&quot; shall be used for all multiple turns and under special conditions for longitudinal striping, as shown in Note 11.</p>
<p>2. Cat tracks are 4&quot; in width and should be approximately one foot in length, 7&#x27; center to center (CA MUTCD <a class="sref dref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/markings/line-types/#detail-40" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/linetypes/detail-40.svg">Detail 40</a> and 41). They shall be the same color as the stripe they extend, except that cat tracks connecting to double yellow lines shall be 4&quot; yellow.</p>
<p>3. Solid intersection striping should be installed for single or double right turns where one of the lanes is a trap lane (see S-491.0). Trap lanes shall be separated from adjacent through lanes by a longitudinal line.</p>
<p>Multiple turns</p>
<p>4. The beginning-of-curve of cat tracks for left turns shall be tangent to the barrier line or its prolongation at or downstream of the interior crosswalk line. The end-of-curve shall be parallel to the receiving lane line or its prolongation at or upstream of the exterior crosswalk line and located as described in Note 8.</p>
<p>5. The beginning-of-curve of cat tracks for right turns should be at the radial extension of the beginning of the curb return and tangent to the barrier line. Where the curb lane width is sufficiently wide to allow a larger curve for the cat track than one beginning as described above, in order to provide an improved travel path, the beginning of curve may begin upstream of the BCR. When the barrier line at the beginning-of-curve is not parallel to the curb, the curve shall be tangent to the barrier line or its prolongation. The beginning of curve should be positioned to maintain a minimum width at the curb return equal to the approach width, or 12 feet, whichever is smaller. The portion of curve upstream of the exterior crosswalk line shall be a solid stripe. The end-of-curve shall be parallel to the receiving lane line and located as described in Note 8.</p>
<p>6. The cat track line may be offset 1 foot from the receiving lane line, to provide a wider reception width for the number one turn lane when: 1) the No.1 receiving lane is less than 11 feet; and 2) the curb lane width (or the combined No.2 and No.3 lane widths) equals or exceeds 21 feet with parking, or 13 feet with parking prohibited. When the offset criteria are met, the reception width for the number one lane (lane width plus offset) should be 12 feet where possible and the dimension between the cat track and curb shall equal or exceed 20 feet with parking, or 12 feet with parking prohibited.</p>
<p>7. Intersection striping for multiple turns generally should direct motorists to non-curb lanes whenever possible, since they may be blocked by parked vehicles and the radius would be smaller than necessary.</p>
<p>8. For multiple left turns, where there is no raised median separating the opposing traffic on the receiving end, the crosswalk on the receiving end should be at least 20 feet and the double yellow center line through that crosswalk may be deleted. Where there is a median island, the crosswalk on the receiving end should be 15 feet.</p>
<p>9. Where the distance between cat tracks for multiple left-turns in opposite directions is less than 30 feet, lead-lag left-turn or opposed signal phasing shall be used.</p>
<p>Longitudinal striping</p>
<p>10. Wherever feasible, the need for longitudinal cat tracks should be avoided by designing travel paths through the intersection parallel to raised medians or curb lines.</p>
<p>11. Special conditions where longitudinal cat tracks within the intersection are required include:</p>
<p>A. Where the offset in striping through the intersection is more than 2 feet.</p>
<p>B. Where opposing left turn lanes are offset to the right of each other.</p>
<p>C. Where the striping has a taper or a curve that begins within 50 feet of the approach to an intersection.</p>
<p>D. Where a taper or curve ends within 20 feet of the intersection.</p>
<p>E. Where the design speed associated with the radius of curvature of the travel path through the intersection equals or is less than the applicable speed limit.</p>
<p>F. At intersections with sharp crest vertical curvature where the departure striping is not continuously visible to approaching motorists.</p></details></div>
