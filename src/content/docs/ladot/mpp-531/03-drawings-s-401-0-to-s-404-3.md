<div class="pclean"><p class="psum"><b>Typical Major Highway Striping Treatments.</b> Typical striping layouts for major highways with a two-way left turn lane (open-ended and closed-ended), a raised median, and Class I and Class II highway sections with flare at intersections. Notes set minimum left turn pocket lengths at signalized intersections (100 ft at major and secondary highways, 60 ft at collector streets, 40 ft at local streets), 10 ft wide left turn channelization (12 ft where large vehicles are significant), and 60 ft or 80 ft reversals depending on posted speed. Bike lanes with all-day parking may replace a curb lane where the width is 13 ft or greater.</p><details class="ocr" open><summary>Notes on this sheet (cleaned from the PDF text)</summary><p>MAJOR HIGHWAYS (prior to 11-10-99)</p>
<p>100&#x27; R/W - 80&#x27; roadway</p>
<h5>Notes:</h5>
<p>1. Selection of length of left turn pocket lane depends on demand, spacing between intersections and location of critical driveways to be served by two-way left turn lanes (2WLTL). The desirable length at signalized intersections is at least 40&#x27; of storage per vehicle per signal cycle averaged during the peak hour. However, this desirable length often may not be feasible. Minimum lengths at signalized intersections on highways with continuous median channelization are:</p>
<p>100&#x27; at major highways</p>
<p>100&#x27; at secondary highways</p>
<p>60&#x27; at collector streets</p>
<p>40&#x27; at local streets</p>
<p>Minimum pocket lengths for major highways without continuous median channelization may be less than the above, depending on turning volumes and parking impacts.</p>
<p>2. In general, 10&#x27; wide left turn channelization should be used. However, a 12-foot median should be used if large vehicles are a significant portion of turning vehicles.</p>
<p>3. Utilize 60&#x27; reversals for posted speed limits of 35 mph and less, and 80&#x27; reversals for posted speed limits of 40 mph and greater. A 40&#x27; minimum reversal may be used in exceptional situations for critical driveways.</p>
<p>4. Selection of an open-ended (as shown in first drawing) versus closed ended (as shown in second drawing) 2WLTL is dependent, in part, on the proximity of a critical driveway to be served by a 2WLTL. Minimum distances to critical driveways are shown.</p>
<p>5. The painted reversal for a single left turn lane may be eliminated at a signalized intersection on a selective basis if spillover is recurrent and if it is not feasible to lengthen the left-turn pocket.</p>
<p>6. For new raised median installations, the width of the island should be limited to 8&#x27; to allow wider adjacent through lanes.</p>
<p>7. The 4&#x27; wide island adjacent to the left turn lane should be removed, where feasible, so as to avoid left turn lane offsets which reduce left turn driver visibility of approaching through vehicles. Where an island adjacent to the left-turn lane is proposed, or is to be retained, protected left-turn phasing shall be considered. See Standard Plan S-497.0.</p>
<p>8. Third through lanes should be installed when conditions so justify. Right turn only lanes may be installed instead of the third through lane, as appropriate.</p>
<p>9. Bike lanes with all day parking may be installed in place of a curb lane or right turn lane where the width is 13&#x27; or greater (or 12&#x27; in selected cases).</p>
<p>Drawings: open-ended 2WLTL with 60&#x27; or 80&#x27; reversal and 30&#x27; min. to a critical driveway, and closed-ended 2WLTL with taper, 100&#x27; to 120&#x27; transition and 70&#x27; min. to a critical driveway. Stopping prohibition shown along the curb. Lane widths shown include 10&#x27; to 12&#x27; median and 11&#x27; to 13&#x27; curb lanes. 100&#x27; R/W, 80&#x27; roadway (40&#x27; each side of centerline).</p></details></div>
