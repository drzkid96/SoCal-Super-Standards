<div class="pclean"><p class="psum"><b>Operation and Design of Signalized Jogged Intersections (Sheet 1 of 12, Operational Challenges for the Minor Street Approaches).</b> Sheet 1 introduces the S-100.0 series on signalized jogged intersections and illustrates the operational problems on the minor street approaches. It shows a right jog and a left jog and lists issues such as pedestrians and opposing vehicles not being seen early and opposing left turns interlocking. It refers the reader to Sheet 2 for selecting a design.</p><details class="sdesc" open><summary>What this sheet shows</summary><p>Sheet 1 of 12, Drawing No. S-100.0, City of Los Angeles Department of Transportation. Title: Operation and Design of Signalized Jogged Intersections. Approved June 24, 2010 (Rita L. Robinson, General Manager, signed for). Drawn EC, 6-10-10. Sheet caption: Operational Challenges for the Minor Street Approaches to Signalized Jogged Intersections.</p>
<p>Right Jog (upper diagram): Plan view of a jogged intersection where the minor street legs enter the arterial (running east to west) at offset locations, labeled &#x27;Right Jog&#x27;. The intersection box is outlined with crosswalks on the west and east sides and short stop-bar crosswalks on the minor street legs. Three vehicles are shown with dashed path lines crossing in the middle of the box, with a circled S symbol, representing a conflict between the opposing left-turning and through paths. A pedestrian pair is shown in the east crosswalk.</p>
<p>Left Jog (lower diagram): Same layout mirrored, labeled &#x27;Left Jog&#x27;, with the minor street legs offset the other way. A left-turning vehicle with its path and a second vehicle, a circled S, and pedestrians in the east crosswalk are shown.</p>
<p>Bullets beside the Right Jog diagram: pedestrians might not be seen early-on by right-turning motorists, opposing vehicles might not be seen early-on, opposing arterial left turns might interlock. Bullets beside the Left Jog diagram are in the notes.</p></details><details class="ocr" open><summary>Notes on this sheet (cleaned from the scan)</summary><p>1. Right Jog: Pedestrians might not be seen early-on by right-turning motorists. Opposing vehicles might not be seen early-on. Opposing arterial left turns might interlock.</p>
<p>2. Left Jog: Pedestrians might not be seen early-on by left-turning motorists. Opposing vehicles might not be seen early-on. Opposing left turns interlock. Difficult to determine if motorist is making a left turn or jogged through move. So-called opposing moves momentarily can be positioned at approximately right angles to each other.</p>
<p>3. Operational Challenges for the Minor Street Approaches to Signalized Jogged Intersections.</p>
<p>4. To select the most appropriate design for minimizing the above challenges see Sheet 2.</p>

### Page 22 - Operation and Design of Signalized Jogged Intersections (Sheet 2 of 12, Design Selection Procedure)

<div class="pmeta">Drawings: <strong>S-100.0</strong> &middot; Topics: crosswalks, signals, striping, intersections</div>
<figure class="dfig"><a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ladot/mpp-531/p0022.webp" target="_blank"><img loading="lazy" src="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ladot/mpp-531/p0022.webp" alt="MPP 531 page 22 S-100.0 Operation and Design of Signalized Jogged Intersections"></a><figcaption>MPP 531, page 22 (scanned, cleaned and sharpened). <div class="pclean"><p class="psum"><b>Operation and Design of Signalized Jogged Intersections (Sheet 2 of 12, Design Selection Procedure).</b> Sheet 2 defines the geometry terms theta 1, theta 2, X1, X2 and D and gives a four-step procedure to pick among the jogged intersection designs on Sheets 3 through 12. The choice depends on left or right jog, internal distance D against 40 ft, sight angles against 20 degrees, and X distances against 50 ft.</p><details class="sdesc" open><summary>What this sheet shows</summary><p>Sheet 2 of 12, Drawing No. S-100.0, City of Los Angeles Department of Transportation.</p>
<p>Definitions with diagram: Three definitions at the top (see notes) are tied to a schematic plan view of a jogged intersection. A vehicle on the north minor street at left and a vehicle on the south minor street at right are shown. Dashed sight lines run diagonally across the intersection between them, one angle theta 2 (T2) at the left vehicle, measured between the straight-ahead line and the line to the back of the opposing vehicle, and theta 1 (T1) at the right vehicle. X2 is dimensioned along the arterial at the lower left, from the crosswalk or limit line at the far side of the first intersecting leg to the signal pole, and X1 is dimensioned at the upper right the same way. D is dimensioned twice (upper and lower) as the internal storage distance between the hypothetical interior limit lines drawn as vertical lines. Signal poles are drawn with mastarm symbols and heads with arrows.</p>
<p>Selection procedure: A text list gives four steps (see notes). Below it is a note on lighting. No tables.</p></details><details class="ocr" open><summary>Notes on this sheet (cleaned from the scan)</summary><p>1. Theta 1 and theta 2 (symbols T1 and T2) = angle between motorist looking straight ahead and back of opposing vehicle.</p>
<p>2. X1 and X2 = Distance along arterial from the crosswalk line or limit line to the signal pole or BCR at the far side of the first intersecting leg.</p>
<p>3. D = Internal storage distance between real or hypothetical interior limit lines. For minor offset D may be a small or negative number.</p>
<p>4. To select the most appropriate design:</p>
<p>1) Determine if there is a Left Jog or a Right Jog. See Sheets 3, 5, 7, 9, 10 and 11 for left jogged intersections and Sheets 4, 6, 8 and 12 for right jogged intersections.</p>
<p>2) Determine D. If D is 40&#x27; or more then interior limit lines are required. If D is less than 40&#x27; then the jog is operated as a single intersection. See Sheets 10, 11 and 12 for intersections with interior limit lines and Sheets 3 through 9 for single intersection operation.</p>
<p>3) Determine theta 1 and theta 2. If theta 1 or theta 2 is greater than 20 degrees and D is less than 40&#x27; then some form of opposed phasing is required for the minor street. See Sheets 7 through 9 for situations requiring opposed phasing. See Sheets 3 through 6 for situations for which concurrent phasing is adequate. Note that for Sheets 4 and 6 opposed phasing is required if diagonal crosswalks are not feasible, due to the unexpected presence of pedestrians crossing the path of right-turning motorists.</p>
<p>4) Determine X1 and X2. If X1 and X2 are 50&#x27; or more and D is less than 40&#x27; then only internal mastarms are to be used on the arterial street. If X1 and X2 are less than 50&#x27; then only external mastarms are to be used on the arterial street. See Sheets 3, 4, 7, and 8 for internal mastarm designs and Sheets 5, 6 and 9 for external mastarm designs.</p>
<p>5. Note: The drawings on Sheets 3 through 12 are not intended to show the required safety lighting design. Final lighting designs may not include the depicted mastarm lighting and likely will include other lighting locations not shown.</p>
<p>6. &#x27;BCR&#x27; is as printed. The printed text of step 4 reads &#x27;X1 and X&#x27; in the less than 50&#x27; condition (apparently X2).</p></details></div>
