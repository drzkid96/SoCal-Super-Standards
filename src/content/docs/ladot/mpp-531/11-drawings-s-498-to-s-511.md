<div class="pclean"><p class="psum"><b>Restricted Sight Distance Criteria for Protected Left-Turn Phasing.</b> Figure 6 on sheet 7 of 7 of S-497.0 shows frequently restricted sight distance where moderate opposing left-turn demand combines with a right horizontal curve. Either <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w1-6/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W1-6.svg">W1-6</a> or <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w1-8/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W1-8.svg">W1-8</a> signs are used for this purpose.</p>

Calculate the arc length by the formula L = 2 pi R Theta / 360, and determine if <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w1-6/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W1-6.svg">W1-6</a> or <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w1-8/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W1-8.svg">W1-8</a> signs are to be used.</p>
<p>5. If W 1-8 signs are to be used, the spacing is determined by the 20 degree half cone-of-vision, where three should be seen within this angle. Thus, each sign is 6.7 degrees apart, which corresponds to a 13.3 degree central angle. The maximum spacing is calculated from the formula S = 2 pi R (13.3) / 360 and rounded values are shown in the table.</p>
<p>6. Adjust S downward from the value derived from the formula calculation or table, where the arc length is not an integer multiple of the value. Along a given arc length S may vary somewhat due to trees, driveways or other spacing obstructions.</p></details></div>
