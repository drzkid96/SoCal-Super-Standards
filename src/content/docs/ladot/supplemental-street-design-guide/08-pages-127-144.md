### Page 133 - Bus Bulbs

<figure class="dfig"><a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ladot/supplemental-street-design-guide/p0133.webp"><img loading="lazy" src="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ladot/supplemental-street-design-guide/p0133.webp" alt="Supp. Street Design Guide page 133 "></a><figcaption>Supp. Street Design Guide, page 133.</figcaption></figure>
<details class="ocr"><summary>Drawing Notes &amp; Specifications</summary><p>1. A minimum 5-foot wide by 8-foot deep boarding and alighting area, with a maximum slope of 2% in any direction, is required at forward loading area adjacent to the bus door. The 8-foot depth may include the adjacent curbline, but is exclusive of any railing or battered curb space. As such the physical width of the bus bulb may need to exceed 8-feet. A 4-foot minimum clear accessible route must be provided between the boarding and alighting area and the sidewalk. An accessible route must also be provided between any provided bus shelters and the boarding and alighting area. If a bus bulb serves multiple transit vehicle stops simultaneously, boarding and alighting areas must be provided at each vehicle door and accessible routes provided accordingly.</p>
<p>2. The minimum bus bulb length is identified in the table on sheet 13, but the preferable length is based on the length of the bus(es) expected to use the bus stop. If multiple buses are expected to use the stop at the same time, the length should be based on the bus length(s) with 20-feet of separation between the buses. The length of a bus bulb is exclusive of all pedestrian ramps.</p>
<p>3. Length of sidewalk replacement will vary based the width of the bus bulb, existing roadway cross slopes, curb reveal, and the slope and grade of existing sidewalks.</p>
<p>4. Modifications to existing street paving, cold plane and overlay asphalt or completely reconstruct paving as necessary to restore a smooth transition and street crown (Standard Plan S-433, similar), match paving materials and thickness, see S-410 for standard curbs and gutters, and warp new gutter to join invert at inlet. Curb with gutter may be used along the street edge where necessary to improve constructability or to manage stormwater flow.</p>
<p>5. Construct concrete sidewalk when specified on plan. Sidewalk minimum clearance 48&quot;.</p>
<p>6. All concrete and sidewalks shall be Class 520-C-2500 Portland cement concrete. Install contraction, expansion and weakened plane joints in concrete per S-480 and S-430.</p>
<p>7. Minimum relative compaction of earthwork under new paving shall be per SSPWC. See S-480 for further requirements.</p>
<p>8. Existing curb height generally varies from 5 inches to 8 inches.</p>
<p>9. In areas with an existing 8 inch curb height in place, a curb height less than 8 inches may be used to obtain traversable slopes and drainage towards the gutter. Curb heights of at least 6 inches are preferred and may require partial or full roadway regrading. Curb heights of 4 inches or less may be used to achieve positive drainage without roadway regrading. The use of curb heights below 5&quot; requires the approval of the City Engineer.</p>
<p>10. Slope to remain traversable and drain towards the gutter. Slopes not to exceed 2%.</p>
<p>11. See Standard Plan S-442 for details involving curb ramps.</p>
<p>12. Overall curb height will vary based on existing grades, curb reveals, and opportunities to adjust the curbline elevation at the edge of road. Ensure that the curb depth extends at least 8 inches below finished grade. For curb reveal heights that exceed 9 inches, design curb as an isolated reinforced concrete retaining curb.</p>
<p>13. A handrail is required if the curb reveal (drop-off from the bus bulb to the adjacent bike lane) exceeds 8 inches. However, a handrail may be considered for any curb reveal or adjacent to sidewalk level bike lanes to channelize pedestrians to the crosswalks.</p>
<p>14. Pedestrian crossings from the sidewalk to bus bulb may be provided at intersections, at locations separate from the intersection, and/or at the ends furthest from intersections. Two pedestrian crossings are preferable based on natural pedestrian desire lines.</p>
<p>15. Adjustments to the bike lane elevation may be necessary if roadway cross slopes exceed 2%. If adjusting grade, ensure that positive drainage is maintained along the curbline or provide additional drainage structures.</p>
<p>16. The intersection corner radius should be established following the corner radius policy identified in the Supplemental Street Design Guide.</p>
<p>17. Valleys created from reverse slopes shall be drained back to the curb when possible to avoid ponding on the bus bulb. Trench drains with ADA compliant grates are also appropriate. See curb extension detail for design guidance.</p>
<p>18. As depicted in Alternative A, sidewalk panel replacement is not required to be continuous along a bus bulb. 4&#x27; wide (minimum) accessible routes can be considered to connect the sidewalk and bus bulb. In these scenarios, the maximum slope of this connection may be increased from 2% to 5%. Ensure that these accessible routes are located to coincide with boarding and alighting areas and provide access to curb ramps and bus shelters as applicable.</p>
<p>19. The design depicted in plan view shows a street-elevation Class IV bike lane. Based on existing roadway cross slopes and desired bike lane elevation, an intermediate- or sidewalk-elevation Class IV bike lane may be provided. Sheets 2 and 7 through 12 depict a variety of bike lane elevations which can be considered.</p>
<p>20. Where positive drainage cannot be achieved along curblines, install side-opening catch basin per Standard Plan S-351 and connect to existing stormwater conveyance system.</p>
<p>21. At locations where the sidewalk is less than 5 feet in width, a constrained bus bulb should be considered.</p>
<p>22. The bike lane surface should contrast with the adjacent sidewalk and bus bulb by providing green colored surfacing approved by LADOT.</p>
<p>23. A minimum 12-foot x 4-foot clear space, with a maximum slope of 2% in any direction is recommended at all rear bus doors. If the rear door serves as the wheelchair accessible loading door, a 5-foot x 8-foot loading area is required.</p></details>

### Page 136 - Crossing Islands

<figure class="dfig"><a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ladot/supplemental-street-design-guide/p0136.webp"><img loading="lazy" src="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ladot/supplemental-street-design-guide/p0136.webp" alt="Supp. Street Design Guide page 136 "></a><figcaption>Supp. Street Design Guide, page 136.</figcaption></figure>
<details class="ocr"><summary>Drawing Notes &amp; Specifications</summary><p>1. A minimum of 6 foot width is required to provide pedestrian refuge. Widths of 8 to 10 feet are prefred [sic] where pedestrian volumes are high, where people with mobility devices cross, or where bicyclists cross. The minimum width is 4 feet, below which a hardened centerline is preferred where it is desired to slow left-turning motorists.</p>
<p>2. The total length of the crossing islands and crosswalk shall be limited to 30 feet or less on streets with only one through lane in each direction.</p>
<p>3. At signalized intersections, if the crossing island is at least 6 feet wide, then a pedestrian push button may be provided within the crossing island with LADOT approval. Push buttons should be located at the center of the island, but wider islands may necessitate two push buttons closer to the edge of the island. Push buttons should be located within 18 inches from curb face. If crossing islands are less than 6 feet wide, then pedestrian crossing signals must be timed to allow complete crossing and no push buttons shall be located in the crossing island.</p>
<p>4. Detectable warning surfaces shall be used if crossing island serves as a pedestrian refuge. Detectable warning shall be 36&quot; deep when the crossing island is 8&#x27; or more in width. Between 6&#x27; and 8&#x27;, the detectable warning shall be 24&quot; deep. The detectable warning shall be placed at the edges of the pedestrian island or cut-through median, and shall be separated by 24&quot; minimum of walking surface without detectable warnings.</p>
<p>5. If the pedestrian median opening is greater than 9 feet wide, then a bollard may be considered to prevent vehicles from encroaching into the pedestrian refuge space. Install bollards in detectable warning surface (DWS) at 2&#x27; maximum, allowing a minimum of 4&#x27; path in between bollards. From the flow line narrowing the pedestrian median opening to less than the crosswalk width may be considered in order to create sufficient space for an approach nose.</p>
<p>6. When diagonal ramps are present, an approach nose may be added by narrowing the crossing width (see note 5). When this is not desirable, a rubberized mountable approach nose shall be used instead.</p>
<p>7. Landscaping of crossing islands shall follow Section E 467.3 of the Bureau of Engineering Street Design Manual.</p>
<p>8. Raised pavement markers (RPM&#x27;s) one-way clear (white) or one-way amber (yellow) spaced 2&#x27; center-to-center may be installed on the crossing island depending on its location. See LADOT Standard Drawing S-525.0.</p></details>

### Page 144 - Raised Crossings

<figure class="dfig"><a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ladot/supplemental-street-design-guide/p0144.webp"><img loading="lazy" src="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ladot/supplemental-street-design-guide/p0144.webp" alt="Supp. Street Design Guide page 144 "></a><figcaption>Supp. Street Design Guide, page 144.</figcaption></figure>
<details class="ocr"><summary>Drawing Notes &amp; Specifications</summary><p>1. The width of the top of raised crosswalks should match the width of the connecting sidewalk, shared use path, or desired crosswalk, but not less than 10 ft in width.</p>
<p>2. Bottom edge of raised crossing approach ramp to be aligned with curb line of intersecting street to minimize impacts to surface stormwater flowline.</p>
<p>3. Modifications to existing street paving, cold plane and overlay asphalt or completely reconstruct paving as necessary to restore a smooth transition and street crown (Standard Plan S-433, similar), match paving materials and thickness, see S-410 for standard curbs and gutters, and warp new gutter to join invert at inlet.</p>
<p>4. Where positive drainage cannot be achieved, install side-opening catch basin per Standard Plan S-351, and connect to existing stormwater conveyance system.</p>
<p>5. A raised crossing set back from the main street may create a new low point in front of the raised crossing. Drainage and catch basin placement will need to be considered.</p>
<p>6. Place crosswalk 2 ft (MIN) from poles, hydrants, or other vertical obstructions.</p>
<p>7. The Case C section (see Sheet 5) is appropriate on roadways with parking. The raised crossing should reach full height outside of active travel lanes. The Case 3 section [as printed] may not be appropriate on abnormally crowned roadways, where the cross slope exceeds 3.3%.</p>
<p>8. For stop controlled side street, the maximum change in grade is 8%. For uncontrolled or signalized intersections, or for mid-block locations, the maximum change in grade is 5%.</p>
<p>9. Crosswalk cross slopes should be no greater than 2%, however, at locations without stop control the cross slopes may be 5% max. and at midblock locations the cross slope may match the existing street grade.</p></details>
