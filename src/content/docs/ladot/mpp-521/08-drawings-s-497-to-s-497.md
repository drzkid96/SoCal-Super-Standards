<div class="pclean"><p class="psum"><b>Restricted Sight Distance Criteria for Protected Left-Turn Phasing.</b> Supplements Table 1 of Section 521 and sets when restricted sight distance justifies protected left-turn phasing at signalized intersections. Table A gives minimum sight distances for permissive left turns by speed (15 to 55 mi/hr, using the speed limit or advisory speed plus 5 mi/hr) and number of opposing through lanes, based on an acceptable gap of (5.0 + 0.5n) seconds. Figures 1 to 6 show restrictions from horizontal curves, vertical alignment, physical obstructions, and negatively offset left turn lanes, including the 10,000 ADT and 15% queue-not-clearing thresholds and the 40V/N 85th percentile queue estimate.</p><details class="sdesc" open><summary>What this sheet shows</summary><p>Figure 6 (sheet 7 of 7, Drawing No. S-497.0, City of Los Angeles Department of Transportation): Example of Frequently Restricted Sight Distance Due To Moderate Opposing Left Turn Demand in Combination with Right Horizontal Curvature. Plan view of an arterial intersection where the arterial curves to the right on the upstream (opposing) approach, with a W and X table at the left.</p>
<p>Plan view: The curved approach has a left turn pocket separated from the through lanes by a hatched median (shown as a double line). Several vehicles are lined up in the pocket with lane width W dimensioned at several points and lateral offset X dimensioned at the stop bar and beside the vehicles. Two sight lines are labeled LINE A (defined by the first vehicle in the queue) and LINE B (defined by the last vehicle in an 85th percentile queue). Sight distance leaders drawn around the curve are labeled &#x27;See Table A for 5.5 sec.&#x27;, &#x27;See Table A for 6.0 sec.&#x27;, &#x27;See Table A 5.5 sec. maximum&#x27; and &#x27;See Table A 6.0 sec. maximum&#x27;. Inside the intersection box, the waiting left-turn vehicle is dimensioned with 3&#x27; and 1&#x27; lateral offsets, 8&#x27; vehicle length, a 10&#x27; separation to the opposing vehicle, a 1&#x27; offset and a 5&#x27; dimension at the opposing vehicle, and a dashed &#x27;Turning path for 10&#x27; separation&#x27;. A left-turn arrow is drawn at the bottom.</p>
<p>Table (W and X): W, ft: 9, 10, 11, 12 and X, ft: 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0 respectively.</p>
<p>Text block: The paragraph at the upper left explains Line A, Line B and the queue length estimate (see notes).</p></details><details class="ocr" open><summary>Notes on this sheet (cleaned from the scan)</summary><p>1. Figure 6: Example of frequently restricted sight distance due to moderate opposing left turn demand in combination with right horizontal curvature.</p>
<p>2. Left turning motorists can see downstream of Line A (which is defined by the first vehicle in a queue) and upstream of Line B which is defined by the last vehicle in an 85th percentile queue. They cannot, however, see between Lines A and B. If opposing vehicles can be seen at the required sight distances, shown in Table A, either downstream of Line A or upstream of Line B, then protected left turn phasing is not required. If however, the required sight distances lie between Lines A and B then visibility is inadequate for left turning decisions and protected left turn phasing is required. In the absence of documented observations, the 85th percentile queue length, in feet, is assumed to be 40 V/N, where V equals the peak hour left turn volume and N equals the number of signal cycles per hour.</p>
<p>3. Sight distance leaders: See Table A for 6.0 seconds (sec) maximum. See Table A for 5.5 sec maximum. See Table A for 6.0 sec. See Table A for 5.5 sec.</p>
<p>4. Table, W (ft) and X (ft): W = 9 and X = 1.5; W = 10 and X = 2.0; W = 11 and X = 2.5; W = 12 and X = 3.0.</p>
