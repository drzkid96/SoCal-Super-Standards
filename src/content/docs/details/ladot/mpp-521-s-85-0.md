<p class="dwg-sum" data-pagefind-weight="6">Explains how to check whether a signal head is seen by drivers on conflicting approaches, using visibility nomographs for standard, beveled, long visor and programmed visibility (PV) heads. The nomograph is placed with its centerline along the assumed alignment of the head to see whether the 50 percent lens visibility angle crosses the limit line of a conflicting approach. The sheets here show the standard visor nomograph (54 degree angle of 50 percent visibility), the beveled visor nomographs (40 degrees), an Arden Street and Diagonal Street beveled visor example, and a Clarita Avenue and Diagonal Street programmed visibility example.</p>

</details><details class="ocr" open><summary>Notes on this sheet (cleaned from the PDF text)</summary><p>Lateral visibility: beveled visor application</p>
<p>Arden St. and Diagonal St.</p>
<p>A = 54 degrees. Angle of 50% lens visibility of a standard signal head. The signal head shown is intended for Arden Street motorists. However, it can be seen too easily by Diagonal Street motorists, since the Angle A shading crosses that limit line.</p>
<p>B = 40 degrees. Angle of 50% lens visibility of a left-beveled visor signal head. Since the Angle B shading does not cross the limit line on Diagonal Street, a left-beveled visor would adequately restrict lateral visibility.</p>
<p>Sheet 9 of 11.</p></details>
