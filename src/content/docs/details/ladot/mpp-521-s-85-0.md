<p class="dwg-sum" data-pagefind-weight="6">Sheet 4 of 11 of LADOT standard S-85.0, a nomograph for a standard visor signal head. Place the center line along the assumed head alignment to see whether the 50 percent lens visibility lines (27 degrees each side, 54 degrees total) cross the limit line of a conflicting approach. The 0 percent visibility lines are at 45 degrees each side.</p>

Limit lines (stop lines) are drawn across each approach.</p>
<p>Signal head and nomograph overlay: A dot at the corner near the top marks the signal head, with a line labeled Center of Signal Head Alignment running down along Arden Street toward the approach, and an arrow at the head. Two shaded fans are overlaid from the head. The wider fan is marked A (54 degrees) and the narrower fan marked B (40 degrees), each with an arc dimension. Fan A crosses the limit line on Diagonal Street. Fan B stays clear of that limit line.</p>
<p>Text blocks (right side): A = 54 degrees: Angle of 50% lens visibility of a standard signal head. The signal head shown is intended for Arden Street motorists. However, it can be seen too easily by Diagonal Street motorists, since the Angle A shading crosses that limit line. B = 40 degrees: Angle of 50% lens visibility of a left-beveled visor signal head. Since the Angle B shading does not cross the limit line on Diagonal Street a left-beveled visor would adequately restrict lateral visibility.</p>
<p>Caption under the plan: Lateral Visibility: Beveled Visor Application, followed by two small head symbols (an arrow and a beveled visor block, shown for the left or right beveled head).</p>
