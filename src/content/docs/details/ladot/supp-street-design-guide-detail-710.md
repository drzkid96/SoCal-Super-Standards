<p class="dwg-sum" data-pagefind-weight="6">Plan of a four-leg mini roundabout with a mountable center island, mountable splitter islands, pedestrian refuges, curb ramps and non-walkable landscaped corner areas. A typical section between crosswalks shows a 5 ft minimum sidewalk beside a 3.0 ft non-walkable cobble landscape strip with 9 in curbs. Eight notes cover island corner radii, mountable and painted islands, curb ramps, island paving, detectable warning and non-walkable surfaces.</p>

### Sheet 1 of 2 (document page 147)

<figure class="dfig"><a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ladot/supplemental-street-design-guide/p0147.webp" target="_blank"><img loading="lazy" src="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ladot/supplemental-street-design-guide/p0147.webp" alt="Detail 710 sheet 1"></a><figcaption>Supp. Street Design Guide Detail 710, sheet 1. <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ladot/supplemental-street-design-guide/09-pages-145-162/#p147">Open in the document</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<details class="sdesc" open><summary>What this sheet shows</summary><p>Plan view: A four-leg intersection in plan with a center island (see Note 6) shown as a circle with a thin ring around it, and a splitter island on each leg between the crosswalk and the circulating roadway. Dashed gray arcs show the circulating path. Splitter island corner radius on exit, see Notes 1 and 3. Splitter island corner radius on approach, see Notes 1 and 2. Splitter island width varies by site conditions, see Note 4. On the left leg, dimensions are given: splitter island 15 ft MIN, crosswalk 10 ft (TYP), and 20 ft from the crosswalk to the end of the splitter island. An 11 ft to 14 ft dimension is shown between the end of the splitter island and the corner curb return. Curb ramp type varies, see S-442. Pedestrian refuge, see Note 7, is the gap between the crosswalk bars through the splitter island. Splitter island, see Note 6. Each corner has a landscaped non-walkable area drawn with cobble stones, see Note 8, and 9 in curb height. Crosswalks are drawn as gray transverse bars at each leg. Section cut markers A/2 (through the center island), B/2 (through a splitter island) and C/1 (through a corner non-walkable area) are drawn on the plan.</p>
<p>Section C (callout 1), Typical Section Between Crosswalks, Non-Walkable with Raised Landscaped Area, N.T.S.: From left: sidewalk, 5 ft (MIN), with a slope arrow labeled VARIES. A 9 in curb height separates the sidewalk from the 3.0 ft non-walkable landscaped area filled with cobble stones. A second 9 in curb height at the street side, on an integral curb and gutter, then the street pavement. Callouts: 9 in curb height (2 locations), landscaped area, cobble stones, integral curb and gutter.</p>
<p>Title block: Mini Roundabouts, Supplemental Design Guide, Detail 710, Sheet 1 of 2.</p></details>
<details class="ocr" open><summary>Notes on this sheet (cleaned from the PDF text)</summary><p>1. All splitter island corners use minimum 1 ft radius.</p>
<p>2. Splitter island corner on approach use small radius (MIN 1 ft) to encourage entry deflection.</p>
<p>3. Splitter island corner on exit use larger radius to minimize damage from impacts.</p>
<p>4. Mountable splitter islands are preferred as shown. Painted islands may be used when site conditions lead to splitter islands less than 50 SF in size. Coordinate with DOT.</p>
<p>5. Install new curb ramps and crosswalks per Standard Plan S-442. Existing ramps within the circulating lane must be removed.</p>
<p>6. PCC pavement within islands shall be distinct from the roadway and sidewalks. PCC application may use integral color, and surface texture may also be applied.</p>
<p>7. If the splitter island is at least 6 feet wide, detectable warning shall be used within the refuge area. Detectable warning shall be 36 in deep when the splitter island is 8 ft or more in width. Between 6 ft and 8 ft, the detectable warning shall be 24 in deep.</p>
<p>8. A non-walkable surface or curb should be installed at the corners to prevent pedestrians from crossing at the intersection.</p></details>

### Sheet 2 of 2 (document page 148)

<figure class="dfig"><a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ladot/supplemental-street-design-guide/p0148.webp" target="_blank"><img loading="lazy" src="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ladot/supplemental-street-design-guide/p0148.webp" alt="Detail 710 sheet 2"></a><figcaption>Supp. Street Design Guide Detail 710, sheet 2. <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ladot/supplemental-street-design-guide/09-pages-145-162/#p148">Open in the document</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<details class="sdesc" open><summary>What this sheet shows</summary><p>Mountable Integral Curb and Gutter: Cross section of a 12 in deep concrete curb and gutter. From the island side: a 5 in flat top, then a 2 in sloped face that drops 3 in with an R 1/2 in rounded edge at its toe, then a 6 in wide gutter at the curb line. Small 1/4 in dimensions are shown at the top of the island side edge and at the gutter lip. The curb line is marked at the base of the sloped face. The label ISLAND SIDE marks the left edge.</p>
<p>Section A, Center Island and Section B, Splitter Island (callout 1, one drawing for both): A cross section of the island on top of the pavement. At left, existing asphalt (6 in HMA) is removed for 2 ft, then a mountable curb. The island surface is 8 in PCC (see Note 3) over 6 in MIN untreated base material, sloping 1% MIN. down from the center high point (see Note 1) on each side. Dimension A across the island is labeled: for Section A, for diameter dimensions of the center island, see Sheet 3. For Section B, the width of island varies, see plan view dimensions. On the right, the island ends at a mountable curb and meets existing pavement. Callouts: remove asphalt, mountable curb, 1% MIN., see Note 1, 6 in HMA, 6 in MIN untreated base material, 8 in PCC see Note 3, existing pavement.</p>
<p>Title block: Mini Roundabouts, Supplemental Design Guide, Detail 710, Sheet 2 of 2.</p></details>
<details class="ocr" open><summary>Notes on this sheet (cleaned from the PDF text)</summary><p>1. Center of island typically designed as high point. In certain locations a constant slope across island may be desirable. Final dimensions to be determined by Engineer.</p>
<p>2. Mountable splitter islands are preferred, as shown. Painted islands may be used when site conditions lead to splitter islands less than 50 SF in size.</p>
<p>3. PCC pavement within islands shall be distinct from the roadway and sidewalks. PCC application may use integral color, and surface texture may also be applied.</p>
<p>Section A callout: A. For diameter dimensions of the center island, see Sheet 3. Section B callout: A. Width of island varies. See plan view dimensions.</p></details>
