<p class="dwg-sum" data-pagefind-weight="6">Gives the method for placing supplemental curve warning signs on the outside of a curve where the curve design speed is at least 10 mi/hr below the speed limit. <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w1-6/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W1-6.svg">W1-6</a> signs with <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/om1-3/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/OM1-3.svg">OM1-3</a> markers are used on short curves (arc length under 100 ft), and <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w1-8/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W1-8.svg">W1-8</a> chevron signs on long curves (100 ft or more), spaced so that three can be seen at all times. Maximum average spacing S depends on the outside curve radius R, from 25 ft at R = 100 ft up to 100 ft at R of 450 ft or more.</p>
<p class="dwg-topics">Topics: <a class="chip" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/topics/striping-intersections/">Lane lines, striping and intersection markings</a></p>

</details><details class="ocr" open><summary>Notes on this sheet (cleaned from the scan)</summary><p>Notes</p>
<p>1. Supplemental curve warning signing may be used along any curve and should be used where the design speed of the curve is at least 10 miles per hour below the speed limit. Either <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w1-6/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W1-6.svg">W1-6</a> or <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w1-8/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W1-8.svg">W1-8</a> signs are used for this purpose.</p>
<p>2. <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w1-6/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W1-6.svg">W1-6</a> signs (in combination with <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/om1-3/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/OM1-3.svg">OM1-3</a> signs) are used for &quot;short&quot; curves. One installation is posted on the outside of the curve facing each direction near the tangent of the curve. For purposes of this standard, &quot;short&quot; curves have arc lengths less than 100 feet.</p>
<p>3. <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w1-8/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W1-8.svg">W1-8</a> signs are used for &quot;long&quot; curves. Three signs must be seen in each direction at all times throughout the curve. They are installed on the outside of the curve. For purposes of this standard, &quot;long&quot; curves have arc lengths of 100 feet or more.</p>
<p>4. Determine R and Theta of the curve for the outside curb line of the street. Calculate the arc length by the formula L = 2 pi R Theta / 360, and determine if <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w1-6/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W1-6.svg">W1-6</a> or <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w1-8/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W1-8.svg">W1-8</a> signs are to be used.</p>
<p>5. If <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w1-8/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W1-8.svg">W1-8</a> signs are to be used, the spacing is determined by the 20 degree half cone of vision, where three should be seen within this angle. Thus, each sign is 6.7 degrees apart, which corresponds to a 13.3 degree central angle. The maximum spacing is calculated from the formula S = 2 pi R (13.3) / 360 and rounded values are shown in the table.</p>
<p>6. Adjust S downward from the value derived from the formula calculation or table where the arc length is not an integer multiple of the value. Along a given arc length S may vary somewhat due to trees, driveways or other obstructions.</p></details>
