<p class="dwg-sum" data-pagefind-weight="6">First sheet of five on channelized right turns. It gives the criteria for the two simplest controls and shows an intersection plan for each. Case 1 uses an <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r1-1/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R1-1.svg">R1-1</a> stop sign where the curb return radius is under 20 ft, pedestrian traffic is high, cross-traffic visibility is restricted or a yield sign has proved inadequate. Case 2 uses an <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r1-2/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R1-2.svg">R1-2</a> yield sign where the radius is 20 ft or more and pedestrian traffic is low. Approved February 13, 2006. The merge, added lane cases and tables are on later sheets.</p>

<details class="ocr" open><summary>Notes on this sheet (cleaned from the PDF text)</summary><p>CASE 1: STOP SIGN CONTROL. Note: Use Stop sign operation where the curb return radius is less than 20 feet (10 miles per hour design speed), pedestrian traffic across the channelized right turn is high, cross-traffic visibility is restricted, or where the use of a Yield sign has been shown to be inadequate.</p>
<p>CASE 2: YIELD SIGN CONTROL. Note: Use Yield sign operation where the curb return radius is 20 feet (10 miles per hour design speed) or greater and pedestrian traffic across the channelized right turn is low.</p>
