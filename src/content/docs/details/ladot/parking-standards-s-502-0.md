<p class="dwg-sum" data-pagefind-weight="6">Table 1 from LADOT MPP Section 531 (May 2020, page 15) listing common striping and marking components, where each is applied, and the matching California MUTCD figure. It covers skip, partial passing and double yellow centerlines, striped medians, two-way left-turn lanes, left edge and channelization lines, cigar noses, reversals and left edge cross hatch. A lead-in paragraph says the department allows certain signs under S-502.0 with a Street Use Permit but does not inventory or maintain them.</p>

<p>Table 1. Application of Striping and Pavement Markings</p>
<p>Striping/marking component | Application | Reference to California MUTCD</p>
<p>Skip Centerline | Allows passing of opposing traffic on two lane streets. | Figure 3A-101 (CA), <a class="sref dref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/markings/line-types/#detail-1" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/linetypes/detail-1.svg">Detail 1</a> and Figure 3B-1, a</p>
<p>Partial Passing Centerline | Allows passing of opposing traffic generally on two-lane streets from one direction only. | Figure 3A-103 (CA), <a class="sref dref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/markings/line-types/#detail-15" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/linetypes/detail-15.svg">Detail 15</a> and Figure 3B-3, a</p>
<p>Double Yellow Centerline | Prohibits passing of opposing traffic, but allows crossing. | Figure 3A-104 (CA), <a class="sref dref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/markings/line-types/#detail-21" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/linetypes/detail-21.svg">Detail 21</a> and Figure 3B-2, a</p>
<p>Striped Median | Striping that separates opposing traffic flows and prohibits passing and crossing. Internal separation must be at least two feet for enforcement. | Figure 3A-107 (CA), <a class="sref dref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/markings/line-types/#detail-28" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/linetypes/detail-28.svg">Detail 28</a> and Figure 3B-4</p>
<p>Two-Way Left-Turn Lane (2WLTL) | Provides midblock left-turn storage. May be used at non-signalized intersections to provide storage for both intersectional left turns and nearby driveways from the other direction. | Figure 3A-108 (CA), <a class="sref dref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/markings/line-types/#detail-31" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/linetypes/detail-31.svg">Detail 31</a> and Figure 3B-7 (CA)</p>
<p>Left Edge Line | Delineates the left edge of roadway or median and is parallel to the edge of roadway. | Figure 3A-105 (CA), <a class="sref dref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/markings/line-types/#detail-24" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/linetypes/detail-24.svg">Detail 24</a></p>
<p>Left Channelization Line | Delineates left edge of travel path where it is not parallel to the edge of roadway. | Figure 3A-104 (CA), <a class="sref dref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/markings/line-types/#detail-21" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/linetypes/detail-21.svg">Detail 21</a></p>
<p>Cigar Nose | Delineates a tapered striped median at &quot;T&quot; intersection crossings. It is commonly used where the left-turn radius from the stem roadway of the &quot;T&quot; is less than 70 feet or where it is not necessary to provide left-turn storage into driveways near the top of the &quot;T&quot; via a 2WLTL. | Figure 3B-2, b</p>
<p>Reversal | Identifies and provides the reverse curve travel path into a unidirectional left-turn lane or a 2WLTL. | Figure 3B-2, b</p>
<p>Left Edge Cross Hatch | Provides emphasis in striped medians or between the left edge of roadway and Left Channelization Line or Left Edge Line, at least 5 feet in width. Also, it clarifies that the area is not part of the traveled way. Is aligned at 45 degrees diagonally forward with respect to the direction of travel. | Figures 3B-5</p></details>
