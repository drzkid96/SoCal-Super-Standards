<p class="dwg-sum" data-pagefind-weight="6">LADOT typical layout for a pedestrian-activated flashing beacon warning device at a marked crosswalk, with advance signing, pavement markings and red curb. It shows the beacon flash pattern (LED heads, Model 2070 controller, 60 flash periods per minute with three pulses within 0.5 seconds and a 0.5 second pause, alternating between the two beacons) and signs including <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w11-2/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W11-2.svg">W11-2</a>, <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r1-1/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R1-1.svg">R1-1</a>, <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r9-3a/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R9-3a.svg">R9-3a</a>, R9-3b and R62E(CA). School crossings use school signs and markings per S-481.0.</p>

<h5>Notes:</h5>
<p>1. The flash pattern utilizes LED heads and a Model 2070 controller. There are 60 flash periods per minute. The flash period for each beacon includes three pulses within 0.5 seconds and a pause of 0.5 seconds. The pulses of one beacon alternate with the pause of the other beacon (timing diagram over 0 to 1.0 sec for Beacon One and Beacon Two, with On/Off, pulse intensity and duty cycle).</p>
<p>2. Flash length is 3.5 seconds and retimes with each call. Flashing beacon activated by pedestrian push button.</p>
<p>3. See S-481.0 for required advance signing, pavement marking, and red curb.</p>
<p>4. At school crossings, school related signs and pavement markings shall be used in place of those shown hereon, as per S-481.0.</p>
