<p class="dwg-sum" data-pagefind-weight="6">First of five sheets on pedestrian crossing islands. A plan of a crossing island in a street with diagonal curb ramps at the corner, with an 8 ft minimum island length, yellow mountable rubber speed humps, channelizing bollards, 8 inch and 12 inch solid yellow lines and optional contrasting colored surface. A second detail shows the option when the island is less than 6 ft wide, with a 4 ft minimum cut-through and an 8 ft minimum length. Approved 02-20-2020.</p>

</details><details class="sdesc" open><summary>What this sheet shows</summary><p>Crossing island with diagonal curb ramp (plan view, upper): A four-leg intersection with corner curb returns, a diagonal curb ramp at each corner, and crosswalks across the street on the left, shown with a dashed curb line prolongation. A crossing island is placed in the center of the street at the crosswalk. The island is a 8&#x27; MIN long gray rectangle with bollard dots (channelizing bollards, see Note 1) along its perimeter and a &#x27;CONTRASTING COLORED SURFACE (OPTIONAL, SEE NOTE 2)&#x27; callout. <p>Option when island is less than 6&#x27; wide (plan view, lower): The same crossing island, with the island width labeled &#x27;LESS THAN 6&#x27;&#x27;, 8&#x27; MIN length, a 4&#x27; MIN width with &#x27;SEE NOTE 6&#x27; on the extension, and channelizing bollards and a yellow mountable rubber speed hump at the crosswalk. The total crossing is marked &#x27;SEE NOTE 10&#x27; and the crosswalk width is &#x27;MATCH CROSSWALK WIDTH WHERE POSSIBLE (SEE NOTE 5)&#x27;. The label CROSSING ISLAND is shown at the right.</p>

</details><details class="sdesc" open><summary>What this sheet shows</summary><p>Case B - Diagonal ramps (plan view, whole sheet): A four-leg intersection plan shown with a two-way street running left to right across the middle of the sheet and a cross street running vertically on the left. Each corner has a diagonal curb ramp (labeled DIAGONAL CURB RAMP) with a detectable warning surface at the corner. A crosswalk made of heavy transverse bars crosses the main street at the left, with its limits marked by two dashed vertical lines. The dashed line on the left is labeled CURB LINE PROLONGATION. A pedestrian crossing island sits in the center median of the main street, with a left-turn arrow shown in the adjacent lane. The island has an approach nose at the left (the end facing the crosswalk) and a wider body at the right. The same island is drawn twice, once at the top in the main view and again at the bottom as an option.</p>
<p>Main view, crossing island: The island is drawn as a rounded rectangle with the detectable warning surfaces shown as stippled strips on both sides of a cut-through path, which carries a 2% MAX cross slope arrow. The approach nose at the left end is labeled APPROACH NOSE, DESIGN FOR SITE CONDITIONS, SEE NOTE 5, and measures 2&#x27; MIN in the direction of travel, with its edge aligned with the base of the diagonal curb ramps (callout ALIGN EDGE WITH BASE OF DIAGONAL CURB RAMPS). The main island body is 8&#x27; MIN long. A vertical dimension on the island is labeled 6&#x27; MIN, SEE NOTE 6, and the cut-through between the nose and the island is labeled DETECTABLE WARNING SURFACE - SEE NOTE 4 and MATCH CROSSWALK WIDTH WHERE POSSIBLE (SEE NOTE 5). Each island segment carries <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r4-7/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R4-7.svg">R4-7</a> and <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/om1-1/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/OM1-1.svg">OM1-1</a> signs (TYP) and RPMs (raised pavement markers, TYP, SEE NOTE 9). The TOTAL CROSSING, SEE NOTE 10, is dimensioned from the curb line prolongation across the full crosswalk width to the far end of the island body.</p>
<p>Striping at the island: Beyond the island nose a 4&quot; SOLID YELLOW LINE 1&#x27; OFF CENTER MEDIAN runs along both edges of the double yellow median. Between those lines a series of 12&quot; SOLID YELLOW LINE diagonals SPACED 20&#x27; C-C fills the approach and departure area of the island, sloping across the roadway.</p>
<p>Option when island is less than 6&#x27; wide (SEE NOTE 3): A second, smaller copy of the layout shown at the bottom of the sheet. The island body is labeled LESS THAN 6&#x27; in width, and a separate vertical dimension on the striped area to its right is labeled 4&#x27; MIN, SEE NOTE 6. The 2&#x27; MIN approach nose, the 8&#x27; MIN island length, the TOTAL CROSSING (SEE NOTE 10) dimension, MATCH CROSSWALK WIDTH WHERE POSSIBLE (SEE NOTE 5), the <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r4-7/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R4-7.svg">R4-7</a> and <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/om1-1/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/OM1-1.svg">OM1-1</a> signs and the 12&quot; diagonal yellow lines are repeated as in the main view. The view title is CASE B - DIAGONAL RAMPS (upper right) and OPTION WHEN ISLAND IS LESS THAN 6&#x27; WIDE (bottom).</p>
<p>Title block: City of Los Angeles Department of Transportation, title CROSSING ISLANDS, drawing number S-525.0, sheet 4 of 5.</p></details>
