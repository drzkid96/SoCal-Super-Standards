<p class="dwg-sum" data-pagefind-weight="6">Sheet 1 introduces the S-100.0 series on signalized jogged intersections and illustrates the operational problems on the minor street approaches. It shows a right jog and a left jog and lists issues such as pedestrians and opposing vehicles not being seen early and opposing left turns interlocking. It refers the reader to Sheet 2 for selecting a design.</p>

<p>Definitions with diagram: Three definitions at the top (see notes) are tied to a schematic plan view of a jogged intersection. A vehicle on the north minor street at left and a vehicle on the south minor street at right are shown. Dashed sight lines run diagonally across the intersection between them, one angle theta 2 (T2) at the left vehicle, measured between the straight-ahead line and the line to the back of the opposing vehicle, and theta 1 (T1) at the right vehicle. X2 is dimensioned along the arterial at the lower left, from the crosswalk or limit line at the far side of the first intersecting leg to the signal pole, and X1 is dimensioned at the upper right the same way. D is dimensioned twice (upper and lower) as the internal storage distance between the hypothetical interior limit lines drawn as vertical lines. <p>Selection procedure: A text list gives four steps (see notes). Below it is a note on lighting. No tables.</p></details>
<details class="ocr" open><summary>Notes on this sheet (cleaned from the scan)</summary><p>1. Theta 1 and theta 2 (symbols T1 and T2) = angle between motorist looking straight ahead and back of opposing vehicle.</p>
<p>2. X1 and X2 = Distance along arterial from the crosswalk line or limit line to the signal pole or BCR at the far side of the first intersecting leg.</p>
<p>3. D = Internal storage distance between real or hypothetical interior limit lines. For minor offset D may be a small or negative number.</p>
<p>4. To select the most appropriate design:</p>
<p>1) Determine if there is a Left Jog or a Right Jog. See Sheets 3, 5, 7, 9, 10 and 11 for left jogged intersections and Sheets 4, 6, 8 and 12 for right jogged intersections.</p>
<p>2) Determine D. If D is 40&#x27; or more then interior limit lines are required. If D is less than 40&#x27; then the jog is operated as a single intersection. See Sheets 10, 11 and 12 for intersections with interior limit lines and Sheets 3 through 9 for single intersection operation.</p>
<p>3) Determine theta 1 and theta 2. If theta 1 or theta 2 is greater than 20 degrees and D is less than 40&#x27; then some form of opposed phasing is required for the minor street. See Sheets 7 through 9 for situations requiring opposed phasing. See Sheets 3 through 6 for situations for which concurrent phasing is adequate. Note that for Sheets 4 and 6 opposed phasing is required if diagonal crosswalks are not feasible, due to the unexpected presence of pedestrians crossing the path of right-turning motorists.</p>
<p>4) Determine X1 and X2. If X1 and X2 are 50&#x27; or more and D is less than 40&#x27; then only internal mastarms are to be used on the arterial street. If X1 and X2 are less than 50&#x27; then only external mastarms are to be used on the arterial street. See Sheets 3, 4, 7, and 8 for internal mastarm designs and Sheets 5, 6 and 9 for external mastarm designs.</p>
<p>5. Note: The drawings on Sheets 3 through 12 are not intended to show the required safety lighting design. Final lighting designs may not include the depicted mastarm lighting and likely will include other lighting locations not shown.</p>
<p>6. &#x27;BCR&#x27; is as printed. The printed text of step 4 reads &#x27;X1 and X&#x27; in the less than 50&#x27; condition (apparently X2).</p></details>
