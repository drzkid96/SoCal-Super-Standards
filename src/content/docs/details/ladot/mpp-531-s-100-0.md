<p class="dwg-sum">Guides the design and signal operation of signalized left-jogged and right-jogged intersections. Sheet 2 gives a selection procedure based on the interior storage distance D (single intersection operation if D is under 40 ft, interior limit lines if D is over 40 ft), the angles θ1 and θ2 (opposed phasing for the minor street if over 20 degrees), and the mastarm distances X1 and X2 (internal mastarms if over 50 ft, external if under 50 ft). The remaining sheets show typical layouts for each case, including <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r9-3a/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R9-3a.svg">R9-3a</a> signs, APS, beveled visors, PV heads, alternate phasing, KEEP CLEAR markings, and slot clearance.</p>

</details><details class="ocr" open><summary>Notes on this sheet (cleaned from the scan)</summary><p>Operational challenges for the minor street approaches to signalized jogged intersections.</p>
<h5>Right jog:</h5>
<p>- Pedestrians might not be seen early on by right-turning motorists.</p>
<p>- Opposing vehicles might not be seen early on.</p>
<p>- Opposing arterial left turns might interlock.</p>
<h5>Left jog:</h5>
<p>- Pedestrians might not be seen early on by left-turning motorists.</p>
<p>- Opposing vehicles might not be seen early on.</p>
<p>- Opposing left turns interlock.</p>
<p>- Difficult to determine if a motorist is making a left turn or a jogged through move.</p>
<p>- So-called opposing moves momentarily can be positioned at approximately right angles to each other.</p>
<p>To select the most appropriate design for minimizing the above challenges see Sheet 2.</p></details>

</details><details class="ocr" open><summary>Notes on this sheet (cleaned from the scan)</summary><h5>Definitions:</h5>
<p>θ1 and θ2 = angle between motorist looking straight ahead and back of opposing vehicle.</p>
<p>X1 and X2 = distance along arterial from the crosswalk line or limit line to the signal pole or BCR at the far side of the first intersecting leg.</p>
<p>D = internal storage distance between real or hypothetical interior limit lines. For minor offset D may be a small or negative number.</p>
<h5>To select the most appropriate design:</h5>
<p>1) Determine if there is a Left Jog or a Right Jog. See Sheets 3, 5, 7, 9, 10 and 11 for left jogged intersections and Sheets 4, 6, 8 and 12 for right jogged intersections.</p>
<p>2) Determine D. If D &gt; 40&#x27; then interior limit lines are required. If D &lt; 40&#x27; then the jog is operated as a single intersection. See Sheets 10, 11 and 12 for intersections with interior limit lines and Sheets 3 through 9 for single intersection operation.</p>
<p>3) Determine θ1 and θ2. If θ1 or θ2 is &gt; 20 degrees and D &lt; 40&#x27; then some form of opposed phasing is required for the minor street. See Sheets 7 through 9 for situations requiring opposed phasing. See Sheets 3 through 6 for situations for which concurrent phasing is adequate. Note that for Sheets 4 and 6 opposed phasing is required if diagonal crosswalks are not feasible, due to the unexpected presence of pedestrians crossing the path of right-turning motorists.</p>
<p>4) Determine X1 and X2. If X1 and X2 &gt; 50&#x27; and D &lt; 40&#x27; then only internal mastarms are to be used on the arterial street. If X1 and X2 &lt; 50&#x27; then only external mastarms are to be used on the arterial street. See Sheets 3, 4, 7 and 8 for internal mastarm designs and Sheets 5, 6 and 9 for external mastarm designs.</p>
<p>Note: The drawings on Sheets 3 through 12 are not intended to show the required safety lighting design. Final lighting designs may not include the depicted mastarm lighting and likely will include other lighting locations not shown.</p></details>
