<p class="dwg-sum">Sets out how to apply and lay out roadside (ground-mounted) freeway guide signs on cross streets that lead to freeways. It covers Lane Assignment, Action and Advance sign types, with formats for Signs A through Z, arrow styles, shield sizes and interchange examples. Roadside signing is used where freeway access is only from the right lane, where overhead signs are not feasible, or as interim signing. Action signs go 100 to 200 feet in advance of the turn, and 4'x8', 5'x6', 4'x6' and 3'x8' signs need dual posts.</p>

</details><details class="ocr" open><summary>Notes on this sheet (cleaned from the scan)</summary><p>General Notes</p>
<p>1. See S-418.6 regarding the use of overhead versus roadside freeway guide signs.</p>
<p>2. Use roadside freeway guide signing where access to the freeway is provided only by the right lane, where overhead signing may not be feasible, or as interim signing until overhead signing can be installed.</p>
<p>3. See S-476.1 regarding mounting of roadside freeway guide signing. 4&#x27;x8&#x27;, 5&#x27;x6&#x27;, 4&#x27;x6&#x27; and 3&#x27;x8&#x27; signs require dual posts, while 3&#x27;x6&#x27; signs may be installed on an electrolier or single post. Signs up to 10 sq ft may be installed on steel electroliers within concrete sidewalks.</p>
<p>4. Lane Assignment freeway guide signs on multilane cross streets that have access to freeways are required per Section 2D.45 of the California MUTCD. Where roadside signs are to be used, apply the appropriate formats as shown in Signs B, C, F, G, I, L, R, S, W and X.</p>
<p>5. Action freeway guide signs are an essential follow-up to Lane Assignment freeway guide signs for assisting motorists in accessing the desired directions to freeways. Where roadside signs are to be used, apply the appropriate formats as shown in Signs D, H, J, K, M, N, O, P, T, U, Y and Z. They should be placed 100&#x27; to 200&#x27; in advance of the turn. See Note 9.</p>
<p>6. Advance freeway guide signs are necessary in advance of a pair of roadside Lane Assignment freeway guide signs in order to advise motorists early on that both directions of the freeway are accessible. See the appropriate formats in Signs A, E, Q and V.</p>
<p>7. Freeway names are to be used only on the Advance sign, or on the Lane Assignment sign if an Advance sign is not appropriate, and only where the on-ramps are within one mile of a freeway-to-freeway interchange, as shown in Signs A and E. Where a long freeway name does not fit within the 8&#x27; wide format, as shown in Sign A, Series C upper and lower case letters may be used. Where the freeway name and the word &quot;Fwy&quot; can fit on the first line, the freeway shield shall be shown alone on the second line.</p>
<p>8. The first capital letter of the cardinal direction is larger so as to provide improved recognition.</p>
<p>9. The dual direction Action freeway guide sign format shown in Signs D and H, versus the split directional formats shown in Signs J, K, M, O, T, U, Y and Z, is to be used only when physical constraints preclude effective placement of split signs.</p>
<p>10. The text on each line is to be centered.</p></details>
