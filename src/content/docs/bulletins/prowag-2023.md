## Status (checked November 18, 2026)

- The Access Board published the final guidelines on August 8, 2023 (88 FR 53604).
- U.S. DOT adopted them, without changes, as its ADA standard for new construction and alterations of transit stops in the public right-of-way. The rule (89 FR 102800, December 18, 2024) took effect January 17, 2025, and DOT deferred enforcement to March 20, 2025.
- The Department of Justice has not adopted PROWAG for Title II entities generally. For city sidewalks, crossings and curb ramps outside transit stops, PROWAG is the current technical guidance and is widely used as the design basis, while the 2010 ADA Standards remain the adopted standard where they apply.

## Signing and striping items to know

<table class="btab wide3"><thead><tr><th>Item</th><th>PROWAG section</th><th>What it requires</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Detectable warning surfaces</td><td>R305</td><td>Truncated domes in a square or radial grid that contrast visually with the adjacent surface (light-on-dark or dark-on-light), at least 24 in. deep in the direction of travel. Placement on perpendicular, parallel and blended curb ramps follows R305.2.</td></tr><tr><td>Accessible pedestrian signals</td><td>R209</td><td>Where pedestrian signals are provided at a street crossing, they must include accessible pedestrian signals and pedestrian pushbuttons. The final rule does not list specific alteration triggers: signals follow the same alteration rules as other pedestrian facilities.</td></tr><tr><td>On-street parking</td><td>R214, R309</td><td>Where on-street parking on a block perimeter is marked or metered, a share of the spaces must be accessible, scaled by the total marked or metered spaces on that block perimeter. Angled or perpendicular accessible spaces need an 8 ft access aisle.</td></tr><tr><td>Rail crossings</td><td>R305</td><td>Pedestrian at-grade rail crossings inside a street at a crosswalk do not get detectable warnings next to the railway.</td></tr></tbody></table>

The table paraphrases the guidelines. Use the official text for design.
