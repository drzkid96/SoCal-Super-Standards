## 3B.01 Yellow Center Line Pavement Markings


<div class="secmeta">Section 3B.01 &middot; manual page 540 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b01">CA amended: 3B.01</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/3B.01/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Center line pavement markings, when used, shall be the pavement markings used to delineate the separation of traffic lanes that have opposite directions of travel on a roadway and shall be yellow.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Center line pavement markings may be placed at a location that is not the geometric center of the roadway.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> On roadways without continuous center line pavement markings, short sections may be marked with center line pavement markings to control the position of traffic at specific locations, such as around curves, over hills, on approaches to grade crossings, at grade crossings, and at bridges.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> The center line markings on two-lane, two-way roadways shall be one of the following as shown in <a href="#fig-3b-1">Figure 3B-1</a>: A. Two-direction passing zone markings consisting of a normal width broken yellow line where crossing the center line markings for passing with care is permitted for traffic traveling in either direction; B. One-direction no-passing zone markings consisting of a double yellow line, one of which is a normal width broken yellow line and the other is a normal width solid yellow line, where crossing the center line markings for passing with care is permitted for the traffic traveling adjacent to the broken line, but is prohibited for traffic traveling adjacent to the solid line; or C. Two-direction no-passing zone markings consisting of two normal width solid yellow lines where crossing the center line markings for passing is prohibited for traffic traveling in either direction.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> A single solid yellow line shall not be used as a center line marking on a two-way roadway.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Except where a reversible lane (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3b/#3b04">Section 3B.04</a>) or a two-way left-turn lane (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3b/#3b05">Section 3B.05</a>) is present, the center line markings on undivided two-way roadways with four or more lanes for moving motor vehicle traffic always available shall be the two-direction no-passing zone markings consisting of normal width double solid yellow lines as shown in <a href="#fig-3b-2">Figure 3B-2</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3b/#3b11">Section 3B.11</a> contains information for application of pavement markings through intersections or interchanges.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> On two-way roadways with three through lanes for moving motor vehicle traffic, two lanes should be designated for traffic in one direction by using one-direction or two-direction no-passing zone markings as shown in <a href="#fig-3b-3">Figure 3B-3</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> A curved transition may be used where a center line changes direction.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> An example of a location where a curved transition can have value is a turn lane.</p>
</div>

## 3B.02 Warrants for Yellow Center Lines


<div class="secmeta">Section 3B.02 &middot; manual page 542 &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b02">CA amended: 3B.02</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/3B.02/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Center line markings shall be placed on all paved undivided two-way urban arterials and collectors that have a traveled way of 20 feet or more in width and an ADT of 6,000 vehicles per day or greater. Center line markings shall also be placed on all paved undivided two-way streets or highways that have three or more lanes for moving motor vehicle traffic.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Center line markings should be placed on paved urban arterials and collectors that have a traveled way of 20 feet or more in width and an ADT of 4,000 vehicles per day or greater. Center line markings should also be placed on all rural arterials and collectors that have a traveled way of 18 feet or more in width and an ADT of 3,000 vehicles per day or greater. Center line markings should also be placed on other traveled ways where an engineering study indicates such a need.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Engineering judgment should be used in determining whether to place center line markings on traveled ways that are less than 16 feet wide because of the potential for traffic encroaching on the pavement edges, traffic being affected by parked vehicles, and traffic encroaching into the opposing traffic lane.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Center line markings may be placed on other paved two-way traveled ways that are 16 feet or more in width.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> If a traffic count is not available, the ADTs described in this Section may be estimates that are based on engineering judgment.</p>
</div>

## 3B.03 No-Passing Zone Pavement Markings


<div class="secmeta">Section 3B.03 &middot; manual page 542 &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b03">CA amended: 3B.03</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/3B.03/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> No-passing zones shall be marked by either the one-direction no-passing zone pavement markings or the two-direction no-passing zone pavement markings described in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3b/#3b01">Section 3B.01</a> and shown in <a href="#fig-3b-1">Figures 3B-1</a> and 3B-3.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> No-passing zone markings shall be used on: A. Two-way roadways at lane-reduction transitions (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3b/#3b12">Section 3B.12</a>), B. Approaches to obstructions that must be passed on the right (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3b/#3b13">Section 3B.13</a>), C. Approaches to grade crossings (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/8c/#8c02">Section 8C.02</a>), and D. Approaches to crosswalks.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> On two-way, two-lane or three-lane roadways where center line markings are installed, no-passing zones shall be established at vertical and horizontal curves and other locations where an engineering study indicates that passing must be prohibited because of inadequate sight distances or other special conditions.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> On roadways with center line markings, no-passing zone markings shall be used at horizontal or vertical curves where the passing sight distance is less than the minimum shown in <a href="#fig-3b-1">Table 3B-1</a> for the 85th-percentile speed or the speed limit.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> The passing sight distance on a vertical curve is the distance at which an object 3.5 feet above the pavement surface can be seen from a point 3.5 feet above the pavement (see <a href="#fig-3b-4">Figure 3B-4</a>). Similarly, the passing sight distance on a horizontal curve is the distance measured along the center line (or right- hand lane line of a three-lane roadway) between two points 3.5 feet above the pavement on a line tangent to the embankment or other obstruction that cuts off the view on the inside of the curve (see <a href="#fig-3b-4">Figure 3B-4</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> The upstream end of a no-passing zone at point “a” in <a href="#fig-3b-4">Figure 3B-4</a> is that point where the sight distance first becomes less than that specified in <a href="#fig-3b-1">Table 3B-1</a>. The downstream end of the no-passing zone at point “b” in <a href="#fig-3b-4">Figure 3B-4</a> is that point at which the sight distance again becomes greater than the minimum specified.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Where the distance between successive no-passing zones is less than 400 feet, no-passing zone markings should connect the zones.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> No-passing zone signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2b/#2b36">Sections 2B.36</a>, 2B.37, and 2C.53) are sometimes used to emphasize the existence and extent of a no-passing zone.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> On three-lane roadways where the direction of travel in the center lane transitions from one direction to the other, a no-passing buffer zone, consisting of a flush median island (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3j/#3j03">Section 3J.03</a>) at least 50 feet in length, shall be provided in the center lane as shown in <a href="#fig-3b-5">Figure 3B-5</a>. A lane-reduction transition (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3b/#3b12">Section 3B.12</a>) shall be provided approaching each end of the buffer zone.</p>
</div>

## 3B.04 Yellow Pavement Markings for Reversible Lanes


<div class="secmeta">Section 3B.04 &middot; manual page 543 &middot; <a class="badge ca-same" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b04">CA MUTCD 2026: 3B.04</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/3B.04/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> If reversible lanes are used, the lane line pavement markings on each side of reversible lanes shall consist of a normal width broken double yellow line to delineate the edge of a lane in which the direction of travel is reversed from time to time, such that each of these markings serve as the center line markings of the roadway during some period (see <a href="#fig-3b-6">Figure 3B-6</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2b/#2b34">Section 2B.34</a>), lane-use control signals (see Chapter 4T), or both shall be used to supplement reversible lane pavement markings.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3e/#3e02">Section 3E.02</a> contains additional applications of pavement markings for counter-flow preferential lanes that also operate as reversible lanes.</p>
</div>

## 3B.05 Pavement Markings for Two-Way Left-Turn Lanes


<div class="secmeta">Section 3B.05 &middot; manual page 543 &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b05">CA amended: 3B.05</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/3B.05/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> If a two-way left-turn lane that is never operated as a reversible lane is used, the lane line pavement markings on each side of the two-way left-turn lane shall consist of a normal width broken yellow line and a normal width solid yellow line to delineate the edges of a lane that can be used by traffic in either direction as part of a left-turn maneuver. These markings shall be placed with the broken line toward the two-way left-turn lane and the solid line toward the adjacent traffic lane as shown in <a href="#fig-3b-7">Figure 3B-7</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> White two-way left-turn lane-use arrows should be used at or just downstream from the beginning of a two- way left-turn lane.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Additional two-way left-turn lane-use arrow markings may be used at other locations along a two-way left-turn lane where engineering judgment determines that such additional markings are needed to emphasize the proper use of the lane.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> A single-direction lane-use arrow shall not be used in a lane bordered on both sides by yellow two-way left-turn lane longitudinal markings.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Signs should be used in conjunction with the two-way left-turn markings (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2b/#2b32">Section 2B.32</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Two-way left-turn lane markings should not extend to intersections (see definition in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/1c/#1c02">Section 1C.02</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Two-way left-turn lanes may be transitioned to mandatory left-turn lanes as shown in <a href="#fig-3b-7">Figure 3B-7</a> or painted median islands where they approach an intersection.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/8a/#8a06">Section 8A.06</a> contains guidance information for discontinuing a two-way left-turn lane in the immediate vicinity of a highway-rail or highway-LRT grade crossing.</p>
</div>

## 3B.06 White Lane Line Pavement Markings


<div class="secmeta">Section 3B.06 &middot; manual page 546 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b06">CA amended: 3B.06</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/3B.06/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> When used, lane line pavement markings delineating the separation of traffic lanes that have the same direction of travel shall be white.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Lane line markings shall be used on all freeways and Interstate highways.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Lane line markings should be used: A. On all roadways that are intended to operate with two or more adjacent traffic lanes in the same direction of travel, except as otherwise required for reversible lanes. B. At congested locations where the roadway will accommodate more traffic lanes with lane line markings than without the markings.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Examples of lane line markings are shown in <a href="#fig-3b-2">Figures 3B-2</a>, 3B-3, and 3B-7 through 3B-13.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Except as provided in Paragraph 1 of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3b/#3b07">Section 3B.07</a>, where crossing the lane line markings with care is not discouraged or prohibited, the lane line markings shall consist of a normal width broken white line.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Where crossing the lane line markings is discouraged, the lane line markings shall consist of a normal width solid white line.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> A solid white lane line marking should be used on approaches to: A. Intersections to separate a through lane from a mandatory turn lane. B. Intersections to separate contiguous mandatory turn lanes from each other. C. Toll collection points to separate toll lanes, payment methods, channelized movements, or obstructions.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Solid white lane line markings may be used: A. On approaches to intersections to separate contiguous through lanes. B. To separate through traffic lanes from auxiliary lanes, such as an added uphill truck lane. C. On approaches to crosswalks across multi-lane roadways.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Wide solid lane line markings may be used for greater emphasis.</p>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> A curved transition may be used where a lane line changes direction.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> Examples of locations where a curved transition can have value include freeway exit and entrance ramps.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> Where crossing the lane line markings is prohibited, the lane line markings shall consist of a double solid white line (see <a href="#fig-3b-8">Figure 3B-8</a>).</p>
</div>

## 3B.07 White Lane Line Markings for Non-Continuing Lanes


<div class="secmeta">Section 3B.07 &middot; manual page 548 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b07">CA amended: 3B.07</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/3B.07/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> A normal width dotted white line marking shall be used as the lane line to separate a through lane that continues beyond the interchange or intersection from an adjacent deceleration or acceleration lane.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> For exit ramps with a parallel deceleration lane, a normal width dotted white lane line extension shall be installed from the upstream end of the taper to the theoretical gore or to the upstream end of a solid white lane line, if used, that extends upstream from the theoretical gore as shown in Drawings A and C in <a href="#fig-3b-9">Figure 3B-9</a>.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> For an exit ramp with a tapered deceleration lane, a normal width dotted white line extension shall be installed from the theoretical gore through the taper area such that it meets the edge line at the upstream end of the taper as shown in Drawing B in <a href="#fig-3b-9">Figure 3B-9</a>.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> For entrance ramps with a parallel acceleration lane, a normal width dotted white lane line shall be installed from the theoretical gore or from the downstream end of a solid white lane line, if used, that extends downstream from the theoretical gore, to a point at least one-half the distance from the theoretical gore to the downstream end of the acceleration taper, as shown in Drawing A in <a href="#fig-3b-10">Figure 3B-10</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> For entrance ramps with a parallel acceleration lane, a normal width dotted white line extension may be installed from the downstream end of the dotted white lane line to the downstream end of the acceleration taper, as shown in Drawing A in <a href="#fig-3b-10">Figure 3B-10</a>.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> For entrance ramps with a tapered acceleration lane, a normal width dotted white line extension may be installed from the downstream end of the channelizing line adjacent to the through lane to the downstream end of the acceleration taper, as shown in Drawings B and C in <a href="#fig-3b-10">Figure 3B-10</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> A wide dotted white lane line shall be used: A. As a lane drop marking in advance of lane drops at exit ramps to distinguish a lane drop from a normal exit ramp (see Drawings A, B, and C in <a href="#fig-3b-11">Figure 3B-11</a>), B. In advance of freeway route splits with dedicated lanes (see Drawing D in <a href="#fig-3b-11">Figure 3B-11</a>), C. In advance of freeway route splits with an option lane (see Drawing E in <a href="#fig-3b-11">Figure 3B-11</a>), D. To separate a through lane that continues beyond an interchange from an adjacent continuous auxiliary lane between an entrance ramp and an exit ramp (see Drawing F in <a href="#fig-3b-11">Figure 3B-11</a>), E. As a lane drop marking in advance of lane drops at intersections to distinguish a lane drop from an intersection through lane (see Drawing A in <a href="#fig-3b-12">Figure 3B-12</a>), and F. To separate a through lane that continues beyond an intersection from an adjacent auxiliary lane between two intersections (see Drawing B in <a href="#fig-3b-12">Figure 3B-12</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Lane drop markings used in advance of lane drops at freeway and expressway exit ramps should begin at least ½ mile in advance of the theoretical gore.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> On the approach to a multi-lane exit ramp having an optional exit lane that also carries through traffic, lane line markings should be used as illustrated in Drawing B in <a href="#fig-3b-11">Figure 3B-11</a>.</p>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> Lane drop markings used in advance of lane drops at intersections should begin a distance in advance of the intersection that is determined by engineering judgment as suitable to enable drivers who do not desire to make the mandatory turn to move out of the lane being dropped prior to reaching the queue of vehicles that are waiting to make the turn. The lane drop markings should begin no closer to the intersection than the most upstream regulatory or warning sign associated with the lane drop.</p>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> The dotted white lane lines that are used for lane drop markings and that are used as a lane line separating through lanes from auxiliary lanes should consist of line segments that are 3 feet in length separated by 9-foot gaps.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3b/#3b21">Sections 3B.21</a> and 3B.23 contain information regarding other markings that are associated with lane drops, such as ONLY word pavement markings and lane-use arrows.</p>

<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>

<p><span class="pn">15</span> A curved transition may be used where a dotted extension lane line changes direction.</p>

## 3B.08 Channelizing Lines


<div class="secmeta">Section 3B.08 &middot; manual page 553 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b08">CA amended: 3B.08</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/3B.08/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Channelizing lines are used to form neutral areas where traffic traveling in the same general direction is permitted on both sides including entrance and exit ramps, access and egress points to and from managed lanes, toll-plaza bypasses, and left-turn lanes separated from through lanes. Channelizing lines are also sometimes used to alter travel paths for speed management or other purposes.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Chapter 3J contains information for the application of channelizing lines used in conjunction with islands.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Except as provided in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3e/#3e04">Section 3E.04</a> and Paragraph 6 of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3j/#3j05">Section 3J.05</a>, a channelizing line shall be a solid wide or double solid white line.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Examples of channelizing line applications are shown in <a href="#fig-3b-9">Figures 3B-9</a>, 3B-10, 3B-11, Drawing C in <a href="#fig-3b-15">Figure 3B-15</a>, <a href="#fig-3j-1">Figures 3J-1</a> through 3J-5, and Drawing B in <a href="#fig-3j-6">Figure 3J-6</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> For all exit ramps and for entrance ramps with parallel acceleration lanes, channelizing lines shall be placed on both sides of the neutral area (see <a href="#fig-3b-9">Figures 3B-9</a> and 3B-11 and Drawing A in <a href="#fig-3b-10">Figure 3B-10</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> For entrance ramps with tapered acceleration lanes, channelizing lines shall be placed along both sides of the neutral area to a point at least one-half of the distance to the theoretical gore (see Drawing C in <a href="#fig-3b-10">Figure 3B-10</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Channelizing lines shall be placed on both sides of the neutral area for bifurcations created from open- road tolling lanes that bypass a conventional toll plaza.</p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Where neutral areas are formed at access and egress points to and from a managed-lane facility, channelizing lines shall be placed on both sides of the neutral area (see <a href="#fig-2g-8">Figures 2G-8</a>, 2G-10, 2G-13, 2G-16, 2G-22, 2G-23, 2G-27, and 2G-28).</p>

<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>

</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> Other pavement markings in the neutral area shall be white.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> Pavement markings within the neutral area include chevron markings (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3b/#3b25">Section 3B.25</a>), retroreflective raised pavement markers (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3b/#3b16">Section 3B.16</a>), and internally illuminated raised pavement markers (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3b/#3b16">Section 3B.16</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> A curved transition may be used where a channelizing line changes direction.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> Examples of locations where a curved transition can have value include freeway exit and entrance ramps and separated turn lanes.</p>

## 3B.09 Edge Line Pavement Markings


<div class="secmeta">Section 3B.09 &middot; manual page 562 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b09">CA amended: 3B.09</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/3B.09/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> If used, edge line pavement markings shall delineate the right or left edges of a roadway.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Except as provided in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3e/#3e04">Section 3E.04</a>, right edge line pavement markings, if used, shall consist of a normal width solid white line to delineate the right-hand edge of the roadway.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> If used on the roadways of divided highways or one-way streets, or on any ramp in the direction of travel, left edge line pavement markings shall consist of a normal width solid yellow line to delineate the left-hand edge of a roadway or to indicate driving or passing restrictions left of these markings, except as provided in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3e/#3e04">Section 3E.04</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Edge line markings provide visual references to guide road users during adverse weather and visibility conditions.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Wide solid edge line markings may be used for greater emphasis.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Increasing edge line width from 4 inches to at least 6 inches can be a beneficial countermeasure on all facility types in both urban and rural areas.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Edge line markings should not be continued through intersections, except for the following situations: A. Dotted edge line extensions (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3b/#3b11">Section 3B.11</a>), or B. Through that part of an intersection with no intersection approach (such as the far side of a T-intersection).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3b/#3b11">Section 3B.11</a> contains information on the use and application of edge lines through intersections, interchanges, and driveways.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p>A curved transition may be used where an edge line changes direction.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> Examples of locations where a curved transition can have value include freeway exit and entrance ramps and turn lanes.</p>
</div>

## 3B.10 Warrants for Use of Edge Lines


<div class="secmeta">Section 3B.10 &middot; manual page 562 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b10">CA amended: 3B.10</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/3B.10/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Edge line markings shall be placed on paved streets or highways with the following characteristics: A. Freeways, B. Expressways, and C. Rural arterials with a traveled way of 20 feet or more in width and an ADT of 6,000 vehicles per day or greater.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Edge line markings should be placed on paved streets or highways with the following characteristics: A. Rural arterials and collectors with a traveled way of 20 feet or more in width and an ADT of 3,000 vehicles per day or greater. B. On other paved streets and highways where an engineering study indicates a need for edge line markings.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Edge line markings should not be placed where an engineering study or engineering judgment indicates that providing them is likely to decrease safety for all road users.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Edge line markings may be placed on streets and highways with or without center line markings.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Edge line markings may be excluded, based on engineering judgment, for reasons such as if the traveled way edges are delineated by curbs, parking, or other markings.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> If a bicycle lane is marked on the outside portion of the traveled way, the edge line that would mark the outside edge of the bicycle lane may be omitted.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Edge line markings may be used where edge delineation is desirable to minimize unnecessary driving on paved shoulders or on refuge areas that have lesser structural pavement strength than the adjacent roadway.</p>
</div>

## 3B.11 Application of Pavement Markings through Intersections or Interchanges


<div class="secmeta">Section 3B.11 &middot; manual page 563 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b11">CA amended: 3B.11</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/3B.11/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Pavement markings extended into or continued through an intersection or interchange area shall be the same color as the line markings they extend (see <a href="#fig-3b-13">Figure 3B-13</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Pavement markings extended into or continued through an intersection or interchange area should be at least the same width as the line markings they extend.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Where highway design or reduced visibility conditions make it desirable to provide control or to guide vehicles through an intersection or interchange, such as at offset, skewed, complex, or multi-leg intersections, on curved roadways, where multiple turn lanes are used, or where offset left-turn lanes might cause driver confusion, dotted lane line extension markings consisting of 2-foot line segments and 2-foot to 6-foot gaps should be used to extend longitudinal line markings through an intersection or interchange area.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Where greater restriction is preferred, solid lane lines or channelizing lines should be extended into or continued through intersections.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Extensions of center lines through intersections shall be dotted lines.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Where a double line is extended through an intersection, a single line of equal width to one of the lines of the double line should be used.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Solid lines shall not be used to extend edge lines into or through intersections except through that part of an intersection with no intersecting approach (such as at the far side of a T-intersection).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Edge line markings should be discontinued across intersecting approaches at intersections or interchanges.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Driveways that do not meet the definition of an intersection (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/1c/#1c02">Section 1C.02</a>) should have edge line markings maintained across the intersecting approach of the driveway.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> Dotted edge line extensions may be placed through intersections.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>

## 3B.12 Lane-Reduction Transitions


<div class="secmeta">Section 3B.12 &middot; manual page 566 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b12">CA amended: 3B.12</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/3B.12/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> A lane-reduction is where the number of through lanes is reduced at a location that is not at an interchange or intersection because of narrowing of the roadway or because of a section of on-street parking in what would otherwise be a through lane.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3b/#3b07">Section 3B.07</a> contains information on pavement markings for lane drops and splits.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2c/#2c47">Section 2C.47</a> contains information for warning signing used for lane reductions.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Lane-reduction transitions (see <a href="#fig-3b-14">Figure 3B-14</a>) shall include the following elements: A. A no-passing zone (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3b/#3b03">Section 3B.03</a>) to prohibit passing in the direction of the convergence and through the transition area except where not applicable such as one-way streets, expressways, and freeways; and B. An edge line (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3b/#3b09">Section 3B.09</a>) in the direction of the convergence and through the transition area, except as provided in Paragraph 6 of this Section.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Except as provided in Paragraph 6 of this Section, the edge line marking should be installed from the location of the Lane Ends warning sign to beyond the beginning of the narrower roadway.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> On roadways with operating speeds less than 25 mph where curbs clearly define the roadway edge in the lane- reduction transition, or where a through lane becomes a parking lane, the edge line may be omitted as determined by engineering judgment.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Lane-reduction transitions should include the following elements: A. Delineators installed adjacent to the lane or lanes reduced for the full length of the transition and should be so placed and spaced (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3g/#3g04">Section 3G.04</a>) to show the reduction except as provided in Paragraph 13 of this Section and except as provided in Paragraph 2 of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3g/#3g03">Section 3G.03</a> for freeways and expressways, B. Lane-reduction arrow markings (see Drawing I in <a href="#fig-3b-21">Figure 3B-21</a>) on the roadway with a speed limit of 45 mph or more, and C. A termination of the broken white lane line at a point that is ¼ of the advance placement distance (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2c/#2c04">Section 2C.04</a>) between the Lane Ends sign (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2c/#2c47">Section 2C.47</a>) and the point where the transition taper begins.</p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> For roadways having a speed limit of 45 mph or greater, the transition taper length for a lane-reduction transition should be computed by the formula L = WS, where L equals the taper length in feet, W equals the width of the offset distance in feet, and S equals the 85th-percentile speed or the speed limit in mph, whichever is higher. For roadways where the speed limit is less than 45 mph, the formula L = WS2/60 should be used to compute the taper length.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> The minimum lane reduction transition taper length should be 100 feet in urban areas and 200 feet in rural areas.</p>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> Where observed speeds exceed speed limits, longer tapers should be used.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> The minimum taper length may be less than 100 feet on roadways where the operating speed is less than 25 mph.</p>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> On new construction, where no speed limit has been established, the design speed may be used in the transition taper length formula.</p>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> On low-speed urban roadways where curbs clearly define the roadway edge in the lane-reduction transition, or where a through lane becomes a parking lane, delineators may be omitted as determined by engineering judgment.</p>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> Where a lane-reduction transition occurs on a roadway with a speed limit of less than 45 mph, lane-reduction arrow markings may be used.</p>
<p><span class="pn">15</span> Lane-reduction arrow markings may be used in long acceleration lanes based on engineering judgment.</p>
<p><span class="pn">16</span> A dotted white line may be used between the point where the broken white lane line is terminated to the point where the transition taper begins.</p>
</div>

## 3B.13 Approach Markings for Obstructions


<div class="secmeta">Section 3B.13 &middot; manual page 568 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-same" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b13">CA MUTCD 2026: 3B.13</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/3B.13/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Pavement markings shall be used to guide traffic away from fixed obstructions within a paved roadway. Approach markings for bridge supports, refuge islands, median islands, toll plaza islands, and raised channelization islands shall consist of a tapered line or lines extending from the center line or the lane line to a point 1 to 2 feet to the right-hand side, or to both sides, of the approach end of the obstruction (see <a href="#fig-3b-15">Figure 3B-15</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> For roadways having a speed limit of 45 mph or greater, the taper length of the tapered line markings should be computed by the formula L = WS, where L equals the taper length in feet, W equals the width of the offset distance in feet, and S equals the 85th-percentile speed or the speed limit, whichever is higher. For roadways where the speed limit is less than 45 mph, the formula L = WS2/60 should be used to compute the taper length.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> The minimum taper length should be 100 feet in urban areas and 200 feet in rural areas.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> The minimum taper length may be less than 100 feet on roadways where the operating speed is less than 25 mph.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> If traffic is required to pass only to the right of the obstruction, the markings shall consist of a two- direction no-passing zone marking at least twice the length of the diagonal portion as determined by the appropriate taper formula (see Drawing A in <a href="#fig-3b-15">Figure 3B-15</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> If traffic is required to pass only to the right of the obstruction, yellow diagonal markings (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3b/#3b25">Section 3B.25</a>) may be placed in the flush median islands (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3j/#3j03">Section 3J.03</a>) between the no-passing zone markings as shown in Drawings A and B in <a href="#fig-3b-15">Figure 3B-15</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> If traffic can pass either to the right or left of the obstruction, the markings shall consist of two channelizing lines diverging from the lane line, one to each side of the obstruction. In advance of the point of divergence, a wide solid white line or normal width double solid white line shall be extended in place of the broken lane line for a distance equal to the length of the diverging lines (see Drawing C in <a href="#fig-3b-15">Figure 3B-15</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> If traffic can pass either to the right or left of the obstruction, additional white chevron markings (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3b/#3b25">Section 3B.25</a>) may be placed in the flush neutral area between the channelizing lines as shown in Drawing C in <a href="#fig-3b-15">Figure 3B-15</a>. Other markings, such as white delineators, white channelizing devices, white raised pavement markers, and white crosswalk markings may also be placed in the flush neutral area.</p>
</div>

## 3B.14 Raised Pavement Markers – General


<div class="secmeta">Section 3B.14 &middot; manual page 568 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b14">CA amended: 3B.14</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/3B.14/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> The color of raised pavement markers under both daylight and nighttime conditions shall conform to the color of the marking for which they serve as a positioning guide, or for which they supplement or substitute.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> The side of a raised pavement marker that is visible to traffic proceeding in the wrong direction may be red (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3a/#3a03">Section 3A.03</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Retroreflective or internally illuminated raised pavement markers may be used in the roadway immediately adjacent to curbed approach ends of raised medians and curbs of islands, or on top of such curbs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3j/#3j06">Section 3J.06</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> When used, internally illuminated raised pavement markers shall be steadily illuminated and shall not be flashed.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Flashing raised pavement markers are considered to be In-Roadway Warning Lights (see Chapter 4U).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> The spacing of raised pavement markers used to supplement or substitute for other types of longitudinal markings should correspond with the pattern of broken lines for which the markers supplement or substitute.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> The value of N cited in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3b/#3b15">Sections 3B.15</a> through 3B.17 for the spacing of raised pavement markers shall equal the length of one line segment plus one gap of the broken lines used on the highway.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> For additional emphasis, retroreflective raised pavement markers may be spaced closer than described in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3b/#3b15">Sections 3B.15</a> through 3B.17, as determined by engineering judgment or engineering study.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/9a/#9a03">Section 9A.03</a> contains information for the application of raised pavement markers to bicycle facilities.</p>

## 3B.15 Raised Pavement Markers as Vehicle Positioning Guides with Other Longitudinal Markings


<div class="secmeta">Section 3B.15 &middot; manual page 571 &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b15">CA amended: 3B.15</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/3B.15/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Retroreflective or internally illuminated raised pavement markers may be used as positioning guides with longitudinal line markings without necessarily conveying information to the road user about passing or lane-use restrictions. In such applications, markers may be positioned in line with or immediately adjacent to a single line marking, or positioned between the two lines of a double center line or double lane line marking.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Except as otherwise provided in Paragraphs 3 and 4 of this Section, the spacing for such applications should be 2N (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3b/#3b14">Section 3B.14</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Where it is desired to alert the road user to changes in the travel path, such as on sharp curves or on transitions that reduce the number of lanes or that shift traffic laterally, the spacing may be reduced to N or less.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> On freeways and expressways, the spacing may be increased to 3N for relatively straight and level roadway segments where engineering judgment indicates that such spacing will provide adequate delineation under wet night conditions.</p>
</div>

## 3B.16 Raised Pavement Markers Supplementing Other Markings


<div class="secmeta">Section 3B.16 &middot; manual page 571 &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b16">CA amended: 3B.16</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/3B.16/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> The use of retroreflective or internally illuminated raised pavement markers for supplementing longitudinal line markings should comply with the following: A. Lateral Positioning 1. When supplementing double line markings, pairs of raised pavement markers placed laterally in line with or immediately outside of the two lines should be used. 2. When supplementing wide line markings, pairs of raised pavement markers placed laterally adjacent to each other should be used. B. Longitudinal Spacing 1. When supplementing solid line markings, raised pavement markers at a spacing no greater than N (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3b/#3b14">Section 3B.14</a>) should be used, except that when supplementing channelizing lines or edge line markings, a spacing of no greater than N/2 should be used. 2. When supplementing broken line markings, a spacing no greater than 3N should be used. However, when supplementing broken line markings identifying reversible lanes, a spacing of no greater than N should be used. 3. When supplementing dotted lane line markings, a spacing appropriate for the application should be used. 4. When supplementing longitudinal line extension markings through at-grade intersections, one raised pavement marker for each short line segment should be used. 5. When supplementing line extensions through freeway interchanges, a spacing of no greater than N should be used.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Raised pavement markers should not supplement right-hand edge lines unless an engineering study or engineering judgment indicates the benefits of enhanced delineation of a curve or other location would outweigh possible impacts on bicyclists using the shoulder, and the spacing of raised pavement markers on the right-hand edge does not simulate a broken line during wet night conditions.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Raised pavement markers also may be used to supplement other markings such as channelizing islands, gore areas, approaches to obstructions, or wrong-way arrows.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> To improve the visibility of horizontal curves, center lines may be supplemented with retroreflective or internally illuminated raised pavement markers for the entire curved section as well as for a distance in advance of the curve that approximates 5 seconds of travel time.</p>
</div>

## 3B.17 Raised Pavement Markers Substituting for Pavement Markings


<div class="secmeta">Section 3B.17 &middot; manual page 572 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b17">CA amended: 3B.17</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/3B.17/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Retroreflective or internally illuminated raised pavement markers, or non-retroreflective raised pavement markers supplemented by retroreflective or internally illuminated markers, may be substituted for markings of other types.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> If used, the pattern of the raised pavement markers should simulate the pattern of the markings for which they substitute.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Non-retroreflective raised pavement markers shall not be used alone, without supplemental retroreflective or internally illuminated markers, as a substitute for other types of pavement markings.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/6j/#6j03">Section 6J.03</a> contains information for flexible temporary pavement markers used during surface treatment paving operations.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> If raised pavement markers are used to substitute for broken line markings, a group of three to five markers equally spaced at a distance no greater than N/8 (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3b/#3b14">Section 3B.14</a>) shall be used. If N is other than 40 feet, the markers shall be equally spaced over the line segment length (at ½ points for three markers, at ¹⁄³ points for four markers, and at ¼ points for five markers). At least one retroreflective or internally illuminated marker per group shall be used or a retroreflective or internally illuminated marker shall be installed midway in each gap between successive groups of non-retroreflective markers.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> When raised pavement markers substitute for solid line markings, the markers shall be equally spaced at no greater than N/4, with retroreflective or internally illuminated units at a spacing no greater than N/2.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Raised pavement markers should not substitute for right-hand edge line markings unless an engineering study or engineering judgment indicates the benefits of enhanced delineation of a curve or other location would outweigh possible impacts on bicyclists using the shoulder, and the spacing of raised pavement markers on the right-hand edge line does not simulate a broken line during wet night conditions.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> When raised pavement markers substitute for dotted lines, they shall be spaced at no greater than N/4, with not less than one raised pavement marker per dotted line segment. At least one raised marker every N shall be retroreflective or internally illuminated.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> When substituting for wide lines, raised pavement markers may be placed laterally adjacent to each other to simulate the width of the line.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/5b/#5b02">Section 5B.02</a> contains information on raised pavement marker considerations for driving automation systems.</p>
</div>

## 3B.18 Curb Markings for Parking Regulations


<div class="secmeta">Section 3B.18 &middot; manual page 572 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b18">CA amended: 3B.18</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/3B.18/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Except as provided in Paragraph 3 of this Section, since yellow and white curb markings are frequently used for curb delineation and visibility, parking regulations should be established through the installation of standard signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2b/#2b53">Sections 2B.53</a> and 2B.54), especially in areas where curb markings are frequently obscured by snow and ice accumulation.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Except as provided in Paragraph 3 of this Section, when curb markings are used without signs to convey parking regulations, a legible word marking regarding the regulation (such as “No Parking” or “No Standing”) should be placed on the curb.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Curb markings without word markings or signs may be used to convey a general prohibition by statute of parking within a specified distance of a STOP sign, YIELD sign, driveway, fire hydrant, or crosswalk.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Local highway agencies may prescribe special colors for curb markings to supplement standard signs for parking regulation.</p>
</div>

## 3B.19 Stop and Yield Lines


<div class="secmeta">Section 3B.19 &middot; manual page 573 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b19">CA amended: 3B.19</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/3B.19/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Stop lines may be used to indicate the point behind which vehicles are required to stop in compliance with a STOP (R1-1) sign, a Stop Here for Pedestrians (R1-5b) sign, a Stop Here for School Crossing (R1-5c) sign, a Stop Here for Trail Crossing (R-5e) sign, or some other traffic control device that requires vehicles to stop, except YIELD signs that are not associated with passive grade crossings.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Stop lines shall consist of solid white lines extending across approach lanes to indicate the point at which the stop is intended or required to be made.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Except as provided in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/8c/#8c03">Section 8C.03</a>, stop lines shall not be used at locations where drivers are required to yield in compliance with a YIELD (R1-2) sign, a Yield Here to Pedestrians (R1-5) sign, a Yield Here to School Crossings (R1-5a) sign, a Yield Here to Trail Crossings (R1-5d) sign, or at locations on uncontrolled approaches where drivers or bicyclists are required by State law to yield to pedestrians.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Stop lines should be used to indicate the point behind which vehicles are required to stop in compliance with a traffic control signal (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/4d/#4d08">Section 4D.08</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Stop lines should be 12 to 24 inches wide.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Stop lines may be omitted at ramp control signals.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/4j/#4j02">Section 4J.02</a> contains information regarding the use and application of stop lines in conjunction with a pedestrian hybrid beacon.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> If used, a yield line pavement marking shall not be installed without a Yield (R1-2) sign, a Yield Here to Pedestrians (R1-5) sign, a Yield Here to School Crossings (R1-5a) sign, a Yield Here to Trail Crossings (R1-5d) sign, or some other traffic control device that requires vehicles to yield (see <a href="#fig-3b-16">Figure 3B-16</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Yield lines shall not be used at locations where drivers are required to stop in compliance with a STOP (R1-1) sign, a Stop Here for Pedestrians (R1-5b) sign, a Stop Here for School Crossing (R1-5c) sign, a Stop Here for Trail Crossing (R1-5e) sign, a traffic control signal, or some other traffic control device.</p>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> Yield lines shall consist of a row of solid white isosceles triangles pointing toward approaching vehicles extending across approach lanes to indicate the point at which the yield is intended or required to be made.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> If a yield line marking is used on a bicycle facility, a Bicycles Yield to Pedestrians (R9-6) sign (see Section 9B-12) may be used.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> The individual triangles comprising the yield line should have a base of 12 to 24 inches wide and a height equal to 1.5 times the base. The space between the triangles should be 3 to 12 inches.</p>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> If used, stop and yield lines should be placed a minimum of 4 feet in advance of the nearest crosswalk line at controlled intersections, except for yield lines at roundabouts as provided for in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3d/#3d04">Section 3D.04</a> and at midblock crosswalks. In the absence of a marked crosswalk, the stop line or yield line should be placed at the desired stopping or yielding point, but should not be placed more than 30 feet or less than 4 feet from the nearest edge of the intersecting traveled way.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> If yield (stop) lines are used at a crosswalk that crosses an uncontrolled multi-lane approach, Yield Here to (Stop Here for) Pedestrians (R1-5 series) signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2b/#2b19">Section 2B.19</a>) shall be used.</p>
</div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>

</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>

## 3B.20 Word, Symbol, and Arrow Pavement Markings – General


<div class="secmeta">Section 3B.20 &middot; manual page 575 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b20">CA amended: 3B.20</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/3B.20/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Word, symbol, and arrow markings on the pavement are used for the purpose of regulating, warning, or guiding traffic. These pavement markings can be helpful to road users in some locations by supplementing signs and providing additional emphasis for important regulatory, warning, or guidance messages, because the markings do not require diversion of the road user’s attention from the roadway surface. Symbol messages are preferable to word messages. Examples of standard word and arrow pavement markings are shown in <a href="#fig-3b-17">Figures 3B-17</a> and 3B-21, respectively.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Word, symbol, and arrow pavement markings may be used as determined by engineering judgment to supplement signs and/or to provide additional emphasis for regulatory, warning, or guidance messages provided by other devices.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/8c/#8c04">Section 8C.04</a> contains information for arrow pavement markings in the vicinity of grade crossings.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Word, symbol, and arrow markings shall be white, except as otherwise provided in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3a/#3a03">Sections 3A.03</a> and 3B.22.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Pavement marking letters, numerals, symbols, and arrows shall be installed in accordance with the design details in the Pavement Markings chapter of the “Standard Highway Signs” publication (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/1a/#1a05">Section 1A.05</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Word, symbol, and/or arrow markings that are grouped together to formulate one interrelated message should not exceed three lines of information.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Except for the two opposing white arrows of a two-way left-turn lane marking (see <a href="#fig-3b-7">Figure 3B-7</a>) and the pavement word marking messages described in Items B and D of Paragraph 2 of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3b/#3b26">Section 3B.26</a>, the longitudinal space between word, symbol, and/or arrow markings that are used together to formulate one interrelated message should be at least four times the height of the characters for low-speed roads, but not more than ten times the height of the characters under any conditions.</p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Except for the SCHOOL word marking (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/7c/#7c02">Section 7C.02</a>), pavement word, symbol, and arrow markings should be no more than one lane in width.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Pavement word, symbol, and arrow markings should be proportionally scaled to fit within the width of the facility upon which they are applied.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> On narrow, low-speed shared-use paths, the pavement words, symbols, and arrows may be smaller than suggested, but to the relative scale.</p>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> On roadways where the operating speed is less than 25 mph, word, symbol, and arrow markings may be proportionally reduced by 25 percent.</p>
</div>

## 3B.23 Lane-Use Arrows


<div class="secmeta">Section 3B.23 &middot; manual page 578 &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b23">CA amended: 3B.23</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/3B.23/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Lane-use arrow markings (see <a href="#fig-3b-21">Figure 3B-21</a>) are used to indicate the mandatory or permissible movements in certain lanes (see <a href="#fig-3b-22">Figure 3B-22</a>) and in two-way left-turn lanes (see <a href="#fig-3b-7">Figure 3B-7</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/8c/#8c04">Section 8C.04</a> contains information about the placement of lane-use arrow markings in the vicinity of grade crossings.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Lane-use arrow markings should be used in lanes and turn bays designated for the exclusive use of a turning movement, except where engineering judgment determines that physical conditions or other markings (such as a dotted extension of the lane line through the taper into the turn bay) clearly discourage unintentional use of a turn bay by through vehicles. Lane-use arrow markings should also be used in lanes from which movements are allowed that are contrary to the normal rules of the road (see Drawing B in <a href="#fig-3b-13">Figure 3B-13</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> When used in turn lanes, at least two arrows should be used, one at or near the upstream end of the full- width turn lane and one an appropriate distance upstream from the stop line or intersection (see Drawing A in <a href="#fig-3b-12">Figure 3B-12</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Where opposing offset channelized left-turn lanes exist, lane-use arrow markings should be placed near the downstream terminus of the offset left-turn lanes to reduce wrong-way movements (see <a href="#fig-2b-20">Figure 2B-20</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> An additional arrow or arrows may be used in a turn lane. When arrows are used for a short turn lane, the second (downstream) arrow may be omitted based on engineering judgment.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> An arrow at the downstream end of a turn lane can help to prevent wrong-way movements.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Where through lanes approaching an intersection become mandatory turn lanes, turn lane-use arrow markings (see Drawing A in <a href="#fig-3b-12">Figure 3B-12</a> and <a href="#fig-3b-21">Figure 3B-21</a>) shall be used and shall be accompanied by standard signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2b/#2b28">Section 2B.28</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Where through lanes approaching an intersection become mandatory turn lanes, ONLY word markings (see <a href="#fig-3b-17">Figure 3B-17</a>) should be used in addition to signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2b/#2b27">Sections 2B.27</a> and 2B.28) and the required turn lane-use arrow markings. These signs and markings should be placed well in advance of the turn and should be repeated as necessary to provide the through motorist advance notification to vacate the lane prior to reaching a point where roadway geometrics or a queue of waiting vehicles forces the motorist to make an unintended turn.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> On freeways or expressways where a through lane becomes a mandatory exit lane, lane-use arrow markings may be used on the approach to the exit in the dropped lane and in an adjacent optional through-or-exit lane if one exists.</p>
</div>

## 3B.26 Do Not Block Intersection Markings


<div class="secmeta">Section 3B.26 &middot; manual page 581 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b26">CA amended: 3B.26</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/3B.26/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Do Not Block Intersection markings may be used to mark the edges of an intersection area that is in close proximity to a signalized intersection, railroad crossing, or other nearby traffic control that might cause vehicles to stop within the intersection and impede other traffic entering the intersection. If authorized by law, Do Not Block Intersection markings with appropriate signs may also be used at other locations.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> If used, Do Not Block Intersection markings (see <a href="#fig-3b-24">Figure 3B-24</a>) shall consist of one of the following alternatives: A. Wide solid white lines that outline the intersection area that vehicles must not block; B. Wide solid white lines that outline the intersection area that vehicles must not block and a white word message such as DO NOT BLOCK or KEEP CLEAR; C. Wide solid white lines that outline the intersection area that vehicles must not block and white cross-hatching within the intersection area; or D. A white word message, such as DO NOT BLOCK or KEEP CLEAR, within the intersection area that vehicles must not block.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> DO NOT BLOCK INTERSECTION markings shall be accompanied by one or more DO NOT BLOCK INTERSECTION (DRIVEWAY) (CROSSING) (R10-7) signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2b/#2b59">Section 2B.59</a>), one or more DO NOT STOP ON TRACKS (R8-8) signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/8b/#8b07">Section 8B.07</a>), or one or more similar signs.</p>

## 3B.28 Speed Reduction Markings


<div class="secmeta">Section 3B.28 &middot; manual page 583 &middot; <a class="badge ca-same" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b28">CA MUTCD 2026: 3B.28</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/3B.28/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Speed reduction markings (see <a href="#fig-3b-25">Figure 3B-25</a>) are transverse markings that are placed on the roadway within a lane (along both edges of the lane) in a pattern of progressively reduced spacing to give drivers the impression that their speed is increasing.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Speed reduction markings have been shown to enhance safety around curves and locations with a history of run-off-the-road crashes when applied in combination with horizontal alignment warning signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2c/#2c05">Section 2C.05</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Speed reduction markings may be placed in advance of an unexpectedly severe horizontal or vertical curve or other roadway feature where drivers need to decelerate prior to reaching the feature and where the desired reduction in speeds has not been achieved by the installation of warning signs and/or other traffic control devices.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> If used, speed reduction markings should be reserved for unexpected curves or other usages based on engineering judgment. Speed reduction markings should not be used on long tangent sections of roadway or in areas frequented mainly by local or familiar drivers, such as school zones. If used, speed reduction markings should supplement the appropriate warning signs and other traffic control devices and should not substitute for these devices.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Speed reduction markings shall be a series of white transverse lines on both sides of the lane that are perpendicular to the center line, edge line, or lane line.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> The longitudinal spacing between the markings should be progressively reduced from the upstream to the downstream end of the marked portion of the lane.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Speed reduction markings should not be greater than 12 inches in width, and should not extend more than 18 inches into the lane.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Speed reduction markings shall be used only in lanes that have a longitudinal line (center line, edge line, or lane line) on both sides of the lane.</p>
</div>

## 3B.31 Markings for a Diamond Interchange with a Transposed Alignment Crossroad


<div class="secmeta">Section 3B.31 &middot; manual page 586 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b31">CA amended: 3B.31</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/3B.31/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Markings used in a diamond interchange with a transposed alignment crossroad can be advantageous for minimizing wrong-way movements. The potential for wrong-way movements is greatest at the crossover intersections where the alignment becomes transposed.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> On the transposed alignment, each direction shall be considered a one-way roadway whereas the edge line convention shall be in accordance with <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3b/#3b09">Section 3B.09</a>. Both yellow and white edge lines shall be used.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> A lane-use arrow (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3b/#3b23">Section 3B.23</a>) shall be used in each approach lane at the crossover intersection.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3c/#3c11">Section 3C.11</a> contains information on crosswalks and pedestrian movements for diamond interchanges with a transposed alignment crossroad.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Flush median islands (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3j/#3j03">Section 3J.03</a>) shall not be used to divide the inverted flow of traffic.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Edge line and lane line extensions (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3b/#3b11">Section 3B.11</a>) should be provided through the crossing points.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
