## 2D.01 Scope of Conventional Road Guide Sign Standards and Application


<div class="secmeta">Section 2D.01 &middot; manual page 205 &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d01">CA amended: 2D.01</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2D.01/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> The provisions of this Chapter shall apply to any road or street other than expressways and freeways, except as otherwise provided in this Manual.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Guide signs direct road users along streets and highways; inform them of intersecting routes; direct them to cities, towns, villages, or other important destinations; identify nearby rivers and streams, parks, forests, and historical sites; and provide information that will help them along their way in the most simple and direct manner possible.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> The selection of primary or control destinations (those displayed consistently over longer distances along a route) displayed on guide signs should be meaningful to road users in navigation and orientation. The destinations selected should be identifiable on official maps.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> The familiarity of the road users with the road should be considered in determining the need for guide signs on low-volume roads.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Low-volume roads generally do not require guide signs to the extent that they are needed on higher classes of roads. Because guide signs are typically only beneficial as a navigational aid for road users who are unfamiliar with a low-volume road, guide signs might not be needed on low-volume roads that serve only local traffic.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Guide signs, other than Street Name signs, generally are not used on low-volume rural roads except as needed to guide road users back to the major roadways.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> If used on low-volume roads, destination names should be as specific and descriptive as possible. Destinations such as campgrounds, ranger stations, recreational areas, and the like should be clearly indicated so that they are not interpreted to be communities or locations with road user services.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Guide signs may be used on low-volume roads at intersections to provide information for road users returning to a higher class of roads.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2a/">Chapter 2A</a> addresses placement, location, and other general criteria for signs.</p>
</div>

## 2D.02 Color, Retroreflection, and Illumination


<div class="secmeta">Section 2D.02 &middot; manual page 205 &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d02">CA amended: 2D.02</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2D.02/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Requirements for illumination, retroreflection, and color are stated under the specific headings for individual guide signs or groups of signs. General provisions are given in 2A.06, 2A.21, and 2A.22.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Except as otherwise provided in this Manual for individual signs or groups of signs, guide signs on streets and highways shall have a white message and border on a green background. All messages, borders, and legends shall be retroreflective and all backgrounds shall be retroreflective or illuminated.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Color coding is sometimes used to help road users distinguish between multiple potentially confusing destinations. Examples of valuable uses of color coding include guide signs for roadways approaching or inside an airport property with multiple terminals serving multiple airlines, and community wayfinding guide signs for various traffic generator destinations within a community or area.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Except as otherwise provided in this Manual, different color sign backgrounds shall not be used to provide color coding of destinations. The color coding shall be accomplished by the use of different colored square or rectangular sign panels on the face of the guide signs (see <a href="#fig-2d-1">Figure 2D-1</a>).</p>
</div>

## 2D.03 Size of Signs


<div class="secmeta">Section 2D.03 &middot; manual page 206 &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d03">CA amended: 2D.03</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2D.03/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Except as provided in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2a/#2a07">Section 2A.07</a>, the minimum sizes of conventional road guide signs that have standardized designs shall be as shown in <a href="#fig-2d-1">Table 2D-1</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2a/#2a07">Section 2A.07</a> contains information regarding the applicability of the various columns in <a href="#fig-2d-1">Table 2D-1</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Signs larger than those shown in <a href="#fig-2d-1">Table 2D-1</a> may be used (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2a/#2a07">Section 2A.07</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> For other guide signs, the legends are so variable that a standardized design or size is not appropriate. The sign size is determined primarily by the length of the message, and the size of lettering and spacing necessary for proper legibility.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Reduced letter height, reduced interline spacing, and reduced edge spacing may be used on guide signs if sign size must be limited by factors such as lane width or vertical or lateral clearance.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Reduced spacing between the letters or words on a line of legend should not be used as a means of reducing the overall size of a guide sign, except where determined necessary by engineering judgment to meet unusual lateral-space constraints. In such cases, the legibility distance of the sign legend should be the primary consideration in determining whether to reduce the spacing between the letters or the words or between the words and the sign border, or to reduce the letter height.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> When a reduction in the prescribed size is necessary, the design used should be as similar as possible to the design for the standard size.</p>
</div>

## 2D.04 Lettering Style


<div class="secmeta">Section 2D.04 &middot; manual page 206 &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d04">CA amended: 2D.04</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2D.04/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> The design of upper-case letters, lower-case letters, numerals, route shields, and spacing shall be as provided in the “Standard Highway Signs” publication (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/1a/#1a05">Section 1A.05</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> The lettering for names of places, streets, and highways on conventional road guide signs shall be a combination of lower-case letters with initial upper-case letters (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2a/#2a08">Section 2A.08</a>). The nominal loop height of the lower-case letters shall be ¾ the height of the initial upper-case letter. When a mixed-case legend letter height is specified referring only to the initial upper-case letter, the height of the lower-case letters that follow shall be determined by this proportion. When the height of a lower-case letter is referenced, the reference is made to the nominal loop height. The height of the initial upper-case letter shall also be determined by this proportion.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> All other word legends on conventional road guide signs shall be in upper-case letters.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> The unique letter forms for each of the Standard Alphabet series shall not be stretched, compressed, warped, or otherwise manipulated. Modifications to the length of a word for a given letter height and series shall be accomplished only by the methods described in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d03">Section 2D.03</a>.</p>
</div>

## 2D.05 Size of Lettering


<div class="secmeta">Section 2D.05 &middot; manual page 208 &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d05">CA amended: 2D.05</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2D.05/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Sign legibility is a direct function of letter size and spacing. Legibility distance has to be sufficient to give road users enough time to read and comprehend the sign. Under optimum conditions, a guide sign message can be read and understood in a brief glance. The legibility distance takes into account factors such as inattention, blocking of view by other vehicles, unfavorable weather, inferior eyesight, or other causes for delayed or slow reading. Where conditions permit, repetition of guide information on successive signs gives the road user more than one opportunity to obtain the information needed.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Design layouts for conventional road guide signs showing interline spacing, edge spacing, and other specification details shall be as shown in the “Standard Highway Signs” publication (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/1a/#1a05">Section 1A.05</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Except as otherwise provided in this Manual, the principal legend on post-mounted guide signs shall be in letters and numerals at least 6 inches in height for all upper-case letters, or a combination of 6 inches in height for upper-case letters and 4.5 inches in nominal loop height (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d04">Section 2D.04</a>) for lower-case letters. On low-volume roads with speeds of 25 mph or less, and on urban streets with speeds of 25 mph or less, the principal legend on post-mounted guide signs shall be in letters at least 4 inches in height for all upper-case letters, or a combination of 4 inches in height for upper-case letters and 3 inches in nominal loop height for lower-case letters.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Except as otherwise provided in this Manual, the principal legend on overhead guide signs shall be in letters and numerals at least 6 inches in height for all upper-case letters, or a combination of 6 inches in height for upper-case letters and 4.5 inches in nominal loop height (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d04">Section 2D.04</a>) for lower- case letters.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Lettering sizes should be consistent on any particular class of highway.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> The minimum lettering and numeral sizes provided in this Manual (see <a href="#fig-2d-2">Table 2D-2</a>) should be exceeded where conditions indicate a need for greater legibility.</p>
</div>

## 2D.06 Amount of Legend


<div class="secmeta">Section 2D.06 &middot; manual page 209 &middot; <a class="badge ca-same" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d06">CA MUTCD 2026: 2D.06</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2D.06/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> The longer the legend on a guide sign, the longer it will take road users to recognize and comprehend it, regardless of letter size.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Except where otherwise provided in this Manual, guide signs should be limited to no more than three lines of destinations, which include place names, route numbers, street names, and cardinal directions. Where two or more signs are included in the same overhead display, the amount of legend should be further minimized. Where appropriate, a distance message or action information, such as an exit number, NEXT RIGHT, or directional arrows, should be provided on guide signs in addition to the destinations.</p>
</div>

## 2D.07 Abbreviations


<div class="secmeta">Section 2D.07 &middot; manual page 209 &middot; <a class="badge ca-same" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d07">CA MUTCD 2026: 2D.07</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2D.07/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> The use of commonly recognized abbreviations for certain words can be useful in reducing the reading time and improve quicker comprehension of a sign message. Descriptors and directional or quadrant orientations for street names and destinations, such as Boulevard (Blvd), North (N), and Southwest (SW), are some examples of commonly recognized abbreviations. Examples of the use of some guide sign abbreviations are shown in <a href="#fig-2d-2">Figure 2D-2</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> The words NORTH, SOUTH, EAST, and WEST shall not be abbreviated when used to indicate cardinal directions of numbered or named highways on guide signs.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Abbreviations should be kept to a minimum; however, they are useful when complete destination messages produce excessively long signs. If used, abbreviations should be unmistakably recognized by road users (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/1d/#1d08">Section 1D.08</a>). Longer commonly used words that are not part of a proper name and are readily recognizable, such as street name descriptors (such as Street, Boulevard, or Avenue), should be abbreviated as provided in <a href="#fig-2d-3">Table 2D-3</a> to expedite recognition of the sign legend by reducing the amount and complexity of the legend. Shorter street name descriptors, such as those shown in <a href="#fig-2d-4">Table 2D-4</a>, should not be abbreviated.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Periods, apostrophes, question marks, ampersands, or other punctuation or characters that are not letters, numerals, or hyphens should not be used in abbreviations, unless necessary to avoid confusion.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> The solidus is intended to be used for fractions only and should not be used to separate words on the same line of legend. Instead, a hyphen should be used for this purpose, such as “TRUCKS – BUSES.”</p>
</div>

## 2D.08 Arrows


<div class="secmeta">Section 2D.08 &middot; manual page 213 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d08">CA amended: 2D.08</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2D.08/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Arrows are used for lane assignment and to indicate the direction toward designated routes or destinations. <a href="#fig-2d-3">Figure 2D-3</a> shows the various standard arrow designs that have been approved for use on guide signs. Detailed drawings are shown for these arrows in the “Standard Highway Signs” publication (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/1a/#1a05">Section 1A.05</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Except for Overhead Arrow-per- Lane signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d37">Section 2D.37</a>), on overhead signs where it is desirable to indicate a lane to be followed, a down arrow shall be positioned over the approximate center of the lane and shall point vertically downward toward the approximate center of that lane. Down arrows shall be used only on overhead guide signs that restrict the use of specific lanes to traffic bound for the destination(s) and/or route(s) indicated by these arrows. Down arrows shall not be used unless an arrow can be located over and pointed to the approximate center of each lane that can be used to reach the destination displayed on the sign.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> If down arrows are used, having more than one down arrow pointing to the same lane on a single overhead sign (or on multiple signs on the same overhead sign structure) shall not be permitted.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Where a roadway is leaving the through lanes, a directional arrow shall point upward at an angle that approximates the alignment of the exit roadway in the vicinity of the point of departure.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> The Type E directional arrow for circular intersections shall not be used on any sign that is not associated with a circular intersection</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> The Type A directional arrow should be used on guide signs on freeways, expressways, and conventional roads to indicate the direction to a specific destination or group of destinations, except as otherwise provided in this Section and in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2e/#2e18">Section 2E.18</a>.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> When a directional arrow in a vertical, upward- pointing orientation is placed to the side of a group of destinations to indicate a through movement, the Type A directional arrow should be used. When a directional arrow in a vertical, upward-pointing orientation is placed to the side of a single destination or under a destination or group of destinations, the Type B directional arrow should be used.</p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> The Type B directional arrow should be used on guide signs on conventional roads when placed at any angle to the side of a single destination or when placed in a horizontal orientation to the side of a group of destinations.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> The Type C advance turn directional arrow should be used on conventional road guide signs placed in advance of an intersection where a turn must be made to reach a posted destination or group of destinations.</p>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> The Type D directional arrow should be used primarily for sign applications other than guide signs, except as provided in Paragraph 15 of this Section.</p>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> If the Type E directional arrow is used, the principles set forth in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d26">Sections 2D.26</a> through 2D.29 should be followed.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> The Type A-Extended directional arrow may be used on guide signs where additional emphasis regarding the direction is needed relative to the amount of legend on the sign.</p>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> The Type C directional arrow may be used to the side of the legend of an overhead guide sign to accentuate a sharp turn exit maneuver from a mainline roadway (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2e/#2e25">Section 2E.25</a> for additional information regarding Exit Direction signs for low advisory ramp speeds).</p>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> On conventional roads on the approach to an intersection where the Combination Lane-Use/Destination overhead guide sign (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d38">Section 2D.38</a>) is not used, the Type C advance turn directional arrow may be used beneath the legend of an overhead guide sign to indicate the fact that a turn must be made from a mandatory movement lane over which the sign is placed to reach the destination or destinations displayed on the sign.</p>
<p><span class="pn">15</span> The Type D directional arrow may be used on post-mounted guide signs on conventional roads with lower operating speeds if the height of the text on the sign is 8 inches or less. Type D arrows may be used on a Street Name (D3-1 only) sign displaying two street names to indicate the different direction of travel for each street.</p>
<p><span class="pn">16</span> The Type E directional arrow may be used on guide signs on approaches to circular intersections to represent the intended driver paths to destinations involving left-turn movements around the central island.</p>
<p><span class="pn">17</span> The directional and down arrows shown in <a href="#fig-2d-3">Figure 2D-3</a> may be used on signs other than guide signs for the purposes of providing directional guidance and lane assignment.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">18</span> Arrows used on guide signs to indicate the directions toward designated routes or destinations should be pointed at the appropriate angle to clearly convey the direction to be taken. A horizontally-oriented directional arrow design should be used at right-angle intersections.</p>
<p><span class="pn">19</span> On a post-mounted guide sign, a directional arrow for a straight-through movement should point upward. Except as provided in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d50">Section 2D.50</a>, for a turn, the arrow on a guide sign should point horizontally or at an upward angle that approximates the sharpness of the turn.</p>
<p><span class="pn">20</span> At an exit, an arrow should be placed at the side of the sign that will reinforce the movement of exiting traffic. The directional arrow design should be used.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">21</span> If used, the Type C advance turn directional arrow shall display a right or left arrow, the shaft of which is bent at a 90-degree or oblique angle.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">22</span> Arrows may be placed below the principal sign legend or on the appropriate side of the legend that is consistent with the direction of the movement.</p>
<p><span class="pn">23</span> On a post-mounted sign at an exit where placement of the arrow to the side of the legend farthest from the roadway would create an unusually wide sign that limits the road user’s view of the arrow, the directional arrow may be placed at the bottom portion of the sign, centered under the legend.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">24</span> The width across the arrowhead for the Types A, B, and C directional arrows should be between 1.5 and 1.75 times the height of the upper-case letters of the principal legend on the sign. The width across the arrowhead for the Type D directional arrow should be at least equal to the height of the upper-case letters of the principal legend on the sign. For down arrows used on overhead signs, the width across the arrowhead should be approximately 2 times the height of the upper-case letters of the principal legend on the sign.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">25</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d37">Section 2D.37</a> contains the provisions for arrows used in Overhead Arrow-per-Lane signs on approaches to conventional road intersections. <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2e/#2e41">Section 2E.41</a> contains the provisions for arrows used in Diagrammatic Advance guide signing on approaches to conventional road intersections other than circular intersections. <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d39">Section 2D.39</a> contains the provisions for diagrammatic arrows used in Destination signs on the approaches to circular intersections (see <a href="#fig-2d-11">Figure 2D-11</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">26</span> The “Standard Highway Signs” publication (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/1a/#1a05">Section 1A.05</a>) contains design details and standardized sizes of the various arrows based on ranges of letter heights of principal legends.</p>
</div>

## 2D.09 Numbered Highway Systems


<div class="secmeta">Section 2D.09 &middot; manual page 216 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d09">CA amended: 2D.09</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2D.09/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> The purpose of numbering and signing highway systems is to identify routes and facilitate travel.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> The Interstate and United States (U.S.) highway systems are numbered by the American Association of State Highway and Transportation Officials (AASHTO) upon recommendations of the State highway organizations because the respective States own these systems. State and county road systems are numbered by the appropriate authorities.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> The basic policy for numbering the Interstate and U.S. highway systems is contained in the following Purpose and Policy statements published by AASHTO: A. “Establishment and Development of United States Numbered Highways,” and B. “Establishment of a Marking System of the Routes Comprising the National System of Interstate and Defense Highways.”</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> The principles of these policies should be followed in establishing the highway systems described in Paragraph 3 of this Section and any other systems, with effective coordination between adjacent jurisdictions. Care should be taken to avoid the use of numbers or other designations that have been assigned to Interstate, U.S., or State routes in the same geographic area. Overlapping numbered routes should be kept to a minimum.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Route systems shall be given preference in this order: Interstate, United States, State, and county. The preference shall be given by installing the highest-priority route number on the top or the left of the sign, except as provided in Paragraph 7 of this Section.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Interstate route numbering shall be approved by the FHWA.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> The prioritization of route systems may be modified when a different prioritization would better accommodate the expectancy of the road user and provide more effective direction, such as for separate decision points for routes that are encountered in a particular order.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d56">Section 2D.56</a> contains information regarding the signing of unnumbered highways to enhance route guidance and facilitate travel.</p>
</div>

## 2D.11 Design of Route Signs


<div class="secmeta">Section 2D.11 &middot; manual page 216 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d11">CA amended: 2D.11</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2D.11/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> The design of standard route signs shall conform to the designs provided in the “Standard Highway Signs” publication (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/1a/#1a05">Section 1A.05</a>). The design of other route signs shall be established by the authority having jurisdiction and shall be in general conformance with the designs provided in the “Standard Highway Signs” publication.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Interstate Route (M1-1 and M1-1a) signs (see <a href="#fig-2d-4">Figure 2D-4</a>) shall be used on all Interstate routes and in connection with Route Sign assemblies on intersecting highways.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Except as otherwise provided in this Manual, a 24 x 24-inch minimum sign size shall be used for Interstate route numbers with one or two digits, and a 30 x 24-inch minimum sign size shall be used for Interstate route numbers having three digits.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> When the Interstate Route sign is used in a Route Sign assembly (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d29">Section 2D.29</a>), the M1-1a sign, containing the State name in white upper-case letters on a blue background as detailed in the “Standard Highway Signs” publication (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/1a/#1a05">Section 1A.05</a>), may be used in place of the M1-1 sign.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Use of the M1-1a sign shall be limited to Route Sign assemblies.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Off-Interstate Business Route (M1-2 and M1-3) signs (see <a href="#fig-2d-4">Figure 2D-4</a>) shall consist of a cutout shield displaying the number of the connecting Interstate route and the words BUSINESS and either LOOP (when the route rejoins the same Interstate route) or SPUR (when the route leaves the corresponding Interstate route and does not rejoin) in upper-case letters. The legend and border shall be white on a green background, and the shield shall be the same shape and dimensions as the Interstate Route sign. In no instance shall the word INTERSTATE appear on the Off-Interstate Business Route sign.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> The Off-Interstate Business Route sign may be used on a major highway that is not a part of the Interstate system, but one that serves the business area of a city from an interchange on the system.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> U.S. Route signs (see <a href="#fig-2d-4">Figure 2D-4</a>) shall consist of black numerals on a white shield surrounded by a rectangular black background without a border. This sign shall be used on all U.S. routes and in connection with Route Sign assemblies on intersecting highways.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> A 24 x 24-inch minimum sign size shall be used for U.S. route numbers with one or two digits, and a 30 x 24-inch minimum sign size shall be used for U.S. route numbers having three digits.</p>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> State Route signs shall be designed by the individual State highway agencies.</p>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> The legend on State Route signs shall conform to the Standard Alphabets contained in the “Standard Highway Signs” publication (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/1a/#1a05">Section 1A.05</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> State Route signs (see <a href="#fig-2d-4">Figure 2D-4</a>) should be rectangular and should be approximately the same size as the U.S. Route sign. State Route signs should also be similar to the U.S. Route sign by containing approximately the same size black numerals on a white area surrounded by a rectangular black background without a border, and should be devoid of complex graphics. The shape of the white area should be circular in the absence of any determination to the contrary by the individual State concerned.</p>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> Where U.S. or State Route signs are used as components of guide signs, only the distinctive shape of the shield itself and the route numerals within should be used. The rectangular background upon which the distinctive shape of the shield is mounted, such as the black area around the outside of the shields on the M1-4 and standard M1-5 signs, should not be included on the guide sign. Where U.S. or State Route signs are used as components of other signs of non-contrasting background colors, the rectangular background should be used so that recognition of the distinctive shape of the shield can be maintained.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> If county road authorities elect to establish and identify a special system of important county roads, a statewide policy for such signing shall be established that includes a uniform numbering system to uniquely identify each route. The County Route (M1-6) sign (see <a href="#fig-2d-4">Figure 2D-4</a>) shall consist of a pentagon shape with a yellow county name and route number and border on a blue background. County Route signs shall be a minimum size of 24 x 24 inches.</p>
<p><span class="pn">15</span> If a jurisdiction uses letters instead of numbers to identify routes, all references to numbered routes in this Chapter shall be interpreted to also include lettered routes.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">16</span> If used with other route signs in common assemblies, the County Route sign should be of a size compatible with that of the other route signs.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">17</span> When the County Route (M1-6) sign is used as a component of a guide sign, the county name and the legend COUNTY may be omitted from the County Route sign.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">18</span> The design of the National Forest Route (M1-7) sign (see <a href="#fig-2d-4">Figure 2D-4</a>) shall be as detailed in the “Standard Highway Signs” publication (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/1a/#1a05">Section 1A.05</a>). Route signs for other park and forest roads shall be designed with an appropriate level of distinctiveness and adequate legibility, but in general compliance with the design principles for route signs and of a size compatible with other route signs used in common assemblies.</p>
</div>

## 2D.15 Cardinal Direction Auxiliary Plaques (M3-1P through M3-4P)


<div class="secmeta">Section 2D.15 &middot; manual page 219 &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d15">CA amended: 2D.15</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2D.15/">short link</a></div>

## 2D.29 Route Sign Assemblies


<div class="secmeta">Section 2D.29 &middot; manual page 223 &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d29">CA amended: 2D.29</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2D.29/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> A Route Sign assembly shall consist of a route sign and auxiliary plaques that further identify the route and indicate the direction. Except as provided in Paragraph 9 of this Section, Route Sign assemblies shall be installed on all approaches to numbered routes that intersect with other numbered routes.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Where two or more routes follow the same section of highway, the route signs for Interstate, U.S., State, and county routes shall be mounted in that order from the left in horizontal arrangements and from the top in vertical arrangements. Subject to this order of precedence, route signs for lower-numbered routes shall be placed at the left or top.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Within groups of assemblies, information for routes intersecting from the left shall be mounted at the left in horizontal arrangements and at the top or center of vertical arrangements. Similarly, information for routes intersecting from the right shall be at the right or bottom, and for straight-through routes at the center in horizontal arrangements or top in vertical arrangements.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Route Sign assemblies shall be mounted in accordance with the general specifications for signs (<a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2a/">Chapter 2A</a>), with the lowest sign in the assembly at the height prescribed for single signs.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Assemblies for two or more routes, or for different directions on the same route, should be mounted in groups on a common support.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Where more than four route signs would be needed in a single Advance Route Turn or Directional assembly, the route signs should instead be mounted in a guide sign to minimize the need for repetition of the same information on multiple Cardinal Direction and Directional Arrow auxiliary plaques (see <a href="#fig-2d-7">Figure 2D-7</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Route Sign assemblies may be installed on the approaches to numbered routes on unnumbered roads and streets that carry an appreciable amount of traffic destined for the numbered route.</p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> If engineering judgment indicates that groups of assemblies that include overlapping routes or multiple turns might be confusing, route signs or auxiliary signs may be omitted or combined, provided that clear directions are given to road users.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Route Sign assemblies may be omitted for routes that are part of an agency’s internal numbering system, such as for maintenance or other purposes, and are not publicly mapped or intended to be used for navigational purposes by the general public. Similarly, numbered routes that are not maintained during certain times of year, such as not being plowed during winter months, may be omitted from Route Sign assemblies.</p>
</div>

</div>

## 2D.30 Junction Assembly


<div class="secmeta">Section 2D.30 &middot; manual page 223 &middot; <a class="badge ca-same" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d30">CA MUTCD 2026: 2D.30</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2D.30/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> A Junction assembly shall consist of a Junction auxiliary plaque (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d13">Section 2D.13</a>) and a route sign. The route sign shall display the number of the intersected or joined route.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> The Junction assembly shall be installed in advance of every intersection where a numbered route is intersected or joined by another numbered route.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> In urban areas, the Junction assembly should be installed in the block preceding the intersection. In urban areas where speeds are low, the Junction assembly should not be installed more than 300 feet in advance of the intersection.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> In rural areas, the Junction assembly should be installed at least 400 feet in advance of the intersection. In rural areas, the minimum distance between a Junction assembly and either a Destination sign or an Advance Route Turn assembly should be 200 feet.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Where speeds are high, greater spacings should be used.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Where two or more routes are to be indicated, a single Junction auxiliary plaque may be used for the assembly and all route signs grouped in a single mounting, or a Combination Junction (M2-2) sign (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d14">Section 2D.14</a>) may be used.</p>
</div>

## 2D.31 Advance Route Turn Assembly


<div class="secmeta">Section 2D.31 &middot; manual page 230 &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d31">CA amended: 2D.31</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2D.31/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> An Advance Route Turn assembly shall consist of a route sign, an Advance Turn Arrow or word message auxiliary plaque, and a Cardinal Direction auxiliary plaque, if needed. It shall be installed in advance of an intersection where a turn must be made to remain on the indicated route.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> The Advance Route Turn assembly may be used to supplement the required Junction assembly in advance of intersecting routes.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Where a multi-lane highway approaches an interchange or intersection with a numbered route, the Advance Route Turn assembly should be used to provide advance notice so that road users know the correct lane(s) from which to make their turn.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Lane Designation auxiliary plaques (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d27">Section 2D.27</a>) may be used in Advance Route Turn Assemblies in place of the Advance Turn Arrow auxiliary plaques where engineering judgment indicates that specific lane information associated with each route is needed and overhead signing is impracticable and the designated lane is a mandatory movement lane. An assembly with the Lane Designation auxiliary plaques may supplement or substitute for an assembly with Advance Turn Arrow auxiliary plaques.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> In low-speed areas, the Advance Route Turn assembly should be installed not less than 200 feet in advance of the turn. In high-speed areas, the Advance Route Turn assembly should be installed not less than 300 feet in advance of the turn. In rural areas, the minimum distance between an Advance Route Turn assembly and either a Destination sign or a Junction assembly should be 200 feet.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> An assembly that includes an Advance Turn Arrow auxiliary plaque shall not be placed where there is an intersection between it and the designated turn.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Sufficient distance should be allowed between the assembly and any preceding intersection that could be mistaken for the indicated turn.</p>
</div>

## 2D.32 Directional Assembly


<div class="secmeta">Section 2D.32 &middot; manual page 230 &middot; <a class="badge ca-same" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d32">CA MUTCD 2026: 2D.32</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2D.32/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> A Directional assembly shall consist of a Cardinal Direction auxiliary plaque, if needed; a route sign; and a Directional Arrow auxiliary plaque. The uses of Directional assemblies shall comply with the following: A. Turn movements (indicated in advance by an Advance Route Turn assembly) shall be marked by a Directional assembly with a route sign displaying the number of the turning route and a single- headed arrow pointing in the direction of the turn. B. The beginning of a route (indicated in advance by a Junction assembly) shall be marked by a Directional assembly with a route sign displaying the number of that route and a single-headed arrow pointing in the direction of the route. C. An intersected route (indicated in advance by a Junction assembly) on a crossroad where the route is designated on both legs shall be designated by: 1. Two Directional assemblies, each with a route sign displaying the number of the intersected route, a Cardinal Direction auxiliary plaque, and a single-headed arrow pointing in the direction of movement on that route; or 2. A Directional assembly with a route sign displaying the number of the intersected route and a double-headed arrow, pointing at appropriate angles to the left, right, or ahead. D. An intersected route (indicated in advance by a Junction assembly) on a side road or on a crossroad where the route is designated only on one of the legs shall be designated by a Directional assembly with a route sign displaying the number of the intersected route, a Cardinal Direction auxiliary plaque, and a single-headed arrow pointing in the direction of movement on that route.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Straight-through movements should be indicated by a Directional assembly with a route sign displaying the number of the continuing route and a vertical arrow. A Directional assembly should not be used for a straight- through movement in the absence of other assemblies indicating right or left turns, as the Confirming assembly sign beyond the intersection normally provides adequate guidance.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Directional assemblies should be located on the near right corner of the intersection. At major intersections and at Y or offset intersections, additional Directional assemblies should be installed on the far right or left corner to confirm the near-side assemblies. When the near-corner position is impractical for Directional assemblies, the far right corner should be the preferred alternative, with oversized signs, if necessary, for legibility. Where unusual conditions exist, the location of a Directional assembly should be determined by engineering judgment with the goal being to provide the best possible combination of view and safety.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> It is more important that guide signs be readable, and that the information and direction displayed thereon be readily understood, at the appropriate time and place than to be located with absolute uniformity.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> <a href="#fig-2d-8">Figure 2D-8</a> shows typical placements of Directional assemblies.</p>
</div>

## 2D.33 Confirming or Reassurance Assemblies


<div class="secmeta">Section 2D.33 &middot; manual page 231 &middot; <a class="badge ca-same" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d33">CA MUTCD 2026: 2D.33</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2D.33/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> If used, Confirming or Reassurance assemblies shall consist of a Cardinal Direction auxiliary plaque and a route sign. Where the Confirming or Reassurance assembly is for an alternative route, the appropriate auxiliary plaque for an alternative route (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d16">Section 2D.16</a>) shall also be included in the assembly.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> A Confirming assembly should be installed just beyond intersections of numbered routes. It should be placed 25 to 200 feet beyond the far shoulder or curb line of the intersected highway.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> If used, Reassurance assemblies should be installed between intersections in urban areas as needed, and beyond the built-up area of any incorporated city or town.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Route signs for either confirming or reassurance purposes should be spaced at such intervals as necessary to keep road users informed of their routes.</p>
</div>

## 2D.34 Trailblazer Assembly


<div class="secmeta">Section 2D.34 &middot; manual page 231 &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d34">CA amended: 2D.34</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2D.34/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Trailblazer assemblies provide directional guidance to a particular road facility from other highways in the vicinity. This guidance is accomplished by installing Trailblazer assemblies at strategic locations to indicate the direction to the nearest or most convenient point of access. The use of the word TO indicates that the road or street where the sign is posted is not a part of the indicated route, and that a road user is merely being directed progressively to the route.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> A Trailblazer assembly shall consist of a TO auxiliary plaque (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d21">Section 2D.21</a>), a route sign for a numbered or named highway (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d56">Section 2D.56</a>) or an identification sign for a byway, historic trail, or auto tour route sign (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d57">Sections 2D.57</a> and 2D.58), and a single-headed Directional Arrow auxiliary plaque pointing in the direction leading to the route. Where the Trailblazer assembly is for an alternative route, the appropriate auxiliary plaque for an alternative route (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d16">Section 2D.16</a>) shall also be included in the assembly.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> A Cardinal Direction auxiliary plaque (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d15">Section 2D.15</a>) may be used in a Trailblazer assembly where the direction leading to the route provides access only to one direction of travel for that route.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> The TO auxiliary plaque, Cardinal Direction auxiliary plaque, and Directional Arrow auxiliary plaque should be of the standard size provided for auxiliary plaques of their respective type. The route sign should be the size provided in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d11">Section 2D.11</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Trailblazer assemblies may be installed with other Route Sign assemblies, or alone, in the immediate vicinity of the designated facilities.</p>
</div>

## 2D.35 Destination and Distance Signs


<div class="secmeta">Section 2D.35 &middot; manual page 232 &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d35">CA amended: 2D.35</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2D.35/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> In addition to guidance by route numbers, it is desirable to supply the road user information concerning the destinations that can be reached by way of numbered or unnumbered routes. This is done by means of Destination signs and Distance signs.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Route shields and cardinal directions may be included on the Destination sign with the destinations and arrows.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> If Route shields and cardinal directions are included on a Destination sign, the height of the route shields should be at least two times the height of the upper-case letters of the principal legend and not less than 18 inches, and the letter height of cardinal directions should be at least the minimum letter height specified for these signs.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> If used, destination names on low-volume rural roads should be as specific and descriptive as possible. Destinations such as campgrounds, ranger stations, and recreational areas should be clearly indicated so that they are not interpreted to be communities or locations with road user services.</p>
</div>

## 2D.36 Destination Signs (D1 Series)


<div class="secmeta">Section 2D.36 &middot; manual page 232 &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d36">CA amended: 2D.36</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2D.36/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Except on approaches to interchanges (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d49">Section 2D.49</a>), the Destination (D1-1 through D1-3) signs (see <a href="#fig-2d-9">Figure 2D-9</a>), if used, shall be a horizontal rectangle displaying the name of a city, town, village, or other traffic generator, and a directional arrow.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> The distance (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d43">Section 2D.43</a>) to the place named may also be displayed on the Destination (D1-1a through D1-3a) signs (see <a href="#fig-2d-9">Figure 2D-9</a>). If several destinations are to be displayed at a single point, the several names may be placed on a single sign with an arrow (and the distance, if desired) for each name. If more than one destination lies in the same direction, a single arrow may be used for such a group of destinations.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Adequate separation should be made between any destinations or group of destinations in one direction and those in other directions by suitable design of the arrow, spacing of lines of legend, heavy lines entirely across the sign, or separate signs.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Separation of destinations by direction by the use of a horizontal separator line can enhance the readability of a Destination sign by relating an arrow and its corresponding destination(s) and by eliminating the need for multiple arrows that point in the same direction and excessive space between lines of legend.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Except as otherwise provided in this Manual, an arrow pointing to the right shall be at the extreme right of the sign, and an arrow pointing left or up shall be at the extreme left. The distance numerals, if used, shall be placed to the right of the destination names.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> An arrow pointing up may be placed at the extreme right of the sign when the sign is mounted to the left of the traffic to which it applies.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Unless a sloping arrow will convey a clearer indication of the direction to be followed, the directional arrows should be horizontal or vertical.</p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> If several individual name signs are assembled into a group, all signs in the assembly should be of the same horizontal width.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Destination signs should be used: A. At the intersections of U.S. or State numbered routes with Interstate, U.S., or State numbered routes; and B. At points where they serve to direct traffic from U.S. or State numbered routes to the business section of towns, or to other destinations reached by unnumbered routes.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> Where a total of three or fewer destinations are displayed on the Advance guide (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2e/#2e23">Section 2E.23</a>) and Supplemental guide (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2e/#2e51">Section 2E.51</a>) signs, no more than three destination names shall be displayed on a Destination sign. Where four destinations are displayed on the Advance guide and Supplemental guide signs, no more than four destination names shall be displayed on a Destination sign.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> If space permits, four destinations should be displayed on two separate signs at two separate locations.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> Where space does not permit, or where all four destinations are in one direction, a single sign may be used. Where a single sign is used and all destinations are in the same direction, the arrow may be placed below the destinations for the purpose of enhancing the conspicuity of the arrow.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> Where a single four-name sign assembly is used, a heavy line approximating the width of the sign border entirely across the sign or separate signs shall be used to separate destinations by direction.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> The closest destination lying straight ahead should be at the top of the sign or assembly, and below it the closest destinations to the left and to the right, in that order. The destination displayed for each direction should ordinarily be the next county seat or the next principal city, rather than a more distant destination. In the case of overlapping routes, only one destination should be displayed in each direction for each route.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">15</span> If more than one destination is displayed in the same direction, the name of a nearer destination shall be displayed above the name of a destination that is farther away.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">16</span> Overhead destination guide signs are sometimes helpful on multi-lane conventional roadways with complex or unusual roadway alignments or geometrics at intersecting highways to provide positive direction to destinations and to assign lanes to be used for destinations.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">17</span> Overhead Destination signs may be used to provide lane assignment and destination information for some or all of the lanes on the approach to a multi-lane intersection. Destination information may include cardinal directions, route numbers, street names, and/or place names.</p>
<p><span class="pn">18</span> Overhead signs using the Arrow-per-Lane sign design configuration (see <a href="#fig-2d-10">Figure 2D-10</a>) may be used to provide lane assignments for some or all lane destinations at the approach to a multi-lane intersection (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d37">Section 2D.37</a>).</p>
</div>

## 2D.37 Overhead Arrow-per-Lane Destination Guide Signs


<div class="secmeta">Section 2D.37 &middot; manual page 234 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-same" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d37">CA MUTCD 2026: 2D.37</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2D.37/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Overhead Arrow-per-Lane destination guide signs are sometimes used on multi-lane conventional roadways to provide positive direction to destinations and to indicate lanes to be used for those destinations. These locations typically include complex or unusual roadway alignments or geometrics. Overhead Arrow-per-Lane signs on conventional roads do not always have arrows for every lane. Sheet 2 of <a href="#fig-2a-4">Figure 2A-4</a> and Sheet 1 of <a href="#fig-2d-10">Figure 2D-10</a> show examples of the use of an Overhead Arrow-per-Lane Guide sign on a conventional road. Unlike the Combined Lane-Use/Destination (D15-1) sign (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d38">Section 2D.38</a>), Overhead Arrow-per-Lane signs can be used to provide lane assignments where the designated lane is not a mandatory movement lane.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> At complex intersection approaches involving multiple lanes and destinations, an Overhead Arrow-per- Lane destination guide sign may be used to provide destination information for some or all lanes. Destination information may include cardinal direction, route numbers, street names, and/or place names.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Overhead Arrow-per-Lane signs for conventional roads shall only be used for multi-lane approaches to intersections that have an option lane.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide signs used on conventional roads shall include as a minimum one arrow above each mandatory turn lane and a bifurcated arrow for the option lane from which both the through and turning movements are allowed.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Displaying an arrow over each through movement lane that does not allow turning should be considered for providing additional positive guidance.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Overhead Arrow-per-Lane signs for conventional roads shall be designed in accordance with the following criteria: A. The shaft of each arrow shall be located over the approximate center of the lane to which it applies. B. Arrows for continuing through lanes shall be vertically upward-pointing (see <a href="#fig-2d-10">Figure 2D-10</a>). C. The arrow for a lane that must turn shall be curved in the direction of the turn and shall be accompanied by a black-on-yellow ONLY (E11-1b) sign panel (see <a href="#fig-2e-17">Figure 2E-17</a>) adjacent to the lower end of the arrow shaft. D. The arrow for an optional exit lane that also carries the through route shall have a single shaft that bifurcates into a vertically upward-pointing arrow and a curving arrow corresponding to the configuration of the through and turn lanes. E. A vertical white line shall be used to separate the route shields and destinations for the two diverging movements from each other. F. The number of lanes displayed on a sign shall correspond to the number of lanes being signed for at the location of that sign. An advance sign shall not depict lanes that are added downstream of a sign location.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide signs used on conventional roads should be designed in accordance with the following additional criteria: A. No more than one destination should be displayed for each movement, and no more than three destinations should be displayed per sign. B. The arrowhead(s) for the diverging movement should be positioned lower on the sign than the arrowhead(s) for the movement that continues straight ahead. C. Route shields, cardinal directions, and destinations should be positioned on the sign such that they are clearly related to the arrowhead(s) for the movement to which they apply. D. The vertical white line that is used to separate the route shields and destinations for the two diverging movements from each other should not descend below the top of the arrowheads for the through lanes, and should be positioned approximately halfway between the diverging arrowheads for the optional movement lane.</p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Destination information should be kept to a minimum necessary to provide positive guidance without overloading the road user.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> The minimum height of arrows on an Overhead Arrow-per-Lane sign used on a conventional road shall be as shown in <a href="#fig-2d-5">Table 2D-5</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> When letter heights and other sign legend elements are enlarged there should be a corresponding increase in the arrow size used.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">32</span> Curved-stem arrows may be substituted on Overhead Arrow-per- Lane signs on multi-lane approaches to a</p>
<p><span class="pn">21</span> circular intersection with an option lane (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d39">Section 2D.39</a>).</p>
</div>

## 2D.38 Combination Lane-Use/Destination Overhead Guide Sign (D15-1)


<div class="secmeta">Section 2D.38 &middot; manual page 237 &middot; <a class="badge ca-same" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d38">CA MUTCD 2026: 2D.38</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2D.38/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> At intersection approaches involving multiple turn lanes and destinations, a Combination Lane-Use/ Destination (D15-1) overhead guide sign (see <a href="#fig-2d-9">Figure 2D-9</a>) that combines a lane-use regulatory sign with destination information such as a cardinal direction, a route number, a street name, and/or a place name may be used.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> At such locations, the combined information on the D15-1 signs can be even more effective than separate lane- use and guide signs for conveying to unfamiliar drivers which lane or lanes to use for a particular destination.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> <a href="#fig-2d-9">Figure 2D-9</a> shows an example of a D15-1 sign that combines lane-use and street name information and an example of a D15-1 sign that combines lane-use, cardinal direction, and route number information.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> The Combination Lane-Use/Destination (D15-1) overhead guide sign shall be used only where the designated lane is a mandatory movement lane. The D15-1 sign shall not be used for lanes with optional movements.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> The D15-1 sign shall have a green background with a white border. As shown in <a href="#fig-2d-9">Figure 2D-9</a>, the lane- use sign (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2b/">Chapter 2B</a>) shall be placed near the bottom of the sign and the destination information shall be placed near the top of the sign. The D15-1 sign shall be located over the approximate center of the lane to which it applies.</p>
</div>

## 2D.39 Destination Signs at Circular Intersections


<div class="secmeta">Section 2D.39 &middot; manual page 238 &middot; <span class="badge muted">no CA counterpart found</span> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2D.39/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Destination signs that are used at circular intersections shall comply with the provisions of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d36">Section 2D.36</a>, except as provided in this Section.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Exit Destination (D1-1d and D1-1e) signs (see <a href="#fig-2d-11">Figure 2D-11</a>) with diagonal upward-pointing arrows or Directional assemblies (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d32">Section 2D.32</a>) may be used to designate a particular exit from a circular intersection.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Destination (D1-2d and D1-3d) signs (see <a href="#fig-2d-11">Figure 2D-11</a>) with curved-stem arrows may be used on approaches to circular intersections to represent the left-turn movements.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Curved-stem arrows on circular intersection destination signs may point in diagonal directions to depict the location of an exit relative to the approach roadway and entry into the intersection.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> An Overhead Arrow-per-Lane Destination sign (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d37">Section 2D.37</a>) with curved-stem arrows may be used on multi-lane approaches to circular intersections that have an option lane.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> A Destination (D1-5 or D1-5a) sign (see <a href="#fig-2d-11">Figure 2D-11</a>) with a diagram of the circular intersection may be used on approaches to circular intersections.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> If curved-stem arrows are used on destination signs, then this arrow type should also be used consistently on any regulatory lane-use signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2b/">Chapter 2B</a>), Directional assemblies (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d32">Section 2D.32</a>), and pavement markings (see Part 3) for a particular destination or movement.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> <a href="#fig-2d-12">Figure 2D-12</a> shows examples of guide signing for circular intersections.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Circular Intersection Diagrammatic (D1-5 or D1-5a) signs might be preferable where space is available and where the geometry of the circular intersection is non-typical, such as where more than four legs are present or where the legs are not at approximately 90-degree angles to each other. In such cases, minimizing the amount of legend for each destination and designing the sign so that the arrows for each destination clearly align with the roadway geometry will aid road user understanding of the sign and navigation through the area.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> If used, the Circular Intersection Diagrammatic signs shall not depict the number of lanes within the circulatory roadway of the intersection, or on its approaches or exits, through the use of lane lines, multiple arrow shafts for the same movement, or other methods.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2b/">Chapter 2B</a> contains information regarding regulatory signs at circular intersections, <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2c/">Chapter 2C</a> contains information regarding warning signs at circular intersections, and <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3d/">Chapter 3D</a> contains information regarding pavement markings at circular intersections.</p>
</div>

## 2D.43 Distance Signs (D2 Series)


<div class="secmeta">Section 2D.43 &middot; manual page 239 &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d43">CA amended: 2D.43</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2D.43/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> If used, the Distance (D2-1 through D2-3) signs (see <a href="#fig-2d-9">Figure 2D-9</a>) shall be a horizontal rectangle of a size appropriate for the required legend, displaying the names of no more than three cities, towns, junctions, or other traffic generators, and the distance (to the nearest mile) to those places.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> The distance numerals shall be placed to the right of the destination names as shown in <a href="#fig-2d-9">Figure 2D-9</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> The distance displayed should be selected on a case-by-case basis by the jurisdiction that owns the road or by statewide policy. A well-defined central area or central business district should be used where one exists. In other cases, the layout of the community should be considered in relation to the highway being signed and the decision based on where it appears that most drivers would feel that they are in the center of the community in question.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> The top name on the Distance sign should be that of the next place on the route having a post office or a railroad station, a route number or name of an intersected highway, or any other significant geographical identity. The bottom name on the sign should be that of the next major destination or control city. If three destinations are displayed, the middle line should be used to indicate communities of general interest along the route or important route junctions.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> The choice of names for the middle line may be varied on successive Distance signs to give road users additional information concerning communities served by the route.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> The control city should remain the same on all successive Distance signs throughout the length of the route until that city is reached.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> If more than one distant point may properly be designated, such as where the route divides at some distance ahead to serve two destinations of similar importance, and if these two destinations cannot appear on the same sign, the two names may be alternated on successive signs.</p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> On a route continuing into another State, destinations in the adjacent State may be displayed.</p>
</div>

## 2D.45 Street Name Signs (D3-1 and D3-1a)


<div class="secmeta">Section 2D.45 &middot; manual page 247 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d45">CA amended: 2D.45</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2D.45/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Street Name signs at intersections and along roadways provide road users with important navigation information. <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2h/#2h10">Section 2H.10</a> contains information about signs used to identify the names of grade-separated streets, railways, bikeways, or other transportation facilities.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Street Name (D3-1 or D3-1a) signs (see <a href="#fig-2d-14">Figure 2D-14</a>) should be installed in urban areas at all street intersections regardless of other route signs that might be present and should be installed in rural areas to identify important roads that are not otherwise signed.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> To minimize wrong-way movements onto freeway or expressway exit ramps, Street Name signs should not be used at the intersection of a freeway or expressway exit ramp with the crossroad to display the name of the freeway or expressway to traffic on the crossroad.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> For streets that are part of a U.S., State, or county numbered route, a D3-1a Street Name sign (see <a href="#fig-2d-14">Figure 2D-14</a>) that incorporates a route shield may be used to assist road users who might not otherwise be able to associate the name of the street with the route number.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> The lettering for names of streets and highways on Street Name signs shall be composed of a combination of lower-case letters with initial upper-case letters (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2a/#2a08">Section 2A.08</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> The determination of letter heights to be used on Street Name signs should be based on, but not limited to, the following considerations: A. Use of Advance Street Name signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d46">Section 2D.46</a>); B. Number of lanes on the intersection approach; C. Length of turn lanes; D. Distance the Street Name sign is located across the intersection (if a sign is not provided on the near side of the intersection).</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Letter heights on street name signs should be as shown in <a href="#fig-2d-6">Table 2D-6</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> For two-lane local roadways with speed limits of 25 mph or less, the lettering on post-mounted Street Name signs may be composed of initial upper-case letters at least 4 inches in height and lower-case letters at least 3 inches in nominal loop height.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> The recommended minimum letter heights for Street Name signs are summarized in <a href="#fig-2d-6">Table 2D-6</a>. The speed limits specified and the recommended minimum letter heights provided in this Section apply to the roadway that each Street Name sign faces rather than to the street that has its name displayed on the Street Name sign. The letter heights specified in <a href="#fig-2d-6">Table 2D-6</a> are the combination of lower-case letters with the initial upper-case letter of a mixed-case legend.</p>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> A minimum upper-case letter height of 12 inches with a lower-case nominal loop height of 9 inches is recommended for all overhead Street Name signs regardless of posted speed limit as Street Name signs generally require greater legibility distances for road users to properly react.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> Each Street Name sign in a sign assembly may use different letter heights determined by the speed limit of the street that each sign faces.</p>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> The letter height of the street name descriptor (such as St, Ave, or Rd), the directional legend (such as NW), or any other supplemental legend (such as block or house numbers) on the D3-1 and D3-1a signs may be smaller than that of the street name itself.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> The letter height of the street name descriptor, the directional legend, or any other supplemental legend on the D3-1 and D3-1a signs should be at least two-thirds of the letter height of the street name itself, but not less than 3 inches for the initial upper-case letters and not less than 2.25 inches for the nominal loop height of the lower-case letters.</p>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> Conventional abbreviations (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/1d/#1d08">Section 1D.08</a>) should be used except for the street name itself. Acceptable abbreviations for street name descriptors such as “Ave” for Avenue and “Blvd” for Boulevard should be as provided in <a href="#fig-2d-3">Table 2D-3</a> (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d07">Section 2D.07</a>). The street name descriptors that are provided in <a href="#fig-2d-4">Table 2D-4</a> should not be abbreviated (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d07">Section 2D.07</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">15</span> Block or house numbers may be displayed as a supplemental legend on a Street Name sign to aid emergency responders and road users in locating addresses.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">16</span> If block or house numbers are displayed on a Street Name sign where only a single Street Name sign is provided for the crossroad, the block or house numbers for the left and right blocks should be positioned at the left and right sides of the sign, respectively.</p>
<p><span class="pn">17</span> If block or house numbers are displayed on a Street Name sign where two Street Name signs are provided for the crossroad, such as on diagonally opposite corners of an intersection, each Street Name sign should display only the block or house numbers associated with that block of the crossroad.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">18</span> A pictograph (see definition in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/1c/#1c02">Section 1C.02</a>) representing the municipality, in accordance with the provisions of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2a/#2a04">Section 2A.04</a>, may be used on a D3-1 sign. For street networks under the primary jurisdiction of another governmental-approved entity, such as within a college or university campus, within a military base, or within a transportation facility (such as an airport or port), a pictograph representing that entity, in accordance with the provisions of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2a/#2a04">Section 2A.04</a>, may be used on a D3-1 sign within the jurisdictional boundaries of that entity.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">19</span> Pictographs shall not be displayed on D3-1a or Advance Street Name (D3-2) signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d46">Section 2D.46</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">20</span> If a pictograph is used on a D3-1 sign, the height and width of the pictograph shall not exceed the upper- case letter height of the principal legend of the sign.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">21</span> The pictograph should be positioned to the left of the street name.</p>
<p><span class="pn">22</span> Pictographs should not be used on a D3-1 sign that contains directional arrows.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">23</span> The Street Name sign shall be retroreflective or illuminated in accordance with the provisions of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2a/#2a21">Section 2A.21</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">24</span> The border may be omitted from a post-mounted Street Name sign.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">25</span> The decision to omit the border from a post-mounted Street Name sign should be based on such factors as the visual complexity of the environment and the degree of conspicuity needed to provide for adequate recognition of the sign by the road user.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">26</span> An alternative background color (see Paragraph 28 of this Section) other than the standard guide sign color of green may be used for Street Name (D3-1 or D3-1a) signs where the highway agency determines this is necessary to assist road users in determining jurisdictional authority for roads.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">27</span> Alternative background colors shall not be used for Advance Street Name (D3-2) signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d46">Section 2D.46</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">28</span> The only acceptable alternative background colors for Street Name (D3-1 or D3-1a) signs shall be blue, brown, or white. Regardless of whether green, blue, or brown is used as the background color for Street Name (D3-1 or D3-1a) signs, the legend (and border, if used) shall be white. For Street Name signs that use a white background, the legend (and border, if used) shall be black.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">29</span> An alternative background color for Street Name signs, if used, should be applied to the Street Name (D3-1 or D3-1a) signs on all roadways under the jurisdiction of a particular highway agency.</p>
<p><span class="pn">30</span> In business or commercial areas and on principal arterials, Street Name signs should be placed at least on diagonally opposite corners. In residential areas, at least one Street Name sign should be mounted at each intersection. Signs naming both streets should be installed at each intersection. They should be mounted with their faces parallel to the streets they name.</p>
<p><span class="pn">31</span> Where used, Street Name signs should display their legends on both the front and back sides of the sign to facilitate navigation for pedestrians.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">32</span> To optimize visibility, Street Name signs may be mounted overhead. Street Name signs may also be placed above a regulatory or STOP or YIELD sign with no required vertical separation.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">33</span> In urban or suburban areas, especially where Advance Street Name signs for signalized and other major intersections are not used, the use of overhead Street Name signs should be strongly considered.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">34</span> At intersection crossroads where the same road has two different street names for each direction of travel, both street names may be displayed on the same Street Name (D3-1) sign along with Type D directional arrows, except where one arrow would point in a direction opposing the flow of traffic on a one-way street or where a turn in the direction of the arrow is not allowed.</p>
<p><span class="pn">35</span> On lower-speed roadways, historic street name signs within locally identified historic districts that are consistent with the criteria contained in 36 CFR 60.4 for such structures and districts may remain in service without complying with the provisions of Paragraphs 5, 7, 8, 13, 14, 20, 21, 23, and 28 through 30 of this Section.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">36</span> Streets or segments of a street that have been memorialized or dedicated should not use a second Street Name sign to display the memorial or dedication name (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d56">Section 2D.56</a>). When signed, the Memorial or Dedication sign should be located to minimize its conspicuity and potential for confusion.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>

<p><span class="pn">38</span> Information regarding the identification of overcrossing and undercrossing roadways at grade separations is contained in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2h/#2h10">Section 2H.10</a>.</p>

## 2D.46 Advance Street Name Signs (D3-2 Series)


<div class="secmeta">Section 2D.46 &middot; manual page 250 &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d46">CA amended: 2D.46</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2D.46/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Advance Street Name (D3-2) signs (see <a href="#fig-2d-14">Figure 2D-14</a>) identify a downstream intersection. Although this is often the next intersection, it could also be several intersections away in cases where the next signalized intersection is referenced.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Advance Street Name (D3-2) signs, if used, shall supplement rather than be used instead of the Street Name (D3-1 or D3-1a) signs at the intersection.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Advance Street Name (D3-2) signs may be installed in advance of signalized or unsignalized intersections to provide road users with advance information to identify the name(s) of the next intersecting street to prepare for crossing traffic and to facilitate timely deceleration and/or lane changing in preparation for a turn.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> On arterial highways in rural areas, Advance Street Name signs should be used in advance of all signalized intersections and in advance of all intersections with mandatory turn lanes.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> In urban areas, Advance Street Name signs should be used in advance of all signalized intersections on major arterial streets, except where signalized intersections are so closely spaced that advance placement of the signs is impracticable.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> The heights of the letters on Advance Street Name signs should comply with the provisions of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d05">Section 2D.05</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> If used, Advance Street Name signs shall have a white legend and border on a green background. Alternative background colors shall not be used on Advance Street Name signs.</p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> If used, Advance Street Name signs shall provide the name(s) of the intersecting street(s) on the top line(s) of the legend and the distance to the intersecting streets or messages such as NEXT SIGNAL, NEXT INTERSECTION, NEXT CIRCLE, or directional arrow(s) on the bottom line of the legend.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Pictographs shall not be displayed on Advance Street Name signs.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> Directional arrow(s) may be placed to the right or left of the street name or message such as NEXT SIGNAL, as appropriate, rather than on the bottom line of the legend. Curved-stem arrows may be used on Advance Street Name signs on approaches to circular intersections.</p>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> For intersecting crossroads where the same road has a different street name for each direction of travel, the different street names may be displayed on the same Advance Street Name sign along with directional arrows.</p>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> In advance of two closely-spaced intersections where it is impracticable to install separate Advance Street Name signs, the Advance Street Name sign may include the street names for both intersections along with appropriate supplemental legends for both street names, such as NEXT INTERSECTION, 2ND INTERSECTION, or NEXT LEFT and NEXT RIGHT, or directional arrows.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> If two street names are used on the Advance Street Name sign, the street names should be displayed in the following order: A. For a single intersection where the same road has a different street name for each direction of travel, the name of the street to the left should be displayed above the name of the street to the right; or B. For two closely-spaced intersections, the name of the first street encountered should be displayed above the name of the second street encountered, and the arrow associated with the second street encountered should be an advance arrow, such as the arrow shown on the <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w16-6p/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W16-6P.svg">W16-6P</a> arrow plaque (see <a href="#fig-2c-16">Figure 2C-16</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> An Advance Street Name (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w16-8p/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W16-8P.svg">W16-8P</a> or <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w16-8ap/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W16-8aP.svg">W16-8aP</a>) plaque (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2c/#2c65">Section 2C.65</a>) with black legend on a yellow background, installed to supplement an Intersection (W2 series) or Advance Traffic Control (W3 series) warning sign may be used instead of an Advance Street Name guide sign.</p>
</div>

## 2D.49 Signing on Conventional Roads on Approaches to Interchanges


<div class="secmeta">Section 2D.49 &middot; manual page 252 &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d49">CA amended: 2D.49</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2D.49/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Because there are a number of different ramp configurations that are commonly used at interchanges with conventional roads, drivers on the conventional road cannot reliably predict whether they will be required to turn left or right in order to enter the correct ramp to access the freeway or expressway in the desired direction of travel. Consistently applied signing for conventional road approaches to freeway or expressway interchanges is highly desirable.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> On multi-lane conventional roads approaching an interchange, guide signs shall be provided to identify which direction of turn is to be made and/or which specific lane to use for ramp access to each direction of the freeway or expressway.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> The signing of conventional roads with one lane of traffic approaching an interchange should consist of a sequence containing the following signs (see <a href="#fig-2d-15">Figure 2D-15</a>): A. Junction Assembly B. Destination sign C. Directional Assembly or Entrance Direction sign for the first ramp D. Advance Route Turn Assembly or Advance Entrance Direction sign with an advance turn arrow E. Directional Assembly or Entrance Direction sign for the second ramp</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> If used, the Entrance Direction sign shall consist of a white legend and border on a green background. It shall contain the freeway or expressway route shield(s), cardinal direction, and directional arrow(s).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> The Entrance Direction sign may contain a destination(s) and/or an action message such as NEXT RIGHT.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> At minor interchanges (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2e/#2e30">Section 2E.30</a>), the following sequence of signs may be used (see <a href="#fig-2d-16">Figure 2D-16</a>): A. Junction Assembly B. Directional Assembly for the first ramp C. Directional Assembly for the second ramp</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> On multi-lane conventional roads approaching an interchange, the sign sequence should contain the following signs (see <a href="#fig-2d-17">Figures 2D-17</a> through 2D-19): A. Junction Assembly B. Advance Entrance Direction sign(s) for both directions (if applicable) of travel on the freeway or expressway C. Entrance Direction sign for first ramp D. Advance Turn Assembly E. Entrance Direction sign for the second ramp</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Advance Entrance Direction signs are used to direct road users to the appropriate lane(s).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> The Advance Entrance Direction sign shall consist of a white legend and border on a green background. It shall contain the freeway or expressway route shield(s) and cardinal direction(s).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> The Advance Entrance Direction sign may have destinations, directional arrows, and/or an action message such as KEEP LEFT, NEXT LEFT, or SECOND RIGHT. Signs in this sequence may be mounted overhead to improve visibility as shown in <a href="#fig-2d-17">Figures 2D-17</a> through 2D-19.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> A post-mounted Advance Entrance Direction diagrammatic sign (see <a href="#fig-2d-20">Figure 2D-20</a>), within the sequence of approach guide signing described in Paragraphs 3, 6, and 7 of this Section, might be helpful in depicting the location of a freeway or expressway entrance ramp that is in close proximity to an intervening intersection on the same side of the approach roadway and where signing for only the ramp might cause confusion to road users.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> If used, the post-mounted Advance Entrance Direction diagrammatic sign shall display only the two successive turns from the same side of the roadway, one of which shall be the entrance ramp. The post- mounted Advance Entrance Direction sign shall depict only the successive turns and shall not depict lane use with lane lines, multiple arrow shafts for the approach roadway, action messages, or other representations.</p>
</div>

## 2D.51 WEIGH STATION Signing (D8 Series)


<div class="secmeta">Section 2D.51 &middot; manual page 261 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d51">CA amended: 2D.51</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2D.51/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Independent facilities or areas have been added along many highways where certain commercial vehicles are directed to stop to be weighed and/or inspected. These areas are sometimes permanent, such as in a roadside area, or temporary mobile facilities deployed along the roadway.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> The general concept for signing permanent Weigh Stations is similar to signing Rest Areas (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2i/#2i05">Section 2I.05</a>) because in both cases traffic using either area remains within the right-of-way.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> The standard sequence of signs for a Weigh Station on a conventional highway shall include three basic signs (see <a href="#fig-2d-23">Figure 2D-23</a>): A. Weigh Station Ahead (D8-1a) or Advance Weigh Station Distance (D8-1) sign, B. Weigh Station Advance Direction (D8-2) or Advance Weigh Station Distance (D8-1) sign, and C. Weigh Station Entrance Direction (D8-3) sign.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> A Gore sign with the same basic legend as the Weigh Station Entrance Direction (D8-3) sign should also be used to emphasize the entrance to the weigh station.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Where State law requires trucks of a certain weight to enter the Weigh Station, a Weigh Station (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r13-1/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R13-1.svg">R13-1</a>) regulatory sign (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2b/#2b65">Section 2B.65</a>) may be located following the Weigh Station Ahead sign (see <a href="#fig-2d-23">Figure 2D-23</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Where only commercial vehicle inspections are conducted in the inspection area, the WEIGH STATION legend of the D8 series signs may be replaced with the alternate legend, COMMERCIAL VEHICLE INSPECTION.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> The Weigh Station Advance Direction (D8-2) sign, the Weigh Station Ahead (D8-1a) sign, or the Advance Weigh Station Distance (D8-1) sign should display, either on the sign or on a supplemental plaque or sign panel, the changeable legend OPEN or CLOSED.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> When the WEIGH STATION legend of the D8 series signs is replaced with the COMMERCIAL VEHICLE INSPECTION legend, as provided in Paragraph 6 of this Section, the WEIGH STATION legend of the <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r13-1/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R13-1.svg">R13-1</a> sign shall likewise be replaced with the alternate legend.</p>
</div>

## 2D.55 Community Wayfinding Signs


<div class="secmeta">Section 2D.55 &middot; manual page 268 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d55">CA amended: 2D.55</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2D.55/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Community wayfinding guide signs are part of a coordinated and continuous system of signs that direct tourists and other road users to key civic, cultural, visitor, and recreational attractions and other similar secondary destinations within a city or a local urbanized or downtown area.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Community wayfinding guide signs are a type of destination guide sign for conventional roads with a common color and/or identification marker for destinations within an overall wayfinding guide sign plan for an area.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> <a href="#fig-2d-30">Figures 2D-30</a> through 2D-32 illustrate various examples of the design and application of community wayfinding guide signs.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> The use of community wayfinding guide signs shall be limited to conventional roads. Community wayfinding guide signs shall not be installed on freeway or expressway mainlines or ramps. Direction to community wayfinding destinations from a freeway or expressway shall be limited to the use of a Supplemental guide sign (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2e/#2e51">Section 2E.51</a>) on the mainline and a Destination sign (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d36">Section 2D.36</a>) on the ramp to direct road users to the area or areas within which community wayfinding guide signs are used. The individual wayfinding destinations shall not be displayed on the Supplemental guide and Destination signs except where the destinations are in accordance with the State or agency policy on Supplemental guide signs.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Community wayfinding guide signs shall not be used to provide direction to primary destinations or highway routes or streets. Destination or other guide signs shall be used for this purpose as described elsewhere in this Chapter and shall have priority over any community wayfinding sign in placement, prominence, and conspicuity.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Because regulatory, warning, and other guide signs have a higher priority, community wayfinding guide signs shall not be installed where adequate spacing cannot be provided between the community wayfinding guide sign and other higher-priority signs. Community wayfinding guide signs shall not be installed in a position where they would obscure the road users’ view of other traffic control devices.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Community wayfinding guide signs shall not be mounted overhead.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> If used, a community wayfinding guide sign system should be established on a local municipal or equivalent jurisdictional level or for an urbanized area of adjoining municipalities or equivalent that form an identifiable geographic entity that is conducive to a cohesive and continuous system of signs. Community wayfinding guide signs should not be used on a regional or statewide basis where infrequent or sparse placement does not contribute to a continuous or coordinated system of signing that is readily identifiable as such to the road user. In such cases, Destination or other guide signs detailed in this Chapter should be used to direct road users to an identifiable area in which the type of eligible destination described in Paragraph 1 of this Section is located.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> When a system of community wayfinding guide signs is being considered, the system of existing guide signs should be evaluated for applicability and general compliance with the provisions of this Manual to ensure road user directional guidance is adequately being addressed.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> The specific provisions of this Section regarding the design of community wayfinding sign legends apply to vehicular community wayfinding signs and do not apply to those signs that are intended only to provide information or direction to pedestrians or other users of a sidewalk or roadside area.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> Because pedestrian wayfinding signs typically use smaller legends that are inadequately sized for viewing by vehicular traffic and because they can provide direction to pedestrians that might conflict with that appropriate for vehicular traffic, wayfinding signs designed for and intended to provide direction to pedestrians or other users of a sidewalk or other roadside area should be located to minimize their conspicuity to vehicular traffic. Such signs should be located as far as practicable from the street, such as at the far edge of the sidewalk. Where locating such signs farther from the roadway is impracticable, the pedestrian wayfinding signs should have their conspicuity to vehicular traffic minimized by employing one or a combination of the following methods: A. Locating signs away from intersections where high-priority traffic control devices are present. B. Facing the pedestrian message toward the sidewalk and away from the street. C. Cantilevering the sign over the sidewalk if the pedestrian wayfinding sign is mounted at a height consistent with vehicular traffic signs, removing the pedestrian wayfinding signs from the line of sight in a sequence of vehicular signs.</p>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> To further minimize their conspicuity to vehicular traffic during nighttime conditions, pedestrian wayfinding signs should not be retroreflective.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> Color coding is sometimes used on community wayfinding guide signs to help road users distinguish between multiple potentially confusing traffic generator destinations located in different neighborhoods or subareas within a community or area.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> At the boundaries of the geographical area within which community wayfinding guide signing is used, an informational guide sign may be posted to inform road users about the presence of wayfinding signing and to identify the meanings of the various color codes or pictographs that are being used.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">15</span> These informational guide signs shall have a white legend and border on a green background and shall have a design similar to that illustrated in <a href="#fig-2d-1">Figure 2D-1</a> and shall be consistent with the basic design principles for guide signs. These informational guide signs shall not be installed on freeway or expressway mainlines or ramps.</p>
<p><span class="pn">16</span> The color coding or a pictograph of the identification markers of the community wayfinding guide signing system shall be included on the informational guide sign posted at the boundary of the community wayfinding guide signing area. The color coding or pictographs shall apply to a specific, identifiable neighborhood or geographical subarea within the overall area covered by the community wayfinding guide signing. Color coding or pictographs shall not be used to distinguish between different types of destinations that are within the same designated neighborhood or subarea. The color coding shall be accomplished by the use of different colored square or rectangular panels on the face of the informational guide sign, each positioned to the left of the neighborhood or named geographic area to which the color-coding panel applies. The height of the colored square or rectangular panels shall not exceed 2 times the height of the upper-case letters of the principal legend on the sign.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">17</span> The different colored square or rectangular panels may include either a black or a white (whichever provides the better contrast with the color of the panel) letter, numeral, or other appropriate designation to identify the destination.</p>
<p><span class="pn">18</span> Except for the informational guide sign posted at the boundary of the wayfinding guide sign area, community wayfinding guide signs may use background colors other than green in order to provide a color identification for the wayfinding destinations by geographical area within the overall wayfinding guide signing system. Color-coded community wayfinding guide signs may be used with or without the boundary informational guide sign displaying corresponding color-coding panels described in Paragraphs 13 through 16 of this Section. Except as provided in Paragraph 19 of this Section, in addition to the colors that are approved in this Manual for use on official traffic control signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2a/#2a06">Section 2A.06</a>), other background colors may also be used for the color coding of community wayfinding guide signs.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">19</span> The standard colors of red, orange, yellow, purple, or the fluorescent versions thereof, fluorescent yellow-green, and fluorescent pink shall not be used as background colors for community wayfinding guide signs, in order to minimize possible confusion with critical, higher-priority regulatory and warning sign color meanings readily understood by road users.</p>
<p><span class="pn">20</span> The minimum contrast value of legend color to background color for community wayfinding guide signs shall be at least 0.70 (or 70%).</p>
<p><span class="pn">21</span> All messages, borders, legends, and backgrounds of community wayfinding guide signs and any identification markers shall be retroreflective (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2a/#2a21">Sections 2A.21</a> and 2A.22).</p>
<p><span class="pn">22</span> Community wayfinding guide signs, exclusive of any identification marker used, shall be rectangular in shape.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">23</span> Simplicity and uniformity in design, position, and application as described in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2a/#2a04">Section 2A.04</a> are important and should be incorporated into the community wayfinding guide sign design and location plans for the area.</p>
<p><span class="pn">24</span> Community wayfinding guide signs should be limited to three destinations per sign (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d06">Section 2D.06</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">25</span> Abbreviations (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/1d/#1d08">Section 1D.08</a>) should be kept to a minimum, and should include only those that are commonly recognized and understood.</p>
<p><span class="pn">26</span> Horizontal lines of a color that contrasts with the sign background color should be used to separate groups of destinations by direction from each other.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">27</span> The basic requirement for all highway signs, including community wayfinding signs, is that they be legible to those for whom they are intended and that they be understandable in time to permit a proper response. <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2a/#2a04">Section 2A.04</a> contains additional information on the design of signs, including desirable attributes of effective designs.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">28</span> Word messages should be as brief as practical and the lettering should be large enough to provide the necessary legibility distance.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">29</span> The minimum specific ratio of letter height to legibility distance shall comply with the provisions of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2a/#2a08">Section 2A.08</a>. The size of lettering used for destination and directional legends on community wayfinding signs shall comply with the provisions of minimum letter heights as provided in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d05">Section 2D.05</a>.</p>
<p><span class="pn">30</span> Interline and edge spacing shall comply with the provisions of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d05">Section 2D.05</a>.</p>
<p><span class="pn">31</span> Except as provided in Paragraph 34 of this Section, the lettering style used for destination and directional legends on community wayfinding guide signs shall comply with the provisions of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d04">Section 2D.04</a>.</p>
<p><span class="pn">32</span> The lettering for destinations on community wayfinding guide signs shall be a combination of lower-case letters with initial upper-case letters (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d04">Section 2D.04</a>). All other word messages on community wayfinding guide signs shall be in all upper-case letters.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">33</span> Except as provided in Paragraphs 34 and 35 of this Section, letters, numerals, and other characters should be composed of the Standard Alphabets as detailed in the “Standard Highway Signs” publication (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/1a/#1a05">Section 1A.05</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">34</span> A lettering style other than the Standard Alphabets provided in the “Standard Highway Signs” publication (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/1a/#1a05">Section 1A.05</a>) may be used on community wayfinding guide signs if an engineering study determines that the legibility and recognition values for the chosen lettering style meet or exceed the values for the Standard Alphabets for the same legend height and stroke width.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">35</span> If a lettering style other than the Standard Alphabets is used, the alternative lettering style shall be conventional in form. The letters, numerals, and other characters shall not be italic, oblique, script, highly decorative, or of other unusual forms.</p>
<p><span class="pn">36</span> In accordance with <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2a/#2a04">Section 2A.04</a>, except for signs that are designed and located with the intent to be viewed only by pedestrians, bicyclists stopped out of the flow of traffic, or occupants of parked vehicles, Internet and e-mail addresses, including domain names and uniform resource locators (URL), and scanning graphics for the purpose of obtaining information (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2a/#2a04">Section 2A.04</a>), shall not be displayed on any community wayfinding guide sign or sign assembly.</p>
<p><span class="pn">37</span> The arrow location and priority order of destinations shall follow the provisions described in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d08">Sections 2D.08</a> and 2D.36. Arrows shall be of the designs provided in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d08">Section 2D.08</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">38</span> Pictographs (see definition in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/1c/#1c02">Section 1C.02</a>) may be used on community wayfinding guide signs.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">39</span> If a pictograph is used, its height shall not exceed 2 times the height of the upper-case letters of the principal legend on the sign.</p>
<p><span class="pn">40</span> Except for pictographs, symbols that are not approved in this Manual for use on guide signs shall not be used on community wayfinding guide signs.</p>
<p><span class="pn">41</span> Business logos, commercial graphics, or other forms of advertising (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/1d/#1d07">Section 1D.07</a>) shall not be used on community wayfinding guide signs or sign assemblies.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">42</span> Other graphics that specifically identify the wayfinding system, including identification markers, may be used on the overall sign assembly and sign supports.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">43</span> An identification marker consists of a shape, color, and/or pictograph that is used as a visual identifier for the community wayfinding guide signing system for an area. <a href="#fig-2d-30">Figure 2D-30</a> shows examples of identification marker designs that can be used with community wayfinding guide signs.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">44</span> An identification marker may be used in a community wayfinding guide sign assembly, or may be incorporated into the overall design of a community wayfinding guide sign, as a means of visually identifying the sign as part of an overall system of community wayfinding signs and destinations.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">45</span> The sizes and shapes of identification markers shall be smaller than the community wayfinding guide signs themselves. Identification markers shall not be designed to have an appearance that could be mistaken by road users as being a traffic control device.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">46</span> The area of the identification marker should not exceed ¹⁄5 of the area of the community wayfinding guide sign with which it is mounted in the same sign assembly.</p>
</div>

## 2D.56 Signing of Named Highways for Mapping and Address Purposes


<div class="secmeta">Section 2D.56 &middot; manual page 272 &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d56">CA amended: 2D.56</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2D.56/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> A highway name is the officially designated name of a freeway, expressway, or conventional road for navigational, official mapping, and address purposes. Some highways are named in addition to or in lieu of being assigned a highway route number. Memorial, honorary, ceremonial, or other secondary names, such as touring route and byway names, are not considered to be highway names.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Guide signs may contain street or highway names if the purpose is to enhance driver communication and guidance; however, they are to be considered as supplemental information to route numbers.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Highway names shall not replace official numeral designations.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Memorial, honorary, or other secondary names (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2m/#2m10">Section 2M.10</a>) shall not appear on supplemental signs or on any other information sign on or along the highway or its intersecting routes.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> The use of route signs shall be restricted to signs officially used for guidance of traffic in accordance with this Manual and the “Purpose and Policy” statement of the American Association of State Highway and Transportation Officials that applies to Interstate and U.S. numbered routes.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Unnumbered routes having major importance to proper guidance of traffic may be signed if carried out in accordance with the aforementioned policies. For unnumbered highways, a name to enhance route guidance may be used where the name is applied consistently throughout its length.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Only one name should be used to identify any highway, whether numbered or unnumbered.</p>
</div>

## 2D.57 National Scenic Byways Sign and Plaque (M10-1 and M10-1aP)


<div class="secmeta">Section 2D.57 &middot; manual page 273 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-same" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d57">CA MUTCD 2026: 2D.57</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2D.57/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Certain roads have been designated by the U.S. Secretary of Transportation as National Scenic Byways or All-American Roads based on their archeological, cultural, historic, natural, recreational, or scenic qualities.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> State and local highway agencies may install the National Scenic Byways (M10-1) sign or (M10-1aP) plaque (see <a href="#fig-2d-33">Figure 2D-33</a>) at entrance points to a route that has been recognized by the U.S. Secretary of Transportation as a National Scenic Byway or an All-American Road. The M10-1 sign may be installed as independent Directional (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d32">Section 2D.32</a>) or Confirming (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d33">Section 2D.33</a>) assemblies at periodic intervals along the designated route and near intersections where the designated route turns or follows a different numbered highway. The M10-1aP plaque may be installed below a route sign in a Confirming assembly. At locations where roadside features have been developed to enhance the traveler’s experience such as rest areas, historic sites, interpretive facilities, or scenic overlooks, the National Scenic Byways sign or plaque may be placed on the associated sign assembly to inform travelers that the site contributes to the byway travel experience.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Where the byway is identified only by the National Scenic Byways sign, the Directional assembly should consist of the M10-1 sign and an M5 series or M6 series auxiliary plaque when indication of a turn is necessary to remain on the byway route.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Where the name of the byway is to be displayed on identification signs or plaques along the byway route, the name should be displayed in a Directional or Confirming assembly as follows (see <a href="#fig-2d-34">Figure 2D-34</a>): A. On a Byway Identification (M10-2aP) plaque (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d58">Section 2D.58</a>) mounted below the M10-1 sign; or B. On a Byway Identification (M10-2) sign (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d58">Section 2D.58</a>) with the M10-1aP plaque mounted below the sign.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> In either case, the size of the National Scenic Byways (M10-1) sign, (M10-1aP) plaque, Byway Identification (M10-2) sign, and Byway Identification (M10-2aP) plaque should be consistent with that specified for route signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d10">Section 2D.10</a>) for the roadway classification.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Where the name of the byway is to be displayed along the byway route as provided in Paragraph 4 of this Section, the byway Directional or Confirming assemblies should be located separately from any Route Sign assemblies or Destination guide signs.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> When a National Scenic Byways sign is installed on a National Scenic Byway or an All-American Road, the design shown for the M10-1 sign or M10-1aP plaque in <a href="#fig-2d-33">Figure 2D-33</a> shall be used. Use of this design shall be limited to routes that have been designated as a National Scenic Byway or All-American Road by the U.S. Secretary of Transportation.</p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> If used, the M10-1 sign or M10-1aP plaque shall be placed such that the highway route signs have primary visibility for the road user.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> The M10-1 sign or the M10-1aP plaque shall not be installed as sign panels on a guide sign or as part of a guide sign assembly.</p>
</div>

## 2D.58 State-Designated Scenic Byway, Historic Trail, and Auto Tour Route Signs


<div class="secmeta">Section 2D.58 &middot; manual page 274 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d58">CA amended: 2D.58</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2D.58/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Signing for State-designated scenic byways, historic trails, and auto tour routes, is similar in concept to that for National Scenic Byways as provided in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d57">Section 2D.57</a>.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Named highways are officially designated and shown on official maps and serve the purpose of providing route guidance, primarily on unnumbered highways, and property addresses. A highway that is also designated as a trail, auto tour route, or byway is not considered to be a highway name for the purposes of highway signing or road user navigation and orientation. <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d56">Section 2D.56</a> contains provisions for the signing of named highways.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/1d/#1d09">Section 1D.09</a> provides information on the authority for placement of traffic control devices within the highway right-of-way.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Route Sign assemblies and Destination guide signs should have priority in visibility and location over signing related to historic trails, auto tour routes, and byways.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Identification signs for a State scenic byway may be installed along conventional roads that have been designated as part of a State scenic byway system. A Byway Identification (M10-2) sign (see <a href="#fig-2d-34">Figure 2D-34</a>) with the name of the byway displayed may be installed in a Directional or Confirming assembly with the SCENIC BYWAY (M10-3bP) plaque (see <a href="#fig-2d-34">Figure 2D-34</a>) mounted below the M10-2 sign.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Where a National Scenic Byway is part of a State scenic byway system, the National Scenic Byways (M10-1aP) plaque (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d57">Section 2D.57</a>) may be installed in a Directional or Confirming assembly below the Byway Identification (M10-2) sign or State Scenic Byway (M10-3 or M10-3a) sign (see <a href="#fig-2d-34">Figure 2D-34</a>) for the State scenic byway.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> A State Scenic Byway System (M10-3) sign may be installed in a Directional or Confirming assembly with the name of the byway displayed on a Byway Identification (M10-2aP) plaque below the sign (see <a href="#fig-2d-34">Figure 2D-34</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> A State Scenic Byway (M10-3a) sign with a simple graphic and the name of the byway displayed may be installed in a Directional or Confirming assembly with the SCENIC BYWAY (M10-3bP) plaque mounted below the M10-3a sign.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Identification signs for a historic trail, such as the National Historic Trails administered by the National Park Service, may be installed along segments of conventional roads that coincide with the original route of the trail. National Historic Trail Identification (M11-1) signs (see <a href="#fig-2d-34">Figure 2D-34</a>) may be installed in a Directional or Confirming assembly with a HISTORIC ROUTE (M11-1aP), CROSSING (M11-1bP), or AUTO TOUR ROUTE (M11-1cP) auxiliary plaque (see <a href="#fig-2d-34">Figure 2D-34</a>) mounted below the M11-1 sign. The beginning and end of a historic trail route or auto tour route may be indicated with a BEGIN (M4-14P) or END (M4-6P) auxiliary plaque (see <a href="#fig-2d-5">Figure 2D-5</a>) with a white legend and border on a brown background mounted above the historic trail identification sign. The length of the route may be identified by a NEXT XX MILES (M11-1dP) auxiliary plaque mounted below the M11-1aP or M11-1cP auxiliary plaque.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> The design and size of historic trail and State scenic byway identification or system signs should comply with the general provisions and principles for route signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d10">Section 2D.10</a>). Designs should be simple, dignified, and devoid of complex graphics. The size of the signs should not exceed the size of the route signs used along a particular route.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> Scenic byway, historic trail, and auto tour route sign designs shall not have a similar design to or resemble a highway route sign</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> Where used, historic trail and State scenic byway identification signs should be installed as Directional (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d32">Section 2D.32</a>) or Confirming (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d33">Section 2D.33</a>) assemblies at independent locations, separate from other Route Sign assemblies and Destination guide signs. Where used, Confirming assemblies for the trail or byway should be installed at less frequent intervals than Confirming assemblies for the numbered route.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> Where all or part of the original route of a historic trail does not follow a roadway, an auto tour route is sometimes established along a conventional road in the general vicinity of the historical route of the trail. Examples include auto tour routes following other routes that parallel the original routes of the Lewis and Clark National Historic Trail, the Oregon National Historic Trail, and the Santa Fe National Historic Trail.The auto tour route is shown on touring maps along State or other highways and provides access to sites on the trail from those highways.</p>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> A system of signing providing direction along conventional roads for a historic trail with an auto tour route is shown in <a href="#fig-2d-35">Figure 2D-35</a>. Examples of Destination and Supplemental guide signs providing direction to historic trail sites from a freeway or expressway interchange are shown in <a href="#fig-2d-36">Figure 2D-36</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">15</span> Signing for historic trails should be limited to Destination signs for the sites related to the trail and to Directional and Confirming assemblies for the original portions of the trail itself. If an auto tour route has been designated along other highways to provide access to sites along the original trail as described in Paragraph 13 of this Section, then the signing should be limited to Destination signs for those sites and directional signing to access the original route of the trail. Identification signs for the auto tour route should not be installed. Instead, direction along the auto tour route should rely on the touring map and other directional signs for the highways that the auto tour route follows.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">16</span> Identification signs for historic trails, auto tour routes, and scenic byways shall not be installed on freeways or expressways, except as necessary to provide continuity between discontinuous segments of conventional roadways that are designated as a trail, auto tour route, or byway, for which the freeway or expressway provides the only connection between the segments. If installed on freeways or expressways, the identification signs shall be installed as independent trailblazer assemblies (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2d/#2d34">Sections 2D.34</a> and 2E.55) and shall not be installed with other route signs or Confirming assemblies or on guide signs. If installed on freeways or expressways, the trailblazer assemblies for the trail, auto tour route, or byway shall be installed at less frequent intervals than Confirming assemblies for the highway route.</p>
<p><span class="pn">17</span> Identification signs for historic trails, auto tour routes, and scenic byways shall not be installed as sign panels on a guide sign or as part of a guide sign assembly.</p>
</div>

## 2D.59 Emergency Routing Signs and Plaques (M4-11 and M4-12 Series)


<div class="secmeta">Section 2D.59 &middot; manual page 279 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-same" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d59">CA MUTCD 2026: 2D.59</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2D.59/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> As part of an agency’s transportation incident management plan it is sometimes desirable to permanently sign routes that provide rerouting of traffic around highway segments susceptible to traffic incidents. Permanently- installed Emergency Routing signs and plaques (see <a href="#fig-2d-37">Figure 2D-37</a>) provide direction on conventional roads from an exit located upstream of an area that can be susceptible to traffic incidents back to the original route at a point downstream of the incident-susceptible area.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> EMERGENCY ROUTE (M4-11 and M4-11a) signs and EMERGENCY ROUTE TO (M4-11bP and M4-11cP) plaques mounted on a directional assembly may be permanently installed on conventional roadways to provide trailblazing along a designated diversionary route to bypass a traffic incident.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> The purpose of Emergency Routing signs and plaques is for corridor management along routes that have recurring incidents and have reasonable rerouting paths available. These signs are intended to be permanently installed to provide instant rerouting guidance to road users when traffic congestion or backups first begin even before emergency responders could provide temporary traffic control for rerouting traffic. These signs can be used as a stand-alone system or be a part of a larger system which might also incorporate other devices such as changeable message signs. These signs provide road users assurance that a diversionary route will lead them back to their original route of travel.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Emergency Routing signs and plaques shall only be installed at departure points and along diversion routes for directing road users around highway segments in areas that are more susceptible to traffic incidents (see <a href="#fig-2d-38">Figure 2D-38</a>). EMERGENCY ROUTE and EMERGENCY ROUTE TO signs shall be placed at each turning decision point along the designated route until it rejoins the original route or until other directional signs leading back to the original route are provided.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Emergency Routing signs shall have a white legend and border on a green background.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> For additional emphasis the legend EMERGENCY ROUTE or EMERGENCY ROUTE TO may be displayed in a yellow panel with black letters near the top of the sign (see <a href="#fig-2d-37">Figure 2D-37</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Orange or pink shall not be used as alternate colors on permanently-installed signs or plaques for rerouting traffic during an incident or other event. If a route shield is displayed as part of the message, the wording of the sign or plaque shall be EMERGENCY ROUTE TO as shown in <a href="#fig-2d-37">Figure 2D-37</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> An EMERGENCY ROUTE TO plaque with either a white legend and border on a green background (M4-11bP) or black legend and border on a yellow background (M4-11cP) may be added to the top of a conventional Route assembly on a diversion route to provide direction back to the original route downstream of the incident (see <a href="#fig-2d-37">Figures 2D-37</a> and 2D-38).</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> A combination warning and regulatory message sign with flashing beacons mounted above and the legend, WHEN FLASHING [ROUTE] CLOSED AHEAD/ USE EMERGENCY ROUTE [NEXT RIGHT or EXIT XX] may be used in advance of the emergency route entrance to inform road users of the closure and require exiting the primary route and use of the emergency route (see <a href="#fig-2d-37">Figures 2D-37</a> and 2D-38).</p>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> The End Emergency Route (M4-12) sign may be used at a point along the emergency route just prior to the junction where traffic is to reenter the primary route past the closed section of roadway (see <a href="#fig-2d-37">Figures 2D-37</a> and 2D-38).</p>
</div>

## 2D.60 Signing at Airports


<div class="secmeta">Section 2D.60 &middot; manual page 282 &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d60">CA amended: 2D.60</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2D.60/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Many roadways within airport facilities (including terminal curbside roadways) are considered to be conventional roads because they typically have frequent driveways and at-grade intersections and might have pedestrian activity along and/or across them.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Some airport roadways have full or partial control of access and operating speeds higher than 45 mph and thus would be classified as freeways or expressways for signing purposes (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2e/">Chapter 2E</a>). Freeway or expressway conditions typically exist on the approaches to the airport from other highways; on the approaches to access points to terminals, parking, and other patron facilities; and on roadways that provide exits from the airport facility to connect with the local or regional highway network.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Roadways within airports and other similarly-contained roadway networks with multiple closely-spaced access points to multiple destinations (such as terminals, parking facilities, rental car facilities, and other airport services) often present challenges for the application of guide signing. Closely-spaced signs, excessive sign messaging either co-located or in succession, and the resulting excessive informational load imposed on the road user are of particular concern for such roadways. The Transportation Research Board’s Airport Cooperative Research Program Report 52, “Wayfinding and Signing Guidelines for Airport Terminal and Landside,” contains additional information on the application of traffic control devices to the unique geometrics and roadway environment that are typical of airports.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> An example of major guide signing on the approaches to and within an airport facility roadway network is shown in <a href="#fig-2d-39">Figure 2D-39</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> If adequate sign spacing cannot be provided due to the site and roadway characteristics of an airport or similar facility, then measures should be taken to reduce the speeds of vehicles on the roadway to provide road users with adequate time to comprehend and respond to the sign messages. Consideration should also be given to increasing the sign letter heights to provide greater viewing distances and decision times. Where a single terminal serves a large number of airlines, the airline information should be displayed on separate signs that appear in sequence to limit the number of airlines displayed on a single sign or at a single location. Changeable message signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2l/">Chapter 2L</a>) should not be used to rotate the display of airlines to an approaching road user.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> There are various methods that can be used to help reduce vehicle speeds, including roadway geometric changes, implementing traffic calming measures, and increased enforcement. Provisions on setting speed limits are found in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2b/#2b21">Section 2B.21</a>.</p>
</div>
