## 9E.01 Bicycle Lanes


<div class="secmeta">Section 9E.01 &middot; manual page 1086 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/9e/#9e01">CA amended: 9E.01</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/9E.01/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Pavement markings designate that portion of the roadway for preferential use by bicyclists. Markings inform all road users of the restricted nature of the bicycle lane.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Longitudinal pavement markings and bicycle lane symbol or word markings (see <a href="#fig-9e-1">Figure 9E-1</a>) shall be used to define bicycle lanes.</p>
</div>
<div class="mfig-inline single"><figure class="mfig"><span id="fig-9e-1"></span><a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/mutcd/p1086.webp"><img loading="lazy" src="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/mutcd/p1086.webp" alt="Figure 9E-1. Word, Symbol, and Arrow Pavement Markings for Bicycle Lanes <span class="src-page">p 1086</span></figcaption></figure></div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> The first symbol or word marking in a bicycle lane should be placed at the beginning of the bicycle lane and downstream symbol or word markings should be placed after major intersections. Additional symbol or word markings should be placed at periodic intervals along the bicycle lane based on engineering judgment.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> An arrow marking (see <a href="#fig-9e-1">Figure 9E-1</a>) may be used in conjunction with the bicycle lane symbol or word marking, placed downstream from the symbol or word marking.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Where the bicycle lane symbols or word markings are used, Bicycle Lane signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/9b/#9b04">Section 9B.04</a>) may also be used, but not necessarily adjacent to every set of pavement markings in order to avoid overuse of the signs.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3h/#3h06">Section 3H.06</a> contains information on green-colored pavement for use in bicycle lanes.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> The bicycle symbol or BIKE LANE pavement word marking and the pavement marking arrow shall not be used in a shoulder.</p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> A portion of the roadway shall not be established as both a shoulder and a bicycle lane.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Where a shoulder is provided or is of sufficient width to meet the expectation of a highway user in that it can function as a space for emergency, enforcement, or maintenance activities, or avoidance or recovery maneuvers, <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/9b/#9b16">Section 9B.16</a> contains information regarding the Bicycles Use Shoulder Only sign that can be used to denote locations on a freeway or expressway where bicycles are permitted on an available and usable shoulder.</p>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> Examples of pavement markings for bicycle lanes on a two-way street are shown in <a href="#fig-9e-2">Figure 9E-2</a>.</p>
</div>

## 9E.02 Bicycle Lanes at Intersection Approaches


<div class="secmeta">Section 9E.02 &middot; manual page 1087 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/9e/#9e02">CA amended: 9E.02</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/9E.02/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Except as provided in Paragraph 2 of this Section, a through bicycle lane shall not be positioned to the right of a right turn only lane or to the left of a left turn only lane.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> A through bicycle lane may be positioned to the right of a right turn only lane or to the left of a left turn only lane provided that the bicycle lane is controlled by a traffic signal that displays bicycle signal indications (see Chapter 4H).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Unless controlled by a bicycle signal indication, a bicyclist continuing straight through an intersection from the right of a right turn only lane or from the left of a left turn only lane would be inconsistent with normal traffic behavior and would violate the expectations of right-turning or left-turning motorists.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> When the right (left) through lane is dropped to become a mandatory right-turn (left-turn) lane, the bicycle lane markings should stop at least 100 feet before the beginning of the right-turn (left-turn) lane. Through bicycle lane markings should resume to the left (right) of the mandatory right-turn (left-turn) lane.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Except as provided in Paragraph 2 of this Section, an optional through-right (through-left) turn lane next to a mandatory right-turn (left-turn) lane should not be used where there is a through bicycle lane.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> A bicycle lane located on an intersection approach between general-purpose lanes for motor vehicle movements shall be marked with at least one bicycle symbol or word marking and at least one arrow pavement marking as provided in Paragraph 4 of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/9e/#9e01">Section 9E.01</a>.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> A bicycle lane shall not be marked within a general-purpose lane, either with dotted or any other line markings.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Where there is insufficient width in the roadway to include both a bicycle lane and a general-purpose turn lane, bicycle travel may be accommodated within the turn lane or general-purpose lane using shared-lane markings.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Where a general-purpose turn lane is controlled by a traffic control signal, through bicycle movements shall not be accommodated in the turn lane unless the turning movement is always permitted to proceed simultaneously with the adjacent through movement.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> Examples of bicycle lane markings on approaches to intersections are shown in <a href="#fig-9e-3">Figures 9E-3</a>, 9E-4, and 9E-9.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> The longitudinal line defining a bicycle lane should be dotted on approaches to intersections where turning vehicles are permitted to cross the path of through-moving bicycles (see <a href="#fig-9d-7">Figure 9D-7</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> Buffer-separated and separated bicycle lanes require additional considerations at intersections, including sight distances for bicycles and other road users, user expectations, and intersection geometry.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> A buffer-separated or separated bicycle lane may be shifted closer to, or farther away from the adjacent general-purpose lane depending upon site-specific conditions (see Drawings D and E in <a href="#fig-9e-7">Figure 9E-7</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> A buffer-separated or separated bicycle lane shifted away from the adjacent general-purpose lane at an intersection can create space for a motor vehicle to queue between the general-purpose lane and the extension of the bicycle lane. This design can also improve the safety and comfort of bicyclists by reducing the speed of turning motor vehicles, improving sightlines, and creating additional buffer space prior to the conflict point with turning motor vehicles.</p>
<p><span class="pn">15</span> The purpose of shifting a buffer-separated or separated bicycle lane away from the adjacent general-purpose lane is to allow the driver of a turning vehicle to undertake the tasks of turning and scanning for bicycle cross traffic in isolation versus simultaneously. Sufficient sight distance for both drivers and bicyclists is important in this design (see Drawing E in <a href="#fig-9e-7">Figure 9E-7</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">16</span> The purpose of shifting a buffer-separated or separated bicycle lane toward the adjacent general-purpose lane is to improve the visibility of bicyclists to the adjacent traffic and avoid conflicts between turning motor vehicles and bicyclists (see Drawing D in <a href="#fig-9e-7">Figure 9E-7</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">17</span> Staggering stop lines (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3b/#3b19">Section 3B.19</a>) so that general-purpose lanes stop further in advance from the intersection than the bicycle lane can improve the visibility of bicyclists for drivers of turning vehicles (see Drawing D in <a href="#fig-9e-7">Figure 9E-7</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">18</span> Where a general-purpose mandatory turn lane is provided at an intersection and the approach also includes a separated or buffer-separated bicycle lane, a mixing zone may be established to allow general-purpose turning traffic to share the roadway space with bicyclists (see <a href="#fig-9e-5">Figure 9E-5</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">19</span> Mixing zones shall be used only where the bicycle lane is one-way in the same direction of travel as the adjacent general-purpose lane.</p>
<p><span class="pn">20</span> Mixing zones with a yielding area shall have yield markings indicating where general-purpose traffic entering the shared space shall yield to bicyclists.</p>
<p><span class="pn">21</span> Where a mixing zone continues to the intersection itself sharing space between bicyclists and general- purpose turning traffic, shared-lane markings and turn arrows shall be provided in the lane.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">22</span> Mixing zones require bicycles and general traffic to share space, interrupting a buffer-separated or separated bicycle lane where bicycle traffic is otherwise separated from general traffic. The preference is to provide a dedicated bicycle facility for the intersection approach; if that is not possible, the mixing zone needs to indicate that bicyclists and motorists are entering a shared condition.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">23</span> Where a mixing zone provides for the re-establishment of a bicycle lane after bicycles and general-purpose lanes cross paths, a buffered or physically-separated space should be provided between the bicycle lane and the adjacent general-purpose lane (see Drawing C in <a href="#fig-9e-5">Figure 9E-5</a>).</p>
</div>

## 9E.03 Extensions of Bicycle Lanes through Intersections


<div class="secmeta">Section 9E.03 &middot; manual page 1093 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/9e/#9e03">CA amended: 9E.03</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/9E.03/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Extensions of bicycle lanes through intersections can help identify the paths of bicyclists and guide them on movements that could be difficult to discern. Extensions of bicycle lanes through intersections also assist other road users of the intersection to identify where bicyclists are expected to operate and to recognize potentially unexpected conflict points.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> The design, placement, and maintenance of bicycle lane extensions through intersections are important considerations, especially when contiguous to a crosswalk, to avoid potential confusion to pedestrians with vision disabilities.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> The width and color of lane extension markings are discussed in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3b/#3b11">Section 3B.11</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> The bicycle symbol, the arrow marking, pavement word markings, or a combination thereof may be used in bicycle lane extensions through intersections.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Green-colored pavement may be used in a bicycle lane extension in accordance with the provisions of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3h/#3h06">Section 3H.06</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Shared-lane markings or chevron markings shall not be used in bicycle lanes or bicycle lane extensions (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/9e/#9e09">Section 9E.09</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Extensions of bicycle lanes through intersections shall use dotted line patterns.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Separated and buffer-separated bicycle lanes may have alignments that are not as obvious within an intersection as a standard bicycle lane, therefore additional conspicuity is important where these types of bicycle lanes cross intersections.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Lane extension markings should be used to extend a buffer-separated or separated bicycle lane through intersections and driveways.</p>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> The extension of a bicycle lane through an intersection should use two lines defining both lateral limits of the bicycle lane.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> Where the path of the bicycle lane through the intersection is contiguous to a crosswalk, two longitudinal dotted lines shall be provided to establish the lateral limits of the bicycle lane extension. The transverse line establishing one side of the crosswalk, or the limit of a high-visibility crosswalk pattern (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3c/#3c05">Section 3C.05</a>) that does not employ a transverse line, shall not be used to demarcate one side of the bicycle lane extension.</p>
</div>

## 9E.05 Bicycle Lanes at Circular Intersections


<div class="secmeta">Section 9E.05 &middot; manual page 1095 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/9e/#9e05">CA amended: 9E.05</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/9E.05/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Bicycle lanes shall not be provided in the circulatory roadway of an unsignalized circular intersection that includes conflicts at entry or exit points (see Chapter 3D) except as provided in Paragraph 4 of this Section.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Bicycle lane markings should stop at least 100 feet before the crosswalk, or if no crosswalk is provided, at least 100 feet before the yield line, or if no yield line is provided, then at least 100 feet before the edge of the circulatory roadway.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> If used, bicycle crossings should be a minimum of 20 feet from the edge of the circulatory roadway.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Separated bicycle lanes may be used in circular intersections.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Separated bicycle lanes allow bicycles to navigate a circular intersection and its crossing points without merging into traffic and without dismounting and using a crosswalk at the intersection crossing point. This is beneficial at multi-lane and higher-speed circular intersections.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/9e/#9e10">Section 9E.10</a> contains information on using shared-lane markings to facilitate the bicycle movement through a circular intersection.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> The “Guide for the Development of Bicycle Facilities,” 2012 Fourth Edition, American Association of State Highway and Transportation Officials, contains information on designing for bicycles on the sidewalk in lieu of, or in addition to, using shared-lane markings in the circulatory roadway of the intersection.</p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> The “Improving Intersections for Pedestrians and Bicyclists Informational Guide” (FHWA-SA-22-017), FHWA, contains information on incorporating separated bicycle lanes and other bicycle facilities into circular intersections.</p>
</div>

## 9E.06 Buffer-Separated Bicycle Lanes


<div class="secmeta">Section 9E.06 &middot; manual page 1095 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/9e/#9e06">CA amended: 9E.06</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/9E.06/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Buffer-separated bicycle lanes provide additional lateral separation between a bicycle lane and a general- purpose lane by a pattern of pavement markings without the presence of vertical elements. Providing a buffer space between a bicycle lane and a general-purpose lane creates more separation between motor vehicles and bicycles, can reduce vehicle encroachment into the bicycle lane, and can increase the comfort of bicyclists.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Providing a buffer space between a bicycle lane and a parking lane can reduce crashes involving bicycles and the opening of vehicle doors from the parking lane.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> If used, and except as provided in Paragraph 5 of this Section, a buffer space shall be marked with a solid white line along both edges of the buffer space where crossing is discouraged.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Engineering judgment should be used to establish intermittent breaks or interruptions in the buffer space, such as for driveways, transit stops, or on-street parallel parking lanes, in order to convey access points or an otherwise general legal movement to cross the buffer space (see <a href="#fig-9e-6">Figure 9E-6</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Buffer spaces may be established without specific longitudinal lines if contiguous facilities have longitudinal lines or other pavement markings themselves that, when installed, automatically demarcate the buffer space (see Drawing D in <a href="#fig-9e-6">Figure 9E-6</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Except as provided in Paragraph 7 of this Section, a through buffer-separated bicycle lane shall not be positioned to the right of a mandatory right-turn lane or to the left of a mandatory left-turn lane.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> A buffer-separated bicycle lane may be placed to the right of a mandatory right-turn lane (or to the left of a mandatory left-turn lane) only if a bicycle signal face (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/4h/#4h01">Section 4H.01</a>) is used and the signal phasing and signing eliminates any potential conflicts between the bicycle movement and the turning movement.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> The width of the buffer space should be at least 3 times the width of the normal or wide longitudinal line used to mark the buffer space.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Where a buffer space is 2 to 3 feet wide, chevron or diagonal markings (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3b/#3b25">Section 3B.25</a>) should be applied within the buffer space.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> Where a buffer space is less than 2 feet wide, diagonal markings or no markings at all in the buffer space may be applied within the buffer space.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> If used, diagonal markings shall slant away from traffic in the adjacent travel lane for motor- vehicle traffic.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> Where used, the spacing of chevrons or diagonal markings should be 10 feet or greater.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> Chevron and diagonal markings convey that the buffer space is not an additional bicycle lane or other travel lane open to traffic.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> Where a buffer space is more than 3 feet wide, chevron or diagonal markings shall be applied within the buffer space.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">15</span> Lane extension markings should be used to extend a buffer-separated bicycle lane across intersections and driveways.</p>
</div>

## 9E.07 Separated Bicycle Lanes


<div class="secmeta">Section 9E.07 &middot; manual page 1097 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/9e/#9e07">CA amended: 9E.07</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/9E.07/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Separated bicycle lanes provide a physical separation between a general-purpose lane and a bicycle lane through the use of vertical objects or vertical separation between the general-purpose lane and bicycle lane. Providing a physical separation between a bicycle lane and a general-purpose lane can reduce vehicle encroachment into the bicycle lane beyond a marked buffer alone and can in some cases prevent that encroachment altogether.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Physical separation between general-purpose lanes and bicycle lanes introduces additional design considerations over buffer-separated bicycle lanes, including the awareness of a potentially unexpected conflict point for turning motor vehicles and the provision of adequate sight distance for all users at intersections and driveway crossings.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Vertical elements used to provide physical separation between general-purpose lanes and bicycle lanes may include, but are not limited to, tubular markers, raised islands, or parked vehicles.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Where on-street parking is provided adjacent to the buffer area of a separated bicycle lane, pedestrians will need to access those vehicles.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> BIKE LANE (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-17/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-17.svg">R3-17</a>) signs (see <a href="#fig-9b-1">Figure 9B-1</a>) should be used to distinguish a separated bicycle lane from a general-purpose lane.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Where an on-street parking lane serves as the separation between a general-purpose lane and a separated bicycle lane, a buffer space should be provided between the parking lane and the bicycle lane to allow for opening doors of parked vehicles.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Separated bicycle lanes may be designed for one-way or two-way bicycle travel.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Providing one-way separated bicycle lanes in the same direction as and on the right-hand side of the general- purpose lane, whether on a one-way or two-way roadway, accommodates the expectations of road users and might result in fewer conflict points at intersections or driveway crossings.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Separated bicycle lanes may be provided on one or both sides of a roadway or in a center median.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> The presence of two-way separated bicycle lanes on one side of a roadway or in a center median can introduce additional challenges and conflict points, which can warrant additional design considerations when selecting the design for a separated bicycle lane. These considerations include design requirements for pedestrians who would interact with the separated bicycle lane.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> The edge line and lane line colors used for separated bicycle lanes shall conform to the requirements in Chapter 3A (see <a href="#fig-9e-7">Figure 9E-7</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> Directional arrows shall be used in conjunction with the bicycle lane symbol or word marking in separated bicycle lanes, placed downstream from the symbol or word marking.</p>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> Turns on red shall be prohibited across separated bicycle lanes while bicyclists are allowed to proceed through the intersection.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> Additional information on signals for bicycle facilities is found in Chapter 4H.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">15</span> The edges of the buffer space for a separated bicycle lane shall be marked with solid longitudinal lines.</p>
<p><span class="pn">16</span> A marked buffer space that is 2 feet or wider for a separated bicycle lane, including those buffer spaces where tubular markers are provided, shall use chevron or diagonal markings within the buffer, unless physical separation is provided that occupies the majority of the buffer space, such as raised islands or other physical dividers, or such as where an on-street parking lane occupies the majority of the buffer space.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">17</span> Where used in the buffer area of a separated bicycle lane, the spacing of chevrons or diagonal markings should be 10 feet or greater.</p>
<p><span class="pn">18</span> Crosswalks that cross a separated bicycle lane should be marked consistent with the style of crosswalk marking provided across the adjacent general-purpose lane.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">19</span> Where on-street parking is provided as the physical separation adjacent to the buffer area of a separated bicycle lane, the chevron or diagonal marking provisions in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/9e/#9e06">Section 9E.06</a> apply to the area outside of the marked parking area within the buffer (see <a href="#fig-9e-7">Figure 9E-7</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">20</span> Intersection treatments for separated bicycle lanes can vary depending on the geometric and operational conditions at the intersection (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/9e/#9e02">Section 9E.02</a>).</p>
</div>

## 9E.08 Counter-Flow Bicycle Lanes


<div class="secmeta">Section 9E.08 &middot; manual page 1098 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/9e/#9e08">CA amended: 9E.08</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/9E.08/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Counter-flow bicycle lanes are one-directional and provide a lawful path of travel for bicycles in the opposite direction from general traffic on a roadway that allows general traffic to travel in only one direction.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Counter-flow bicycle lanes establish two-way traffic on a roadway. <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/9b/#9b21">Section 9B.21</a> contains information on the Left Turn Yield to Bicycles (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r10-12b/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R10-12b.svg">R10-12b</a>) sign used with traffic signals and counter-flow bicycle lanes.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Where used, a counter-flow bicycle lane should be marked such that bicycles in the counter-flow lane travel on their right-hand side of the road in accordance with normal rules of the road, with opposing traffic on the left.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Counter-flow bicycle lanes located at the edge of the roadway shall use double yellow center line pavement markings (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3b/#3b01">Section 3B.01</a>), a painted median island, a raised median island (see Chapter 3J), or some form of physical separation where the speed limit is 30 mph or less.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> For speed limits 35 mph or greater, a buffer per <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3b/#3b25">Section 3B.25</a>, a painted or raised median island, or some form of physical separation shall be used to separate a counter-flow bicycle lane from the adjacent travel lane.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Lane extension markings should be used where counter-flow bicycle movements cross intersections.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Counter-flow bicycle lanes should not be used between a general-purpose lane and an on-street parallel parking lane for motor vehicles.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Counter-flow bicycle lanes located between a general-purpose lane and an on-street parallel parking lane for motor vehicles can limit visibility of bicycles for vehicles exiting the parking lane, potentially impacting the safety of bicyclists. Locating counter-flow bicycle lanes at the edge of the roadway can reduce conflicts for bicycles.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Where signs are provided to regulate turns from streets or driveways that intersect with a roadway that has a counter-flow bicycle lane, ONE WAY signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2b/#2b49">Section 2B.49</a>) shall not be used. Movement Prohibition signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2b/#2b26">Section 2B.26</a>) with supplemental EXCEPT BICYCLES (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-7bp/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-7bP.svg">R3-7bP</a>) regulatory plaque(s) shall be used (see <a href="#fig-9e-8">Figure 9E-8</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> If a DO NOT ENTER (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r5-1/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R5-1.svg">R5-1</a>) sign(s) is used at egress points for motor vehicle traffic, the EXCEPT BICYCLES (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-7bp/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-7bP.svg">R3-7bP</a>) regulatory plaque(s) shall be placed under the DO NOT ENTER sign (see <a href="#fig-9e-8">Figure 9E-8</a>) where a counter-flow bicycle lane is used.</p>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> Where intersection traffic controls are provided (such as STOP or YIELD signs or traffic signals), appropriate devices shall be provided and oriented toward bicyclists in the counter-flow lane.</p>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> At signalized locations, appropriate bicycle signalization (see Chapter 9F) shall be provided and oriented toward bicyclists in the counter-flow lane, including a method for counter-flow bicycles to actuate the green phase for the counter-flow movement.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> Higher levels of traffic control or additional signalization, signing, and/or pavement marking treatments can be helpful for intersecting traffic where the counter-flow bicycle movement is unexpected.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> A Bicycle Cross Traffic warning plaque (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/9c/#9c06">Section 9C.06</a>) should be used below a STOP sign on the crossroad at intersections where a counter-flow bicycle lane is provided on the primary street.</p>
</div>

## 9E.09 Shared- Lane Marking


<div class="secmeta">Section 9E.09 &middot; manual page 1101 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/9e/#9e09">CA amended: 9E.09</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/9E.09/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> The “Standard Highway Signs” publication (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/1a/#1a05">Section 1A.05</a>) contains details on the shared-lane marking symbol.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> The shared-lane marking shown in <a href="#fig-9e-9">Figure 9E-9</a> may be used to: A. Assist bicyclists with lateral positioning in a shared lane with on-street parallel parking in order to reduce the chance of a bicyclist impacting the open door of a parked vehicle, B. Assist bicyclists with lateral positioning in lanes that are too narrow for a motor vehicle and a bicycle to travel side-by-side within the same traffic lane, C. Alert road users of the lateral location bicycles are likely to occupy within the traveled way, D. Encourage safe passing of bicycles by motor vehicles, E. Reduce the incidence of wrong-way bicycling in the roadway, and F. Assist bicyclists with lateral positioning in mixing zones.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> The shared-lane marking should not be placed on roadways that have a speed limit of 40 mph or greater.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Shared-lane markings shall not be used in: A. Shoulders; B. Bicycle lanes or in designated extensions of bicycle lanes through intersections or driveways, C. A travel lane in which light-rail transit vehicles also travel; D. The transition area where a motor vehicle entering a mandatory turn lane must weave across bicyclists in bicycle lanes; E. Two-stage turn boxes; F. Bicycle boxes; G. Shared-use paths or shared-use path crossings; or H. Physically-separated bikeways, either in the roadway or on an independent right-of-way.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Green-colored pavement shall not be applied as a background to shared-lane markings (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3h/#3h06">Section 3H.06</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Black background markings (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3a/#3a03">Section 3A.03</a>) may be used in combination with shared-lane markings to enhance contrast.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> If used in a shared lane with on-street parallel parking, shared-lane markings should be placed so that the centers of the markings are a minimum of 12 feet from the face of the curb, or from the edge of the pavement where there is no curb.</p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> If used on a street without on-street parking that has an outside travel lane that is less than 14 feet wide, shared-lane markings should be placed so that the centers of the markings are a minimum of 4 feet from the face of the curb, or from the edge of the pavement where there is no curb.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> At non-intersection locations, the shared-lane marking should be spaced at intervals of not less than 50 feet or greater than 250 feet.</p>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> The first shared-lane marking downstream from an intersection should be placed no more than 50 feet from the intersection.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/9b/#9b14">Section 9B.14</a> describes a Bicycles Allowed Use of Full Lane sign that may be used in addition to or instead of the shared-lane marking to inform road users that bicyclists might occupy the travel lane.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> If the Bicycles Allowed Use of Full Lane (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r9-20/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R9-20.svg">R9-20</a>) sign is used as an addition to shared-lane marking, the shared- lane marking should be placed so that the center of the marking is in the approximate center of the travel lane.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> The shared-lane marking may be used (see <a href="#fig-9e-9">Figure 9E-9</a>) where the width of the roadway is insufficient to continue a bicycle lane or separated bikeway on the approach to the intersection, or it is advantageous to terminate the bicycle lane or separated bikeway in order to provide for a shared lane.</p>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> The shared-lane marking may be used on an approach to an intersection (see <a href="#fig-9e-5">Figure 9E-5</a>) in a mandatory turn lane to indicate a shared space for bicyclists and motorists where there is insufficient width in the roadway for both the bicycle lane and turn lane.</p>

## 9E.11 Two-Stage Bicycle Turn Boxes


<div class="secmeta">Section 9E.11 &middot; manual page 1103 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/9e/#9e11">CA amended: 9E.11</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/9E.11/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Two-stage bicycle turn boxes allow bicyclists the opportunity to make turns at an intersection or crossing point instead of requiring them to merge into traffic upstream or to dismount and use a crosswalk at the intersection or crossing point.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/9b/#9b18">Section 9B.18</a> contains information on regulatory signing that shall be used in conjunction with a two-stage bicycle turn box pavement marking where bicyclists are required to use the turn box.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/9d/#9d13">Section 9D.13</a> contains information on guide signing that can be used in conjunction with a two-stage bicycle turn box pavement marking where bicyclists are not required to use the turn box.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> If used, two-stage bicycle turn boxes shall be located: A. In an area between the closest through bicycle or motor vehicle movement and the parallel crosswalk (see Drawing A in <a href="#fig-9e-10">Figure 9E-10</a>), B. In an area between the through bicycle movement and the parallel pedestrian crossing movement if no crosswalk is established (see Drawing B in <a href="#fig-9e-10">Figure 9E-10</a>), C. On the innermost side of the bicycle facility provided that the two-stage turn box is located in a portion of the intersection where parallel motor vehicle traffic does not travel, such as projections of islands or parking lanes (see Drawing C in <a href="#fig-9e-10">Figure 9E-10</a>), or D. In an area between the through bicycle movement and a pedestrian facility for T-intersections (see Drawing D in <a href="#fig-9e-10">Figure 9E-10</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> A two-stage bicycle turn box shall consist of at least one bicycle symbol pavement marking and at least one pavement marking arrow.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> A turn arrow in the appropriate direction shall be used if a two-stage turn box is used with a one-way bicycle lane, and a through arrow in the appropriate direction shall be used if a two-stage turn box is used with a two-way bikeway (see <a href="#fig-9e-11">Figure 9E-11</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> A two-stage bicycle turn box shall be bounded on all sides by a solid white line.</p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> For two-stage bicycle turn boxes that facilitate turns from a one-way bikeway, the bicycle symbol shall precede the pavement marking turn arrow in the direction of bicycle travel (see <a href="#fig-9e-10">Figure 9E-10</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Passive detection of bicycles in the two-stage bicycle turn box shall be provided if the signal phase that permits bicycles to enter the intersection during the second stage of their turn is actuated.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> Engineering judgment should be used to develop the size of the two-stage bicycle turn box. Factors considered should include intersection geometry and keeping queued bicycles away from moving traffic, as well as peak hour bicycle volumes to avoid overflow of the two-stage turn box that subjects any bicyclist to conflicting movements.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> The two-stage turn box may use green-colored pavement.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> If used, green-colored pavement shall encompass all of the two-stage turn box.</p>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> Where the path of vehicles lawfully turning on red would pass through a two-stage bicycle turn box, a full-time no-turn-on-red prohibition (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2b/#2b60">Section 2B.60</a>) shall be provided for the crossroad approach.</p>
</div>

## 9E.12 Bicycle Box Figure 9E-11. Example of a Two-Stage Turn Box Location


<div class="secmeta">Section 9E.12 &middot; manual page 1105 &middot; <a class="badge ca-same" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/9e/#9e12">CA MUTCD 2026: 9E.12</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/9E.12/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> A bicycle box (see <a href="#fig-9e-12">Figure 9E-12</a>) may be used to increase the visibility of stopped bicycles on the approach to a signalized intersection during the portion of the signal cycle when a red signal indication is being displayed to motor vehicles in the approach lane(s) that is behind the box.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Providing a bicycle box on a signalized intersection approach where a discernible number of conflicts between vehicles turning across through bicycles in a bicycle lane has been demonstrated during the green interval of a signal should be evaluated based on engineering judgment or study.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Other treatments should be considered for conflicts between turning vehicles and through bicycles such as using leading or exclusive signal phases, or separating turning traffic from through traffic through mandatory turn lanes.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> A bicycle lane should be used on the approach to a bicycle box.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> A bicycle box should not be contiguous with a crosswalk. A stop line on the downstream end of the bicycle box should be used to mark the location where bicycles are required to stop.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> If used, the distance from the upstream edge of the bicycle box that is nearest to the stop line for motor vehicles to the downstream edge of the bicycle box that is nearest the crosswalk or intersection shall be at least 10 feet. At least one bicycle symbol marking (see <a href="#fig-9e-12">Figure 9E-12</a>) shall be used in the bicycle box.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Where an existing stop line for motor vehicles is relocated upstream to install a new bicycle box, the yellow change and red clearance intervals (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/4f/#4f17">Section 4F.17</a>) shall be recalculated and if necessary, reprogrammed to accommodate the length of the bicycle box.</p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Countdown pedestrian signals (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/4i/#4i04">Section 4I.04</a>) for the crosswalk or pedestrian crossing movement that crosses the approach shall accompany bicycle boxes that extend across more than one approach lane for motor vehicles. Countdown pedestrian signals used with bicycle boxes shall display the pedestrian change interval countdown without the need for actuation.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Turns on red shall be prohibited from the lane where a bicycle box is placed.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> Countdown pedestrian signals can inform bicyclists whether there is adequate time remaining to an adjacent lane before the onset of the green signal phase for that approach.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> Countdown pedestrian signals for the crosswalk or pedestrian crossing movement that crosses the approach should accompany single-lane bicycle boxes where it is demonstrated that bicycles arrive at the intersection at or near the end of the red signal indication being displayed to traffic in the approach lane(s) that is behind the box.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> Green-colored pavement may be used in a bicycle box.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> If used, green-colored pavement shall be used in the full limits of the bicycle box.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/9b/#9b02">Section 9B.02</a> contains information on the EXCEPT BICYCLES (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-7bp/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-7bP.svg">R3-7bP</a>) regulatory plaque that can be used below the STOP HERE ON RED (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r10-6/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R10-6.svg">R10-6</a> or <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r10-6a/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R10-6a.svg">R10-6a</a>) sign (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2b/#2b59">Section 2B.59</a>) to exempt bicyclists from the requirement of the advance stop line.</p>

## 9E.13 Shared-Use Paths

<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>

<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>

<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> If parallel bicycle and pedestrian crossing markings are used where a shared-use path crosses a roadway, crossing areas for bicycles should use green-colored pavement if the shared-use path crossing has a high volume of either mode.</p>

## 9E.17 Raised Devices Figure 9E-15. Example of Placement of


<div class="secmeta">Section 9E.17 &middot; manual page 1110 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/9e/#9e17">CA amended: 9E.17</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/9E.17/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Chapter 3I contains information on using channelizing devices to emphasize pavement marking patterns associated with certain bicycle facilities. A common application is the use of flexible raised devices to create separated bicycle lanes (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/9e/#9e07">Section 9E.07</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Using inflexible raised devices immediately adjacent to the travel path of a bicyclist without a buffer creates a collision potential for bicyclists.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> In accordance with Chapter 3I, channelizing devices may be used to emphasize a pavement marking pattern that establishes a bicycle lane or other bicycle facility provided that the installation of channelizing devices does not prevent motor vehicles from turning when the turn requires the motor vehicle to merge with the bicycle lane or facility as required by law or ordinance.</p>

<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> If used, channelizing devices for bicycle facilities should be tubular markers (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3i/#3i02">Section 3I.02</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> The selection of a raised device for use with bicycle facilities should consider the collision potential of both the post and the base since the base might still be present in the event the post is struck and missing.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Measures to reduce the likelihood of a road user striking a channelizing device include marking a buffer space, improving lighting, improving retroreflectivity, or the periodic addition of taller vertical elements within runs of shorter elements.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Channelizing devices that are used to emphasize the pavement marking patterns of bicycle facilities shall not incorporate the color green into either the device or its retroreflective element to supplement the presence of green-colored pavement.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
