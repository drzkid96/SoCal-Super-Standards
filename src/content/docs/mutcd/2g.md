## 2G.01 Scope


<div class="secmeta">Section 2G.01 &middot; manual page 406 &middot; <a class="badge ca-same" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2g/#2g01">CA MUTCD 2026: 2G.01</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2G.01/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Preferential lanes are lanes designated for special traffic uses such as high-occupancy vehicles (HOVs), light rail, buses, or taxis. Preferential lane treatments might be as simple as restricting a turning lane to a certain class of vehicles during peak periods, or as sophisticated as providing a separate roadway system within a highway corridor for certain vehicles.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Preferential lanes might be barrier-separated (on a separate alignment or physically separated from the other travel lanes by a barrier or median), buffer-separated (separated from the adjacent general-purpose lanes only by a narrow buffer area created with longitudinal pavement markings), or contiguous (separated from the adjacent general-purpose lanes only by a lane line). Preferential lanes might allow continuous access with the adjacent general-purpose lanes or restrict access only to designated locations. Preferential lanes might be operated in a constant direction or operated as reversible lanes. Some reversible preferential lanes on a divided highway might be operated counter-flow to the direction of traffic on the immediately adjacent general-purpose lanes.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Preferential lanes might be operated on a 24-hour basis, for extended periods of the day, during peak travel periods only, during special events, or during other activities.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Open-road tolling lanes and toll plaza lanes that segregate traffic based on payment method are not considered preferential lanes. Chapter 2F contains information regarding signing of open-road tolling lanes and toll plaza lanes.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Managed lanes typically restrict access with the adjacent general-purpose lanes to designated locations only.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Under certain operational strategies, such as the occupancy requirement of an HOV lane changing in response to actual congestion levels, a managed lane is a special type of preferential lane (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2g/#2g03">Sections 2G.03</a> through 2G.07).</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> A managed lane operated on a real-time basis in response to changing conditions might be operated as an HOV lane for a period of time as needed to manage congestion levels.</p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2g/#2g17">Sections 2G.17</a> through 2G.19 contain additional information regarding signs for managed lanes that use tolling or pricing as a management strategy.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/9b/#9b04">Section 9B.04</a> contains information regarding Preferential Lane signs for bicycle lanes.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> Unless otherwise provided, the provisions of this Chapter shall not apply to bicycle lanes.</p>

## 2G.03 Regulatory Signs for Preferential Lanes – General


<div class="secmeta">Section 2G.03 &middot; manual page 409 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2g/#2g03">CA amended: 2G.03</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2G.03/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> When a preferential lane is established, the Preferential Lane regulatory signs (see <a href="#fig-2g-1">Figure 2G-1</a>) and pavement markings (see Chapter 3E) for these lanes shall be used to advise road users.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Preferential Lane (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-10/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-10.svg">R3-10</a> series through <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-15/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-15.svg">R3-15</a> series) regulatory signs (see <a href="#fig-2g-1">Figure 2G-1</a>) consist of several different general types of regulatory signs as follows: A. Vehicle Occupancy Definition signs define the vehicle occupancy requirements applicable to an HOV lane (such as “2 OR MORE PERSONS PER VEHICLE”) or types of vehicles not meeting the minimum occupancy requirement (such as motorcycles or Inherently Low Emission Vehicles (ILEVs)) that are allowed to use an HOV lane (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2g/#2g04">Section 2G.04</a>). B. Preferential Lane Operation signs notify road users of the days and hours during which the preferential restrictions are in effect (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2g/#2g05">Section 2G.05</a>). C. Preferential Lane Advance signs notify road users that a preferential lane restriction begins ahead (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2g/#2g06">Section 2G.06</a>). D. Preferential Lane Ends signs notify users of the termination point of the preferential lane restrictions (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2g/#2g07">Section 2G.07</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Regulatory signs applicable only to a preferential lane shall be distinguished from regulatory signs applicable to general-purpose lanes by the inclusion of the applicable symbol(s) and/or word(s) (see <a href="#fig-2g-1">Figure 2G-1</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> The symbol and word message displayed on a particular Preferential Lane regulatory sign will vary based on the specific type of allowed traffic and on other related operational constraints that have been established for a particular lane, such as an HOV lane, a bus lane, or a taxi lane.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Changeable message signs may supplement, substitute for, or be incorporated into static Preferential Lane regulatory signs where travel conditions change or where multiple types of operational strategies (such as variable occupancy requirements or vehicle types) are used and varied throughout the day or week, or on a real-time basis, to manage the use of, control of, or access to preferential lanes.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> <a href="#fig-2g-1">Figure 2G-1</a> illustrates examples of changeable messages incorporated into static Preferential Lane regulatory signs displaying open and closed status using lane-use control signal indications (see Chapter 4T).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> When changeable message signs (see Chapter 2L) are used as regulatory signs for preferential lanes, they shall be the required sign size and shall display the required letter height and legend format that corresponds to the type of roadway facility and design speed.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> When Preferential Lane regulatory signs are used on conventional roads, the decision regarding whether to use a post-mounted or overhead version of a particular type of sign should be based on an engineering study that considers the available space, the existing signs for the adjacent general-purpose traffic lanes, roadway and traffic characteristics, the proximity to existing overhead signs, the ability to install overhead signs, and any other unique local factors.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> If overhead regulatory signs applicable only to a preferential lane are located in approximately the same longitudinal position along the highway as overhead signs applicable only to the general-purpose lanes, the signs for the preferential lane should be separated laterally from the signs for the general-purpose lanes to the maximum extent practicable to minimize conflicting information, while maintaining their visual relationship to the lanes below necessitated by specific legend or arrows indicating lane assignment.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> If used, overhead Preferential Lane (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-13/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-13.svg">R3-13</a> series, <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-14/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-14.svg">R3-14</a> series, and <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-15/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-15.svg">R3-15</a> series) regulatory signs shall be installed on the side of the roadway where the entrance to the preferential lane is located and any appropriate adjustments shall be made to the sign message.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> Where a median of sufficient width is available, the <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-13/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-13.svg">R3-13</a> series and <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-15/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-15.svg">R3-15</a> series signs may be post-mounted.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> The sizes for Preferential Lane regulatory signs will differ to reflect the design speeds for each type of roadway facility. <a href="#fig-2g-1">Table 2G-1</a> provides sizes for each type of roadway facility.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> The edges of Preferential Lane regulatory signs that are post-mounted on a median barrier should not project beyond the outer edges of the barrier, including in areas where lateral clearance is limited.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> Where lateral clearance is limited, Preferential Lane regulatory signs that are post-mounted on a median barrier and that are 72 inches or less in width may be skewed up to 45 degrees in order to fit within the barrier width or may be mounted higher, such that the vertical clearance to the bottom of the sign, light fixture, or structural support, whichever is lowest, is not less than 17 feet above any portion of the pavement and shoulders.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">15</span> Where lateral clearance is limited, Preferential Lane regulatory signs that are post-mounted on a median barrier and that are wider than 72 inches shall be mounted with a vertical clearance that complies with the provisions of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2a/#2a15">Section 2A.15</a> for overhead mounting if any portion of the sign extends over the roadway or shoulder.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">16</span> On conventional roadways, Preferential Lane regulatory sign spacing should be determined by engineering judgment based on speed, block length, distances from adjacent intersections, and other site-specific considerations.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">17</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2g/#2g04">Sections 2G.04</a> and 2G.05 contain provisions regarding the placement of Preferential Lane regulatory signs on freeways and expressways.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">18</span> The signs illustrated in <a href="#fig-2g-1">Figure 2G-1</a> that incorporate the diamond symbol shall be used exclusively with preferential lanes for high-occupancy vehicles to indicate the particular occupancy requirement and time restrictions applying to that lane. The signs illustrated in <a href="#fig-2g-1">Figure 2G-1</a> that do not have a diamond symbol shall be used with preferential lanes that are not HOV lanes, but are designated for use by other types of vehicles (such as bus and/or taxi use).</p>
<p><span class="pn">19</span> The diamond symbol shall not be used on the bus, taxi, or bicycle Preferential Lane signs.</p>
<p><span class="pn">20</span> Vehicle Occupancy Definition, Preferential Lane Operation, and Preferential Lane Advance regulatory signs for HOV lanes shall display the minimum allowable vehicle occupancy requirement established for each HOV lane, displayed immediately after the word message HOV.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">21</span> The agencies that own and operate HOV lanes have the authority and responsibility to determine how they are operated and the minimum occupancy requirements. Information about federal requirements for certain types of vehicles not meeting the minimum occupancy requirement to be eligible to use HOV lanes that receive Federal- aid program funding and about requirements associated with proposed significant changes to the operation of an existing HOV lane and certain vehicles are contained in the “Federal-Aid Highway Program Guidance on High Occupancy Vehicle (HOV) Lanes.”</p>
<p><span class="pn">22</span> <a href="#fig-2g-2">Figures 2G-2</a> and 2G-3 illustrate the use of regulatory signs for the beginning, along the length, and at the end of contiguous or buffer-separated preferential lanes that provide continuous access with the adjacent general- purpose lanes.</p>
</div>

## 2G.04 Vehicle Occupancy Definition Signs (R3-10 Series and R3-13 Series)


<div class="secmeta">Section 2G.04 &middot; manual page 412 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2g/#2g04">CA amended: 2G.04</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2G.04/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> The <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-10/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-10.svg">R3-10</a>, <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-13/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-13.svg">R3-13</a>, and <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-13a/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-13a.svg">R3-13a</a> Vehicle Occupancy Definition signs (see <a href="#fig-2g-1">Figure 2G-1</a>) shall be used where agencies determine that it is appropriate to provide a sign that defines the minimum occupancy of vehicles that are allowed to use an HOV lane.</p>

<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> The Inherently Low Emission Vehicle (ILEV) (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-10a/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-10a.svg">R3-10a</a>) sign (see <a href="#fig-2g-1">Figure 2G-1</a>) should be used when it is permissible for a properly labeled and certified ILEV, regardless of the number of occupants, to use an HOV lane. When used, the ILEV signs should be post-mounted in advance of and at intervals along the HOV lane based upon engineering judgment and the placement of other Preferential Lane regulatory signs. The <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-10a/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-10a.svg">R3-10a</a> sign is only applicable to HOV lanes and should not be used with other preferential lane applications.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> ILEVs are defined by the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) as vehicles having no fuel vapor (hydrocarbon) emissions and are certified by the EPA as meeting the emissions standards and requirements specified in 40 CFR §88.311-93 and 40 CFR §88.312-93(c).</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2g/#2g18">Section 2G.18</a> contains information regarding the legends of Vehicle Occupancy Definition signs for a priced managed lane that has an occupancy requirement for non-toll travel.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> For barrier-separated, buffer-separated, or contiguous preferential lanes where access between the preferential and general-purpose lanes is restricted to designated locations on freeways and expressways, an overhead Vehicle Occupancy Definition (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-13/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-13.svg">R3-13</a> or <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-13a/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-13a.svg">R3-13a</a>) sign shall be installed at least ½ mile in advance of the beginning of or initial entry point to an HOV lane. These signs shall only be displayed in advance of the beginning of or initial or intermediate entry point to HOV lanes.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> For buffer-separated or contiguous HOV lanes where access is restricted to designated locations on freeways and expressways, the sequence of a post-mounted Preferential Lane Operation (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-11a/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-11a.svg">R3-11a</a>) sign (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2g/#2g05">Section 2G.05</a>) followed by a post-mounted Vehicle Occupancy Definition (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-10/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-10.svg">R3-10</a>) sign shall be located at intervals not greater than ½ mile along the length of designated gaps where vehicles are allowed to legally access the HOV lane, and within designated enforcement areas as defined by the operating agency.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> For buffer-separated or contiguous HOV lanes where access is restricted to designated locations on freeways and expressways, the sequence of a post-mounted Preferential Lane Operation (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-11a/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-11a.svg">R3-11a</a>) sign (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2g/#2g05">Section 2G.05</a>) followed by a post-mounted Vehicle Occupancy Definition (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-10/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-10.svg">R3-10</a>) sign may be located at intervals of approximately ½ mile along the length of the HOV lane.</p>

## 2G.05 Preferential Lane Operation Signs (R3-11 Series and R3-14 Series)


<div class="secmeta">Section 2G.05 &middot; manual page 415 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2g/#2g05">CA amended: 2G.05</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2G.05/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> The standardized sizes of post-mounted Preferential Lane Operation (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-11/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-11.svg">R3-11</a> series) signs are consistent to accommodate any future addition or removal of a single line of legend for each sign. Each size accommodates two lines of legend for the times of day and days of week that the regulation is in effect. Consistent sign sizes are beneficial for agencies when ordering sign materials, as well as when making legend changes to existing signs if changes occur to operating times or occupancy restrictions in the future.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Where the regulation is in effect during more than one time period of the day, such as during the morning and afternoon peak periods, the height of the <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-11/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-11.svg">R3-11</a> and <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-14/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-14.svg">R3-14</a> series signs should be suitably increased to accommodate the additional line(s) of legend.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> When used, the post-mounted Preferential Lane Operation (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-11/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-11.svg">R3-11</a> series) signs shall be located adjacent to the preferential lane, and the overhead Preferential Lane Operation (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-14/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-14.svg">R3-14</a> series) signs shall be mounted directly over the lane.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> The legend format of the post-mounted Preferential Lane Operation (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-11/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-11.svg">R3-11</a> series) signs shall have the following sequence: A. Top Lines: Lanes applicable, such as “RIGHT LANE” or “2 RIGHT LANES” B. Middle Lines: Eligible uses, such as “HOV 2+ ONLY” (or 3+ or 4+, if appropriate) or “BUSES ONLY” or other applicable uses or eligible turning movements C. <p><span class="pn">05</span> The legend format of the overhead Preferential Lane Operation (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-14/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-14.svg">R3-14</a> series) signs shall have the following sequence: A. <p><span class="pn">06</span> For preferential lane restrictions that are in effect on a full-time (24 hours per day, seven days per week) basis, the Preferential Lane Operation (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-11b/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-11b.svg">R3-11b</a>, <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-11c/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-11c.svg">R3-11c</a>, <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-11e/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-11e.svg">R3-11e</a>, and <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-14b/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-14b.svg">R3-14b</a>) signs that include a period of operation legend shall be modified to display no legend relative to the period of operation except as provided in Paragraph 7 of this Section.</p>

<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> In lieu of removing the period of operation text from the Preferential Lane Operation signs for full-time operation, the legend 24 HOURS may be substituted for the times and days of the week on the Preferential Lane Operation (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-11/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-11.svg">R3-11</a>, <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-11a/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-11a.svg">R3-11a</a>, <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-11d/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-11d.svg">R3-11d</a>, <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-11f/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-11f.svg">R3-11f</a>, <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-11g/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-11g.svg">R3-11g</a>, <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-14/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-14.svg">R3-14</a>, <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-14a/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-14a.svg">R3-14a</a>, and <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-14c/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-14c.svg">R3-14c</a>) signs for preferential lane restrictions that are in effect on a full-time basis.</p>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>

</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> The full-time Preferential Lane Operation (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-11b/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-11b.svg">R3-11b</a>, <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-11c/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-11c.svg">R3-11c</a>, <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-11e/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-11e.svg">R3-11e</a>, and <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-14b/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-14b.svg">R3-14b</a>) signs that do not display a period of operation legend, as described in Paragraph 6 of this Section, shall not be used where the preferential lane restriction is in effect only on a part-time basis.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>

For all barrier-separated and buffer-separated preferential lanes, post-mounted Preferential Lane Operation (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-11/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-11.svg">R3-11</a> series) signs shall be used only as a supplement to the overhead signs on freeways, expressways, and at locations on conventional roadways where the preferential lane is not the outermost lane of the roadway at the beginning or initial entry point, or at any intermediate entry points or gaps in the barrier or buffer.</p>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> For buffer-separated or contiguous preferential lanes where continuous access with the adjacent general-purpose lanes is provided, including those where a preferential lane is added to the roadway (see <a href="#fig-2g-2">Figure 2G-2</a> for HOV lanes) and those where a general-purpose lane transitions into a preferential lane (see <a href="#fig-2g-3">Figure 2G-3</a> for HOV lanes), an overhead Preferential Lane Operation (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-14/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-14.svg">R3-14</a> series) sign shall be used at the beginning or initial entry point of the preferential lane on freeways and expressways.</p>

<p><span class="pn">16</span> Additional overhead (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-14/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-14.svg">R3-14</a> series) or post-mounted (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-11/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-11.svg">R3-11</a> series) Preferential Lane Operation signs may be provided along the length of any type of preferential lane.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>

<p><span class="pn">19</span> Lane-use control signals (see Chapter 4T) may be used at access points to preferential lanes to indicate that a ramp or access roadway leading to the preferential lane or facility, or one or more specific lanes of the facility, are open or closed (see <a href="#fig-2g-1">Figure 2G-1</a>).</p>

<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Additional post-mounted or overhead Preferential Lane Advance signs may be placed farther in advance of or closer to the beginning or initial entry points to a preferential lane.</p>

## 2G.08 Warning Signs on Median Barriers for Preferential Lanes

<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> When a warning sign applicable only to a preferential lane is installed on a median barrier with limited lateral clearance to the adjacent travel lanes or shoulders, the warning sign may have a vertically-oriented rectangular shape. For a high-occupancy vehicle lane, such signs may be used instead of using the HOV (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w16-11p/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W16-11P.svg">W16-11P</a>) plaque (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2g/#2g09">Section 2G.09</a>) with a standard diamond-shaped warning sign.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> When a vertically-oriented rectangular-shaped warning sign applicable only to a preferential lane is installed on a median barrier, the top portion of the sign shall be comprised of a white symbol or legend denoting the type of preferential lane (such as the diamond symbol for HOV or the legend BUS LANE) on a black background with a white border, and the bottom portion of the sign shall be comprised of the standard word message or symbol of the standard warning sign as a black legend on a yellow background with a black border (see <a href="#fig-2g-4">Figure 2G-4</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Where lateral clearance is limited, such as when a post-mounted warning sign applicable only to a preferential lane is installed on a median barrier, the edges of the sign should not project beyond the outer edges of the barrier.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Where lateral clearance is limited, warning signs applicable only to a preferential lane that are post-mounted on a median barrier and that are 72 inches or less in width may be skewed up to 45 degrees in order to fit within the barrier width or may be mounted higher, such that the vertical clearance to bottom of the sign, light fixture, or its structural support, whichever is lowest, is not less than 17 feet above any portion of the pavement and shoulders.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Where lateral clearance is limited, Preferential Lane warning signs that are post-mounted on a median barrier and that are wider than 72 inches shall be mounted with a vertical clearance that complies with the provisions of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2a/#2a15">Section 2A.15</a> for overhead mounting.</p>
</div>

## 2G.10 Preferential Lane Guide Signs – General


<div class="secmeta">Section 2G.10 &middot; manual page 420 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2g/#2g10">CA amended: 2G.10</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2G.10/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Preferential lanes are used on freeways, expressways, and conventional roads. Except as otherwise provided, <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2g/#2g10">Sections 2G.10</a> through 2G.15 apply only to guide signs for preferential lanes on freeways and expressways.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> On conventional roads, guide signs applicable only to preferential lanes are ordinarily not needed, but if used, they should comply with the provisions for guide signs in Chapter 2D and any principles for Preferential Lane guide signs in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2g/#2g10">Sections 2G.10</a> through 2G.15 that engineering judgment finds to be appropriate for the conditions.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Additional guidance and standards related to the designation, operational considerations, signs, pavement markings, and other considerations for preferential lanes are provided in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2g/#2g03">Sections 2G.03</a> through 2G.07, 2G.09, and Chapter 3E.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> The appropriate combinations of pavement markings and standard overhead and post-mounted regulatory, warning, and guide signs for a specific preferential lane application should be selected based on an engineering study.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> If overhead signs applicable only to a preferential lane are located in approximately the same longitudinal position along the highway as overhead signs applicable only to the general-purpose lanes, the signs for the preferential lane should be separated laterally from the signs for the general-purpose lanes to the maximum extent practicable to minimize conflicting information.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> The Preferential Lane signs should be designed and located to avoid overloading the road user. The order of priority of guide signs should be Advance Guide, Preferential Lane Entrance Direction, and finally Preferential Lane Exit Destination Supplemental guide signs.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Signs applicable only to a preferential lane shall be distinguished from signs applicable to general- purpose lanes by the inclusion of the applicable symbol(s) and/or word(s).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> The symbol and/or word message that appears on a particular guide sign applicable only to a preferential lane will vary based on the specific type of traffic allowed and on other related operational constraints that have been established for a particular lane, such as an HOV lane, a bus lane, or a taxi lane.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> For HOV lanes, the diamond symbol shall appear on each Advance Guide (E8-3) sign (see <a href="#fig-2g-5">Figure 2G-5</a>), Preferential Lane Entrance Direction (E8-2 or E8-2a) sign (see <a href="#fig-2g-6">Figure 2G-6</a>), and Preferential Lane Entrance Gore (E8-1 or E8-1a) sign (see <a href="#fig-2g-7">Figure 2G-7</a>) for the designated entry and exit points for barrier- separated and buffer-separated geometric configurations and direct access ramps to or from such lanes. The diamond symbol shall not be used with preferential lanes for other types of traffic, such as bus lanes or taxi lanes.</p>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> Signing for an HOV lane that is managed by means of varying the occupancy requirement in response to changing conditions shall also comply with these provisions.</p>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> The diamond symbol shall be displayed in the legend of each Preferential Lane guide sign at the designated entry and exit points for all types of HOV lanes (including barrier-separated, buffer-separated, contiguous, and direct access ramps) in order to alert motorists that there is a minimum allowable vehicle occupancy requirement for vehicles to use the HOV lanes. Guide signs shall not display the occupancy requirement for the preferential lane.</p>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> A combination of guide and regulatory signs shall be used in advance of and at the initial entry point and all intermediate entry points from general-purpose lanes or facilities to contiguous, barrier-separated, and buffer-separated preferential lanes where access between the preferential and general-purpose lanes is restricted to designated locations. The regulatory signs shall comply with the provisions of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2g/#2g03">Sections 2G.03</a> through 2G.07.</p>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> Regulatory signs alone shall be used in advance of, at the beginning of, and at periodic intervals along contiguous or buffer-separated preferential lanes that provide continuous access between the adjacent general-purpose lanes and the preferential lane (see <a href="#fig-2g-2">Figures 2G-2</a> and 2G-3). The design and placement of the regulatory signs shall comply with the provisions of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2g/#2g03">Sections 2G.03</a> through 2G.07.</p>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> Except as otherwise provided in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2g/#2g10">Sections 2G.10</a> through 2G.13, guide signs applicable to a preferential lane with a vehicle occupancy requirement shall be distinguished from those applicable to general-purpose lanes by displaying the white diamond symbol on a black background at the left-hand edge of these signs.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">15</span> When post-mounted guide signs applicable only to a preferential lane are installed on a median barrier with limited lateral clearance to the adjacent travel lanes or shoulders, the guide signs may have a vertically-oriented rectangular shape.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">16</span> When vertically-oriented rectangular-shaped guide signs applicable only to a preferential lane are installed on a median barrier, the top portion of the signs shall be comprised of the applicable white symbol or white word message that identifies the type of preferential lane (such as the diamond symbol for an HOV lane) on a black background with a white border, and the bottom portion of the sign shall be comprised of the appropriate guide sign legend on a green background with a white border (see <a href="#fig-2g-7">Figures 2G-7</a> through 2G-9).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">17</span> Where lateral clearance is limited, such as when a post-mounted Preferential Lane guide sign is installed on a median barrier, the edges of the sign should not project beyond the outer edges of the barrier.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">18</span> Where lateral clearance is limited, Preferential Lane guide signs that are 72 inches or less in width may be skewed up to 45 degrees in order to fit within the barrier width or may be mounted higher, such that the vertical clearance to the bottom of the sign, light fixture, or its structural support, whichever is lowest, is not less than 17 feet above any portion of the pavement and shoulders.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">19</span> Where lateral clearance is limited, Preferential Lane guide signs that are post-mounted on a median barrier and that are wider than 72 inches shall be mounted with a vertical clearance that complies with the provisions of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2a/#2a15">Section 2A.15</a> for overhead mounting.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">20</span> Lane-use control signals (see Chapter 4T) may be used at access points to preferential lanes to indicate that a ramp or access roadway leading to or from the preferential lane or facility, or one or more specific lanes of the facility, are open or closed.</p>
<p><span class="pn">21</span> Changeable message signs may supplement, substitute for, or be incorporated into static guide signs (see <a href="#fig-2g-6">Figure 2G-6</a>) where travel conditions change or where multiple types of operational strategies (such as variable occupancy requirements, vehicle types, or pricing policies) are used and varied throughout the day or week to manage the use of, control of, or access to preferential lanes.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">22</span> When changeable message signs (see Chapter 2L) are used as guide signs for preferential lanes, they shall be the required sign size and shall display the required letter height and legend format that correspond to the type of roadway facility and design speed.</p>
<p><span class="pn">23</span> Advance Guide signs, Preferential Lane Entrance Direction signs, and Preferential Lane Entrance Gore signs for the initial entry point and intermediate entry points into a preferential lane from the general- purpose lanes on the same designated route shall not identify the entry point as an exit by using the word “EXIT” on the sign or on a plaque.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">24</span> Advance Guide signs and Preferential Lane Entrance Direction signs for initial and intermediate entry points into a preferential lane should use the word “ENTRANCE,” such as “HOV LANE ENTRANCE” (see <a href="#fig-2g-5">Figures 2G-5</a> and 2G-6), to convey the fact that vehicles are not leaving the designated route.</p>
<p><span class="pn">25</span> Preferential Lane Entrance Gore signs (see <a href="#fig-2g-7">Figure 2G-7</a>) at the initial entry point to a preferential lane should use the word “ENTRANCE.” Preferential Lane Entrance Gore signs at intermediate entry points to a barrier-separated preferential lane where the sign would be located immediately adjacent to and directly viewed by traffic in the preferential lane should not use the word “ENTRANCE.”</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">26</span> When the entry point is on the left-hand side of the general-purpose lanes, a LEFT (E1-5mP) plaque (see <a href="#fig-2e-9">Figure 2E-9</a>) shall be added to the top left edge of the Advance Guide and Preferential Lane Entrance Direction signs. The LEFT plaque shall not be used on a preferential lane regulatory sign.</p>
</div>

## 2G.11 Signing for Initial Entry Points to Preferential Lanes


<div class="secmeta">Section 2G.11 &middot; manual page 423 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2g/#2g11">CA amended: 2G.11</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2G.11/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Except where a buffer-separated or contiguous preferential lane is added or where a general-purpose lane becomes a buffer-separated or contiguous preferential lane, and provides continuous access with the adjacent general-purpose lanes as illustrated in <a href="#fig-2g-2">Figures 2G-2</a> and 2G-3, an Advance Guide sign shall be provided at least ½ mile prior to the initial entry point to all types of preferential lanes in any type of geometric configuration on freeways and expressways. A Preferential Lane Entrance Direction sign shall also be provided at the initial entry point. Advance Guide and Preferential Lane Entrance Direction signs for such entry points shall not include the word “EXIT” (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2g/#2g10">Section 2G.10</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Where a general-purpose lane becomes a preferential lane that does not provide continuous access with the adjacent general-purpose lanes, an Advance Guide sign shall also be provided at approximately 1 mile in advance of the initial entry point. The Advance Guide and Entrance Direction signs in this sequence shall include a panel at the bottom of the sign with a black legend and border on a yellow background displaying a down arrow and the word ONLY as illustrated in <a href="#fig-2g-8">Figure 2G-8</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Unless an Advance Guide Sign is already required in Paragraph 2 of this Section, an Advance Guide sign should also be installed and located approximately 1 mile in advance of the initial entry point to a preferential lane on freeways and expressways that restricts access to the adjacent general-purpose lanes.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> An Advance Guide sign may also be installed and located approximately 2 miles in advance of the initial entry point to a preferential lane that restricts access with the adjacent general-purpose lanes to designated locations.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> For barrier-separated, buffer-separated, or contiguous preferential lanes where entry is restricted to only designated points on freeways and expressways, the Advance Guide and Preferential Lane Entrance Direction signs shall be mounted overhead.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Preferential Lane Exit Destination guide signs, identifying final destination and downstream exit locations accessible from the preferential lane (see <a href="#fig-2g-8">Figures 2G-8</a>, 2G-9, 2G-14, 2G-16, and 2G-17), should be installed in advance of the initial entry points to access-restricted preferential lanes (such as barrier-separated and buffer- separated). These signs should be located based on the priority of the message, the available space, the existing signs on adjacent general-purpose traffic lanes, roadway and traffic characteristics, the proximity to existing overhead signs, the ability to install overhead signs, and other unique local factors.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Advance destination guide signs for preferential lanes shall include an upper section displaying a black legend that includes the type of preferential lane and the word “EXITS,” such as “HOV EXITS,” on a white background. For preferential lanes that incorporate a vehicle occupancy requirement, the white diamond symbol on a black background shall be displayed at the left-hand edge of this upper section (see <a href="#fig-2g-9">Figure 2G-9</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> <a href="#fig-2g-8">Figure 2G-8</a> shows an example of signing for a general-purpose lane that becomes a preferential lane that does not provide continuous access with the adjacent general-purpose lanes.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> <a href="#fig-2g-9">Figure 2G-9</a> shows an example of signs for the initial entry point to a preferential lane.</p>

## 2G.12 Signing for Intermediate Entry Points to Preferential Lanes


<div class="secmeta">Section 2G.12 &middot; manual page 423 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-same" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2g/#2g12">CA MUTCD 2026: 2G.12</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2G.12/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> For barrier-separated, buffer-separated, and contiguous preferential lanes where entry is restricted only to designated points, an overhead Preferential Lane Entrance Direction sign shall be provided at intermediate entry points to the preferential lane from the general-purpose lanes.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> For barrier-separated and buffer-separated preferential lanes where intermediate entry from the general- purpose lanes is provided via a separate lane or ramp (see <a href="#fig-2g-10">Figure 2G-10</a>), at least one Advance Guide sign should be provided in addition to the Preferential Lane Entrance Direction sign.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> For access-restricted preferential lanes where intermediate entrance and egress are at the same designated access location, the Preferential Lane Entrance Direction sign should be located between ½ and ¼ of the length of the designated entry area, as measured from the downstream end of the entry area (see <a href="#fig-2g-11">Figure 2G-11</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> The Advance Guide signs, if used for intermediate entry points to a preferential lane from the general- purpose lanes, shall be overhead.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Advance Guide signs may be provided at approximately ½ mile, 1 mile, and 2 miles in advance of intermediate entry points from the general-purpose lanes to a preferential lane.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Advance Guide and Preferential Lane Entrance Direction signs for intermediate entry points shall not include the word “EXIT” (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2g/#2g10">Section 2G.10</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Exit Destination guide signs, identifying the final destination and downstream exit locations accessible from the preferential lane, should be installed in advance of intermediate entry points from the general-purpose lanes to access-restricted preferential lanes.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>

## 2G.13 Signing for Egress from Preferential Lanes to General-Purpose Lanes


<div class="secmeta">Section 2G.13 &middot; manual page 428 &middot; <a class="badge ca-same" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2g/#2g13">CA MUTCD 2026: 2G.13</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2G.13/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Except as provided in Paragraphs 4 and 5 of this Section, for barrier-separated, buffer-separated, and contiguous preferential lanes where egress is restricted only to designated points, post-mounted Advance Guide (E8-6) and post-mounted Intermediate Egress Direction (E8-5) signs (see <a href="#fig-2g-12">Figure 2G-12</a>) shall be installed in the median or on median barriers that separate two directions of traffic prior to and at the intermediate exit points from the preferential lanes to the general-purpose lanes (see <a href="#fig-2g-11">Figure 2G-11</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> The legends of these signs shall refer to the next exit or exits from the general-purpose lanes by displaying the appropriate destination information, exit number(s), or both. The Intermediate Egress Direction signs for egress from the preferential lanes to the general-purpose lanes shall not refer to the egress as an exit.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2g/#2g10">Section 2G.10</a> contains information on the design of post-mounted guide signs applicable to a preferential lane when installed on a median barrier. <a href="#fig-2g-11">Figures 2G-11</a> and 2G-13 show examples of signs for various geometric configurations of intermediate egress from a barrier-separated or buffer-separated preferential lane where access is restricted to designated locations.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Where two or more adjacent preferential lanes are present in a single direction, consideration should be given to the use of overhead guide signs to display the information related to egress from the preferential lanes.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> For barrier-separated and buffer-separated preferential lanes where egress from a preferential lane to the general-purpose lanes is restricted only to designated points via a separate lane or ramp, the Advance Guide and Intermediate Egress Direction signs for the egress should be mounted overhead and a Pull-Through sign should be mounted with the Intermediate Egress Direction sign (see <a href="#fig-2g-13">Figure 2G-13</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> For preferential lanes that incorporate a vehicle occupancy requirement, the design of the overhead Advance Guide and Egress Direction signs for intermediate egress from the preferential lanes to the general-purpose lanes shall display a white diamond symbol on a black background at the left-hand edge of the signs.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> The design of Pull-Through signs when used in conjunction with an Egress Direction sign at an intermediate egress from the preferential lanes to the general-purpose lanes shall be distinguished from those applicable to general-purpose lanes by inclusion of an upper section with the applicable black legend on a white background, such as HOV LANE. For preferential lanes that incorporate a vehicle occupancy requirement, the white diamond symbol on a black background shall be displayed at the left-hand edge of this upper section.</p>
</div>

## 2G.15 Signing for Direct Exits from Preferential Lanes to Another Highway


<div class="secmeta">Section 2G.15 &middot; manual page 429 &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2g/#2g15">CA amended: 2G.15</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2G.15/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> For contiguous preferential lanes on the left-hand side of the roadway, Advance Guide signs, Exit Direction signs, and Exit Gore (E8-4) signs (see <a href="#fig-2g-15">Figure 2G-15</a>) specifically applicable to the preferential lanes shall be used for exits to direct access ramps, such as HOV lane ramps (see <a href="#fig-2g-16">Figure 2G-16</a>) or ramps to park-and-ride facilities.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> The design of Advance Guide, Exit Direction, and Pull-Through signs for direct exits from preferential lanes shall be distinguished from those applicable to general-purpose lanes by inclusion of an upper section with the applicable black legend on a white background, such as HOV LANE (for Pull-Through signs) or HOV EXIT (for Advance Guide and Exit Direction signs). For preferential lanes that incorporate a vehicle occupancy requirement, the white diamond symbol on a black background shall be displayed at the left- hand edge of this upper section (see <a href="#fig-2g-16">Figures 2G-16</a> and 2G-17).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Advance Guide and Exit Direction signs for exits to direct access ramps from a preferential lane should be mounted overhead. A Pull-Through sign over the preferential lane should be used with the Exit Direction sign at exits to direct access ramps.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Post-mounted guide signs in a vertically-oriented rectangular shape installed on a median barrier shall not be used for the Advance Guide and Exit Direction signs for exits to direct access ramps.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Because direct access ramps for preferential lanes at interchanges connecting two freeways are typically left-hand side exits and typically have design speeds similar to the preferential lane, overhead Advance Guide signs and overhead Exit Direction signs shall be provided in advance of and at the entry point to each freeway-to-freeway preferential lane ramp (see <a href="#fig-2g-17">Figure 2G-17</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> The use of guide signs for preferential lanes at freeway interchanges should comply with the provisions for guide signs established in Chapter 2E of this Manual.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Guide signs for direct access ramps for preferential lanes at interchanges connecting two freeways are similar to those for a connecting ramp between two freeway facilities.</p>
</div>

## 2G.16 Signs for Managed Lanes – General


<div class="secmeta">Section 2G.16 &middot; manual page 434 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-same" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2g/#2g16">CA MUTCD 2026: 2G.16</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2G.16/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> The provisions of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2g/#2g03">Sections 2G.03</a> through 2G.07 regarding regulatory signs for preferential lanes shall apply to managed lanes operated at all times or at certain times by varying vehicle occupancy requirements (HOV) or by using vehicle type restrictions as a congestion management strategy. Such managed lanes shall use changeable message signs or changeable message elements within static signs to display the appropriate regulatory sign messages only when they are in effect.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> When certain types of vehicles (such as trucks) are prohibited from using a managed lane or when a managed lane is restricted to use by only certain types of vehicles during certain operational strategies, regulatory signs or regulatory panels within the appropriate guide signs that include changeable message elements shall be used to display the open/closed status of the managed lane for such vehicle types.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> When the vehicle occupancy required for use of an HOV lane is varied as a part of a managed lane operational strategy, regulatory signs that include changeable message elements shall be used to display the required minimum vehicle occupancy in effect.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2g/#2g18">Section 2G.18</a> contains information about regulatory signs for managed lanes that use tolling or pricing as a congestion management strategy, either exclusively or with other management strategies.</p>

## 2G.17 Signs for Priced Managed Lanes – General


<div class="secmeta">Section 2G.17 &middot; manual page 434 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2g/#2g17">CA amended: 2G.17</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2G.17/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> A priced managed lane is a managed lane that employs tolling or pricing, typically through electronic toll collection, to manage congestion levels and maintain a certain level of service for users of the facility. A priced managed facility typically provides a less-congested alternative to adjacent lanes along the same designated route, or to a nearby facility, that experience recurring congestion during peak periods. A priced managed lane might allow non-toll travel by certain vehicles based on occupancy or other criteria. A variety of operational management strategies might be used in conjunction with tolling or pricing.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> The number and combination of operational strategies that are applied to a managed lane to manage congestion or improve efficiency might be practically limited by the amount of information that can be legibly displayed on signs or in signing sequences and still be readily comprehended by road users. Such factors to consider when evaluating alternatives for managed lanes are locations of signs for general-purpose interchanges and for other roadway conditions, the number of intermediate access points between the managed and general- purpose lanes and the need to repeat the operational information, and the distance over which a signing sequence that displays all of the eligibility requirements can be displayed.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Because managed lanes have the capability to employ a variety of operational strategies on a changing basis, it is not practical to assign a naming convention to such lanes for the purpose of signing based on the specific operational management strategies, as is more readily accomplished with other types of preferential lanes, such as HOV, bus, or bicycle lanes. Instead, the various requirements, restrictions, and eligibility criteria are more appropriately conveyed through a sequence of regulatory and guide signs with a more encompassing designation for the purpose of providing directional information.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> As priced managed lanes have become prevalent as an operational strategy, it is important to maintain a uniform naming convention to distinguish those lanes that are an alternative to travel on adjacent general-purpose lanes on the same designated route to effectively communicate to motorists the range of basic requirements for similar facilities in different regions.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Priced managed lanes that are adjacent to general-purpose lanes along the same designated route shall be signed using the legend EXPRESS or EXPRESS LANE(S) as provided in this Chapter. This provision shall apply when any of the following operational strategies is used for a managed lane: A. All users of the managed lane are charged a fixed or variable toll; B. General-purpose traffic using the managed lane is charged a fixed or variable toll, but HOV traffic is allowed to travel without being charged a toll on either a full-time or part-time basis; C. General-purpose traffic using the managed lane is charged a fixed or variable toll, but HOV traffic is offered a discounted toll on either a full-time or part-time basis; or D. General-purpose traffic using the managed lane is charged a fixed or variable toll, but HOV traffic registered with a local program travels at a discounted toll or without being charged a toll on either a full-time or part-time basis (a transponder or other identifier is typically required of HOVs to indicate registration in conjunction with electronic or visual enforcement and verification of vehicle occupancy).</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> The legends EXPRESS and EXPRESS LANE(S) shall not be used on signs for entrances to highways on which all lanes are managed and there are no adjacent general-purpose lanes on the same designated route. The legends EXPRESS and EXPRESS LANE(S) shall not be used on signs for a managed ramp connection that provides an alternative to a general-purpose ramp connection (see <a href="#fig-2f-13">Figure 2F-13</a>), except where the ramp leads directly to a managed lane as described in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2g/#2g14">Section 2G.14</a>. The legends EXPRESS and EXPRESS LANE(S) shall not be used on signs for open-road tolling lanes that bypass a conventional toll plaza (see Chapter 2F).</p>
</div>

## 2G.18 Regulatory Signs for Priced Managed Lanes


<div class="secmeta">Section 2G.18 &middot; manual page 435 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2g/#2g18">CA amended: 2G.18</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2G.18/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Except as otherwise provided in this Section, the provisions of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2g/#2g03">Sections 2G.03</a> through 2G.07 regarding regulatory signs for Preferential lanes shall apply to priced managed lanes operated at all times or at certain times with a toll payment requirement of some or all vehicles to use the lane(s). Such managed lanes shall use changeable message signs or changeable message elements within static signs to display the appropriate regulatory sign messages only when they are in effect.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Regulatory signs for preferential lanes shall be appropriately modified for adaptation to a priced managed lane, where applicable, as shown in <a href="#fig-2g-18">Figure 2G-18</a>.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Regulatory signs shall be used to indicate the toll charged. If the toll varies the <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-48/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-48.svg">R3-48</a> and <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-48a/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-48a.svg">R3-48a</a> signs that are shown in <a href="#fig-2g-18">Figure 2G-18</a> shall be used to display the actual toll amount in effect at any given time.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> When only vehicles with a registered ETC account are allowed to use a managed lane where some or all vehicles are charged a toll, regulatory signs to indicate such a restriction shall be provided and shall incorporate the pictograph adopted by the toll facility’s ETC payment system and the word ONLY (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2g/#2g19">Section 2G.19</a> for the incorporation of such regulatory legends into the guide signs for the entrances to such facilities). The display of the ETC system pictograph shall comply with the provisions of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2f/#2f02">Sections 2F.02</a> and 2F.03 as shown in <a href="#fig-2g-18">Figures 2G-18</a> and 2G-19.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> When HOV traffic is allowed to use a priced managed lane without paying a toll and registration in a local program is not required to receive the toll exemption, the Vehicle Occupancy Definition (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-10/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-10.svg">R3-10</a> or <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-13/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-13.svg">R3-13</a>) signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2g/#2g04">Section 2G.04</a>) shall be modified to delete the diamond symbol to create priced managed lane Vehicle Occupancy Definition (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-40/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-40.svg">R3-40</a> and <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-43/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-43.svg">R3-43</a>) signs to indicate the minimum occupancy related to the management strategy (see <a href="#fig-2g-18">Figure 2G-18</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> A Priced Managed Lane Operation (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-44/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-44.svg">R3-44</a>, <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-44a/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-44a.svg">R3-44a</a>, or <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-44b/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-44b.svg">R3-44b</a>) sign (see <a href="#fig-2g-18">Figure 2G-18</a>) shall be installed at the beginning or initial entry point, and at any intermediate entry points where vehicles are allowed to legally enter an access-restricted priced managed lane.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> When the vehicle occupancy required for non-toll use of a managed lane is varied as a part of a priced managed lane operational strategy, regulatory signs that include changeable message elements shall be used to display the required vehicle occupancy in effect for non-toll travel.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Where registration in a local program or ETC account is required for HOV traffic to travel in a priced managed lane without being charged a toll or by being charged a discounted toll, such information may be displayed on a separate sign within the sequence of the required regulatory and guide signs.</p>

## 2G.19 Guide Signs for Priced Managed Lanes


<div class="secmeta">Section 2G.19 &middot; manual page 435 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2g/#2g19">CA amended: 2G.19</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2G.19/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Except as otherwise provided in this Section, guide signs for barrier-separated, buffer-separated, and contiguous managed lanes shall follow the specific provisions for Preferential Lane guide signs contained in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2g/#2g10">Sections 2G.10</a> through 2G.15. Except as otherwise provided in this Section, guide signs for highways on which all lanes are managed shall follow the general provisions for freeway and expressway guide signs as contained in Chapter 2E as a whole. Guide signs for highways on which all lanes are managed and tolling or pricing is used as a management strategy shall follow the applicable provisions for toll road guide signs as contained in Chapter 2F, in addition to the general provisions of Chapter 2E.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> If fixed or variable tolls are used as an operational strategy for a managed lane, the guide signs shall comply with the provisions of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2f/#2f02">Sections 2F.02</a>, 2F.03, and 2F.17 regarding the use, size, and placement of ETC-account pictographs.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Guide signs at the initial and intermediate entry points to a priced managed lane in which all general- purpose passenger vehicles are allowed shall include the legend EXPRESS LANE(S). Except as provided in Paragraph 5 of this Section, the guide signs shall incorporate the pictograph of the ETC account system into a header panel within the guide sign in accordance with <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2f/#2f02">Sections 2F.02</a>, 2F.03, and 2F.17. For a priced managed lane that allows non-toll travel by HOV traffic without registration in a local program, the header panel shall be modified to a regulatory format to display both the pictograph of the ETC account system and the minimum occupancy requirement for non-toll travel with a black legend on a white background (see <a href="#fig-2g-19">Figure 2G-19</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Guide signs at the initial and intermediate entry points to a managed lane that allows only HOV traffic with either a fixed or variable occupancy requirement shall follow the provisions of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2g/#2g10">Sections 2G.10</a> through 2G.12 and 2G.14.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> If registration in a toll-account program is not required for travel in a managed lane in which tolls are charged, then the ETC-account pictographs shall not be displayed on primary guide signs directing traffic to the managed lane. In such cases, the purple header panel shall be replaced with a warning header panel with a black legend and border on a yellow background displaying the word TOLL as illustrated in <a href="#fig-2g-20">Figure 2G-20</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> If the managed lane does not accept toll payments from an ETC account system and collects tolls only by post- travel billing of registered vehicle owners, then the legend TOLL BILLED BY MAIL ONLY may be displayed on a separate information sign within the sequence of primary guide signs in advance of the entrance to the managed lane.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> If the managed lane accepts payments from registered ETC accounts, but does not require registration to use the lane, then the pictographs of the accepted ETC account programs may be displayed on a separate information sign within the sequence of primary guide signs in advance of the entrance to the managed lane. The information sign may also display the legend TOLL BILLED BY MAIL OR in addition to the pictograph of the accepted ETC account program.</p>
</div>

## 2G.20 Signs for Part-Time Travel on a Shoulder – General


<div class="secmeta">Section 2G.20 &middot; manual page 447 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-same" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2g/#2g20">CA MUTCD 2026: 2G.20</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2G.20/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> In some cases, paved shoulders are allowed to be used for driving use during peak periods to manage congestion. Configurations might be on freeways and expressways, as well as on conventional roads. Travel on the shoulder during these periods might be restricted to certain classes of vehicles, such as buses or HOV, or might be open to general traffic. When the part-time travel on a shoulder is limited to certain classes of vehicles, the signing is similar to that for preferential lanes. Additional signing is typically used to advise road users that the shoulder is not available for emergency use during these periods. Part-time travel on a shoulder might also employ lane-use control signals and/or blank-out signs to inform traffic of the allowable use of the shoulder. Depending on the design of exit ramp terminals and auxiliary lanes, guide signs must account for exit maneuvers during both shoulder use conditions and might necessitate changeable legend elements. However, additional guide signs are not normally necessary specifically for the condition when the shoulder is used for travel. The pavement markings might also be modified where travel allowed on the shoulder begins and ends.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> <a href="#fig-2g-32">Figure 2G-32</a> shows an example of signing for part-time travel on a shoulder.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> A shoulder that has been opened to travel on a permanent, full-time basis shall be considered a travel lane and shall be signed and marked in accordance with other provisions of this Manual.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3e/#3e04">Section 3E.04</a> contains provisions regarding the placement of markings on paved shoulders that are open for part-time travel.</p>
</div>

## 2G.21 Regulatory Signs and Plaques for Part-Time Travel on a Shoulder


<div class="secmeta">Section 2G.21 &middot; manual page 447 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2g/#2g21">CA amended: 2G.21</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2G.21/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Regulatory signs shall be used to notify road users of the periods of operation that travel is allowed on a paved shoulder. <p><span class="pn">02</span> If certain classes of vehicles are not allowed to use the shoulder during these periods, then a Selective Exclusion (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-51ap/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-51aP.svg">R3-51aP</a> or <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-51bp/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-51bP.svg">R3-51bP</a>) plaque shall be mounted below the <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-51/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-51.svg">R3-51</a> or <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-51d/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-51d.svg">R3-51d</a> sign. <div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>

<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>

## 2G.24 Lane-Use Control Signals for Part-Time Travel on a Shoulder


<div class="secmeta">Section 2G.24 &middot; manual page 455 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2g/#2g24">CA amended: 2G.24</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2G.24/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Lane-use control signals (see Chapter 4T) are sometimes used for part-time travel on a paved shoulder (see Drawing A in <a href="#fig-2g-32">Figure 2G-32</a>), in addition to signs, to indicate the allowable use of the shoulder.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Overhead lane-use control signals may be used above a shoulder on which part-time travel is allowed.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Except as otherwise provided in this Section, lane-use control signals that are used for part-time travel on a shoulder shall comply with the provisions of Chapter 4T. When used for part-time travel on a shoulder, lane-use control signals shall not be required above the lanes adjacent to the shoulder. When used for part- time travel on a shoulder, a steady RED X signal indication shall be displayed when the shoulder is available for emergency stopping only and travel on the shoulder is otherwise prohibited.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> When part-time travel on shoulder is allowed for variable periods of operation, lane-use control signals (see Chapter 4T) shall be used and evenly spaced approximately every ½ mile or less and centered over the shoulder to indicate when the shoulder is open or closed to vehicle travel. The lane-use control signals shall display a steady DOWNWARD GREEN ARROW signal indication during times when travel is allowed on the shoulder, followed by a steady YELLOW X signal indication just before the shoulder is to be closed to travel, and a steady RED X signal indication when shoulder travel is discontinued. Additionally, during the period when travel is allowed on the shoulder, a lane-use control signal that continuously displays a steady YELLOW X signal indication shall be used approximately ½ mile in advance of the location where part-time travel on the shoulder ends, and then displays a steady RED X signal indication when the travel on shoulder ends. A lane-use control signal with a steady RED X signal indication shall be displayed at all times at the location where part-time travel on the shoulder ends.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> For part-time travel on shoulder with variable periods of operation, post-mounted Part-Time Travel on Shoulder Variable Operation (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-51d/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-51d.svg">R3-51d</a>) signs (see Drawing A in <a href="#fig-2g-32">Figure 2G-32</a>) with flashing beacons may be used in lieu of the lane-use control signals at the same intervals.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> The Part-Time Travel on Shoulder on Green Arrow (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-51e/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-51e.svg">R3-51e</a>) sign (see Drawing A in <a href="#fig-2g-32">Figure 2G-32</a>) may be used with a lane-use control signal and may be mounted adjacent to the signal head, elsewhere on the signal support, or post-mounted next to, or in advance of, the signal.</p>
</div>

## 2G.25 Lane-Use Control Signals for Active Lane Management on Freeways and Expressways


<div class="secmeta">Section 2G.25 &middot; manual page 455 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-same" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2g/#2g25">CA MUTCD 2026: 2G.25</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2G.25/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Active lane management is a component of active traffic management in which the use of travel lanes and speed limits might be varied in real time in response to traffic conditions to manage congestion. Active lane management might employ lane-use control signals (see Chapter 4T) and/or changeable message signs (see Chapter 2L). <a href="#fig-2g-33">Figure 2G-33</a> shows an example of lane-use control signals and variable Speed Limit signs for active lane management during an incident</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Except as otherwise provided in this Section, lane-use control signals that are used for active lane management shall comply with the provisions of Chapter 4T. When used for active lane management on a freeway or expressway, a steady YELLOW X signal indication shall be displayed to warn road users to vacate the lane when the next downstream lane-use control signal over the same lane is displaying a steady RED X signal indication.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> A steady YELLOW X signal indication may be displayed on one or more lane-use control signals in advance of the steady YELLOW X signal indication required by Paragraph 2 of this Section as conditions warrant to warn road users to vacate the lane.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Using too many steady YELLOW X signal indications could diminish the effectiveness of the steady YELLOW X signal indication in conveying the lane is closed a short distance ahead and the road user needs to vacate the lane soon.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> When operated in conjunction with a temporary planned lane closure, lane-use control signals shall only supplement the temporary traffic control devices as provided in Part 6 of this Manual.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Spacing of lane-use control signals for active lane management on freeways and expressways should be at ½-mile intervals. Closer spacing should be used where the viewing distance is limited by the roadway geometry, overcrossings or other sight obstructions, or where traffic entering from intervening interchange ramps is not adequately served by the ½-mile spacing.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Combining lane-use control signals with overhead sign support structures should be minimized to avoid overloading road users with too much information or conflicting or incorrect messages, such as exclusive lane use or lane drop implied by the display of a steady DOWNWARD GREEN ARROW signal indication below a guide sign.</p>
</div>

## 2G.26 Variable Speed Limits for Active Traffic Management on Freeways and Expressways


<div class="secmeta">Section 2G.26 &middot; manual page 457 &middot; <span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2g/#2g26">CA amended: 2G.26</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/2G.26/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Active traffic management on freeways and expressways might employ variable speed limits as an element of an overall congestion management plan using variable Speed Limit (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r2-1/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R2-1.svg">R2-1</a>) sign (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2b/#2b21">Section 2B.21</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Careful consideration is needed in locating variable Speed Limit signs along the roadway and potential positioning adjacent to guide signs or lane-use control signals so that the speed displayed is clearly associated with the lane or lanes intended to be regulated and not other adjacent lanes, ramps, or roadways. This might result in the need to place variable Speed Limit signs on separate supports away from guide and other signs or lane-use control signals.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> The regulatory speed displayed on a variable Speed Limit sign shall comply with Paragraph 13 of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2b/#2b21">Section 2B.21</a> and the “Standard Highway Signs” publication (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/1a/#1a05">Section 1A.05</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> The location and positioning of variable Speed Limit signs should clearly associate the speed displayed to the lane or lanes intended to be regulated such that it would not present a conflict or confusion with other posted speed limits or advisory speeds for adjacent lanes, ramps, or roadways. Variable Speed Limit signs should not be located on overhead guide sign installations (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2e/#2e43">Section 2E.43</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> In addition to the post-interchange Speed Limit sign (see Paragraph 17 of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2e/#2e47">Section 2E.47</a>), the spacing of variable Speed Limit signs on freeways and expressways should be based on an engineering study that considers such factors as recurring congestion, high-volume interchanges, weaving sections, and other location-specific factors that are known to affect travel speeds. The variable Speed Limit signs should be placed far enough in advance of known congestion points to adequately adjust the operating speed to minimize the extent of vehicle queuing.</p>
