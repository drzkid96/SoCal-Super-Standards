## 3D.01 General


<div class="secmeta">Section 3D.01 &middot; manual page 595 &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3d/#3d01">CA amended: 3D.01</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/3D.01/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Pavement markings and signing for a circular intersection should be integrally designed to correspond to the geometric design and intended lane use of a circular intersection.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Markings on the approaches to a circular intersection and on the circulatory roadway should be compatible with each other to provide a consistent message to road users. The markings should supplement the signing, both conveying the optional and mandatory movements such that road users will know to choose the proper lane in the approach to the circular intersection and remain in that lane throughout departure from the circulatory roadway.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Common circular intersection types include roundabouts, rotaries, and traffic circles (see definitions in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/1c/#1c02">Section 1C.02</a>). Traffic circles and rotaries are often much larger than roundabouts. Modern roundabouts feature channelized, curved approaches that reduce vehicle speed. Traffic calming circles are smaller and are typically used on urban or suburban neighborhood streets.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> <a href="#fig-3d-1">Figure 3D-1</a> provides an example of the pavement markings for approach and circulatory roadways at a roundabout. <a href="#fig-3d-2">Figures 3D-2</a> through 3D-8 illustrate examples of markings for roundabouts of various geometric and lane-use configurations.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Actuated LED pedestrian warning signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2a/#2a12">Section 2A.12</a>), traffic control signals, pedestrian hybrid beacons, and rectangular rapid flashing beacons (see Part 4) are sometimes used at roundabouts to facilitate the crossing of pedestrians or to meter traffic.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/8a/#8a12">Section 8A.12</a> provides information about circular intersections that contain or are in close proximity to grade crossings.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/9e/#9e05">Section 9E.05</a> contains information regarding bicycle lane markings at circular intersections.</p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3c/#3c09">Section 3C.09</a> contains information regarding crosswalks at circular intersections.</p>
</div>

## 3D.02 White Lane Line Pavement Markings for Roundabouts


<div class="secmeta">Section 3D.02 &middot; manual page 595 &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3d/#3d02">CA amended: 3D.02</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/3D.02/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Multi-lane approaches to roundabouts shall have lane lines.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> A through lane on a roadway that becomes a dropped lane (mandatory left-turn or right-turn lane) at a roundabout shall be marked with a dotted white lane line in accordance with <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3b/#3b07">Section 3B.07</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Multi-lane roundabouts should have lane line markings within the circulatory roadway to continuously channelize traffic in the circulatory roadway and through the departure movement.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Continuous concentric lane lines shall not be used within the circulatory roadway of a roundabout.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Channelizing lines (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3b/#3b08">Section 3B.08</a>) and chevron and diagonal markings (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3b/#3b25">Section 3B.25</a>) may be used on the approaches to and within the circulatory roadway of multi-lane roundabouts to separate traffic lanes, discourage lane changing, and/or compensate for off-tracking of larger trucks and vehicles.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Reducing the spacing between lines of a broken lane line allows better delineation of the lower-radius curves typically found in circular intersections.</p>
</div>

## 3D.03 Edge Line Pavement Markings for Roundabout Circulatory Roadways


<div class="secmeta">Section 3D.03 &middot; manual page 595 &middot; <a class="badge ca-same" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3d/#3d03">CA MUTCD 2026: 3D.03</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/3D.03/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> A white edge line should be used on the outer (right-hand) edge of the circulatory roadway.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Where a white edge line is used for the circulatory roadway, it should be as follows (see <a href="#fig-3d-1">Figure 3D-1</a>): A. A solid line adjacent to the splitter island, and B. A wide dotted line across the lane(s) entering the roundabout.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Edge lines and edge line extensions shall not be placed across the exits from the circulatory roadway at roundabouts.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> A yellow edge line may be placed around the inner (left-hand) edge of the circulatory roadway (see <a href="#fig-3d-1">Figure 3D-1</a>) and may be used to channelize traffic (see Drawing B in <a href="#fig-3d-3">Figure 3D-3</a>).</p>
</div>

<p><span class="pn">02</span> Word and/or route shield pavement markings may be used on an approach to or within the circulatory roadway of a roundabout to provide route and/or destination guidance information to road users (see <a href="#fig-3d-8">Figure 3D-8</a>).</p>

## 3D.06 Arrow Pavement Markings for Roundabouts


<div class="secmeta">Section 3D.06 &middot; manual page 605 &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3d/#3d06">CA amended: 3D.06</a> &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/us/3D.06/">short link</a></div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Lane-use arrow pavement markings should not be used on single-lane approaches to circular intersections.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Lane-use arrows should be used on approaches to circular intersections with double left or double right turns.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Lane-use arrow pavement markings shall not be provided between a crosswalk and a wide dotted line across the lane(s) entering the circular roadway.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Where lane-use arrows are used on the approaches to a roundabout, they may be either normal or curved-stem (see Drawing F in <a href="#fig-3b-21">Figure 3B-21</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> An oval or circle may be used with the lane-use arrows to symbolize the central island (see Drawing F in <a href="#fig-3b-21">Figure 3B-21</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> If lane-use arrows are used on the approaches to a roundabout, the style used should match the style of the lane-use arrows (normal or curved-stem) used on the regulatory lane-use signs on the approach.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> If lane-use arrow pavement markings are used within the circulatory roadway of multi-lane roundabouts, normal lane-use arrows (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3b/#3b23">Section 3B.23</a> and <a href="#fig-3b-21">Figure 3B-21</a>) should be used.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Details and sizes of the standard and curved-stem arrows that can be used for circular intersections are contained in the “Standard Highway Signs” publication (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/1a/#1a05">Section 1A.05</a>).</p>
</div>
