## 9A.01 General


<div class="secmeta"><span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/9a/#9a01">CA amended: 9A.01</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Part 9 covers signs and pavement markings specifically related to bicycle operation on roadways, separated bikeways, and shared-use paths. In jurisdictions where small, low-speed, human or electric-powered transportation devices (often referred to as a micromobility devices) are allowed to use bicycle facilities, they can be regulated by signs, pavement markings, and other traffic control devices related to bicycle operations. Part 4 contains information on highway traffic signals and bicycle signal faces. Part 6 contains information on work zones for bicycle facilities and the mitigation of impacts to bicycle travel through work zones.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Definitions and acronyms pertaining to Part 9 are provided in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/1c/#1c02">Sections 1C.02</a> and 1C.03.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> When operating on a roadway, bicycles are typically defined as vehicles and the operator of a bicycle is given the same rights and duties as an operator of a motor vehicle. Bicyclists are also vulnerable road users who have little to no protection from crash forces.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Designing bicycle facilities and the traffic control devices on those facilities in a manner that encourages predictable behavior and compliance with traffic laws from all roadway users can improve safety and increase public acceptance of bicyclists from other road users. The misuse of traffic control devices for improperly designed bicycle facilities or non-uniform applications can produce ineffective or counterproductive results. <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/1d/#1d01">Section 1D.01</a> provides more information on the importance of uniformity of traffic control devices.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> The “Bikeway Selection Guide” (FHWA-SA-18-077), FHWA, provides information on the designs and configuration of bicycle facilities.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> The operation of bicycles is generally allowed on rights-of-way open to motor vehicles, even if the bicycle- specific traffic control devices outlined in Part 9 are not present.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> All signs, signals, and markings, including those on bicycle facilities, should be properly maintained to command respect from all road users. When installing signs and markings on bicycle facilities, an agency should be designated to maintain these devices.</p>
</div>

## 9A.02 Standardization of Application for Signing


<div class="secmeta"><span class="badge rev">Revision 1 page</span> &middot; <a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/9a/#9a02">CA amended: 9A.02</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> The installation of nonstandard signing on bikeways or modifying standard signing in a manner inconsistent with <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2a/">Chapter 2A</a> of this Manual to draw special attention, educate users or the community, or brand a bicycle facility can contribute to problems with public acceptance and enforcement.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Bicycle signs shall comply with the provisions of this Manual for standard shape, legend, and color.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> All signs installed on bikeways shall be retroreflective, including those on shared-use paths and bicycle lane facilities.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Where signs serve both bicyclists and other road users, vertical mounting height and lateral placement shall be as provided in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2a/#2a15">Sections 2A.15</a> and 2A.16 of this manual.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Where used on a shared-use path, no portion of a sign or its support should be placed less than 2 feet laterally from the near edge of the path, or less than 8 feet vertically over the entire width of the shared-use path (see <a href="#fig-9a-1">Figure 9A-1</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Mounting height for post-mounted signs on shared-use paths should be a minimum of 4 feet, measured vertically from the bottom of the sign to the elevation of the near edge of the path surface (see <a href="#fig-9a-1">Figure 9A-1</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Signs for the exclusive use of bicyclists should be located so that other road users are not confused by them.</p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> The clearance for overhead signs on shared-use paths should be adjusted when appropriate to accommodate path users requiring more clearance, such as equestrians, or typical maintenance or emergency vehicles.</p>
</div>

<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> If the sign or plaque applies to motorists and bicyclists, then the size shall be as shown for conventional roads in <a href="#fig-2b-1">Tables 2B-1</a>, 2C-1, 2D-1, and 8B-1, as applicable.</p>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> The minimum sign and plaque sizes for signs specific to bicycle-only facilities and shared-use paths shall be those shown in <a href="#fig-9a-1">Table 9A-1</a>. These sizes shall be used only for signs and plaques installed specifically for bicyclist applications.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> Larger sizes of signs and plaques may be used on bicycle facilities when appropriate (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/2a/#2a07">Section 2A.07</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> Any diamond-shaped warning sign that is placed such that it is applicable only to bicyclists or pedestrians on shared-use paths or separated bicycle lanes may be 18” x 18”.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> Except for size, the design of signs and plaques for bicycle facilities should be identical to that provided in this Manual for signs and plaques for streets and highways.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> Uniformity in design of bicycle signs and plaques includes shape, color, symbols, arrows, wording, lettering, and illumination or retroreflectivity.</p>
</div>

## 9A.03 Standardization of Application for Markings


<div class="secmeta"><a class="badge ca-amend" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/9a/#9a03">CA amended: 9A.03</a></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Markings indicate the separation of the lanes for road users, assist the bicyclist by indicating assigned travel paths, indicate correct position for traffic control signal actuation, and provide advance information for turning and crossing maneuvers.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Pavement marking word messages, symbols, and/or arrows should be used in bikeways where appropriate.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Consideration should be given to selecting pavement marking materials that will minimize loss of traction for bicycles under wet conditions.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Pavement markings on bicycle facilities that must be visible at night or in low-light conditions shall be retroreflective unless the markings are adequately visible under provided lighting.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> The colors, width of lines, patterns of lines, symbols, and arrows used for marking bicycle facilities shall be as defined in Part 3.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/3h/#3h06">Section 3H.06</a> contains information on green-colored pavement for use with certain traffic control devices for bicycles and bicycle facilities.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/mutcd/9e/#9e17">Section 9E.17</a> contains information on the use of channelizing devices to emphasize the pavement markings for bicycle facilities.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Raised pavement markers should not be used on bicycle lanes or shared-use paths.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> If used around bicycle facilities, raised pavement markers should not be placed immediately adjacent to the travel path of bicyclists in a bicycle lane or on a shared-use path.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> Using raised pavement markers creates a collision potential for bicyclists by placing fixed objects immediately adjacent to the travel path of the bicyclist. Raised pavement markers can cause a bicyclist to lose balance and fall, and might not be visible to a bicyclist who is following another bicyclist.</p>
</div>
