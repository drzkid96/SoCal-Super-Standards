## 3B.01 Yellow Center Line Pavement Markings

<div class="secmeta">Section 3B.01 &middot; manual page 867 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/3B.01/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Center line pavement markings, when used, shall be the pavement markings used to delineate the separation of traffic lanes that have opposite directions of travel on a roadway and shall be yellow.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Center line pavement markings may be placed at a location that is not the geometric center of the roadway.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> On roadways without continuous center line pavement markings, short sections may be marked with center line pavement markings to control the position of traffic at specific locations, such as around curves, over hills, on approaches to grade crossings, at grade crossings, and at bridges.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> The center line markings on two-lane, two-way roadways shall be one of the following as shown in <a href="#fig-3b-1">Figure 3B-1</a>: A.Two-direction passing zone markings consisting of a normal width broken yellow line where crossing the center line markings for passing with care is permitted for traffic traveling in either direction; B.One-direction no-passing zone markings consisting of a double yellow line, one of which is a normal width broken yellow line and the other is a normal width solid yellow line, where crossing the center line markings for passing with care is permitted for the traffic traveling adjacent to the broken line, but is prohibited for traffic traveling adjacent to the solid line; or C.Two-direction no-passing zone markings consisting of two normal width solid yellow lines where crossing the center line markings for passing is prohibited for traffic traveling in either direction.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> A single solid yellow line shall not be used as a center line marking on a two-way roadway.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Except where a reversible lane (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b04">Section 3B.04</a>) or a two-way left-turn lane (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b05">Section 3B.05</a>) is present, the center line markings on undivided two-way roadways with four or more lanes for moving motor vehicle traffic always available shall be the two-direction no-passing zone markings consisting of normal width double solid yellow lines as shown in <a href="#fig-3b-2">Figure 3B-2</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b11">Section 3B.11</a> contains information for application of pavement markings through intersections or interchanges.</p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> On two-way roadways with three through lanes for moving motor vehicle traffic, two lanes should be designated for traffic in one direction by using one-direction or two-direction no-passing zone markings as shown in <a href="#fig-3b-3">Figure 3B-3</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> <span class="ca">Refer to FHWA's List of Known Errors for error in labeling Paragraph 7. Refer to <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1a/#1a04">Section 1A.04</a> for more details.</span></p>
</div>

## 3B.02 Warrants for Yellow Center Lines

<div class="secmeta">Section 3B.02 &middot; manual page 867 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/3B.02/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Center line markings shall be placed on all paved undivided two-way urban arterials and collectors that have a traveled way of 20 feet or more in width and an ADT of 6,000 vehicles per day or greater. Center line markings shall also be placed on all paved undivided two-way streets or highways that have three or more lanes for moving motor vehicle traffic.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Center line markings should be placed on paved urban arterials and collectors that have a traveled way of 20 feet or more in width and an ADT of 4,000 vehicles per day or greater. Center line markings should also be placed on all rural arterials and collectors that have a traveled way of 18 feet or more in width and an ADT of 3,000 vehicles per day or greater. Center line markings should also be placed on other traveled ways where an engineering study indicates such a need.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Engineering judgment should be used in determining whether to place center line markings on traveled ways that are less than 16 feet wide because of the potential for traffic encroaching on the pavement edges, traffic being affected by parked vehicles, and traffic encroaching into the opposing traffic lane.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Center line markings may be placed on other paved two-way traveled ways that are 16 feet or more in width.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> If a traffic count is not available, the ADTs described in this Section may be estimates that are based on engineering judgment.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> <span class="ca">Centerline patterns shall be selected from those shown in Figures 3A-101(CA) and 3A-104(CA). </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> <span class="ca">Raised retroreflective pavement markers shall be used to supplement the centerline markings on State highways. If </span> <span class="ca">used in snow areas, they shall be recessed. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> <span class="ca">Raised retroreflective pavement markers may be used to supplement the centerline markings on highways and conventional </span> <span class="ca">roads. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> <span class="ca">On horizontal curves with radii less than 3280 feet and without street lighting, <a class="sref dref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/markings/line-types/#detail-22" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/linetypes/detail-22.svg">Detail 22</a> instead of <a class="sref dref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/markings/line-types/#detail-21" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/linetypes/detail-21.svg">Detail 21</a> can be helpful in </span> <span class="ca">improving the delineation for centerline markings, as it includes retroreflective raised pavement markers. <a class="sref dref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/markings/line-types/#detail-22" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/linetypes/detail-22.svg">Detail 22</a> can be applied in </span> <span class="ca">advance of the approach to the curve per <a href="#fig-2c-3">Table 2C-3</a> and continued throughout the length of the curve. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> <span class="ca">Refer to CVC § 21460 for Double Lines. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> <span class="ca">Refer to CVC § 21460.5 for Two-Way Left-Turn Lanes. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> <span class="ca">A left edge line shall consist of a solid yellow line of 4 to 6 inches wide for local streets and roads and 6 inches wide on </span> <span class="ca">the State Highway System, yellow reflective pavement markers, yellow-red retroreflective markers, or a combination of line </span> <span class="ca">and markers as shown in <a href="#fig-3a-105ca">Figure 3A-105(CA)</a>. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> <span class="ca">Two normal solid yellow lines may be used as a left edge line on a divided roadway for more emphasis when motorists tend to </span> <span class="ca">use the shoulder for a through lane or where encroachments onto the shoulder occasionally occur. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> <span class="ca">Left edge line patterns for median islands are shown in <a href="#fig-3a-107ca">Figure 3A-107(CA)</a>. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">15</span> <span class="ca">When a passing lane is provided, a two-direction no passing marking (refer to <a href="#fig-3a-104ca">Figure 3A-104(CA)</a>) shall be used when </span> <span class="ca">the Average Daily Traffic (ADT) exceeds 3,000. Refer to <a href="#fig-3b-104ca">Figure 3B-104(CA)</a>. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">16</span> <span class="ca">Passing in both directions may be provided by alternating the direction of the middle lane at about 1 mile intervals. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">17</span> <span class="ca">A one-direction no passing marking (refer to <a href="#fig-3a-103ca">Figure 3A-103(CA)</a>) with one or more YIELD TO UPHILL TRAFFIC (R55(CA)) signs </span> <span class="ca">may be used when the ADT is 3,000 or less. </span></p>
</div>

## 3B.03 No-Passing Zone Pavement Markings

<div class="secmeta">Section 3B.03 &middot; manual page 868 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/3B.03/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> No-passing zones shall be marked by either the one-direction no-passing zone pavement markings or the two- direction no-passing zone pavement markings described in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b01">Section 3B.01</a> and shown in Figures 3B-1 and 3B-3.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> No-passing zone markings shall be used on: A.Two-way roadways at lane-reduction transitions (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b12">Section 3B.12</a>), B.Approaches to obstructions that must be passed on the right (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b13">Section 3B.13</a>), C.Approaches to grade crossings (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/8c/#8c02">Section 8C.02</a>), and D.Approaches to crosswalks.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> On two-way, two-lane or three-lane roadways where center line markings are installed, no- passing zones shall be established at vertical and horizontal curves and other locations where an engineering study indicates that passing must be prohibited because of inadequate sight distances or other special conditions.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> On roadways with center line markings, no-passing zone markings shall be used at horizontal or vertical curves where the passing sight distance is less than the minimum shown in <a href="#fig-3b-1">Table 3B-1</a> for the 85th-percentile speed or the speed limit.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> The passing sight distance on a vertical curve is the distance at which an object 3.5 feet above the pavement surface can be seen from a point 3.5 feet above the pavement (see <a href="#fig-3b-4">Figure 3B-4</a>). Similarly, the passing sight distance on a horizontal curve is the distance measured along the center line (or right-hand lane line of a three-lane roadway) between two points 3.5 feet above the pavement on a line tangent to the embankment or other obstruction that cuts off the view on the inside of the curve (see <a href="#fig-3b-4">Figure 3B-4</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> The upstream end of a no-passing zone at point “a” in <a href="#fig-3b-4">Figure 3B-4</a> is that point where the sight distance first becomes less than that specified in <a href="#fig-3b-1">Table 3B-1</a>. The downstream end of the no-passing zone at point “b” in <a href="#fig-3b-4">Figure 3B-4</a> is that point at which the sight distance again becomes greater than the minimum specified.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Where the distance between successive no-passing zones is less than 400 feet, no-passing zone markings should connect the zones.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">07a</span> <span class="ca">If the gap between successive no-passing zones is less than the sight distance for the prevailing speed shown in Table </span> <span class="ca">3B-1, or 400 feet, whichever is greater, the no-passing zone shall be continuous. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> No-passing zone signs (see Sections 2B.36, 2B.37, and 2C.53) are sometimes used to emphasize the existence and extent of a no-passing zone.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> On three-lane roadways where the direction of travel in the center lane transitions from one direction to the other, a no-passing buffer zone, consisting of a flush median island (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3j/#3j03">Section 3J.03</a>) at least 50 feet in length, shall be provided in the center lane as shown in <a href="#fig-3b-5">Figure 3B-5</a>. A lane-reduction transition (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b12">Section 3B.12</a>) shall be provided approaching each end of the buffer zone.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> <span class="ca">Refer to CVC §§ 21750 through 21759 for overtaking and passing. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> <span class="ca">Refer to CVC § 21460 for Double Lines. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> <span class="ca">CVC § 21752 restricts passing (driving on the left side of a two-way roadway) when approaching within 100 feet of or when </span> <span class="ca">traversing any intersection or railroad grade crossing. CVC § 21752 also restricts passing (driving on the left side of a two-way </span> <span class="ca">roadway) when the view is obstructed upon approaching within 100 feet of any bridge, viaduct, or tunnel. The patterns and policy for </span> <span class="ca">intersection markings are shown in <a href="#fig-3a-109ca">Figure 3A-109(CA)</a>. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> <span class="ca">No-passing zone patterns shall be selected from those shown in Figures 3A-103(CA) and 3A-104(CA). </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> <span class="ca">The no-passing zone markings at intersections, when used, should be between 100 feet and 300 feet in length at the approach </span> <span class="ca">to an intersection and placed in a pattern as shown in <a href="#fig-3a-109ca">Figure 3A-109(CA)</a>. </span></p>
</div>

## 3B.04 Yellow Pavement Markings for Reversible Lanes

<div class="secmeta">Section 3B.04 &middot; manual page 869 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/3B.04/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> If reversible lanes are used, the lane line pavement markings on each side of reversible lanes shall consist of a normal width broken double yellow line to delineate the edge of a lane in which the direction of travel is reversed from time to time, such that each of these markings serve as the center line markings of the roadway during some period (see <a href="#fig-3b-6">Figure 3B-6</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2b/#2b34">Section 2B.34</a>), lane-use control signals (see Chapter 4T), or both shall be used to supplement reversible lane pavement markings.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3e/#3e02">Section 3E.02</a> contains additional applications of pavement markings for counter-flow preferential lanes that also operate as reversible lanes.</p>
</div>

## 3B.05 Pavement Markings for Two-Way Left-Turn Lanes

<div class="secmeta">Section 3B.05 &middot; manual page 869 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/3B.05/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> If a two-way left-turn lane that is never operated as a reversible lane is used, the lane line pavement markings on each side of the two-way left-turn lane shall consist of a normal width broken yellow line and a normal width solid yellow line to delineate the edges of a lane that can be used by traffic in either direction as part of a left-turn maneuver. These markings shall be placed with the broken line toward the two-way left-turn lane and the solid line toward the adjacent traffic lane as shown in <a href="#fig-3b-7">Figure 3B-7</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> White two-way left-turn lane-use arrows should be used at or just downstream from the beginning of a two-way left- turn lane.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Additional two-way left-turn lane-use arrow markings may be used at other locations along a two-way left-turn lane where engineering judgment determines that such additional markings are needed to emphasize the proper use of the lane.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> A single-direction lane-use arrow shall not be used in a lane bordered on both sides by yellow two-way left- turn lane longitudinal markings.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Signs shouldbe used in conjunction with the two-way left-turn markings (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2b/#2b32">Section 2B.32</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Two-way left-turn lane markings should not extend to intersections (see definition in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1c/#1c02">Section 1C.02</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Two-way left-turn lanes may be transitioned to mandatory left-turn lanes as shown in <a href="#fig-3b-7">Figure 3B-7</a> or painted median islands where they approach an intersection.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/8a/#8a06">Section 8A.06</a> contains guidance information for discontinuing a two-way left-turn lane in the immediate vicinity of a highway-rail or highway-LRT grade crossing.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> <span class="ca">On State highways, reversible lanes shall be separated by physical barriers or delineators. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> <span class="ca">A two-way left-turn lane is a lane reserved in the center of a highway for exclusive use of left or U-turning vehicles. Refer to CVC </span> <span class="ca">§ 21460.5. It is normally used where there are many points of access. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> <span class="ca">The two-way left-turn lane markings shall be selected from those shown in Figures 3B-7 and 3A-108(CA). </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> <span class="ca">Optional treatments at signalized, major, and minor intersections, as shown in <a href="#fig-3b-7ca">Figure 3B-7(CA)</a>, may be used. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> <span class="ca">Two-way opposing pavement arrows may be used as shown in <a href="#fig-3b-7ca">Figure 3B-7(CA)</a>. The arrows may be supplemented by a Two-</span> <span class="ca">Way Left Turn Lane (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-9a/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-9a.svg">R3-9a</a> or <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-9b/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-9b.svg">R3-9b</a>) sign at new installations and problem locations. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> <span class="ca">A gap in the markings should be made at all intersections. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">15</span> <span class="ca">For left turn channelization, refer to <a href="#fig-3b-101ca">Figure 3B-101(CA)</a> and Caltrans’ Highway Design Manual, Section 405.2. Refer to Section </span> <span class="ca">1A.11 for information regarding this publication. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">16</span> <span class="ca">Channelized left-turn lanes in combination with continuous raised-curb medians are used instead of two-way left-turn lanes </span> <span class="ca">(TWLTL) if one or more of the following conditions exist: </span> <span class="ca">A.</span><span class="ca">Average daily traffic volumes exceed 20,000 vehicles per day </span> <span class="ca">B.</span><span class="ca">For remediation where there is a demonstrated crash problem, </span> <span class="ca">C.</span><span class="ca">Wherever a need is demonstrated through engineering study. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">17</span> <span class="ca">Refer to CVC § 21460.5 for Two-Way Left-Turn Lanes. </span></p>

## 3B.06 White Lane Line Pavement Markings

<div class="secmeta">Section 3B.06 &middot; manual page 870 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/3B.06/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> When used, lane line pavement markings delineating the separation of traffic lanes that have the same direction of travel shall be white.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Lane line markings shall be used on all freeways and Interstate highways.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>

## 3B.08 Channelizing Lines

<div class="secmeta">Section 3B.08 &middot; manual page 873 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/3B.08/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Channelizing lines are used to form neutral areas where traffic traveling in the same general direction is permitted on both sides including entrance and exit ramps, access and egress points to and from managed lanes, toll-plaza bypasses, and left-turn lanes separated from through lanes. Channelizing lines are also sometimes used to alter travel paths for speed management or other purposes.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Chapter 3J contains information for the application of channelizing lines used in conjunction with islands.</p>

<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Examples of channelizing line applications are shown in Figures 3B-9, 3B-10<span class="ca">, 3B-10(CA)</span>, 3B-11, Drawing C in <a href="#fig-3b-15">Figure 3B-15</a>, Figures 3J-1 through 3J-5, and Drawing B in <a href="#fig-3j-6">Figure 3J-6</a>.</p>

<p><span class="pn">02</span> Except as provided in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3e/#3e04">Section 3E.04</a>, right edge line pavement markings, if used, shall consist of a normal width solid white line to delineate the right-hand edge of the roadway.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> If used on the roadways of divided highways or one-way streets, or on any ramp in the direction of travel, left edge line pavement markings shall consist of a normal width solid yellow line to delineate the left-hand edge of a roadway or to indicate driving or passing restrictions left of these markings, except as provided in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3e/#3e04">Section 3E.04</a>.</p>

## 3B.10 Warrants for Use of Edge Lines

<div class="secmeta">Section 3B.10 &middot; manual page 874 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/3B.10/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Edge line markings shall be placed on paved streets or highways with the following characteristics: A.Freeways, B.Expressways, and C.Rural arterials with a traveled way of 20 feet or more in width and an ADT of 6,000 vehicles per day or greater.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Edge line markings should be placed on paved streets or highways with the following characteristics: A.Rural arterials and collectors with a traveled way of 20 feet or more in width and an ADT of 3,000 vehicles per day or greater. B.On other paved streets and highways where an engineering study indicates a need for edge line markings.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Edge line markings should not be placed where an engineering study or engineering judgment indicates that providing them is likely to decrease safety for all road users.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Edge line markings may be placed on streets and highways with or without center line markings.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Edge line markings may be excluded, based on engineering judgment, for reasons such as if the traveled way edges are delineated by curbs, parking, or other markings.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> If a bicycle lane is marked on the outside portion of the traveled way, the edge line that would mark the outside edge of the bicycle lane may be omitted.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Edge line markings may be used where edge delineation is desirable to minimize unnecessary driving on paved shoulders or on refuge areas that have lesser structural pavement strength than the adjacent roadway.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> <span class="ca">Edge lines shall be used on all State highways, except urban-type streets with curbs and parking provisions. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> <span class="ca">The Two-Way Traffic (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w6-3/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W6-3.svg">W6-3</a>) sign may be used in conjunction with edge lines at locations where road users could perceive that </span> <span class="ca">they are on a one-way roadway when, in fact, they are on a two-lane, two-way highway. Refer to <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2c/#2c51">Section 2C.51</a> for the <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w6-3/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W6-3.svg">W6-3</a> sign. </span></p>
</div>

## 3B.11 Application of Pavement Markings Through Intersections or Interchanges

<div class="secmeta">Section 3B.11 &middot; manual page 875 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/3B.11/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Pavement markings extended into or continued through an intersection or interchange area shall be the same color as the line markings they extend (see <a href="#fig-3b-13">Figure 3B-13</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Pavement markings extended into or continued through an intersection or interchange area should be at least the same width as the line markings they extend.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Where highway design or reduced visibility conditions make it desirable to provide control or to guide vehicles through an intersection or interchange, such as at offset, skewed, complex, or multi-leg intersections, on curved roadways, where multiple turn lanes are used, or where offset left-turn lanes might cause driver confusion, dotted lane line extension markings consisting of 2-foot line segments and 2-foot to 6- foot gaps should be used to extend longitudinal line markings through an intersection or interchange area.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Where greater restriction is preferred, solid lane lines or channelizing lines should be extended into or continued through intersections.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Extensions of center lines through intersections shall be dotted lines.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Where a double line is extended through an intersection, a single line of equal width to one of the lines of the double line should be used.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Solid lines shall not be used to extend edge lines into or through intersections except through that part of an intersection with no intersecting approach (such as at the far side of a T-intersection).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Edge line markings should be discontinued across intersecting approaches at intersections or interchanges.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Driveways that do not meet the definition of an intersection (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1c/#1c02">Section 1C.02</a>) should have edge line markings maintained across the intersecting approach of the driveway.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> Dotted edge line extensions may be placed through intersections.</p>

## 3B.12 Lane-Reduction Transitions

<div class="secmeta">Section 3B.12 &middot; manual page 875 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/3B.12/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> A lane-reduction is where the number of through lanes is reduced at a location that is not at an interchange or intersection because of narrowing of the roadway or because of a section of on-street parking in what would otherwise be a through lane.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b07">Section 3B.07</a> contains information on pavement markings for lane drops and splits.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2c/#2c47">Section 2C.47</a> contains information for warning signing used for lane reductions.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Lane-reduction transitions (see <a href="#fig-3b-14">Figure 3B-14</a> <span class="ca">3B-14(CA)</span>) shall include the following elements: A.A no-passing zone (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b03">Section 3B.03</a>) to prohibit passing in the direction of the convergence and through the transition area except where not applicable such as one-way streets, expressways, and freeways; and B.An edge line (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b09">Section 3B.09</a>) in the direction of the convergence and through the transition area, except as provided in Paragraph 6 of this Section.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Except as provided in Paragraph 6 of this Section, the edge line marking should be installed from the location of the Lane Ends warning sign to beyond the beginning of the narrower roadway.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> On roadways with operating speeds less than 25 mph where curbs clearly define the roadway edge in the lane- reduction transition, or where a through lane becomes a parking lane, the edge line may be omitted as determined by engineering judgment.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Lane-reduction transitions should include the following elements: A.Delineators installed adjacent to the lane or lanes reduced for the full length of the transition and should be so placed and spaced (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3g/#3g04">Section 3G.04</a>) to show the reduction except as provided in Paragraph 13 of this Section and except as provided in Paragraph 2 of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3g/#3g03">Section 3G.03</a> for freeways and expressways, B.Lane-reduction arrow markings (see Drawing F in <a href="#fig-3b-21">Figure 3B-21</a>) on the roadway with a speed limit of 45 mph or more, and C.A termination of the broken white lane line at a point that is ¼ of the advance placement distance (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2c/#2c04">Section 2C.04</a>) between the Lane Ends sign (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2c/#2c47">Section 2C.47</a>) and the point where the transition taper begins.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">07a</span> <span class="ca"> Refer to FHWA’s List of Known Errors for error in Paragraph 07, Item B text. Refer to <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1a/#1a04">Section 1A.04</a> for more details. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> For roadways having a speed limit of 45 mph or greater, the transition taper length for a lane- reduction transition should be computed by the formula L = WS, where L equals the taper length in feet, W equals the width of the offset distance in feet, and S equals the 85th-percentile speed or the speed limit in mph, whichever is higher. For roadways where the speed limit is less than 45 mph, the formula L = WS2/60 should be used to compute the taper length.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> The minimum lane reduction transition taper length should be 100 feet in urban areas and 200 feet in rural areas.</p>

<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>

<p><span class="pn">12</span> On new construction, where no speed limit has been established, the design speed may be used in the transition taper length formula.</p>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> On low-speed urban roadways where curbs clearly define the roadway edge in the lane-reduction transition, or where a through lane becomes a parking lane, delineators may be omitted as determined by engineering judgment.</p>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> Where a lane-reduction transition occurs on a roadway with a speed limit of less than 45 mph, lane- reduction arrow markings may be used.</p>
<p><span class="pn">15</span> Lane-reduction arrow markings may be used in long acceleration lanes based on engineering judgment.</p>
<p><span class="pn">16</span> A dotted white line may be used between the point where the broken white lane line is terminated to the point where the transition taper begins.</p>

## 3B.13 Approach Markings for Obstructions

<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>

<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>

For roadways where the speed limit is less than 45 mph, the formula L = WS2/60 should be used to compute the taper length.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> The minimum taper length should be 100 feet in urban areas and 200 feet in rural areas.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>

</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>

</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>

<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>

## 3B.16 Raised Pavement Markers Supplementing Other Markings

<div class="secmeta">Section 3B.16 &middot; manual page 878 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/3B.16/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> The use of retroreflective or internally illuminated raised pavement markers for supplementing longitudinal line markings should comply with the following: A.Lateral Positioning 1.When supplementing double line markings, pairs of raised pavement markers placed laterally in line with or immediately outside of the two lines should be used. 2.When supplementing wide line markings, pairs of raised pavement markers placed laterally adjacent to each other should be used. B.Longitudinal Spacing 1.When supplementing solid line markings, raised pavement markers at a spacing no greater than N (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b14">Section 3B.14</a>) should be used, except that when supplementing channelizing lines or edge line markings, a spacing of no greater than N/2 should be used. 2.When supplementing broken line markings, a spacing no greater than 3N should be used. However, when supplementing broken line markings identifying reversible lanes, a spacing of no greater than N should be used. 3.When supplementing dotted lane line markings, a spacing appropriate for the application should be used. 4.When supplementing longitudinal line extension markings through at-grade intersections, one raised pavement marker for each short line segment should be used. 5.When supplementing line extensions through freeway interchanges, a spacing of no greater than N should be used.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Raised pavement markers should not supplement right-hand edge lines unless an engineering study or engineering judgment indicates the benefits of enhanced delineation of a curve or other location would outweigh possible impacts on bicyclists using the shoulder, and the spacing of raised pavement markers on the right-hand edge does not simulate a broken line during wet night conditions.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02a</span> <span class="ca">One-way blank-red retroreflective raised pavement markers should supplement wrong-way arrows, limit lines/stop lines, yield </span> <span class="ca">lines, or crosswalk markings used at freeway exit ramps</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Raised pavement markers also may be used to supplement other markings such as channelizing islands, gore areas, approaches to obstructions, or wrong-way arrows.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> To improve the visibility of horizontal curves, center lines may be supplemented with retroreflective or internally illuminated raised pavement markers for the entire curved section as well as for a distance in advance of the curve that approximates 5 seconds of travel time.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> <span class="ca">The widths and patterns of raised pavement markers shall conform to the details shown in Figures 3A-101(CA) through </span> <span class="ca">3A-114(CA). Refer to <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3a/#3a04">Section 3A.04</a>. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> <span class="ca">When the one-way blank-red retroreflective raised pavement markers are placed along the type V arrow, limit lines, </span> <span class="ca">yield lines, or crosswalk markings, the side that is visible to traffic proceeding in the wrong direction shall be red and the </span> <span class="ca">side visible to approach traffic shall be blank. </span></p>

## 3B.18 Curb Markings for Parking Regulations

<div class="secmeta">Section 3B.18 &middot; manual page 880 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/3B.18/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>

<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>

</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Stop lines shall consist of solid white lines extending across approach lanes to indicate the point at which the stop is intended or required to be made.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Except as provided in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/8c/#8c03">Section 8C.03</a>, stop lines shall not be used at locations where drivers are required to yield in compliance with a YIELD (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r1-2/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R1-2.svg">R1-2</a>) sign, a Yield Here to Pedestrians (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r1-5/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R1-5.svg">R1-5</a>) sign, a Yield Here to School Crossings (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r1-5a/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R1-5a.svg">R1-5a</a>) sign, a Yield Here to Trail Crossings (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r1-5d/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R1-5d.svg">R1-5d</a>) sign, or at locations on uncontrolled approaches where drivers or bicyclists are required by State law to yield to pedestrians.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Stop lines should be used to indicate the point behind which vehicles are required to stop in compliance with a traffic control signal (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/4d/#4d08">Section 4D.08</a>).</p>

<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>

<p><span class="pn">09</span> Yield lines shall not be used at locations where drivers are required to stop in compliance with a STOP (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r1-1/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R1-1.svg">R1-1</a>) sign, a Stop Here for Pedestrians (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r1-5b/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R1-5b.svg">R1-5b</a>) sign, a Stop Here for School Crossing (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r1-5c/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R1-5c.svg">R1-5c</a>) sign, a Stop Here for Trail Crossing (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r1-5e/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R1-5e.svg">R1-5e</a>) sign, a traffic control signal, or some other traffic control device.</p>

## 3B.20 Word, Symbol, and Arrow Pavement Markings – General

<div class="secmeta">Section 3B.20 &middot; manual page 882 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/3B.20/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Word, symbol, and arrow markings on the pavement are used for the purpose of regulating, warning, or guiding traffic. These pavement markings can be helpful to road users in some locations by supplementing signs and providing additional emphasis for important regulatory, warning, or guidance messages, because the markings do not require diversion of the road user’s attention from the roadway surface. Symbol messages are preferable to word messages. Examples of standard word and arrow pavement markings are shown in Figures 3B-17 and 3B-21, respectively.</p>
<p><span class="pn">01a</span> <span class="ca"> Normally, pavement word and symbol markings supplement standard signing. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Word, symbol, and arrow pavement markings may be used as determined by engineering judgment to supplement signs and/or to provide additional emphasis for regulatory, warning, or guidance messages provided by other devices.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/8c/#8c04">Section 8C.04</a> contains information for arrow pavement markings in the vicinity of grade crossings.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Word, symbol, and arrow markings shall be white, except as otherwise provided in this Section.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Pavement marking letters, numerals, symbols, and arrows shall be installed in accordance with the design details in the Pavement Markings chapter of the “Standard Highway Signs” publication (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1a/#1a05">Section 1A.05</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">05a</span> <span class="ca"> Refer to Caltrans’ Standard Plans for pavement marking letters, numerals, and symbols. Refer to <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1a/#1a05">Section 1A.05</a> for information </span> <span class="ca">regarding this publication </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Word, symbol, and/or arrow markings that are grouped together to formulate one interrelated message should not exceed three lines of information.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Except for the two opposing white arrows of a two-way left-turn lane marking (see <a href="#fig-3b-7">Figure 3B-7</a>) and the pavement word marking messages described in Items B and D of Paragraph 2 of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b26">Section 3B.26</a>, the longitudinal space between word, symbol, and/or arrow markings that are used together to formulate one interrelated message should be at least four times the height of the characters for low-speed roads, but not more than ten times the height of the characters under any conditions.</p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Except for the SCHOOL word marking (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/7c/#7c02">Section 7C.02</a>), pavement word, symbol, and arrow markings should be no more than one lane in width.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Pavement word, symbol, and arrow markings should be proportionally scaled to fit within the width of the facility upon which they are applied.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> On narrow, low-speed shared-use paths, the pavement words, symbols, and arrows may be smaller than suggested, but to the relative scale.</p>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> On roadways where the operating speed is less than 25 mph, word, symbol, and arrow markings may be proportionally reduced by 25 percent.</p>
</div>

## 3B.28 Speed Reduction Markings

<div class="secmeta">Section 3B.28 &middot; manual page 889 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/3B.28/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Speed reduction markings (see <a href="#fig-3b-25">Figure 3B-25</a>) are transverse markings that are placed on the roadway within a lane (along both edges of the lane) in a pattern of progressively reduced spacing to give drivers the impression that their speed is increasing.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Speed reduction markings have been shown to enhance safety around curves and locations with a history of run-off- the-road crashes when applied in combination with horizontal alignment warning signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2c/#2c05">Section 2C.05</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Speed reduction markings may be placed in advance of an unexpectedly severe horizontal or vertical curve or other roadway feature where drivers need to decelerate prior to reaching the feature and where the desired reduction in speeds has not been achieved by the installation of warning signs and/or other traffic control devices.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> If used, speed reduction markings should be reserved for unexpected curves or other usages based on engineering judgment. Speed reduction markings should not be used on long tangent sections of roadway or in areas frequented mainly by local or familiar drivers, such as school zones. If used, speed reduction markings should supplement the appropriate warning signs and other traffic control devices and should not substitute for these devices.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Speed reduction markings shall be a series of white transverse lines on both sides of the lane that are perpendicular to the center line, edge line, or lane line.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> The longitudinal spacing between the markings should be progressively reduced from the upstream to the downstream end of the marked portion of the lane.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Speed reduction markings should not be greater than 12 inches in width, and should not extend more than 18 inches into the lane.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Speed reduction markings shall be used only in lanes that have a longitudinal line (center line, edge line, or lane line) on both sides of the lane.</p>
</div>

<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> If speed hump markings are used, they shall be a series of white markings placed on a speed hump to identify its location. If markings are used for a speed hump that does not also function as a crosswalk or speed table, the markings shall comply with Option A, B, or C shown in <a href="#fig-3b-26">Figure 3B-26</a>. If markings are used for a speed hump that also functions as a crosswalk or speed table, the markings shall comply with Option A or B shown in <a href="#fig-3b-27">Figure 3B-27</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Where used, center line markings, lane line markings, and edge lines may be discontinued on the profile of the speed hump.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Where a speed hump or a speed table specifically incorporates a crossing movement for pedestrians, bicyclists, or equestrians, and functions as a raised crosswalk, crosswalk markings (see Chapter 3C) shall be provided.</p>
</div>

## 3B.30 Advance Speed Hump and Speed Table Markings

<div class="secmeta">Section 3B.30 &middot; manual page 890 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/3B.30/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Advance speed hump markings (see <a href="#fig-3b-28">Figure 3B-28</a>) may be used in advance of speed humps or other engineered vertical roadway deflections such as dips where added visibility is desired or where such deflection is not expected.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Advance word pavement markings such as BUMP or HUMP (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b20">Section 3B.20</a>) may be used on the approach to a speed hump either alone or in conjunction with advance speed hump markings. Appropriate advance warning signs may be used in compliance with <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2c/#2c27">Section 2C.27</a>.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02a</span> <span class="ca">. </span> <span class="ca">Raised retroreflective pavement markers may be used to supplement advance speed hump markings (Refer to <a href="#fig-3b-28">Figure 3B-28</a>). </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">02b</span> <span class="ca">Provisions for raised pavement markers supplementing other markings are contained in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b14">Section 3B.14</a> and 3B.16. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> If advance speed hump or speed table markings are used, they shall be a series of eight white 12-inch transverse lines that become longer and are spaced closer together as the vehicle approaches the speed hump or other deflection. If advance markings are used, they shall comply with the detailed design shown in <a href="#fig-3b-28">Figure 3B-28</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> If used, advance speed hump markings should be installed in each approach lane.</p>
</div>

## 3B.31 Markings for a Diamond Interchange with a Transposed Alignment Crossroad

<div class="secmeta">Section 3B.31 &middot; manual page 890 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/3B.31/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Markings used in a diverging diamond interchange with a transposed alignment crossroad can be advantageous for minimizing wrong-way movements. The potential for wrong-way movements is greatest at the crossover intersections where the alignment becomes transposed.</p>
<p><span class="pn">01a</span> <span class="ca">Diverging diamond interchanges, also known as double-crossover diamond interchanges, include directional crossovers on </span> <span class="ca">either side of the interchange that transpose the crossroad, which results in vehicles traveling on the left-hand side of the street or </span> <span class="ca">highway between the crossover intersections. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> On the transposed alignment, each direction shall be considered a one-way roadway whereas the edge line convention shall be in accordance with <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b09">Section 3B.09</a>. Both yellow and white edge lines shall be used.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> A lane-use arrow (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b23">Section 3B.23</a>) shall be used in each approach lane at the crossover intersection.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3c/#3c11">Section 3C.11</a> contains information on crosswalks and pedestrian movements for diverging diamond interchanges with a transposed alignment crossroad.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Flush median islands (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3j/#3j03">Section 3J.03</a>) shall not be used to divide the inverted flow of traffic.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Edge line and lane line extensions (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b11">Section 3B.11</a>) should be provided through the crossing points.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> <a href="#fig-3b-29">Figure 3B-29</a> illustrates an example of pavement markings for a diverging diamond interchange with a transposed alignment crossroad.</p>
</div>

## 3B.101(CA) Turnouts

<div class="secmeta">Section 3B.101(CA) &middot; manual page 890 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/3B.101(CA)/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> <span class="ca">Paved turnouts should be marked with an 8-inch-wide single solid white line between the through lane and the turnout. The line </span> <span class="ca">should not extend through the entry and exit areas. Refer to <a href="#fig-3b-105ca">Figure 3B-105(CA)</a> and Caltrans’ Highway Design Manual, Section </span> <span class="ca">204.5 (4). Refer to <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1a/#1a05">Section 1A.05</a> for information regarding this publication. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> <span class="ca">The right edge line should be dropped throughout the length of the turnout. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> <span class="ca">Turnout length may be increased 100 feet on downgrades over 3%. </span></p>
</div>
