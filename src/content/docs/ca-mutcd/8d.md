## 8D.01 Introduction

<div class="secmeta">Section 8D.01 &middot; manual page 1542 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/8D.01/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Active traffic control systems inform road users of the approach or presence of rail traffic at grade crossings. These systems include Exit Gate systems, automatic gates, flashing-light signals, traffic control signals, actuated blank-out and variable message signs, and other active traffic control devices that are used in conjunction with the signs and pavement markings that are described in Chapters 8B and 8C, respectively.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> <a href="#fig-8d-1">Figure 8D-1</a> shows a post-mounted flashing-light signal (two light units mounted in a horizontal line), a flashing- light signal mounted on an overhead structure, and an automatic gate assembly.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Where LRT speed is cited in this Part, it refers to the maximum speed at which LRT equipment is permitted to traverse a particular grade crossing.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03a California Public Utilities Commission General Order 143, “Safety Rules and Regulations Governing Light-Rail Transit”, as  amended, governs the operation and allowable speeds of light rail transit systems.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Post-mounted and overhead flashing-light signals may be used separately or in combination with each other as determined <span class="ca">recommended</span>by the Diagnostic Team <span class="ca">and determined by CPUC</span>. Also, flashing-light signals may be used without automatic gate assemblies, as determined <span class="ca">recommended</span>by the Diagnostic Team <span class="ca">and determined by CPUC</span>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> The meaning of flashing-light signals and automatic gates shall be as stated in Sections 11-701 and 11-703 of the Uniform Vehicle Code <span class="ca">CVC § 22451</span> (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1a/#1a06">Section 1A.06</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Location for flashing-light signals and automatic gates shall be as shown in <a href="#fig-8d-1">Figure 8D-1</a>.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Where there is a curb, a horizontal offset of at least 2 feet shall be provided from the face of the vertical curb to the nearest part of the signal or automatic gate arm in its upright position. Where a cantilevered-arm flashing- light signal is used, the vertical clearance shall be at least 17 feet above the crown of the highway to the lowest point of the signal unit.</p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Where there is a shoulder, but no curb, a horizontal offset of at least 2 feet from the edge of a paved shoulder shall be provided, with an offset of at least 6 feet from the edge of the traveled way.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Where there is no curb or shoulder, the minimum horizontal offset shall be 6 feet from the edge of the traveled way.</p>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> Minimum clearance dimensions for flashing-light signals and automatic gates relative to the closest track shall conform to standards provided by the railroad company and/or transit agency, and the regulatory agency with statutory authority (if applicable) <span class="ca">CPUC</span>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> Equipment housings (controller cabinets) should have a lateral offset of at least 30 feet from the edge of the highway, and where railroad or LRT property and conditions allow, at least 25 feet from the nearest rail.</p>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> If a pedestrian route is provided, sufficient clearance from supports, posts, and automatic gate mechanisms should be maintained for pedestrian travel.</p>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> Where determined <span class="ca">recommended</span>by the Diagnostic Team, a lateral escape route to the right-hand side of the highway in advance of the grade crossing traffic control devices should be kept free of guardrail or other ground obstructions. Where guardrail is not deemed necessary or appropriate, barriers should not be used for protecting signal supports.</p>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> The same lateral offset and roadside safety features should apply to flashing-light signal and automatic gate locations on both the right-hand and left-hand sides of the roadway.</p>
<p><span class="pn">14a The visibility of flashing-light signals to approaching vehicular and/or pedestrian traffic should be the highest priority for flashing- light signal placement and aiming. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">15</span> In industrial or other areas involving only low-speed highway traffic or where signals are vulnerable to damage by turning truck traffic, guardrail may be installed to provide protection for the signal assembly.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">16</span> Where both traffic control signals and flashing-light signals (with or without automatic gates) are in operation at the same highway-LRT grade crossing, the operation of the devices should be coordinated to avoid any display of conflicting signal indications.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">17</span> If highway traffic signals must be located within close proximity to the flashing-light signals, the highway traffic signals may be mounted on the same overhead structure as the flashing-light signals.</p>
</div>

## 8D.02 Flashing-Light Signals

<div class="secmeta">Section 8D.02 &middot; manual page 1543 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/8D.02/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/8d/#8d04">Section 8D.04</a> contains additional information regarding flashing-light signals at highway-LRT grade crossings in semi-exclusive and mixed-use alignments.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> If used, the flashing-light signal assembly (shown in <a href="#fig-8d-1">Figure 8D-1</a>) on the side of the highway shall include a standard Crossbuck (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r15-1/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R15-1.svg">R15-1</a>) sign, and where there is more than one track, a supplemental Number of Tracks (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r15-2p/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R15-2P.svg">R15-2P</a>) plaque, all of which indicate to motorists, bicyclists, and pedestrians the location of a grade crossing.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> The bottom of the Number of Tracks (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r15-2p/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R15-2P.svg">R15-2P</a>) plaque (when used) should be located as low as practicable above the flashing-light backgrounds. The Crossbuck (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r15-1/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R15-1.svg">R15-1</a>) sign should be located just above the Number of Tracks (R15- 2P) plaque or, if no plaque is present, the bottom of the Crossbuck sign should be located as low as practicable above the flashing-light backgrounds.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Additional information regarding sizes and clearances of components used on flashing-light signals can be found in Part 3 of the “2023 AREMA Communications and Signals Manual” published by the American Railway Engineering and Maintenance-of-Way Association (AREMA).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">05 Except for median-mounted flashing-light signals,</span>At <span class="ca">at</span>highway-rail grade crossings, bells or other audible warning devices may <span class="ca">shall </span>be included in the assembly and may <span class="ca">shall</span>be operated in conjunction with the flashing-light signals to provide additional warning for pedestrians, bicyclists, and/or other non-motorized road users.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">05a An audible device such as a bell that is operated in conjunction with flashing-light signals may be mounted on either the top or  side of the mast of the flashing-light signal assembly. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">05b  The minimum mounting height for any part of the audible device including the mounting arm, such as a bell that is  operated in conjunction with flashing-light signals, shall be no lower than the bottom of the flashing-light signals. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> When indicating the approach or presence of rail traffic, the flashing-light signal shall display toward approaching highway traffic two red lights mounted in a horizontal line flashing alternately.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> If used, flashing-light signals shall be placed to the right-hand side of approaching highway traffic on all highway approaches to a grade crossing. They shall be located laterally with respect to the highway in compliance with <a href="#fig-8d-1">Figure 8D-1</a> except where such location would adversely affect signal visibility.</p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> If used at a grade crossing with highway traffic in both directions, back-to-back flashing-light signals shall be placed on each side of the tracks. On multi-lane one-way streets and divided highways, flashing-light signals shall be placed on the approach side of the grade crossing on both sides of the roadway or shall be placed above the highway.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Each red signal unit in the flashing-light signal shall flash alternately. The number of flashes per minute for each lamp shall be 35 minimum and 65 maximum. Each lamp shall be illuminated for approximately the same length of time. The total time of illumination of each pair of lamps shall be the entire operating time.</p>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> Flashing-light units shall use either 8-inch or 12-inch nominal diameter lenses. <span class="ca">Refer to CPUC General Order 75, as  amended.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> In choosing between the 8-inch or 12-inch nominal diameter lenses for use in grade crossing flashing- light signals, consideration should be given to the principles stated in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/4e/#4e02">Section 4E.02</a>.</p>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> If flashing-light signals are used, at least one pair of flashing lights should be provided for <span class="ca">visible from</span>each approach lane of the roadway.</p>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> The center-to-center distance between the two red lights in a flashing-light unit should be approximately 30 inches.</p>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> The mounting height of the flashing-light units, measured from the center of the flashing-light unit housing to the elevation of the crown of the roadway, should be between 8 feet and 9 feet.</p>
<p><span class="pn">15</span> The top of the support pole foundation should be no more than 4 inches above the surface of the ground and should be at the same elevation as the crown of the roadway.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">15a Where site conditions do not allow the top of the support pole foundation to meet the guidance above, the shoulder slope may  need to be re-graded, or the height of the railroad signal mast may need to be adjusted to meet requirements for vertical clearance  above the roadway. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">16</span> Grade crossing flashing-light signals shall operate at a low voltage using storage batteries either as a primary or stand-by source of electrical energy. Provision shall be made to provide a source of energy for charging batteries.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">17</span> Additional flashing-light signals may be mounted on the same supporting post and directed toward vehicular traffic approaching the grade crossing from other than the principal highway route, such as where there are approaching routes on highways closely adjacent to and parallel to the track(s).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">18</span> Where the storage distance for vehicles approaching a grade crossing is less than a design vehicle length, the Diagnostic Team should consider providing additional flashing-light signals aligned toward the movement turning toward the grade crossing.</p>
<p><span class="pn">19</span> The Diagnostic Team should consider the use of additional flashing-light signals to provide supplemental warning to pedestrians, especially on one-way streets and divided highways.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">20</span> References to lenses in this Section shall not be used to limit flashing-light signal optical units to incandescent lamps within optical assemblies that include lenses.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">21</span> Research has resulted in flashing-light signal optical units that are not lenses, such as, but not limited to, light- emitting diode (LED) flashing-light signal modules.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">22</span> If a Diagnostic Team <span class="ca">recommends and CPUC</span>determines that it is appropriate, the flashing-light signals may be installed on overhead structures or cantilevered supports as shown in <a href="#fig-8d-1">Figure 8D-1</a> where needed for additional emphasis, or for better visibility to approaching traffic, particularly on multi-lane approaches or highways with profile restrictions.</p>
<p><span class="pn">23</span> If it is determined <span class="ca">recommended</span>by a Diagnostic Team <span class="ca">and determined by CPUC</span>that one flashing-light signal on the cantilever arm is not sufficiently visible to road users, one or more additional flashing-light signals may be mounted on the supporting post and/or on the cantilever arm.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">23a For an approach to a pre-signal or queue cutter signal at a grade crossing, the Diagnostic Team should consider whether  omission of overhead flashing-light signals would enhance the conspicuity of the pre-signal or queue cutter signal. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">24</span> Breakaway or frangible bases shall not be used on the supporting posts for overhead structures or cantilevered arms that support overhead flashing-light signals.</p>
</div>

## 8D.03 Automatic Gates

<div class="secmeta">Section 8D.03 &middot; manual page 1544 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/8D.03/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> An automatic gate is a traffic control device used in conjunction with flashing-light signals.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> The automatic gate (see <a href="#fig-8d-1">Figure 8D-1</a>) shall consist of a drive mechanism and a fully retroreflective red-and- white-striped gate arm with lights. When in the down position, the gate arm shall extend across the approaching lanes of highway traffic.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> In the normal sequence of operation, unless constant warning time detection or other advanced system requires otherwise, the flashing-light signals and the lights on the gate arm (in its normal upright position) shall be activated immediately upon detection of approaching rail traffic. The gate arm shall start its downward motion not less than 3 seconds after the flashing-light signals start to operate, shall reach its horizontal position at least 5 seconds before the arrival of the rail traffic, and shall remain in the down position until the rail traffic completely clears the grade crossing.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> When the rail traffic clears the grade crossing, and if no other rail traffic is detected, the gate arm shall ascend to its upright position, following which the flashing-light signals and the lights on the gate arm shall cease operation.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Gate arms shall be fully retroreflective on both sides and shall have vertical stripes alternately red and white at 16-inch intervals measured horizontally. The width (which becomes the height of the retroreflective sheeting when the automatic gate is in the down position) of the retroreflective sheeting on the front of the gate arm shall be at least 4 inches for the first 32 feet of gate arm length measured from the center of the gate mast. The front of the gate arm beyond 32 feet to the tip of the gate shall have retroreflective sheeting at least 2 inches in width.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">05a Refer to FHWA’s List of Known Errors for error in Paragraph 5 text. Refer to <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1a/#1a04">Section 1A.04</a> for more details. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> It is acceptable to replace a damaged gate arm with a gate arm having vertical stripes even if the other existing gate arms at the same grade crossing have diagonal stripes; however, it <span class="ca">It</span> is also acceptable to replace a damaged gate arm with a gate arm having diagonal stripes if the other existing gate arms at the same grade crossing have diagonal stripes in order to maintain consistency per the provisions of Paragraph 13 of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1b/#1b03">Section 1B.03</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Gate arms shall have at least three red lights as shown in <a href="#fig-8d-1">Figure 8D-1</a>.</p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> When activated, the gate arm light nearest the tip shall be illuminated continuously and the other lights shall flash alternately in unison with the flashing-light signals such that the left-most flashing gate arm light(s) flashes simultaneously with the left-hand light of the flashing-light signals and the right-most flashing gate arm light(s) flashes simultaneously with the right-hand light of the flashing- light signals.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Typical gate arm lights are approximately 4 inches in diameter if they are circular. Rectangular gate arm lights with approximately the same illuminated surface area are sometimes used on gate arms instead of circular lights.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> The entrance gate arm mechanism shall be designed to fail safe in the down position.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> The gate arm should ascend to its upright position in 12 seconds or less.</p>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> In its normal upright position, when no rail traffic is approaching or occupying the grade crossing, the gate arm should be approximately vertical (see <a href="#fig-8d-1">Figure 8D-1</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> In the design of individual installations, consideration should be given to timing the operation of the gate arm to accommodate large and/or slow-moving motor vehicles.</p>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> The gate arms should cover the approaching highway to block all motor vehicles from being driven around the gate arms without crossing the center line.</p>
<p><span class="pn">15</span> The height of the gate arm when it is in the down position should be between 3.5 feet and 4.5 feet above the crown <span class="ca">surface</span>of the roadway. When the gate arm is in the down position, no portion of the counterweight should extend into the traveled way, sidewalk, or pathway.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">16</span> Channelizing devices and/or raised median islands may be used to discourage driving around lowered automatic gates.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">17</span> Where sufficient space is available, median islands should be at least 60 feet in length.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">18</span> Where automatic gates are located in the median, additional median width may be required to provide the minimum clearance for the counterweight supports.</p>
<p><span class="pn">19</span> Automatic gates may be supplemented by cantilevered flashing-light signals (see <a href="#fig-8d-1">Figure 8D-1</a>) where there is a need for additional emphasis or better visibility.</p>
</div>

## 8D.04 Use of Active Traffic Control Systems at LRT Grade Crossings

<div class="secmeta">Section 8D.04 &middot; manual page 1546 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/8D.04/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> At highway-LRT grade crossings where LRT speeds exceed 25 mph, active traffic control systems (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/8d/#8d01">Section 8D.01</a>) shall be used.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> At highway-LRT grade crossings where LRT speeds exceed 40 <span class="ca">35</span>mph, the active traffic control system shall include automatic gates. <span class="ca">Refer to CPUC General Order 143, as amended.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> The Diagnostic Team may recommend an active traffic control system with automatic gates at highway-LRT grade crossings where LRT speeds do not exceed 40 <span class="ca">35</span>mph.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> At highway-LRT grade crossings where LRT speeds are 25 mph or less, active traffic control systems should be used unless the Diagnostic Team <span class="ca">recommends and CPUC</span>determines that the use of Crossbuck Assemblies, STOP signs alone, or YIELD signs alone would be adequate.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Where the highway-LRT grade crossing is at a location other than an intersection and LRT speeds exceed 20 mph, traffic control signals should not be used in lieu of flashing-light signals.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Sections 8D.02 and 8D.03 contain additional provisions regarding the design and operation of flashing-light signals and automatic gates, respectively.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> If flashing-light signals are in operation at a highway-LRT crossing that is used by pedestrians, bicyclists, and/or other non-motorized road users, an audible device such as a bell shall also be provided and shall be operated in conjunction with the flashing-light signals.</p>
</div>

## 8D.05 Exit Gate and Four-Quadrant Gate Systems

<div class="secmeta">Section 8D.05 &middot; manual page 1546 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/8D.05/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Exit Gate systems may be installed to improve safety at grade crossings where a Diagnostic Team <span class="ca">recommends and  CPUC</span>determines that less restrictive measures, such as automatic gates and median islands, are not effective.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> A grade crossing that includes exit gates on some, but not all, of the exiting lanes is an Exit Gate system, but is not considered to be a Four-Quadrant Gate system.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> The term Four-Quadrant Gate system is used in a generic sense in that it refers to the fact that all entrances and exits from a grade crossing are controlled by automatic gates in order to provide a full closure to all entering and exiting lanes. The term Four-Quadrant Gate system does not refer to the number of gates installed, but rather the fact that a full closure is provided.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> The Exit Gate system shall use a series of automatic gates with fully retroreflective red-and- white-striped gate arms with lights, and when in the down position the gate arms extend individually across the entrance and exit lanes of the roadway as shown in <a href="#fig-8d-2">Figure 8D-2</a>. The provisions contained in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/8d/#8d02">Section 8D.02</a> for flashing-light signals shall be followed for signal specifications, location, and clearance distances.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Gate arm design, colors, and lighting requirements shall be in accordance with the provisions contained in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/8d/#8d03">Section 8D.03</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> The provisions contained in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/8d/#8d03">Section 8D.03</a> for automatic gates are applicable to exit gates.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> In the normal sequence of operation, unless constant warning time detection or other advanced system requires otherwise, the flashing-light signals and the lights on the gate arms (in their normal upright positions) shall be activated immediately upon the detection of approaching rail traffic. The entrance gate arms shall start their downward motion not less than 3 seconds after the flashing-light signals start to operate and shall reach their horizontal position at least 5 seconds before the arrival of the rail traffic. Exit gate arm activation and downward motion shall be based on detection or timing requirements established <span class="ca">recommended</span> by a Diagnostic Team <span class="ca">and as established or determined by CPUC</span>. If an Exit Gate system is present, the queue exit gate clearance time (see AREMA Manual) shall be long enough to permit the exit gate arm to lower after a design vehicle of maximum length is clear of the minimum track clearance distance (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/8a/#8a07">Section 8A.07</a>). The gate arms shall remain in the down position as long as the rail traffic occupies the grade crossing.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>

## 8D.09 Preemption of Highway Traffic Signals at or Near Grade Crossings

<div class="secmeta">Section 8D.09 &middot; manual page 1549 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/8D.09/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Traffic signal preemption for grade crossings is a complex topic that requires a specific understanding of grade crossing warning systems and highway traffic signal operations. While most traffic signal operations are governed only by the traffic signal controller unit and the associated traffic signal equipment, preemption for grade crossings is also governed by the grade crossing warning system. Active grade crossing warning systems include flashing-light signals and possibly automatic gates, as well as various types of train detection equipment. Where the traffic signal controller unit is interconnected with the grade crossing warning system for the purpose of preemption, a combined system is created. It is the combined system that requires a thorough understanding of the design and operating parameters in order to provide proper operation of the preemption system.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> The Federal Railroad Administration (FRA) has issued two documents that provide additional information relating to preemption of highway traffic signals at or near grade crossings. The first document is “Technical Bulletin S-12-01, Guidance Regarding the Appropriate Process for the Inspection of Highway-Rail Grade Crossing Warning System Pre- emption Interconnections with Highway Traffic Signals” and the second document is “Safety Advisory 2010-02, Signal Recording Devices for Highway-Rail Grade Crossing Active Warning Systems that are Interconnected with Highway Traffic Signal Systems.”</p>
<p><span class="pn">02a Preemption of highway traffic signals at or near grade crossings (“railroad preemption”) involves the notification of approaching  rail traffic that is forwarded from the railroad or light rail transit equipment to the highway traffic signal controller unit or assembly.  This results in the traffic signal entering a special control mode of operation. Railroad preemption has different considerations than  light rail transit priority because a railroad preemption design assumes that the train is unable to stop for signal indications provided  by the highway traffic signal system. The priority control of traffic control signals (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/4f/#4f20">Section 4F.20</a>), such as light rail transit priority,  is not considered railroad preemption. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">02b Railroad preemption is evaluated based on many location-specific factors such as the relation of the tracks to the intersection,  the number of phases in the traffic signal, other roadway conditions, and information provided by the railroad or light rail transit  agency. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">02c The “pedestrian change interval” (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/4i/#4i06">Section 4I.06</a>) is an interval during which the flashing UPRAISED HAND (symbolizing  DONT WALK) signal indication is displayed. The “pedestrian change interval” is a component of the “pedestrian clearance time”. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">02d The “track clearance interval” (see paragraph 11) provides an opportunity for motor vehicles at the grade crossing to clear the  track prior to the arrival of rail traffic, typically by displaying a green signal indication to motorists that are moving away from the track.  The “right-of-way transfer time” (see paragraph 20) is the amount of time needed prior to display of the track clearance interval. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">02e Shortening or omitting the pedestrian clearance time presents potential safety conflicts for road users. This is an important  consideration in the vicinity of schools, senior centers, stations, event centers and other significant pedestrian trip generators. Where  the railroad preemption system is designed to include the pedestrian clearance time, the pedestrian clearance time is a component  of the right-of-way transfer time. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">02f Where traffic control signals interconnected with a grade crossing include pedestrian signal heads, the Diagnostic  Team shall evaluate and recommend, subject to CPUC determination, whether the right-of-way transfer time includes  pedestrian clearance time. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">02g The railroad preemption system should be designed to be capable of providing pedestrian clearance time during the approach of  a train. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> If a grade crossing is equipped with flashing-light signals and is located 200 feet or less from an intersection or midblock location controlled by a traffic control signal, a pedestrian hybrid beacon, or an emergency-vehicle hybrid beacon, the intersection should be provided with rail preemption in accordance with <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/4f/#4f19">Section 4F.19</a> unless otherwise determined <span class="ca">recommended </span>by the Diagnostic Team <span class="ca">and determined by CPUC</span>.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Coordination with the flashing-light signals, such as using queue detection and queue cutter signals, blank-out signs, or other alternatives, should be considered where a traffic control signal, a pedestrian hybrid beacon, or an emergency-vehicle hybrid beacon is located more than 200 feet from the grade crossing. Factors to be considered should include traffic volumes, highway vehicle mix, highway vehicle and train approach speeds, frequency of trains, presence of midblock driveways or unsignalized intersections, and the potential for vehicular queues resulting from an adjacent downstream grade crossing or highway traffic signal to extend into the minimum track clearance distance (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/8a/#8a07">Section 8A.07</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> The highway agency or authority with jurisdiction and the regulatory agency with statutory authority, if applicable <span class="ca">CPUC</span>, should jointly determine<span class="ca"> evaluate and recommend, subject to CPUC determination</span>the preemption operation and the timing of highway traffic signals interconnected with grade crossings adjacent to signalized locations.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> If a highway traffic signal is installed 200 feet or less from a passive grade crossing, unless otherwise determined <span class="ca">recommended</span> by the Diagnostic Team <span class="ca">and determined by CPUC</span>, an active grade crossing warning system should be installed at the grade crossing to provide a means to preempt the highway traffic signal in order to clear vehicles from the minimum track clearance distance (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/8a/#8a07">Section 8A.07</a>) upon approach of rail traffic.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> If a highway traffic signal is interconnected with flashing-light signals, the flashing-light signals should be provided with automatic gates to prevent additional vehicles from being drawn into the minimum track clearance distance (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/8a/#8a07">Section 8A.07</a>) during the track clearance interval prior to the arrival of rail traffic unless a Diagnostic Team <span class="ca">recommends and CPUC</span> determines otherwise.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Regular joint inspections by the highway agency or authority with jurisdiction, the regulatory agency with statutory authority, if applicable <span class="ca">CPUC</span>, and the railroad company or transit agency are a best practice and typically include verification of the preemption operation, the amount of warning time and/or preemption time being provided by the grade crossing warning system, and the timing of highway traffic signals interconnected and/or coordinated with the flashing-light signals.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/4f/#4f19">Section 4F.19</a> includes a recommendation that traffic control signals that are adjacent to highway-rail grade crossings and that are coordinated with the flashing-light signals at the grade crossing or that include railroad preemption features be provided with a back-up power supply.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> Information regarding the type of preemption and any related timing parameters shall be provided to the railroad company or transit agency so that the railroad company or transit agency can design the appropriate train detection circuitry.</p>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> If preemption is provided, unless otherwise determined <span class="ca">recommended</span> by a Diagnostic Team <span class="ca">and determined by  CPUC</span>, the normal sequence of highway traffic signal indications shall be preempted upon the approach of a train to provide a track clearance interval to provide an opportunity for motor vehicles at the grade crossing to clear the minimum track clearance distance (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/8a/#8a07">Section 8A.07</a>) prior to the arrival of rail traffic.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> Where train switching or train restarts occur close to a grade crossing, the Diagnostic Team may determine <span class="ca">recommend, subject to CPUC determination,</span> that the preemption time can be reduced in accordance with the operating requirements of the railroad company and/or transit agency.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> Where flashing-light signals are in place at a grade crossing, any highway traffic signal faces installed within 50 feet of any rail shall be preempted upon the approach of rail traffic. The Diagnostic Team shall <span class="ca">evaluate and  recommend, subject to CPUC determination</span>determine the signal indications displayed by the highway traffic signal faces that control movements across the grade crossing in accordance with <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/4f/#4f19">Section 4F.19</a> in order to avoid the display of signal indications that conflict with the flashing-light signals.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> Where the flashing-light signals are in place at a grade crossing, the operation of any flashing yellow beacon installed within 50 feet of any rail should be considered by a Diagnostic Team to determine <span class="ca">recommend</span> whether the operation of the beacon should be terminated during the approach and passage of rail traffic.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">15</span> The preemption special control mode shall be activated by a supervised preemption interconnection using fail-safe design principles between the control circuits of the grade crossing warning system and the traffic signal controller unit. The approach of rail traffic to a grade crossing shall de-energize the interconnection or send a message via a fail-safe data communication protocol (such as the “IEEE Standard for the Interface Between the Rail Subsystem and the Highway Subsystem at a Highway Rail Intersection,” 1570-2002 (R2008), Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers), which in turn shall activate the traffic signal controller preemption sequence. This shall establish and maintain the preemption condition during the time the grade crossing warning system is activated, except that when automatic gates exist, the preemption condition shall not be terminated until the automatic gates are energized to start their upward movement.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">16</span> Advance preemption is the notification of approaching rail traffic that is forwarded to the highway traffic signal controller unit or assembly by the railroad or light rail transit equipment in advance of the activation of the grade crossing warning system.</p>
<p><span class="pn">17</span> The maximum preemption time is the maximum amount of time needed following initiation of the preemption sequence for the highway traffic signals to complete the timing of the right-of-way transfer time, queue clearance time, and separation time.</p>
<p><span class="pn">18</span> The separation time is the component of maximum preemption time during which the minimum track clearance distance is clear of vehicular traffic prior to the arrival of rail traffic.</p>
<p><span class="pn">19</span> Simultaneous preemption is the notification of approaching rail traffic that is forwarded to the highway traffic signal controller unit or assembly and grade crossing warning system at the same time.</p>
<p><span class="pn">20</span> The right-of-way transfer time is the amount of time needed prior to display of the track clearance interval. This includes any time needed by the railroad, light rail transit, or highway traffic signal control equipment to react to a preemption call, and any traffic control signal green, pedestrian walk and clearance if used (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/4f/#4f19">Section 4F.19</a>), yellow change, and red clearance intervals for conflicting traffic.</p>
<p><span class="pn">21</span> A supervised preemption interconnection is one that incorporates both a normally-open and a normally-closed circuit from the grade crossing warning system to verify the proper operation of the interconnection.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">22</span> Instead of supervision, a double-break preemption interconnection circuit that uses two normally- closed circuits that open both the source and return energy circuits may be used.</p>
<p><span class="pn">23</span> A preemption interconnection may incorporate both supervision and double-break circuits.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">24</span> Where train detection circuits are present at a passive grade crossing, the operation of the preemption interconnection should be treated as if active traffic control devices exist at the crossing and the preemption operation should be determined <span class="ca">evaluated</span>by a Diagnostic Team <span class="ca">and consistent with determination by CPUC</span>.</p>
<p><span class="pn">25</span> Where left turns are permitted at a downstream highway-highway traffic control signal from the roadway approach that crosses the track and a delayed or impeded left-turn movement could prevent vehicles from clearing the track, a protected left-turn movement should be provided during the track clearance interval if green signal indications are displayed to the approach for track clearance.</p>
<p><span class="pn">26</span> The decision to implement simultaneous or advance preemption should include consideration of the right-of-way transfer time, the queue clearance time, and the separation time in order to determine the maximum preemption time. These time periods should be compared to and verified with the operation of the grade crossing traffic control devices in order to evaluate the operation of the highway traffic signal and the preemption operation. These factors should be considered regardless of whether simultaneous or advance preemption operation is implemented as they are based on traffic signal minimum timing, vehicle acceleration characteristics, and physical distances along the roadway.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">26a NCHRP Report 812: Signal Timing Manual, Second Edition, published by the National Cooperative Highway Research Program  (NCHRP), provides additional information about preemption considerations for grade crossings, including the entry into preemption. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">27</span> Preemption time variability occurs when the traffic signal controller enters the preemption clearance interval with less than the maximum design right-of-way transfer time or when the speed of a train approaching the grade crossing varies.</p>
<p><span class="pn">28</span> The time interval between the initiation of advance preemption and the operation of the grade crossing warning system for rail traffic will decrease in situations when rail traffic is accelerating or increase in situations when rail traffic is decelerating.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">29</span> Where preemption is used and automatic gates are present, the possibility that an automatic gate might descend upon a vehicle should be analyzed.</p>
<p><span class="pn">30</span> If simultaneous preemption is used, an analysis of extended grade crossing warning time requirements should be conducted.</p>
<p><span class="pn">31</span> If advance preemption is used, an analysis of preemption operation, traffic signal sequencing, and traffic signal phasing should be conducted to identify preemption time variability. The analysis should include both the condition requiring the longest amount of time to enter the track clearance interval and the condition requiring the shortest amount of time to enter the track clearance interval.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">32</span> Where automatic gates are present and green signal indications are displayed at the downstream traffic control signal during the track clearance interval, the preemption sequence shall be designed such that the green signal indications are not terminated until the automatic gate(s) that controls access over the grade crossing toward the downstream intersection is fully lowered.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">33</span> The following are two examples of mutually-exclusive methods to resolve preemption time variability: A.Gate-down circuitry provides a means to hold the traffic signal controller sequence in the track clearance interval until the automatic gate(s) that controls access over the grade crossing toward the downstream intersection is fully lowered. B.Timing correction resolves preemption time variability by adding the right-of-way transfer time to the track clearance interval in the traffic signal controller unit and setting a fixed maximum period of time between the start of advance preemption and the operation of the flashing-light signals. <span class="ca">The fixed maximum period of time can be  implemented in the grade crossing warning system using a time delay relay or other methods generally referred to in this  section as a timing circuit.</span></p>
<p><span class="pn">34</span> The Third Edition of the <span class="ca">FHWA’s</span>“Railroad-Highway Grade <span class="ca">Highway-Rail</span> Crossing Handbook”<span class="ca">,</span><span class="ca">the Second Edition of  the Signal Timing Manual (NCHRP Report 812, 2015),</span> and the “2023 AREMA Communications and Signals Manual” published by the American Railway Engineering and Maintenance-of- Way Association (AREMA) provide additional information about preemption time variability.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">35</span> Where gate-down circuitry is used to resolve preemption time variability and an automatic gate is broken or is not fully lowered, the crossing control circuits shall not terminate the track clearance interval before the rail traffic has entered the grade crossing.</p>
<p><span class="pn">36</span> Where timing correction is used to resolve preemption time variability, a timing circuit shall be used to maintain a maximum time interval between the initiation of advance preemption and the operation of the grade crossing warning system when the approaching rail traffic is decelerating.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">37</span> Where a highway-highway intersection controlled by traffic control signals is interconnected with a grade crossing equipped with exit gates, advance preemption should be used because of the additional operating time that is required for the exit gates.</p>
<p><span class="pn">38</span> Where rail traffic routinely stops and re-starts within or just outside of the approaches to a grade crossing that is interconnected with highway traffic signals, the effects of rail traffic operations on the preemption operation should be analyzed <span class="ca">by a Diagnostic Team</span>.</p>
<p><span class="pn">39</span> Highway traffic signal control equipment should be capable of providing immediate re-service of successive requests for preemption from the railroad warning devices, even if the initial preemption sequence has not been completed. As appropriate, the highway traffic signal control equipment should be able to promptly return to the start of the track clearance interval at any time that the demand for preemption is cancelled and then reactivated. The highway traffic signal control equipment should have the ability to provide this immediate re-service at any point in the preemption sequence.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">40</span> Where traffic control signals are programmed to operate in a flashing mode during the preemption dwell interval (the period following the track clearance interval that lasts for the duration of the preemption interconnection activation), the beginning of the preemption dwell flashing mode shall not occur until the grade crossing equipment indicates that the rail traffic has entered the grade crossing.</p>
<p><span class="pn">41</span> At locations where conflicting preemption calls might be received to serve boats and trains, the Diagnostic Team shall determine <span class="ca">evaluate and recommend</span>the relative priority when conflicting preemption calls occur (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/4f/#4f19">Section 4F.19</a>). Where the boat and the train do not conflict with each other, the Diagnostic Team shall determine <span class="ca">evaluate and recommend</span>the preemption sequence when both preemption calls are occurring simultaneously. The United States Coast Guard or other appropriate authority that regulates the operation of the waterway shall be invited to participate on the Diagnostic Team and/or to provide input to the Diagnostic Team.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">42</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/4c/#4c10">Section 4C.10</a> describes the Intersection Near a Grade Crossing signal warrant that is intended for use at a location where the proximity to the intersection of a grade crossing on an intersection approach controlled by a STOP or YIELD sign is the principal reason to consider installing a traffic control signal.</p>
<p><span class="pn">43</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/4f/#4f19">Section 4F.19</a> describes additional considerations regarding preemption of traffic control signals at or near grade crossings.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">44 Except as provided in paragraph 45 of this section, permissive only mode and/or protected/permissive mode for left-turn  movements should not be used where the opposing movement is a track clearance phase. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">45 A flashing YELLOW ARROW signal indication may be used where the opposing movement is a track clearance phase if evaluated  and recommended by a Diagnostic Team and consistent with determination by CPUC. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">46 Railroad preemption typically operates with a sequence similar to the following. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">47 If there is sufficient advance preemption time available, the pedestrian walk and clearance intervals are provided. If there is not  sufficient advance preemption time available, the pedestrian walk and/or pedestrian change intervals are shortened or omitted. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">48 A yellow change interval and red clearance interval are provided for any signal phase that is green or yellow when preemption is  initiated and which will be red during the track clearance interval. The pedestrian signal faces display a steady UPRAISED HAND. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">49 Phases, which are in the green interval when preemption is initiated, and which will be green during the track clearance interval,  remain green. If permissive left turn movements would conflict with the track clearance green phase, a yellow change interval and red  clearance interval are provided prior to displaying the track clearance green phase. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">50 The track clearance interval is provided, typically a green signal indication, for the signal phase or phases controlling the approach  to the intersection that crosses the railroad tracks, followed by the yellow change and red clearance intervals. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">51 If gates are provided at the crossing, limited service is then typically provided following the track clearance interval. Signal  indications that control movements toward the tracks display steady red. The traffic signal provides a sequence of the phases that do  not control movements toward the tracks. Pedestrian signal phases operate in conjunction with phases that are permitted during the  limited service. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">52 The traffic signal returns to normal operation following release of railroad preemption. </span></p>
</div>

## 8D.10 Movements Prohibited During Preemption

<div class="secmeta">Section 8D.10 &middot; manual page 1553 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/8D.10/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> At a signalized intersection that is located within 100 feet of a grade crossing and the intersection traffic control signals are preempted by the approach of rail traffic, all existing permissive-only turning movements toward the grade crossing should be prohibited, steady red arrow signal indications should be shown to all existing protected/permissive and protected-only turning movements toward the grade crossing, and red signal indications should be shown to the straight-through movement toward the grade crossing during the signal preemption sequences. The prohibition of a permissive-only turning movement toward the grade crossing during preemption should be accomplished through the installation of a <span class="ca">two</span>blank-out turn prohibition (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-1a/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-1a.svg">R3-1a</a> or <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-2a/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-2a.svg">R3-2a</a>) sign <span class="ca">signs</span>(see <a href="#fig-8b-1">Figure 8B-1</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> All movements toward the track may be prohibited at a signalized intersection that is preempted by the approach of rail traffic, even if the clear storage distance is more than 100 feet.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Including the word “TRAIN” as part of the blank-out turn prohibition sign informs road users that the turn prohibition being displayed by the sign is in effect because rail traffic is approaching or occupying a nearby rail grade crossing, and that the turn prohibition will be terminated after the rail traffic has cleared the grade crossing.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Rail operations can include the use of activated blank-out turn prohibition (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-1a/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-1a.svg">R3-1a</a> or <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-2a/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-2a.svg">R3-2a</a>) signs at unsignalized highway-highway intersections in the vicinity of grade crossings, such as where a semi-exclusive or mixed-use alignment is within or parallel to the roadway where road users are normally permitted to turn across the tracks.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> An LRT-activated blank-out turn prohibition (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-1a/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-1a.svg">R3-1a</a> or <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-2a/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-2a.svg">R3-2a</a>) sign should be used during preemption where all three of the following conditions are present: A.There is no active warning system for the LRT grade crossing, B.Vehicles traveling along a parallel roadway would normally be permitted to turn left or right to travel across tracks that are located within 100 feet of the highway-highway intersection or within the median of the intersection, and C.The drivers turning at the highway-highway intersection are not controlled by a traffic control signal.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Blank-out turn prohibition signs that are associated with preemption shall display their message only when a preemption signal is being received from the railroad or LRT equipment or while the automatic gate is activated.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> The provisions contained in Chapter 2L for blank-out signs are applicable to <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-1a/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-1a.svg">R3-1a</a> and R3-2asigns.</p>
<p><span class="pn">08 Blank-out turn prohibition signs that are exclusively associated with preemption typically start to display their message when the  track clearance interval begins or when the grade crossing warning system begins activation, whichever occurs first. </span></p>
</div>

## 8D.11 Pre-Signals at or Near Grade Crossings

<div class="secmeta">Section 8D.11 &middot; manual page 1554 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/8D.11/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> If a grade crossing is located in close proximity to an intersection controlled by a traffic control signal and the clear storage distance is less than the design vehicle length, the use of pre-signals to control traffic approaching the grade crossing in the direction toward the intersection should be considered.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> If a grade crossing equipped with flashing-light signals, but without automatic gates, is located within 200 feet of an intersection controlled by a traffic control signal, a pre-signal should be provided.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> A pre-signal is generally used where the grade crossing is located less than 200 feet from a downstream signalized intersection. <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/8d/#8d12">Section 8D.12</a> contains information for grade crossings located 200 feet or more from a downstream signalized intersection.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Other measures that could be considered instead of or in addition to a pre-signal to minimize the possibility of vehicles queuing across the grade crossing include providing additional lanes, reducing the cycle length, using split phasing, using protected turn phasing, and/or providing an extended green interval for the approach.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Pre-signal faces shall display a steady red signal indication during the track clearance interval of the signal preemption sequence to prohibit additional motor vehicles from entering the minimum track clearance distance (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/8a/#8a07">Section 8A.07</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Pre-signal faces shall not display green signal indications when the grade crossing flashing-light signals are displaying flashing red indications.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Consideration should be given to using visibility-limited signal faces (see definition in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1c/#1c02">Section 1C.02</a>) at the intersection for the downstream signal faces that control the approach that is equipped with pre- signals.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> The duration of the extended green interval may be adjusted by vehicle detection located between the pre-signal and the downstream signalized intersection.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> The pre-signal phase sequencing may be timed with an offset from the downstream signalized intersection <span class="ca">during  normal operation of the traffic signal</span> such that the pre-signal’s green signal indication terminates prior to the downstream intersection’s green signal indication to minimize the possibility of stopping motor vehicles within the minimum track clearance distance (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/8a/#8a07">Section 8A.07</a>) and the clear storage distance.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> If pre-signals are used, the queue clearance time (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/8d/#8d09">Section 8D.09</a>) should be long enough to allow a design vehicle of maximum length stopped just inside the minimum track clearance distance (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/8a/#8a07">Section 8A.07</a>) to start up and move through the downstream intersection, or to clear the minimum track clearance distance if there is sufficient clear storage distance.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> The storage area for mandatory left-turn and right-turn lanes at signalized intersections that are downstream from grade crossings sometimes extends from the signalized intersection back to and across the grade crossing. In such cases, drivers that are in the turn lane are required to make a straight-through movement when they cross the track(s) and then are required to make a turning movement when they reach the downstream signalized intersection.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> A separate pre-signal face for the mandatory left-turn lane and/or right-turn lane should be provided in addition to the pre-signal signal faces provided for the through movement where both of the following conditions are met: A.The storage area for the turn lane extends from the downstream signalized intersection back to and across the grade crossing, and B.The green interval for the turning movement at the downstream intersection does not always begin and end simultaneously with the green interval for the adjacent through movement at the downstream intersection.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> Where adjacent lanes at a pre-signal are controlled separately, all of the signal faces shall be capable of displaying the following signal indications: CIRCULAR RED, CIRCULAR YELLOW, and straight-through GREEN ARROW. Left-turn GREEN ARROW and right-turn GREEN ARROW signal indications shall not be used in pre-signal faces. CIRCULAR GREEN signal indications shall not be used in pre-signal faces where adjacent lanes are controlled separately.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> Where all adjacent lanes at a pre-signal are controlled together, CIRCULAR GREEN signal indications may be used in pre-signal faces.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">15</span> If a separate signal face is provided at a pre-signal for separate control of a mandatory left-turn and/or right- turn lane that extends from the downstream signalized intersection back to and across the grade crossing, the separate signal face shall be devoted exclusively to controlling traffic in the turn lane separately from adjacent lanes, and: A.Shall be visibility-limited from the adjacent through movement, or B.A LEFT (RIGHT) LANE SIGNAL (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r10-10b/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R10-10b__Sheet_1_of_2.svg">R10-10b</a>) sign (see <a href="#fig-8b-1">Figure 8B-1</a>) shall be mounted adjacent to the separate signal face controlling traffic in a single turn lane or in the turn lane that is farthest from the adjacent through lane(s) if multiple turn lanes are present for a particular turning movement, and a LEFT (RIGHT) TURN LANE SIGNAL (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r10-10c/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R10-10c__Sheet_1_of_2.svg">R10-10c</a>) sign (see <a href="#fig-8b-1">Figure 8B-1</a>) shall be mounted adjacent to the separate signal face controlling traffic in the other turn lanes if multiple turn lanes are present for a particular turning movement.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">16</span> Because the signal faces at a pre-signal do not always display the same signal indications as the downstream signalized intersection, the approach to the pre-signal is considered to be a separate approach from the approach to the downstream signalized intersection. This means that the provisions in Sections 4D.05 through 4D.08 regarding the number of signal faces, the visibility and aiming of the signal faces, and the lateral and longitudinal positioning of the signal faces apply separately to the approach to the pre-signal.</p>
<p><span class="pn">17</span> The provisions in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/4d/#4d07">Section 4D.07</a> regarding the lateral positioning of separate turn signal faces are applicable to the separate signal faces that are provided at pre-signals for a mandatory turn lane that extends from the downstream signalized intersection back to and across the grade crossing.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">18</span> A STOP HERE ON RED (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r10-6/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R10-6.svg">R10-6</a> or <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r10-6a/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R10-6a.svg">R10-6a</a>) sign should be installed at the pre-signal’s stop line.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">19</span> If a pre-signal is installed upstream from a signalized intersection, a No Turn on Red (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r10-11/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R10-11.svg">R10-11</a>, <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r10-11a/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R10-11a.svg">R10-11a</a>, or <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r10-11b/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R10-11b.svg">R10-11b</a>) sign (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2b/#2b60">Section 2B.60</a>) shall be installed at the pre-signal for the approach that crosses the track if turns on red would otherwise be permitted at the downstream intersection.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">20</span> DO NOT STOP ON TRACKS (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r8-8/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R8-8.svg">R8-8</a>) signs may be installed in conjunction with a pre-signal.</p>
<p><span class="pn">21</span> Pre-signal faces may be located either upstream or downstream from the grade crossing in order to provide the most effective display to road users approaching the grade crossing.</p>
<p><span class="pn">22</span> If pre-signal faces must be located within close proximity to the flashing-light signals, the pre-signal faces may be mounted on the same overhead structure as the flashing-light signals.</p>
</div>

## 8D.12 Queue Cutter Signals at or Near Grade Crossings

<div class="secmeta">Section 8D.12 &middot; manual page 1556 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/8D.12/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> A queue cutter signal is a traffic control signal that controls one direction of traffic at a grade crossing to minimize the possibility of vehicles stopping within the minimum track clearance distance (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/8a/#8a07">Section 8A.07</a>). Although a queue cutter signal has a similar purpose as a pre-signal (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/8d/#8d11">Section 8D.11</a>), the difference is that a queue cutter signal is independent from the downstream signalized intersection, whereas a pre-signal is part of the downstream signal.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> At grade crossing locations where the queue from a bottleneck (usually a signalized intersection) that is downstream from the grade crossing frequently extends back to and across the grade crossing, a queue cutter signal may be installed.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> A queue cutter signal may be operated in one of the following modes: A.Actuated mode – the queue cutter signal operation is dependent on downstream detection of a growing queue. B.Non-actuated mode – the queue cutter signal operates on a time-of-day plan based on anticipated downstream queues. This mode could be similar to the functional operation of a pre-signal. C.Variable mode – the queue cutter signal operation varies between the actuated mode and the non- actuated mode based on the time of day, on queue detection, or both.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> A non-actuated queue cutter signal is generally used where the grade crossing is located between 200 feet and 400 feet from a downstream bottleneck. An actuated queue cutter signal is generally used where the grade crossing is located more than 400 feet from a downstream bottleneck. <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/8d/#8d11">Section 8D.11</a> contains information for grade crossings located less than 200 feet from a downstream signalized intersection.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Where adjacent lanes at a queue cutter signal are controlled separately, all of the signal faces shall be capable of displaying the following signal indications: CIRCULAR RED, CIRCULAR YELLOW, and straight-through GREEN ARROW. Left-turn GREEN ARROW and right-turn GREEN ARROW signal indications shall not be used in queue cutter signal faces. CIRCULAR GREEN signal indications shall not be used in queue cutter signal faces where adjacent lanes are controlled separately.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Where all adjacent lanes at a pre-signal <span class="ca">queue cutter</span> are controlled together, CIRCULAR GREEN signal indications may be used in queue cutter signal faces.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Queue cutter signal faces may be located either upstream or downstream from the grade crossing in order to provide the most effective display to road users approaching the grade crossing.</p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> If queue cutter signal faces must be located within close proximity to the flashing-light signals, the queue cutter signal faces may be mounted on the same overhead structure as the flashing-light signals.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> A STOP HERE ON RED (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r10-6/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R10-6.svg">R10-6</a> or <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r10-6a/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R10-6a.svg">R10-6a</a>) sign should be installed at the queue cutter signal’s stop line.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> DO NOT STOP ON TRACKS (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r8-8/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R8-8.svg">R8-8</a>) signs may be installed in conjunction with a queue cutter signal.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> Where a queue cutter signal operates in an actuated mode based on vehicle presence detection, the queue detector should be located to provide adequate distance to detect a growing queue, permit the queue cutter signal to complete any programmed minimum green or yellow change interval time, and then allow a design vehicle that lawfully crosses the queue cutter signal’s stop line during the yellow change interval to clear the minimum track clearance distance (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/8a/#8a07">Section 8A.07</a>) before the growing queue extends to the grade crossing.</p>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> A queue cutter signal that is operating in an actuated mode and that is displaying CIRCULAR RED signal indications should continue to display CIRCULAR RED signal indications as long as the downstream detection system continues to detect the presence of a vehicular queue at the detection point on the departure side of the grade crossing.</p>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> Where a queue cutter signal operates in actuated mode based on vehicle presence detection, consideration should be given to the potential for turning movements between the grade crossing and the downstream bottleneck that could create an intermediate queue of vehicles. Supplemental queue detectors should be considered to detect the formation of these intermediate queues to activate the queue cutter signal.</p>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> Where a queue cutter signal is operated in a non-actuated mode, the queue cutter signal should be coordinated with adjacent signals to provide for the progressive movement of traffic.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">15</span> Where a queue cutter signal is always operated in a non-actuated mode based on anticipated queues, the queue cutter signal may be operated in a flashing mode at times when the downstream queues are not expected to extend back to and across the grade crossing.</p>
<p><span class="pn">16</span> When a variable-mode queue cutter signal is operating in the non-actuated mode, the queue detector may be used to extend the display of the CIRCULAR RED signal indication as long as the downstream detection system continues to detect the presence of a vehicular queue at the detection point on the departure side of the grade crossing.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">17</span> A queue cutter signal shall be interconnected with the flashing-light signals at the grade crossing.</p>
<p><span class="pn">18</span> Queue cutter signal faces shall not display green signal indications when the grade crossing flashing-light signals are displaying flashing red indications.</p>
<p><span class="pn">19</span> When a queue cutter signal that is displaying straight-through GREEN ARROW signal indications (when operating in a steady, stop-and-go mode) or flashing CIRCULAR YELLOW signal indications (when operating in a programmed flashing mode) is preempted by the approach of rail traffic, it shall immediately display steady CIRCULAR YELLOW signal indications during the yellow change interval (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/4f/#4f17">Section 4F.17</a>) followed by steady CIRCULAR RED signal indications. The queue cutter signal shall continue to display the steady CIRCULAR RED signal indications until the rail traffic clears the grade crossing and no other rail traffic is detected.</p>
<p><span class="pn">20</span> A queue cutter signal operating in an actuated mode shall display straight-through GREEN ARROW signal indications except when it receives an actuation from the downstream vehicle presence detection system or is preempted by the approach of rail traffic. When it receives an actuation from the vehicle presence detection system, the queue cutter signal shall finish timing any active minimum green interval, if used, and then display steady CIRCULAR YELLOW signal indications during the yellow change interval (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/4f/#4f17">Section 4F.17</a>) followed by steady CIRCULAR RED signal indications. When no preemption call is present and the queue length is such that no vehicles are detected in the detection zone of the downstream vehicle presence detection system, the queue cutter signal shall finish timing any active minimum red interval, if used, and then return to the display of straight-through GREEN ARROW signal indications.</p>
<p><span class="pn">21</span> The failure modes of the queue cutter signal control system and vehicle presence detection circuitry shall be evaluated and accounted for in the design of any such system. Fail-safe design techniques shall be used in the system design. If a queue detector fails, the queue cutter signal shall display flashing CIRCULAR RED signal indications until the normal functioning of the detection system is restored.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">22</span> The storage area for mandatory left-turn and right-turn lanes at signalized intersections that are downstream from grade crossings sometimes extends from the signalized intersection back to and across the grade crossing. In such cases, drivers that are in the turn lane are required to make a straight-through movement when they cross the track(s) and then are required to make a turning movement when they reach the downstream signalized intersection.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">23</span> A separate queue cutter signal face for the mandatory left-turn lane and/or right-turn lane should be provided in addition to the queue cutter signal faces provided for the through movement where both of the following conditions are met: A.The storage area for the turn lane extends from the downstream signalized intersection back to and across the grade crossing, and B.The green interval for the turning movement at the downstream intersection does not always begin and end simultaneously with the green interval for the adjacent through movement at the downstream intersection.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">24</span> If a separate signal face is provided at a queue cutter signal for separate control of a mandatory left-turn and/or right-turn lane that extends from the downstream signalized intersection back to and across the grade crossing, the separate signal face shall be devoted exclusively to controlling traffic in the turn lane separately from adjacent lanes, and: A.Shall be visibility-limited from the adjacent through movement, or B.A LEFT (RIGHT) LANE SIGNAL (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r10-10b/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R10-10b__Sheet_1_of_2.svg">R10-10b</a>) sign (see <a href="#fig-8b-1">Figure 8B-1</a>) shall be mounted adjacent to the separate signal face controlling traffic in a single turn lane or in the turn lane that is farthest from the adjacent through lane(s) if multiple turn lanes are present for a particular turning movement, and a LEFT (RIGHT) TURN LANE SIGNAL (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r10-10c/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R10-10c__Sheet_1_of_2.svg">R10-10c</a>) sign (see <a href="#fig-8b-1">Figure 8B-1</a>) shall be mounted adjacent to the separate signal face controlling traffic in the other turn lanes if multiple turn lanes are present for a particular turning movement.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">25</span> Because the signal faces at a queue cutter signal do not always display the same signal indications as the downstream signalized intersection, the approach to the queue cutter signal is considered to be a separate approach from the approach to the downstream signalized intersection. This means that the provisions in Sections 4D.05 through 4D.08 regarding the number of signal faces, the visibility and aiming of the signal faces, and the lateral and longitudinal positioning of the signal faces apply separately to the approach to the queue cutter signal.</p>
<p><span class="pn">26</span> The provisions in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/4d/#4d07">Section 4D.07</a> regarding the lateral positioning of separate turn signal faces are applicable to the separate signal faces that are provided at queue cutter signals for a turn lane that extends from the downstream signalized intersection back to and across the grade crossing.</p>
<p><span class="pn">27</span> While queue cutter signals and queue jumping signals have similar names, their purpose, design, and operation are quite different. Care must be taken to avoid confusion between queue cutter signals used in conjunction with a grade crossing and queue jumping signals used with transit operations.</p>
</div>

<span id="8d13" class="anchor"></span>

## 8D.13 Warning Beacons or LED-Enhanced Warning Signs at Grade Crossings

<div class="secmeta">Section 8D.13 &middot; manual page 1558 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/8D.13/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Warning Beacons (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/4s/#4s03">Section 4S.03</a>) or LEDs within the legend, symbol, or border of the sign (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2a/#2a12">Section 2A.12</a>) may be used to supplement warning signs installed at or on an approach to a grade crossing if additional emphasis is desired for the warning sign. The Warning Beacon or LED-enhanced sign may operate continuously or be activated upon the approach or presence of rail traffic.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Most of the warning signs that are used at or on an approach to a grade crossing warn of physical conditions that exist at the grade crossing regardless of whether rail traffic is approaching or occupying the grade crossing. In these cases, a Warning Beacon or LED-enhanced sign would typically be operated continuously to enhance the conspicuity of the sign.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Some warning signs, such as a BE PREPARED TO STOP (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w3-4/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W3-4.svg">W3-4</a>) sign (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2c/#2c35">Section 2C.35</a>), if used in advance of a grade crossing and supplemented with a WHEN FLASHING (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w16-13p/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W16-13P.svg">W16-13P</a>) plaque, provide information that is typically not applicable except when rail traffic is approaching or occupying the grade crossing. Likewise, a special word message sign (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2a/#2a04">Section 2A.04</a>) with a legend such as TRAIN WHEN FLASHING provides notice of a condition that only exists when rail traffic is approaching or occupying the grade crossing. These signs would not typically be operated continuously, but instead only when the condition is present.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> If a Warning Beacon or LEDs within the legend, symbol, or border of the sign is activated by the approach or presence of rail traffic in conjunction with a warning sign that includes the legend WHEN FLASHING either on the sign itself or on a supplemental plaque, the activation of the Warning Beacon or LEDs shall be accomplished by a supervised preemption interconnection using fail-safe design principles (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/8d/#8d09">Section 8D.09</a>) between the control circuits of the grade crossing warning system and the Warning Beacon or LED-enhanced sign.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> In the event of a system failure, the normal fault state using a fail-safe interconnection for a Warning Beacon or LED-enhanced sign that is activated by the approach or presence of rail traffic at the grade crossing would be for the Warning Beacon or LEDs to operate when no rail traffic is present.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> A Warning Beacon or LED-enhanced sign that is activated by the approach or presence of rail traffic at the grade crossing may continue to operate for a period of time following the passage of the rail traffic to permit the standing queue to dissipate.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> If a Warning Beacon or LED-enhanced sign is activated by the approach or presence of rail traffic at the grade crossing, the Warning Beacon or LED-enhanced sign should begin operating prior to the activation of the flashing-light signals at the grade crossing based upon the typical travel time from the location of the Warning Beacon or LED- enhanced sign to the stop line for the grade crossing.</p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> If a Warning Beacon or LED-enhanced sign that is activated by the approach or presence of rail traffic at the grade crossing is operated by commercial AC power, a back-up power system should be provided.</p>
</div>

<span id="8d14" class="anchor"></span>

## 8D.14 Traffic Control Signals at or Near Highway-LRT Grade Crossings

<div class="secmeta">Section 8D.14 &middot; manual page 1559 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/8D.14/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> There are two types of traffic control signals for controlling vehicular and LRT movements at interfaces of the two modes. The first is the standard traffic control signal described in Part 4, which is the focus of this Section. The other type of signal is referred to as an LRT signal and is discussed in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/8d/#8d15">Section 8D.15</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> The provisions of Part 4 and Sections 8D.08 through 8D.12 relating to traffic control signal design, installation, and operation, including interconnection with nearby automatic gates or flashing-light signals, shall be applicable as appropriate where traffic control signals are used at highway-LRT grade crossings.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> If traffic control signals are in operation at an LRT grade crossing that is used by pedestrians, bicyclists, and/or other non-motorized road users, an audible device such as a bell <span class="ca">Accessible Pedestrian Signal</span> shall also be provided and shall be operated in conjunction with the traffic control signals.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> If the highway traffic signal has emergency-vehicle preemption capability, it should be coordinated with LRT operation.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Where LRT operates in a wide median, motor vehicles crossing the tracks and being controlled by both near and far side traffic signal faces should receive a protected left-turn phase from the far side signal face to clear motor vehicles from the crossing when LRT traffic is approaching the crossing.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Signal indications that permit the movement of motor vehicles, pedestrians, and bicyclists and do not conflict with LRT movements may be provided during LRT phases.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> A traffic control signal may be installed in addition to Exit Gate systems and automatic gates at a highway-LRT grade crossing if the crossing occurs within a highway-highway intersection and if the installation of the traffic control signal can be justified based on the warrants described in Chapter 4C.</p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Where a highway-LRT grade crossing is at a location other than an intersection and LRT operating speeds are less than 25 mph, traffic control signals may be used in lieu of flashing-light signals.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Typical circumstances for using traffic control signals might include: A.Geometric conditions preclude the installation of highway-LRT grade crossing warning devices, B.LRT vehicles share the same roadway with road users, or C.Traffic control signals already exist.</p>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/4f/#4f18">Section 4F.18</a> contains information regarding traffic control signals at or near highway-LRT grade crossings that are not equipped with highway-LRT grade crossing warning devices.</p>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/4c/#4c10">Section 4C.10</a> describes the Intersection Near a Grade Crossing signal warrant that is intended for use at a location where the proximity to the intersection of a grade crossing on an intersection approach controlled by a STOP or YIELD sign is the principal reason to consider installing a traffic control signal.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> When a highway-LRT grade crossing exists within a signalized intersection, consideration should be given to providing separate turn signal faces (see definition in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1c/#1c02">Section 1C.02</a>) for the movements crossing the tracks.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> Separate turn signal faces that are provided for turn movements toward the crossing shall display a steady red indication during the approach and/or passage of LRT traffic.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/8d/#8d10">Section 8D.10</a> contains information regarding the prohibition of turning movements toward the crossing during preemption.</p>
</div>

<span id="8d15" class="anchor"></span>

## 8D.15 Use of LRT Signals for Control of LRT Vehicles at Highway-LRT Grade Crossings

<div class="secmeta">Section 8D.15 &middot; manual page 1560 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/8D.15/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> LRT signal indications may be used at grade crossings and at intersections in mixed-use alignments in conjunction with standard traffic control signals where special LRT signal phases are used to accommodate turning LRT vehicles or where additional LRT clearance time is desirable.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> LRT signal indications may be used at intersections where special signal phases are used for bus movements.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> If the LRT crossing control is separate from the intersection control, the two shall be interconnected. The LRT signal phase shall not be terminated until after the LRT vehicle has cleared the crossing or intersection.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> If a separate set of standard traffic control signal indications (red, yellow, and green circular and arrow indications) is used to control LRT movements, the indications shall be positioned so they are not visible to motorists, pedestrians, and bicyclists (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/4d/#4d06">Section 4D.06</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> If a signal face used to control LRT movements cannot be positioned where the indications are not visible to road users, the LRT signal indications shown in <a href="#fig-8d-3">Figure 8D-3</a> <span class="ca">or 8D-3(CA) </span>should be used.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05a Diverging route signal aspects should be evaluated and recommended by a Diagnostic Team.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> If special LRT signal indications such as those shown in <a href="#fig-8d-3">Figure 8D-3</a> <span class="ca">or 8D-3(CA)</span>are used, the color of the signal indications shall be white.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> If used, individual LRT signal sections may be displayed to form clustered signal faces or multiple LRT signal indications may be displayed in an individual housing.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07a The diagonal LRT signal indication may be rotated 45 degrees either clockwise or counter-clockwise.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> LRT signal faces should be located at least 3 feet from the nearest highway traffic signal face for the same approach measured either horizontally perpendicular to the approach between the centers of the signal faces or vertically from the center of the lowest signal indication of the top signal face to the center of the highest signal indication of the bottom signal face.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/4f/#4f18">Section 4F.18</a> contains information about the use of the LRT signal indications shown in <a href="#fig-8d-3">Figure 8D-3</a> for the control of exclusive bus movements at “queue jumper lanes” and for the control of exclusive bus rapid transit movements on mixed-use alignments.</p>
</div>
