## 9E.01 Bicycle Lanes (Class II Bikeways (Bike Lane or Buffer-Separated Bicycle Lane))

<div class="secmeta">Section 9E.01 &middot; manual page 1636 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/9E.01/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Pavement markings designate that portion of the roadway for preferential use by bicyclists. Markings inform all road users of the restricted nature of the bicycle lane.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Longitudinal pavement markings and bicycle lane symbol or word markings (see <a href="#fig-9e-1">Figure 9E-1</a>) shall be used to define bicycle lanes.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02a</span> <span class="ca">The BIKE LANE pavement markings shall be placed on the far side of each intersection.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="mfig-inline single"><figure class="mfig"><span id="fig-9e-1"></span><a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ca-mutcd/p1652.webp"><img loading="lazy" src="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ca-mutcd/p1652.webp" alt="Figure 9E-1. Word, Symbol, and Arrow Pavement Markings for Bicycle Lanes Normal white line 72 inches 72 inches 72 inches"></a><figcaption>Figure 9E-1. Word, Symbol, and Arrow Pavement Markings for Bicycle Lanes Normal white line 72 inches 72 inches 72 inches <span class="src-page">p 1652</span></figcaption></figure></div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">02b</span> <span class="ca">The BIKE LANE pavement markings may also be placed at other locations as desired.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">02c</span> <span class="ca">Examples of BIKE LANE pavement markings are shown in various figures in this chapter.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">02d</span> <span class="ca">Optional word, arrow and symbol markings with details as shown in <a href="#fig-9e-1">Figure 9E-1</a> may be used.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> The first symbol, or word,<span class="ca"> and/or green-colored pavement</span> marking in a bicycle lane should be placed at the beginning of the bicycle lane and downstream symbol or word markings should be placed after major intersections. Additional symbol or word markings should be placed at periodic intervals along the bicycle lane based on engineering judgment. <span class="ca">Refer to <a href="#fig-9e-109ca">Figure 9E-109(CA)</a>.</span></p>
<p><span class="pn">03a</span> <span class="ca">Bicycle lane markings on Class II Bikeways (Bike Lane or Buffer-Separated Bicycle Lane) should be placed a constant distance  from the marked lane line or centerline, as appropriate. Bike lanes with parking permitted should not be directed toward the curb at  intersections or localized areas where parking is prohibited. Such a practice prevents bicyclists from following a straight course.  Where transitions from one type of bike lane to another are necessary, smooth tapers should be provided.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">03b</span> <span class="ca">Class II Bikeways (Bike Lane or</span><span class="ca">Buffer-Separated Bicycle Lane) require standard signing and pavement markings as shown in  <a href="#fig-3a-112ca">Figure 3A-112(CA)</a>. Bike lane lines are not typically extended through intersections. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">03c</span> <span class="ca">Where right turns are not permitted, the solid bike lane stripe should extend to the edge of the intersection, and begin again on  the far side. Where there is no right turn only lane, but right turns are permitted, the solid stripe should terminate 50 feet to 200 feet  prior to the intersection.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">03d</span> <span class="ca">A dashed line, as shown in <a href="#fig-3a-112ca">Figure 3A-112(CA)</a>, may be carried to, or near, the intersection. Where city blocks are short (less  than 400 feet), the length of dashed stripe may be 50 feet.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">03e</span> <span class="ca">Where blocks are longer or vehicle speeds are high (greater than 35 mph), the length of dashed stripe should be increased to  200 feet.</span></p>
<p><span class="pn">03f</span> <span class="ca">Markings for a bike lane where vehicle parking is prohibited or permitted are shown in Figures 9E-2, 9E-2(CA), 9E-6(CA),  9E-101(CA), 9E-103(CA), and 9E-106(CA).</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">03g</span> <span class="ca">Where motorist right turns are permitted, the solid bike lane shall either be dropped entirely, or dashed beginning at a  point between 50 feet and 200 feet in advance of the intersection.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">03h</span> <span class="ca">In areas where parking stalls are not necessary (because parking is light), a 4 to 6 inch wide solid white stripe on local agency  roadways and a 6 inch wide stripe on the state highways may be painted to fully delineate the bike lane. This may be advisable  where there is concern that motorists may misconstrue the bike lane to be a traffic lane.</span></p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> An arrow marking (see <a href="#fig-9e-1">Figure 9E-1</a>) may be used in conjunction with the bicycle lane symbol or word marking, placed downstream from the symbol or word marking.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Where the bicycle lane symbols or word markings are used, Bicycle Lane signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/9b/#9b04">Section 9B.04</a>) may also be used, but not necessarily adjacent to every set of pavement markings in order to avoid overuse of the signs.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3h/#3h06">Section 3H.06</a> contains information on green-colored pavement for use in bicycle lanes. <span class="ca">Refer to <a href="#fig-9e-109ca">Figure 9E-109(CA)</a>.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">06a</span> <span class="ca">Raised barriers (e.g., raised traffic bars and asphalt concrete dikes) or raised pavement markers shall not be used to  delineate bike lanes on Class II Bikeways (Bike Lane or Buffer-Separated Bicycle Lane).</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">06b</span> <span class="ca">Raised barriers prevent motorists from merging into bike lanes before making right turns, as required by the CVC, and restrict  the movement of bicyclists desiring to enter or exit bike lanes.</span></p>
<p><span class="pn">06c</span> <span class="ca">They also impede routine maintenance. Raised pavement markers increase the difficulty for bicyclists when entering or exiting  bike lanes, and discourage motorists from merging into bike lanes before making right turns.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">06d</span> <span class="ca">Physical barriers or other vertical elements may be used to convert a Class II Bikeway (Bike Lane or Buffer-Separated Bicycle  Lane) to Class I Bikeway (Bike Path) or Class IV Bikeway (Cycle Track, Separated Bikeway or Separated Bicycle Lane).</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> The bicycle symbol or BIKE LANE pavement word marking and the pavement marking arrow shall not be used in a shoulder.</p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> A portion of the roadway shall not be established as both a shoulder and a bicycle lane.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">08a</span> <span class="ca">The BIKE LANE may count towards the shoulder width per Caltrans Highway Design Manual Index 301.2.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Where a shoulder is provided or is of sufficient width to meet the expectation of a highway user in that it can function as a space for emergency, enforcement, or maintenance activities, or avoidance or recovery maneuvers, <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/9b/#9b16">Section 9B.16</a> contains information regarding the Bicycles Use Shoulder Only sign that can be used to denote locations on a freeway or expressway where bicycles are permitted on an available and usable shoulder.</p>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> Examples of pavement markings for bicycle lanes on a two-way street are shown in <a href="#fig-9e-2">Figure 9E-2</a> <span class="ca">and 9E-2(CA)</span>.</p>
</div>

## 9E.02 Bicycle Lanes at Intersection Approaches (Class II Bikeways (Bike Lane or Buffer-Separated Bicycle Lane))

<div class="secmeta">Section 9E.02 &middot; manual page 1637 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/9E.02/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Except as provided in Paragraph 2 of this Section, a through bicycle lane shall not be positioned to the right of a right turn only lane or to the left of a left turn only lane.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> A through bicycle lane may be positioned to the right of a right turn only lane or to the left of a left turn only lane provided that the bicycle lane is controlled by a traffic signal that displays bicycle signal indications (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/4h/">Chapter 4H</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">02a</span> <span class="ca">A bicycle lane for travel in the same direction as the general purpose lanes may be placed on the left hand side of the general  purpose lanes.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Unless controlled by a bicycle signal indication, a bicyclist continuing straight through an intersection from the right of a right turn only lane or from the left of a left turn only lane would be inconsistent with normal traffic behavior and would violate the expectations of right-turning or left-turning motorists.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">03a</span> <span class="ca">When a bike lane approaches an intersection with right- or left-turn only lanes, <a href="#fig-9e-3">Figure 9E-3</a> and <a href="#fig-9e-3ca">Figure 9E-3(CA)</a> may be used</span><span class="ca">.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="mfig-inline multi"><figure class="mfig"><span id="fig-9e-3"></span><a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ca-mutcd/p1656.webp"><img loading="lazy" src="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ca-mutcd/p1656.webp" alt="Figure 9E-3. Examples of Bicycle Lane Markings on an Approach to an Intersection"></a><figcaption>Figure 9E-3. Examples of Bicycle Lane Markings on an Approach to an Intersection <span class="src-page">p 1656</span></figcaption></figure><figure class="mfig"><span id="fig-9e-3ca"></span><a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ca-mutcd/p1659.webp"><img loading="lazy" src="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ca-mutcd/p1659.webp" alt="Figure 9E-3(CA). Example of Bicycle Lane Markings on an Approach to an Intersection"></a><figcaption>Figure 9E-3(CA). Example of Bicycle Lane Markings on an Approach to an Intersection <span class="src-page">p 1659</span></figcaption></figure></div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">03b</span> <span class="ca">Where through motor vehicle lanes approaching an intersection become mandatory turn lanes, adjacent bike lanes should be  delineated using Figures 9E-3(CA).</span></p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> When the right (left) through lane is dropped to become a mandatory right-turn (left-turn) lane, the bicycle lane markings should stop at least 100 feet before the beginning of the right-turn (left-turn) lane. Through bicycle lane markings should resume to the left (right) of the mandatory right-turn (left-turn) lane.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Except as provided in Paragraph 2 of this Section, an optional through-right (through-left) turn lane next to a mandatory right-turn (left-turn) lane should not be used where there is a through bicycle lane.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05a</span> <span class="ca">A dashed line across the right-turn-only lane should not be used on extremely long lanes, or where there are double right-turn- only lanes. For these types of intersections, all striping should be dropped to permit judgment by the bicyclists to prevail.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> A bicycle lane located on an intersection approach between general-purpose lanes for motor vehicle movements shall be marked with at least one bicycle symbol and at least one arrow pavement marking as provided in Paragraph 4 of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/9e/#9e01">Section 9E.01</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">06a</span> <span class="ca">Refer to FHWA’s List of Known Errors for error in Paragraph 6 text. Refer to <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1a/#1a04">Section 1A.04</a> for more details.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> A bicycle lane shall not be marked within a general-purpose lane, either with dotted or any other line markings.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Where there is insufficient width in the roadway to include both a bicycle lane and a general-purpose turn lane, bicycle travel may be accommodated within the turn lane or general-purpose lane using shared-lane markings.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Where a general-purpose turn lane is controlled by a traffic control signal, through bicycle movements shall not be accommodated in the turn lane unless the turning movement is always permitted to proceed simultaneously with the adjacent through movement.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">09a</span> <span class="ca">A Bicycle Crossing (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w11-1/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W11-1.svg">W11-1</a>) sign may be used to warn road users of the potential for bicyclists crossing their path. Refer to  <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/9b/#9b18">Section 9B.18</a>.</span></p>
<p><span class="pn">09b</span> <span class="ca">When a bike lane approaches ramp intersection that intersects the local facility at or close to 90° (typical of a compact or spread  diamond configuration), then <a href="#fig-9e-3">Figure 9E-3</a> and <a href="#fig-9e-3ca">Figure 9E-3(CA)</a> may be</span><span class="ca">used. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">09c</span> <span class="ca">However, when a bike lane approaches one or more ramp intersections that intersect the local facility at various angles other  than 90° (typically high-speed, skewed ramps), <a href="#fig-9e-102ca">Figure 9E-102(CA)</a> should be used.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> Examples of bicycle lane markings on approaches to intersections are shown in Figures 9E-3, <span class="ca">9E-3(CA)</span>, 9E-4, and 9E-9.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> The longitudinal line defining a bicycle lane should be dotted on approaches to intersections where turning vehicles are permitted to cross the path of through-moving bicycles (see <a href="#fig-9d-7">Figure 9D-7</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">11a</span> <span class="ca">If used, the space for bicycle use shall be delineated by <a class="sref dref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/markings/line-types/#detail-39" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/linetypes/detail-39.svg">Detail 39</a> on the right of the through lane and <a class="sref dref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/markings/line-types/#detail-38a" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/linetypes/detail-38a.svg">Detail 38A</a> on the  left of the right-turn-only lane. Refer to <a href="#fig-3a-112ca">Figure 3A-112(CA)</a>.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> Buffer-separated and separated bicycle lanes require additional considerations at intersections, including sight distances for bicycles and other road users, user expectations, and intersection geometry.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> A buffer-separated or separated bicycle lane may be shifted closer to, or farther away from the adjacent general- purpose lane depending upon site-specific conditions (see Drawings D and E in <a href="#fig-9e-7">Figure 9E-7</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="mfig-inline single"><figure class="mfig"><span id="fig-9e-7"></span><a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ca-mutcd/p1671.webp"><img loading="lazy" src="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ca-mutcd/p1671.webp" alt="Figure 9E-7. Examples of Lane Markings for Separated Bicycle Lanes"></a><figcaption>Figure 9E-7. Examples of Lane Markings for Separated Bicycle Lanes <span class="src-page">p 1671</span></figcaption></figure></div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> A buffer-separated or separated bicycle lane shifted away from the adjacent general-purpose lane at an intersection can create space for a motor vehicle to queue between the general-purpose lane and the extension of the bicycle lane. This design can also improve the safety and comfort of bicyclists by reducing the speed of turning motor vehicles, improving sightlines, and creating additional buffer space prior to the conflict point with turning motor vehicles.</p>
<p><span class="pn">15</span> The purpose of shifting a buffer-separated or separated bicycle lane away from the adjacent general- purpose lane is to allow the driver of a turning vehicle to undertake the tasks of turning and scanning for bicycle cross traffic in isolation versus simultaneously. Sufficient sight distance for both drivers and bicyclists is important in this design (see Drawing E in <a href="#fig-9e-7">Figure 9E-7</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">16</span> The purpose of shifting a buffer-separated or separated bicycle lane toward the adjacent general- purpose lane is to improve the visibility of bicyclists to the adjacent traffic and avoid conflicts between turning motor vehicles and bicyclists (see Drawing D in <a href="#fig-9e-7">Figure 9E-7</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">16a</span> <span class="ca">At locations with right-turn-only lanes where bicycles are not prohibited but Class II bicycle facilities do not exist on the approach,  a minimum 4-foot wide space for bicycle use may be provided between the right-turn and through lane, and where the posted speed  is greater than 40 mph the minimum width should be 6 feet.</span></p>
<p><span class="pn">16b</span> <span class="ca">When the width between the right-turn and through lane is greater than 4-feet, a buffer area may be striped adjacent to the 4’  minimum width for bicycle travel, regardless of the posted speed.</span></p>
<p><span class="pn">16c</span> <span class="ca">The buffer may be placed on the left or on the right of the 4’ space for bicycle travel.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">16d</span> <span class="ca">Refer to Caltrans’ Highway Design Manual, Index 403.6 Turning Traffic.</span></p>
<p><span class="pn">16e</span> <span class="ca">Refer to <a href="#fig-9e-3">Figure 9E-3</a> and <a href="#fig-9e-3ca">Figure 9E-3(CA)</a> for details on striping and <a href="#fig-9e-6">Figure 9E-6</a> and <a href="#fig-9e-6ca">Figure 9E-6(CA)</a> for details on buffer area  striping.</span></p>
<p><span class="pn">17</span> Staggering stop lines (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b19">Section 3B.19</a>) so that general-purpose lanes stop further in advance from the intersection than the bicycle lane can improve the visibility of bicyclists for drivers of turning vehicles (see Drawing D in <a href="#fig-9e-7">Figure 9E-7</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="mfig-inline multi"><figure class="mfig"><span id="fig-9e-6"></span><a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ca-mutcd/p1668.webp"><img loading="lazy" src="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ca-mutcd/p1668.webp" alt="Figure 9E-6. Examples of Markings for Buffer-Separated Bicycle Lanes <span class="src-page">p 1669</span></figcaption></figure></div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">18</span> Where a general-purpose mandatory turn lane is provided at an intersection and the approach also includes a separated or buffer-separated bicycle lane, a mixing zone may be established to allow general- purpose turning traffic to share the roadway space with bicyclists (see <a href="#fig-9e-5">Figure 9E-5</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="mfig-inline single"><figure class="mfig"><span id="fig-9e-5"></span><a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ca-mutcd/p1667.webp"><img loading="lazy" src="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ca-mutcd/p1667.webp" alt="Figure 9E-5. Examples of Pavement Markings for Mixing Zones"></a><figcaption>Figure 9E-5. Examples of Pavement Markings for Mixing Zones <span class="src-page">p 1667</span></figcaption></figure></div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">19</span> Mixing zones shall be used only where the bicycle lane is one-way in the same direction of travel as the adjacent general-purpose lane.</p>
<p><span class="pn">20</span> Mixing zones with a yielding area shall have yield markings indicating where general-purpose traffic entering the shared space shall yield to bicyclists.</p>
<p><span class="pn">21</span> Where a mixing zone continues to the intersection itself sharing space between bicyclists and general-purpose turning traffic, shared-lane markings and turn arrows shall be provided in the lane.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">22</span> Mixing zones require bicycles and general traffic to share space, interrupting a buffer-separated or separated bicycle lane where bicycle traffic is otherwise separated from general traffic. The preference is to provide a dedicated bicycle facility for the intersection approach, If that is not possible, the mixing zone needs to indicate that bicyclists and motorists are entering a shared condition.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>

## 9E.03 Extensions of Bicycle Lanes through Intersections (Class II Bikeways (Bike Lane or Buffer- Separated Bicycle Lane))

<div class="secmeta">Section 9E.03 &middot; manual page 1639 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/9E.03/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Extensions of bicycle lanes through intersections can help identify the paths of bicyclists and guide them on movements that could be difficult to discern. Extensions of bicycle lanes through intersections also assist other road users of the intersection to identify where bicyclists are expected to operate and to recognize potentially unexpected conflict points.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> The design, placement, and maintenance of bicycle lane extensions through intersections are important considerations, especially when contiguous to a crosswalk, to avoid potential confusion to pedestrians with vision disabilities.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> The width and color of lane extension markings are discussed in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b11">Section 3B.11</a>.</p>

<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>

<p><span class="pn">07</span> Extensions of bicycle lanes through intersections shall use dotted line patterns.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Separated and buffer-separated bicycle lanes may have alignments that are not as obvious within an intersection as a standard bicycle lane, therefore additional conspicuity is important where these types of bicycle lanes cross intersections.</p>

<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>

The transverse line establishing one side of the crosswalk, or the limit of a high-visibility crosswalk pattern (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3c/#3c05">Section 3C.05</a>) that does not employ a transverse line, shall not be used to demarcate one side of the bicycle lane extension.</p>

## 9E.05 Bicycle Lanes at Circular Intersections (Class II Bikeways (Bike Lane or Buffer-Separated Bicycle Lane))

<div class="secmeta">Section 9E.05 &middot; manual page 1641 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/9E.05/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Bicycle lanes shall not be provided in the circulatory roadway of an unsignalized circular intersection that includes conflicts at entry or exit points (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3d/">Chapter 3D</a>) except as provided in Paragraph 4 of this Section.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>

<p><span class="pn">03</span> If used, bicycle crossings should be a minimum of 20 feet from the edge of the circular roadway.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">03a</span> <span class="ca">Refer to FHWA’s List of Known Errors for error in Paragraph 3 text. Refer to <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1a/#1a04">Section 1A.04</a> for more details.</span></p>

<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Separated bicycle lanes may be used in circular intersections.</p>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Separated bicycle lanes allow bicycles to navigate a circular intersection and its crossing points without merging into traffic and without dismounting and using a crosswalk at the intersection crossing point. This is beneficial at multi- lane and higher-speed circular intersections.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/9e/#9e10">Section 9E.10</a> contains information on using shared-lane markings to facilitate the bicycle movement through a circular intersection.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> The “Guide for the Development of Bicycle Facilities,” 2012 Fourth Edition, American Association of State Highway and Transportation Officials, contains information on designing for bicycles on the sidewalk in lieu of, or in addition to, using shared-lane markings in the circulatory roadway of the intersection.</p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> The FHWA’s informational guide “Improving Intersections for Pedestrians and Bicyclists” contains information on incorporating separated bicycle lanes and other bicycle facilities into circular intersections.</p>

## 9E.06 Buffer-Separated Bicycle Lanes (Class II Bikeway (Bike Lane or Buffer-Separated Bicycle Lane))

<div class="secmeta">Section 9E.06 &middot; manual page 1641 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/9E.06/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">00a</span> <span class="ca">Refer to Sections 1C.02 and SHC § 890.4.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">00b</span> <span class="ca">All topics in this section referencing buffer, buffer area, buffer space, buffer-separated bicycle lane, shall mean to apply  to Class II Bikeway, bike lane, bicycle lane or buffer-separated bicycle lane.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Buffer-separated bicycle lanes provide additional lateral separation between a bicycle lane and a general-purpose lane by a pattern of pavement markings without the presence of vertical elements. Providing a buffer space between a bicycle lane and a general-purpose lane creates more separation between motor vehicles and bicycles, can reduce vehicle encroachment into the bicycle lane, and can increase the comfort of bicyclists.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Providing a buffer space between a bicycle lane and a parking lane can reduce crashes involving bicycles and the opening of vehicle doors from the parking lane.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">02a</span> <span class="ca">A bicycle lane buffer area may be used to separate a bicycle lane from an adjacent general-purpose lane and/or parking lane.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">02b</span> <span class="ca">BIKE LANE (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-17/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-17.svg">R3-17</a>) signs (refer to <a href="#fig-9b-1">Figure 9B-1</a>) should be used to distinguish a buffer-separated bicycle lane from a general- purpose lane.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> If used, and except as provided in Paragraph 5 of this Section, a buffer space shall be marked with a solid white line along both edges of the buffer space where crossing is discouraged.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03a</span> <span class="ca">Directional arrows shall be used in conjunction with the bicycle lane symbol or word marking in buffer-separated  bicycle lanes, placed downstream from the symbol or word marking.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Engineering judgment should be used to establish intermittent breaks or interruptions in the buffer space, such as for driveways, transit stops, or on-street parallel parking lanes, in order to convey access points or an otherwise general legal movement to cross the buffer space (see <a href="#fig-9e-6">Figure 9E-6</a> <span class="ca">and <a href="#fig-9e-6ca">Figure 9E-6(CA)</a></span>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Buffer spaces may be established without specific longitudinal lines if contiguous facilities have longitudinal lines or other pavement markings themselves that, when installed, automatically demarcate the buffer space (see Drawing D in <a href="#fig-9e-6">Figure 9E-6</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Except as provided in Paragraph 7 of this Section, a through buffer-separated bicycle lane shall not be positioned to the right of a mandatory right-turn lane or to the left of a mandatory left-turn lane.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> A buffer-separated bicycle lane may be placed to the right of a mandatory right-turn lane (or to the left of a mandatory left-turn lane) only if a bicycle signal face (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/4h/#4h01">Section 4H.01</a>) is used and the signal phasing and signing eliminates any potential conflicts between the bicycle movement and the turning movement.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">07a</span> <span class="ca">Where vehicles are expected to cross the buffer area at driveways, both lines should be broken. Otherwise, both lines should be  solid.</span></p>
<p><span class="pn">07b</span> <span class="ca">Buffer areas should end on the approach to the intersection of side streets or major commercial driveways as shown in Figure  9E-6 and <a href="#fig-9e-6ca">Figure 9E-6(CA)</a>.</span></p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> The width of the buffer space should be at least 3 times the width of the normal or wide longitudinal line used to mark the buffer space.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Where a buffer space is 2 to 3 feet wide, chevron or diagonal markings (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b25">Section 3B.25</a>) should be applied within the buffer space.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">09a</span> <span class="ca">The buffer area width includes the width of the parallel white lines. Refer to DIB 89-02 for buffer area width requirements.</span></p>
<p><span class="pn">09b</span> <span class="ca">Markings for buffered bicycle lanes are shown in <a href="#fig-9e-6">Figure 9E-6</a> and <a href="#fig-9e-6ca">Figure 9E-6(CA)</a></span><span class="ca">. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> Where a buffer space is less than 2 feet wide, diagonal markings or no markings at all in the buffer space may be applied within the buffer space.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> If used, diagonal markings shall slant away from traffic in the adjacent travel lane for motor- vehicle traffic.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> Where used, the spacing of chevrons or diagonal markings should be 10 feet or greater.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> Chevron and diagonal markings convey that the buffer space is not an additional bicycle lane or other travel lane open to traffic.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> Where a buffer space is more than 3 feet wide, chevron or diagonal markings shall be applied within the buffer space.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">14a</span> <span class="ca">The use of chevron or diagonal markings should be considered in a bicycle lane buffer area and should be based on Section  3B.25 and engineering judgment.</span></p>
<p><span class="pn">14b</span> <span class="ca">If used, interior chevron or diagonal markings should consist of 4 to 6 inch lines on local agency roadways and 6 inch line on  state highways, angled at 45 degrees and striped at intervals of 10 to 40 feet.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">14c</span> <span class="ca">Increased interior chevron or diagonal marking frequency can increase motorist compliance.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">15</span> Lane extension markings should be used to extend a buffer-separated bicycle lane across intersections and driveways.</p>
</div>

## 9E.07 Separated Bicycle Lanes (Class IV Bikeway (Cycle Track or Separated Bikeways))

<div class="secmeta">Section 9E.07 &middot; manual page 1642 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/9E.07/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">00a</span> <span class="ca">Refer to Sections 1C.02, 9E.06 and SHC § 890.4.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">00b</span> <span class="ca">All topics in this section referencing buffer, buffer area, and buffer space for a buffer-separated bicycle lane shall mean  to apply to a Class II Bikeway. All topics in this section referencing buffer, buffer area, and buffer space for a separated  bicycle lane shall mean to apply to a Class IV Bikeway (Cycle Track or Separated Bikeway).</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Separated bicycle lanes provide a physical separation between a general-purpose lane and a bicycle lane through the use of vertical objects or vertical separation between the general-purpose lane and bicycle lane. Providing a physical separation between a bicycle lane and a general-purpose lane can reduce vehicle encroachment into the bicycle lane beyond a marked buffer alone and can in some cases prevent that encroachment altogether.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Physical separation between general-purpose lanes and bicycle lanes introduces additional design considerations over buffer-separated bicycle lanes, including the awareness of a potentially unexpected conflict point for turning motor vehicles and the provision of adequate sight distance for all users at intersections and driveway crossings.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Vertical elements used to provide physical separation between general-purpose lanes and bicycle lanes may include, but are not limited to, tubular markers, raised islands, or parked vehicles.<span class="ca">, grade separation, flexible delineator posts, bikeway  separator posts, inflexible physical barriers, or on-street parking. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">03a</span> <span class="ca">Refer to <a href="#fig-9e-106ca">Figure 9E-106(CA)</a>. Refer to Caltrans’ Design Information Bulletin Number 89 Class IV Bikeway Guidance (Separated  Bikeways/Cycle Tracks) for more information.</span></p>
<p><span class="pn">03b</span> <span class="ca">Refer to FHWA “Separated Bike Lane Planning and Design Guide” for detailed information on planning and design of separated  bike lanes.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="mfig-inline single"><figure class="mfig"><span id="fig-9e-106ca"></span><a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ca-mutcd/p1689.webp"><img loading="lazy" src="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ca-mutcd/p1689.webp" alt="Figure 9E-106(CA). Examples of Markings for Separated Bikeways"></a><figcaption>Figure 9E-106(CA). Examples of Markings for Separated Bikeways <span class="src-page">p 1689</span></figcaption></figure></div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">03c</span> <span class="ca">Vertical elements, vertical objects or vertical separation between the general-purpose lane and bicycle lane shall be  used to define Class IV bikeway (cycle track, separated bikeway or separated bicycle lane).</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">03d</span> <span class="ca">Vertical elements are not traffic control devices in themselves; however, when placed in a position identical to a line of  channelizing devices and marked and/or equipped with appropriate channelization features to provide guidance and warning both  day and night, they function as traffic control devices.</span></p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Where on-street parking is provided adjacent to the buffer area of a separated bicycle lane, pedestrians will need to access those vehicles.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> BIKE LANE (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-17/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-17.svg">R3-17</a>) signs (see <a href="#fig-9b-1">Figure 9B-1</a>) should be used to distinguish a separated bicycle lane from a general- purpose lane.<span class="ca"> Refer to Paragraph 00a and 00b in this section.</span></p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Where an on-street parking lane serves as the separation between a general-purpose lane and a separated bicycle lane, a buffer space should be provided between the parking lane and the bicycle lane to allow for opening doors of parked vehicles. <span class="ca">Refer to Paragraph 00a and 00b in this section.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Separated bicycle lanes may be designed for one-way or two-way bicycle travel. Providing one-way separated bicycle lanes in the same direction as and on the right-hand side of the general-purpose lane, whether on a one-way or two-way roadway, accommodates the expectations of road users and might result in fewer conflict points at intersections or driveway crossings.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Separated bicycle lanes may be provided on one or both sides of a roadway or in a center median.</p>
<p><span class="pn">08a</span> <span class="ca">A through separated bicycle lane may be positioned to the right of a right turn only lane or to the left of a left turn only lane, if  bicycle signals are used. Refer to <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/4h/#4h101ca">Section 4H.101(CA)</a> for optional use of Bicycle Signal Faces.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> The presence of two-way separated bicycle lanes on one side of a roadway or in a center median can introduce additional challenges and conflict points, which can warrant additional design considerations when selecting the design for a separated bicycle lane. These considerations include design requirements for pedestrians who would interact with the separated bicycle lane.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> The edge line and lane line colors used for separated bicycle lanes shall conform to the requirements in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3a/">Chapter 3A</a> (see <a href="#fig-9e-7">Figure 9E-7</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">10a</span> <span class="ca">Where separated bikeways (cycle track or separated bicycle lane) are designed for two-way travel, a solid yellow line  shall be used to separate the two directions of travel where passing is not permitted. A broken yellow line shall be used  where passing is permitted (Refer to <a href="#fig-9e-106ca">Figure 9E-106(CA)</a>; refer to <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/9e/#9e13">Section 9E.13</a> for marking patterns).</span></p>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> Directional arrows shall be used in conjunction with the bicycle lane symbol or word marking in separated bicycle lanes, placed downstream from the symbol or word marking. <span class="ca">Refer to Paragraph 00a and 00b in this section.</span></p>
<p><span class="pn">11a</span> <span class="ca">The Bike Symbol pavement markings (<a href="#fig-9e-1">Figure 9E-1</a>) shall be placed on the far side of each intersection.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">11b</span> <span class="ca">The dashed bike lane marking, with or without green-colored pavement between left and right dash may be placed through an  intersection.</span></p>
<p><span class="pn">11c</span> <span class="ca">The DO NOT ENTER (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r5-1/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R5-1.svg">R5-1</a>) sign with the supplemental EXCEPT BICYCLES (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-7bp/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-7bP.svg">R3-7bP</a>) regulatory plaque may be used on  separated bikeways (cycle tracks or separated bicycle lanes) to reduce the likelihood of accidental entrance by motor vehicles.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> Turns on red shall be prohibited across separated bicycle lanes while bicyclists are allowed to proceed through the intersection.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> Additional information on signals for bicycle facilities is found in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/4h/">Chapter 4H</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> The buffer space for a separated bicycle lane shall be marked with solid longitudinal lines. <span class="ca">Refer to Paragraph  00a and 00b in this section. </span></p>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>

<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">15</span> A marked buffer space that is 2 feet or wider for a separated bicycle lane, including those buffer spaces where tubular markers are provided, shall use chevron or diagonal markings within the buffer, unless physical separation is provided that occupies the majority of the buffer space, such as raised islands or other physical dividers, or such as where an on-street parking lane occupies the majority of the buffer space. <span class="ca">Refer to Paragraph  00a and 00b in this section.</span></p>
<p><span class="pn">15a</span> <span class="ca">If accessible parking or loading zones are provided on a roadway alongside a separated bikeway (cycle track or  separated bicycle lane), then unobstructed access by bicycles shall be maintained.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>

## 9E.08 Counter-Flow Bicycle Lanes (Class II Bikeway (Bike Lane or Buffer-Separated Bicycle Lane) or Class IV

<div class="secmeta">Section 9E.08 &middot; manual page 1645 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/9E.08/">short link</a></div>

<p><span class="ca">Bikeway (Cycle Track, Separated Bikeway or Separated Bicycle Lane))</span></p>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Counter-flow bicycle lanes are one-directional and provide a lawful path of travel for bicycles in the opposite direction from general traffic on a roadway that allows general traffic to travel in only one direction.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Counter-flow bicycle lanes establish two-way traffic on a roadway. <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/9b/#9b21">Section 9B.21</a> contains information on the Left Turn Yield to Bicycles (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r10-12b/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R10-12b.svg">R10-12b</a>) sign used with traffic signals and counter-flow bicycle lanes.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02a</span> <span class="ca">Counter-flow bicycle lanes can be provided as a Class II (bike lane, bicycle lane or buffer-separated bicycle lane) or as a Class  IV Bikeway (cycle track, separated bikeway or separated bicycle lane), depending upon the physical separation provided between a  general-purpose lane and a bicycle lane. If the physical separation is provided by a pattern of pavement markings without the  presence of vertical elements, then it is a Class II Bikeway and if the physical separation is provided by vertical objects or vertical  separation, then it is a Class IV Bikeway. Refer to Sections 1C.02, 9E.06, 9E.07 and SHC § 890.4.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Where used, a counter-flow bicycle lane should be marked such that bicycles in the counter-flow lane travel on their right-hand side of the road in accordance with normal rules of the road, with opposing traffic on the left.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Counter-flow bicycle lanes located at the edge of the roadway shall use double yellow center line pavement markings (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b01">Section 3B.01</a>), a painted median island, a raised median island (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3j/">Chapter 3J</a>), or some form of physical separation where the speed limit is 30 mph or less.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> For speed limits 35 mph or greater, a buffer per <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b25">Section 3B.25</a>, a painted or raised median island, or some form of physical separation shall be used to separate a counter-flow bicycle lane from the adjacent travel lane.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Lane extension markings should be used where counter-flow bicycle movements cross intersections.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Counter-flow bicycle lanes should not be used between a general-purpose lane and an on-street parallel parking lane for motor vehicles.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Counter-flow bicycle lanes located between a general-purpose lane and an on-street parallel parking lane for motor vehicles can limit visibility of bicycles for vehicles exiting the parking lane, potentially impacting the safety of bicyclists. Locating counter-flow bicycle lanes at the edge of the roadway can reduce conflicts for bicycles.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Where signs are provided to regulate turns from streets or driveways that intersect with a roadway that has a counter-flow bicycle lane, ONE WAY signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2b/#2b49">Section 2B.49</a>) shall not be used. Movement Prohibition signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2b/#2b26">Section 2B.26</a>) with supplemental EXCEPT BICYCLES (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-7bp/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-7bP.svg">R3-7bP</a>) regulatory plaque(s) shall be used (see <a href="#fig-9e-8">Figure 9E-8</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> If a DO NOT ENTER (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r5-1/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R5-1.svg">R5-1</a>) sign(s)<span class="ca">package</span> is used at egress points for motor vehicle traffic, the EXCEPT BICYCLES (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-7bp/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-7bP.svg">R3-7bP</a>) regulatory plaque(s) shall be placed under the DO NOT ENTER sign (see <a href="#fig-9e-8">Figure 9E-8</a>) where a counter-flow bicycle lane is used.</p>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> Where intersection traffic controls are provided (such as STOP or YIELD signs or traffic signals), appropriate devices shall be provided and oriented toward bicyclists in the counter-flow lane.</p>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> At signalized locations, appropriate bicycle signalization (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/9f/">Chapter 9F</a>) shall be provided and oriented toward bicyclists in the counter-flow lane, including a method for counter-flow bicycles to actuate the green phase for the counter-flow movement.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> Higher levels of traffic control or additional signalization, signing, and/or pavement marking treatments can be helpful for intersecting traffic where the counter-flow bicycle movement is unexpected.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> A Bicycle Cross Traffic warning plaque (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/9c/#9c06">Section 9C.06</a>) should be used below a STOP sign on the crossroad at intersections where a counter-flow bicycle lane is provided on the primary street.</p>
</div>

Refer to <a href="#fig-9e-105ca">Figure 9E-105(CA)</a>.</span></p>

When a shared lane is not wide enough to enable passing with adequate clearance, the purpose of the marking is to indicate a  bicyclist line of travel that deters passing within the lane. Spacing</span></p>

<p><span class="pn">10</span> The first shared-lane marking downstream from an intersection should be placed no more than 50 feet from the intersection.</p>

## 9E.11 Two-Stage Bicycle Turn Boxes

In an area between the through bicycle movement and the parallel pedestrian crossing movement if no crosswalk is established (see Drawing B in <a href="#fig-9e-10">Figure 9E-10</a>), C. On the innermost side of the bicycle facility provided that the two-stage turn box is located in a portion of the intersection where parallel or motor vehicle traffic does not travel, such as projections of islands or parking lanes (see Drawing C in <a href="#fig-9e-10">Figure 9E-10</a>), or D. ## 9E.12 Bicycle Box

<div class="secmeta">Section 9E.12 &middot; manual page 1648 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/9E.12/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> A bicycle box (see <a href="#fig-9e-12">Figure 9E-12</a>) may be used to increase the visibility of stopped bicycles on the approach to a signalized intersection during the portion of the signal cycle when a red signal indication is being displayed to motor vehicles in the approach lane(s) that is behind the box.</p>
</div>
<div class="mfig-inline single"><figure class="mfig"><span id="fig-9e-12"></span><a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ca-mutcd/p1677.webp"><img loading="lazy" src="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ca-mutcd/p1677.webp" alt="Figure 9E-12. Examples of Intersection Bicycle Boxes"></a><figcaption>Figure 9E-12. Examples of Intersection Bicycle Boxes <span class="src-page">p 1677</span></figcaption></figure></div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Providing a bicycle box on a signalized intersection approach where a discernible number of conflicts between vehicles turning across through bicycles in a bicycle lane has been demonstrated during the green interval of a signal should be evaluated based on engineering judgment or study.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Other treatments should be considered for conflicts between turning vehicles and through bicycles such as using leading or exclusive signal phases, or separating turning traffic from through traffic through mandatory turn lanes.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> A bicycle lane should be used on the approach to a bicycle box.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> A bicycle box should not be contiguous with a crosswalk. A stop line on the downstream end of the bicycle box should be used to mark the location where bicycles are required to stop.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> If used, the distance from the upstream edge of the bicycle box that is nearest to the stop line for motor vehicles to the downstream edge of the bicycle box that is nearest the crosswalk or intersection shall be at least 10 feet. At least one bicycle symbol marking (see <a href="#fig-9e-12">Figure 9E-12</a>) shall be used in the bicycle box.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Where an existing stop line for motor vehicles is relocated upstream to install a new bicycle box, the yellow change and red clearance intervals (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/4f/#4f17">Section 4F.17</a>) shall be recalculated and if necessary, reprogrammed to accommodate the length of the bicycle box.</p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Countdown pedestrian signals (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/4i/#4i04">Section 4I.04</a>) for the crosswalk or pedestrian crossing movement that crosses the approach shall accompany bicycle boxes that extend across more than one approach lane for motor vehicles. Countdown pedestrian signals used with bicycle boxes shalldisplay the pedestrian change interval countdown without the need for actuation.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Turns on red shall be prohibited from the lane where a bicycle box is placed.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> Countdown pedestrian signals can inform bicyclists whether there is adequate time remaining to an adjacent lane before the onset of the green signal phase for that approach.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> Countdown pedestrian signals for the crosswalk or pedestrian crossing movement that crosses the approach should accompany single-lane bicycle boxes where it is demonstrated that bicycles arrive at the intersection at or near the end of the red signal indication being displayed to traffic in the approach lane(s) that is behind the box.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> Green-colored pavement may be used in a bicycle box.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> If used, green-colored pavement shall be used in the full limits of the bicycle box.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/9b/#9b02">Section 9B.02</a> contains information on the EXCEPT BICYCLES (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-7bp/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-7bP.svg">R3-7bP</a>) regulatory plaque that can be used below the STOP HERE ON RED (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r10-6/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R10-6.svg">R10-6</a> or <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r10-6a/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R10-6a.svg">R10-6a</a>) sign (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2b/#2b59">Section 2B.59</a>) to exempt bicyclists from the requirement of the advance stop line.</p>
</div>

## 9E.13 Shared-Use Paths (Class I Bikeway (Bike Path))

<div class="secmeta">Section 9E.13 &middot; manual page 1649 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/9E.13/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Where shared-use paths are of sufficient width to designate two minimum width lanes, a solid yellow center line may be used to separate the two directions of travel where passing or traveling to the left of the line is not permitted. A broken yellow center line may be used where passing is permitted (see <a href="#fig-9e-13">Figure 9E-13</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="mfig-inline single"><figure class="mfig"><span id="fig-9e-13"></span><a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ca-mutcd/p1679.webp"><img loading="lazy" src="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ca-mutcd/p1679.webp" alt="Figure 9E-13. Examples of Center Line Markings for Shared-Use Paths"></a><figcaption>Figure 9E-13. Examples of Center Line Markings for Shared-Use Paths <span class="src-page">p 1679</span></figcaption></figure></div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Broken lines used on shared-use paths should have a nominal 3-foot segment with a 9-foot gap.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">02a</span> <span class="ca">A centerline marking is particularly beneficial in the following circumstances:  A. Where there is heavy use;  B. On curves with restricted sight distance; and,  C. Where the path is unlighted and nighttime riding is expected.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> A solid white line may be used on shared-use paths to separate different types of users in the same direction. The <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r9-7/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R9-7.svg">R9-7</a> sign (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/9b/#9b13">Section 9B.13</a>) may be used to supplement the solid white line.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03a</span> <span class="ca">A</span><span class="ca"> solid white line may be used to delineate the traveled way of the bike path from the shoulder if the shoulder is paved with the  same material as the bike path.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">03b</span> <span class="ca">Refer to Caltrans’ Highway Design Manual Index 1003.1 Class I Bikeways (Bike Paths).</span></p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Smaller size pavement word markings and symbols may be used on shared-use paths. Where arrows are needed on shared-use paths, half-size layouts of the arrows may be used (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b20">Section 3B.20</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Where a shared-use path crosses a roadway, crosswalk markings shall be used (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3c/">Chapter 3C</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Where pedestrian and bicycle movements on a shared-use path are separated on the approach to a roadway crossing, parallel bicycle and pedestrian crossing markings may be used as shown in <a href="#fig-9e-14">Figure 9E-14</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="mfig-inline single"><figure class="mfig"><span id="fig-9e-14"></span><a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ca-mutcd/p1680.webp"><img loading="lazy" src="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ca-mutcd/p1680.webp" alt="Figure 9E-14. Example of Pavement Markings for a Shared-Use Path with Mode Separation Green-colored pavement (optional) Legend Direction of travel"></a><figcaption>Figure 9E-14. Example of Pavement Markings for a Shared-Use Path with Mode Separation Green-colored pavement (optional) Legend Direction of travel <span class="src-page">p 1680</span></figcaption></figure></div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> If parallel bicycle and pedestrian crossing markings are used where a shared-use path crosses a roadway, crossing areas for bicycles should use green-colored pavement if the shared-use path crossing has a high volume of either mode. <span class="ca">Barrier Posts on Class I Bikeways (Bike Path)</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Before a decision is made to install barrier posts, consideration needs to be given to the implementation of other remedial  measures, such as Bike Path Exclusion (R44A(CA)) signs (refer to <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/9b/#9b07">Section 9B.07</a>) and/or redesigning the path entry so that motorists  do not confuse it with vehicle access.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> It could be necessary to install barrier posts across the width of the bike path, at entrances to bike paths to prevent motor  vehicles from entering or to slow down the bicyclists when approaching an intersection with vehicular traffic. This can occur, where  the bike paths intersect with vehicular roadways, especially if the width of the bike path at its intersection, could lead to confusion for  drivers on the vehicular roadway that the bike path is a vehicular roadway. The number of barriers posts installed need to be an odd  number, as they will result in even number of passages, to provide equal number of passage ways for each direction. When locating  such installations, care needs to be taken to assure that barriers are well marked and visible to bicyclists, day and night (i.e., install  reflectors or reflectorized tape).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> An envelope around the barriers should be striped as shown in <a href="#fig-9e-17">Figure 9E-17</a>. If sight distance is limited, special advance  warning signs or painted pavement warnings should be provided. Where more than one post is necessary, 5 foot spacing should be  used to permit passage of bicycle-towed trailers, adult tricycles, and to assure adequate room for safe bicycle passage without  dismounting. Barrier post installations should be designed so they are removable to permit entrance by emergency and service  vehicles.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> Generally, barrier configurations that preclude entry by motorcycles present safety and convenience problems for bicyclists.  Such devices are used only where extreme problems are encountered.</p>
</div>

## 9E.14 Bicycle Route Pavement Markings (Class III Bikeway (Bike Route))

<div class="secmeta">Section 9E.14 &middot; manual page 1650 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/9E.14/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Bicycle route pavement markings simulating guide signs for bicycle routes (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/9d/#9d02">Section 9D.02</a> through 9D.07) and route auxiliary plaques (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/9d/#9d08">Section 9D.08</a>) may be used to supplement guide signing to help bicyclists in navigation (see <a href="#fig-9e-15">Figure 9E-15</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Bicycle route pavement markings shall be limited to shared-use paths <span class="ca">(Class I Bikeway)</span>, separated bicycle lanes <span class="ca">(Class II or Class IV Bikeway)</span>, or buffer-separated bicycle lanes <span class="ca">(Class II Bikeway)</span>. Bicycle route pavement markings shall not be used in standard bicycle lanes <span class="ca">(Class II Bikeway) </span>or in shared lanes <span class="ca">(Class III Bikeway)</span>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">02a</span> <span class="ca">Class III Bikeways (Bike Route) are shared routes and do not require pavement markings. In some instances, a 4 to 6 inch wide  white edge stripe on local agency roadways and a 6 inch wide stripe on the state highways, separating the traffic lanes from the  shoulder can be helpful in providing for safer shared use. This practice is particularly applicable on rural highways and on major  arterials in urban areas where there is no vehicle parking.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> A systematic methodology of locating guide signs for bicycle routes adjacent to the bicycle route pavement marking should be used that includes locations where either the sign or the pavement marking can exist alone to avoid overuse of the guide sign or the pavement marking.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> The route marker pavement marking should be elongated.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> The location, size, and materials of the route marker pavement marking should be designed in a manner that will minimize the loss of traction for bicyclists under wet conditions.</p>
</div>

## 9E.17 Raised Devices (Class IV Bikeway (Cycle Track, Separated Bikeway or Separated Bicycle Lane))

<div class="secmeta">Section 9E.17 &middot; manual page 1651 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/9E.17/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3i/">Chapter 3I</a> contains information on using channelizing devices to emphasize pavement marking patterns associated with certain bicycle facilities. A common application is the use of flexible raised devices to create separated bicycle lanes (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/9e/#9e07">Section 9E.07</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Using inflexible raised devices immediately adjacent to the travel path of a bicyclist without a buffer creates a collision potential for bicyclists.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> In accordance with <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3i/">Chapter 3I</a>, channelizing devices may be used to emphasize a pavement marking pattern that establishes a bicycle lane or other bicycle facility provided that the installation of channelizing devices does not prevent motor vehicles from turning when the turn requires the motor vehicle to merge with the bicycle lane or facility as required by law or ordinance.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> If used, channelizing devices for bicycle facilities should be tubular markers (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3i/#3i02">Section 3I.02</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> The selection of a raised device for use with bicycle facilities should consider the collision potential of both the post and the base since the base might still be present in the event the post is struck and missing.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Measures to reduce the likelihood of a road user striking a channelizing device include marking a buffer space, improving lighting, improving retroreflectivity, or the periodic addition of taller vertical elements within runs of shorter elements.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Channelizing devices that are used to emphasize the pavement marking patterns of bicycle facilities shall not incorporate the color green into either the device or its retroreflective element to supplement the presence of green-colored pavement.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">07a</span> <span class="ca">Refer to Sections 3A.03 and 3I.01 for the color of channelizing devices, when they are used in bicycle facilities.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> If used in buffer-separated bicycle lanes, channelizing devices should be placed in the buffer space and at least 1 foot from the longitudinal bicycle lane pavement marking.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Refer to FHWA’s List of Known Errors for error in Paragraph 08 text. Refer to <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1a/#1a04">Section 1A.04</a> for more details.</p>
</div>
