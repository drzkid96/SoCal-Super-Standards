## 3G.01 General


<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Delineators are particularly beneficial at locations where the alignment might be confusing or unexpected, such as at lane-reduction transitions and curves. Delineators are effective guidance devices at night and during adverse weather. An important advantage of delineators in certain locations is that they remain visible when the roadway is wet or covered by snow.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Delineators are considered guidance devices to help road users navigate the roadway alignment, rather than warning devices.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Delineators may be used on long continuous sections of highway or through short stretches where there are changes in horizontal alignment.</p>
</div>

<span id="3g02" class="anchor"></span>

## 3G.02 Design


<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Delineators shall consist of retroreflective devices that are capable of clearly retroreflecting light under normal atmospheric conditions from a distance of 1,000 feet when illuminated by the high beams of standard automobile lights. They shall be mounted on crashworthy (see definition in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1c/#1c02">Section 1C.02</a>) supports.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Retroreflective elements for delineators shall have a minimum vertical and horizontal dimension of 3 inches, or a minimum diameter dimension of 3 inches when circular.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Within a series of delineators along a roadway, delineators for a given direction of travel at a specific location are referred to as single delineators if they have one retroreflective element for that direction, double delineators if they have two identical retroreflective elements for that direction mounted together, or vertically-elongated delineators if they have a single retroreflective element with an elongated vertical dimension to approximate the vertical dimension of two separate single delineators.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> A vertically-elongated delineator of appropriate size may be used in place of a double delineator.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> There are two classes of delineator posts and several types of retroreflectorization as shown in <a href="#fig-3g-101ca">Figure 3G-101(CA)</a>.</p>
</div>

## 3G.03 Application


<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> The color of delineators shall comply with the color of edge lines stipulated in Sections 3A.03 and 3B.09.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> A series of single delineators shall be provided on the right-hand side of freeways and expressways and on at least one side of interchange ramps, except when either Condition A or Condition B is met, as follows: A.On tangent sections of freeways and expressways when both of the following conditions are met: 1. Raised pavement markers are used continuously on lane lines throughout all curves and on all tangents to supplement pavement markings, and 2. Roadside delineators are used to lead into all curves, or B.On sections of roadways where continuous lighting is in operation between interchanges.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Delineators may be provided on other classes of roads.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> A series of single delineators may be provided on the left-hand side of roadways.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Chevron Alignment (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w1-8/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W1-8.svg">W1-8</a>) signs may be used instead of or in addition to standard delineators, as provided in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2c/#2c08">Section 2C.08</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Delineators on the left-hand side of a two-way roadway shall be white (see <a href="#fig-3g-1">Figure 3G-1</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> A series of single delineators should be provided on the outside of curves on interchange ramps.</p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Where median crossovers are provided for official or emergency use on divided highways and where these crossovers are to be marked with pavement markings, a double yellow delineator should be placed on the left-hand side of the through roadway on the far side of the crossover for each roadway.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Double or vertically-elongated delineators should be installed at approximately 100-foot intervals along acceleration and deceleration lanes.</p>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> A series of delineators should be used wherever guardrail or other longitudinal barriers are present along a roadway or ramp.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> Red delineators may be used on the reverse side of any delineator where it would be viewed by a road user traveling in the wrong direction on that particular ramp or roadway.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> Except as provided in Paragraph 13 of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b12">Section 3B.12</a>, delineators of the appropriate color should be used to indicate a lane-reduction transition where either an outside or inside lane merges into an adjacent lane.</p>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> When used for lane-reduction transitions, the delineators should be installed adjacent to the lane or lanes reduced for the full length of the transition and should be so placed and spaced to show the reduction (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b12">Section 3B.12</a> and <a href="#fig-3b-14">Figure 3B-14</a> <span class="ca">3B-14(CA)</span>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> On a highway with continuous delineation on either or both sides, delineators should be carried through transitions.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">15</span> When used on a truck escape ramp, delineators shall be red.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">16</span> Red delineators should be placed on both sides of truck escape ramps.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">17</span> Where delineation is required within a paved area, surface mounted channelizers may be used. Refer to <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3i/#3i01">Section 3I.01</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">18</span> Examples of the use of delineators are shown in <a href="#fig-3g-101ca">Figure 3G-101(CA)</a>. Color exceptions are shown in <a href="#fig-3g-103ca">Figure 3G-103(CA)</a> and 3G- 104(CA).</p>
<p><span class="pn">19</span> Following are typical delineators and their uses:  A.<span class="ca"> Type E - White Retroreflector (2 Sided). For use on the left or right of 2-lane 2-way streets and highways when it is  desirable to have a reflector on the front and one on the back of the delineator facing the opposite direction of traffic.  B.</span><span class="ca">Type F - White Retroreflector (1 Sided). For use on the right of freeways and expressways. They may also be used on 2- lane 2-way streets and highways when the Type E is not needed.  C.</span><span class="ca">Type G - Yellow Retroreflector (1 Sided). For use on the left of divided highways and 2-lane highway intersections as shown  in <a href="#fig-3g-102ca">Figure 3G-102(CA)</a>.  D.</span><span class="ca">Type J - Red Retroreflector (1 Sided). For placement on both sides of Truck Escape Ramps as shown in Figure 3G- 103(CA).</span></p>
<p><span class="pn">20</span> As shown in <a href="#fig-3g-102ca">Figure 3G-102(CA)</a> and <a href="#fig-3g-104ca">Figure 3G-104(CA)</a>, Type K(CA), Type P(CA), and Type R(CA) Object Markers are used  along with delineators. Refer to <a href="#fig-2c-17ca">Figure 2C-17(CA)</a> for object marker details.</p>
<p><span class="pn">21</span> Bikeway separator posts are a type of delineator used as vertical elements to define Class IV bikeways. Refer to the FHWA  “Separated Bike Lane Planning and Design Guide” for information on the design of bikeway separator posts. Refer to Sections 9E.07  and 9E.17.</p>
</div>

## 3G.04 Placement and Spacing


<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Except as provided in Paragraph 2 of this Section, delineators should be mounted at a height, measured vertically from the bottom of the lowest retroreflective device to the elevation of the near edge of the roadway, of approximately 4 feet.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> When mounted on the face of or on top of guardrails or other longitudinal barriers, delineators may be mounted at a lower elevation than the normal delineator height recommended in Paragraph 1 of this Section.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Delineators should be placed 2 to 8 <span class="ca">6</span> feet outside the outer edge of the shoulder, or if appropriate, in line with the roadside barrier that is 8 feet or less outside the outer edge of the shoulder.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Delineators should be placed at a constant distance from the edge of the roadway, except that where an obstruction intrudes into the space between the pavement edge and the extension of the line of the delineators, the delineators should be transitioned to be in line with or inside the innermost edge of the obstruction. If the obstruction is a guardrail or other longitudinal barrier, the delineators should be transitioned to be just behind, directly above (in line with), or on the innermost edge of the guardrail or longitudinal barrier.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Delineators should not present a vertical or horizontal clearance obstacle for pedestrians.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Delineators should be spaced 200 to 530 feet apart on mainline tangent sections. Delineators should be spaced 100 <span class="ca">to 200 feet</span> apart on ramp tangent sections.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> On a highway with continuous delineation on either or both sides, the spacing between a series of delineators may be closer.</p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> When uniform spacing is interrupted by such features as driveways and intersections, delineators which would ordinarily be located within the features may be relocated in either direction for a distance not exceeding ¼ of the uniform spacing. Delineators still falling within such features may be eliminated.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Delineators may be transitioned in advance of a lane transition or obstruction as a guide for oncoming traffic.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> The spacing of delineators should be adjusted on approaches to and throughout horizontal curves so that several delineators are always simultaneously visible to the road user. The approximate spacing shown in <a href="#fig-3g-1">Table 3G-1</a> should be used.</p>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> The spacing between red delineators that are placed on both sides of a truck escape ramp should not exceed 50 feet for a distance that is sufficient to identify the ramp entrance. The spacing between red delineators that are placed beyond the ramp entrance should be such that adequate guidance is provided based on the length and design of the escape ramp.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> When needed for special conditions, delineators of the appropriate color may be mounted in a closely- spaced manner on the face of or on top of guardrails or other longitudinal barriers to form a continuous or nearly-continuous “ribbon” of delineation.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> Examples of delineator installations are shown in <a href="#fig-3g-1">Figure 3G-1</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> If used on the State Highway System, delineators should be placed as follows:  A.<span class="ca">On the outsides of highway curves of 3000 feet radius or less (including medians in divided highways), freeway exit and  entrance ramps and connectors. Exception to this is where a median barrier is delineated as shown in the Median Barrier  Delineation Detail in <a href="#fig-3g-105ca">Figure 3G-105(CA)</a>. Delineator spacing on curves is shown in <a href="#fig-3g-1">Figure 3G-1</a> and <a href="#fig-3g-1">Table 3G-1</a>.  B.</span><span class="ca">On the right of tangent sections of freeway entrance and exit ramps, collector roads, freeway connectors and lane reduction  transition sections at 200 feet spacing.  C.</span><span class="ca">On embankments higher than 10 feet and with side slopes steeper than 4:1. The spacing of tangent sections is  approximately 525 feet. For spacing on curves, refer to <a href="#fig-3g-1">Figure 3G-1</a> and <a href="#fig-3g-1">Table 3G-1</a>.  D.</span><span class="ca">On approaches to narrow bridges as shown in <a href="#fig-3g-104ca">Figure 3G-104(CA)</a>.  E.</span><span class="ca">On tangent sections of rural State highways where there are no reflective pavement markers, such as in snow areas.  Delineator spacing is approximately 525 feet.  F.</span><span class="ca">On all new guardrail or bridge rail installations, or when maintenance is required on existing guardrail or bridge rail, within 12  feet of the edge of traveled way and curves of 3000 feet radius or less. The spacing on tangent sections is approximately  525 feet. For spacing on curves, refer to <a href="#fig-3g-1">Figure 3G-1</a> and <a href="#fig-3g-1">Table 3G-1</a>. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="mfig-inline single"><figure class="mfig"><span id="fig-3g-105ca"></span><a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ca-mutcd/p1004.webp"><img loading="lazy" src="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ca-mutcd/p1004.webp" alt="Figure 3G-105(CA). Examples of Median Barrier Delineation NOT TO SCALE Typical Median Barrier Marker METAL BEAM BARRIER CONCRETE BARRIER <span class="src-page">p 1004</span></figcaption></figure></div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">15</span> Delineators may also be placed as follows:  A.<span class="ca">At intersections, road approaches, and median openings, as shown in <a href="#fig-3g-102ca">Figure 3G-102(CA)</a>.  B.</span><span class="ca">On sections of highway with non-standard shoulder width. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">16</span> If the exit gore at an interchange is not illuminated or is partially illuminated, delineators may be placed as shown in Figure 3G- 102(CA) per the following details:  A.<span class="ca">Type F - White Retroreflectors (1 Sided) on the right side, beginning at a distance &gt; 5S from the theoretical gore point at  100 feet spacing.  B.</span><span class="ca">Type G - Yellow Retroreflectors (1 Sided) on the left side of the exit at 10 feet spacing and then shifting to 100 feet spacing.  C.</span><span class="ca">Type F - White Retroreflectors (1 Sided) on the right side of the mainline, downstream of the exit at 10 feet spacing. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>

## 3G.101(CA) Emergency Passageway Marker


<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Except for emergency passageways in median barriers, median openings are not allowed on freeways.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Where freeway median passageways are provided for emergency vehicles, delineation for the crossover should be as follows:  A.<span class="ca">At a point, 1/5 mile in advance of the crossover, one Class 1 Delineator, with a yellow post and two 3 x 12 inch white  retroreflectors stacked vertically (24 inch of white retroreflectance), should be placed on the left side of the through roadway  facing approaching traffic.  B.</span><span class="ca">At a point, 1/10 mile in advance of the crossover, one Class 1 Delineator, with a yellow post and two 3 x 12 inch yellow  retroreflectors stacked vertically, should be placed on the left side as in A.  C.</span><span class="ca">At the far side of the crossover, one Class 1 Delineator, with a yellow post and one 3 x 12 inch white retroreflector over one  3 x 12 inch yellow retroreflector stacked vertically, should be placed on the left side as in A. </span></p>
</div>

## 3G.102(CA) Narrow Bridge Signing and Marking


<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> The placement of warning signs, object markers, delineators, and edge lines at narrow bridges is dependent upon the width of  the bridge and approach roadway.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Narrow bridge signing and marking shall conform to the details shown in <a href="#fig-3g-104ca">Figure 3G-104(CA)</a>.</p>

## 3G.103(CA) Median Barrier Delineation


<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Median barriers should be delineated when the clearance between the barrier and the edge of traveled way is less than 8 feet.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> In general, when delineated, it should be with an approved median barrier marker, the same color as the left edge line. They  should be placed on top of the barrier at 48 foot centers.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Markers placed on the sides of barriers, near the splash zone, should be avoided because of the tendency to collect dirt which  reduces their effectiveness. Refer to <a href="#fig-3g-105ca">Figure 3G-105(CA)</a>.</p>
</div>
