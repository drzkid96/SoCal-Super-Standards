## 3E.01 General

<div class="secmeta">Section 3E.01 &middot; manual page 977 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/3E.01/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Preferential lanes are established for one or more of a wide variety of special uses, including, but not limited to, high-occupancy vehicle (HOV) lanes, electronic toll collection (ETC) lanes, price-managed lanes, bus only lanes, taxi only lanes, and light rail transit only lanes.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> This Chapter contains the pavement marking provisions for preferential lanes used by motor vehicles and light rail transit. Part 9 contains information for pavement markings for bicycle lanes.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Chapter 3H contains information for the use and application of colored pavement that can be used in preferential lanes to supplement the pavement markings described in this Chapter.</p>
</div>

<span id="3e02" class="anchor"></span>

## 3E.02 Longitudinal Markings

<div class="secmeta">Section 3E.02 &middot; manual page 977 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/3E.02/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Preferential lanes can take many forms depending on the level of usage and the design of the facility. They might be barrier-separated or buffer-separated from the adjacent general-purpose lanes, or they might be contiguous with the adjacent general-purpose lanes. Barrier-separated preferential lanes might be operated in a constant direction or be operated as reversible lanes. Some reversible preferential lanes on a divided highway might be operated counter-flow to the direction of traffic on the immediately adjacent general-purpose lanes. <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1c/#1c02">Section 1C.02</a> contains definitions of these terms.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Preferential lanes might be operated full-time (24 hours per day on all days), for extended periods of the day, part- time (restricted usage during specific hours on specified days), or on a variable basis (such as a strategy for a managed lane).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> The left-hand and right-hand edge lines and lane lines used for preferential lanes that are adjacent to general-purpose lanes where traffic is flowing in the same direction shall be in accordance with <a href="#fig-3e-1">Table 3E-1</a>.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> If there are two or more preferential lanes for traffic moving in the same direction, the lane lines between the preferential lanes shall be normal width broken white lines.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Preferential lanes for motor vehicles shall have appropriate regulatory signs in accordance with Sections 2G.03 through 2G.07.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> <a href="#fig-3e-1">Figure 3E-1</a> illustrates pavement markings used for barrier-separated preferential lanes. <a href="#fig-3e-2">Figure 3E-2</a> illustrates pavement markings used for buffer-separated preferential lanes. <a href="#fig-3e-3">Figure 3E-3</a> illustrates pavement markings used for contiguous preferential lanes.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Engineering judgment should determine the need for supplemental devices such as tubular markers, traffic cones, or other channelizing devices (see Chapter 3I).</p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Where preferential lanes and other travel lanes are separated by a buffer space wider than 4 feet and crossing the buffer space is prohibited, chevron markings (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b25">Section 3B.25</a>) should be placed in the buffer area (see Drawing A in <a href="#fig-3e-2">Figure 3E-2</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> The buffer space for a conventional road should be designed so that it is not misinterpreted as on- street parking, a bicycle lane, or any other type of lane.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09a Buffer spaces between 4 feet and 12 feet (refer to <a href="#fig-3a-113ca">Figure 3A-113(CA)</a>, <a class="sref dref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/markings/line-types/#detail-45" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/linetypes/detail-45.svg">Detail 45</a>) should be avoided, except when  transitioning between narrow and wide buffer areas. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> If a full-time or part-time contiguous preferential lane is separated from the other travel lanes by a wide broken single white line (see Drawing C in <a href="#fig-3e-3">Figure 3E-3</a>), the spacing or skip pattern of the line may be reduced and the width of the line may be increased.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">10a Refer to FHWA’s List of Known Errors for error between Paragraphs 10 and 11. Refer to <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1a/#1a04">Section 1A.04</a> for more details. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> At direct exits from a preferential lane, dotted white line markings shall be used to separate the tapered or parallel deceleration lane for the direct exit (including the taper) from the adjacent continuing preferential through lane, to reduce the chance of unintended exit maneuvers.</p>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> Signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2b/#2b34">Section 2B.34</a>), lane-use control signals (see Chapter 4T), or both shall be used to supplement the reversible lane markings on a divided highway where a part-time counter-flow preferential lane is present.</p>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> The longitudinal pavement markings used for preferential lanes that are adjacent to general- purpose lanes where traffic is flowing in the opposite direction (see <a href="#fig-3e-4">Figure 3E-4</a>) shall be in accordance with <a href="#fig-3e-2">Table 3E-2</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> <a href="#fig-3e-4">Figure 3E-4</a> illustrates pavement markings used for counter-flow preferential lanes on divided highways or on transitions to and from other divided highways such as bridges and crossovers.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">15</span> Cones, tubular markers, or other channelizing devices (see Chapter 3I) may also be used in addition to longitudinal markings to separate the opposing lanes when a counter-flow preferential lane operation is in effect.</p>
</div>

## 3E.03 Preferential Lane Word and Symbol Markings

<div class="secmeta">Section 3E.03 &middot; manual page 978 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/3E.03/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Sections 3B.20 through 3B.22 contain information on general applications of word and symbol markings.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> When a preferential lane is established, the preferential lane shall be marked with one or more of the following word or symbol markings for the preferential lane use specified: A.HOV lane—white lines formed in a diamond-shaped symbol or the word message HOV. The diamond shall be at least 2.5 feet wide and 12 feet in length. The lines shall be at least 6 inches in width. <span class="ca">Refer to  <a href="#fig-3e-101ca">Figure 3E-101(CA)</a>.</span> B.ETC Account-Only lane—except as provided in Paragraph 8 of this Section, a word marking or pictograph using the name of the ETC payment system required for use of the lane, such as E-Z PASS ONLY. C.Price<span class="ca">d</span> managed lane—the word marking EXPRESS or EXPRESS LANE(S) (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2g/#2g17">Section 2G.17</a>). D.Bus only lane or bus stop—the word marking BUS ONLY or BUS STOP. E.Taxi only lane or taxi stand —the word marking TAXI ONLY or TAXI STAND. F.Light rail transit lane—the word marking LRT ONLY. G.Other type of preferential lane—a word marking appropriate to the restriction.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> If multiple preferential lane uses are allowed in a single lane, the word or symbol marking for each preferential lane should be used.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Pavement word or symbol markings for motorcycles and Inherently Low Emission Vehicles (ILEV) shall not be used to mark the preferential lane if motorcycles and ILEVs are allowed to use the preferential lane.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Motorcycles and Inherently Low Emission Vehicles (ILEV) that are allowed to use a preferential lane are granted an exception such as through an established High-Occupancy Vehicle (HOV) regulation. Communicating that motorcycles and ILEVs are allowed to use the preferential lane is accomplished through regulatory signing (see Sections 2G.03 and 2G.04) that complements HOV signing.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Static or changeable message regulatory signs (see Sections 2G.03 through 2G.07) shall be used with preferential lane word or symbol markings.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> All preferential lane word and symbol markings shall be white and shall be positioned laterally in the approximate center of the preferential lane.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Preferential lane-use symbol or word markings may be omitted at toll plazas where physical conditions preclude the use of the markings.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Lane-use arrow markings may be placed on the curb lanes on approaches to intersections to signify non-preferential road users can use the lane for turning movements.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> All longitudinal pavement markings, as well as word and symbol pavement markings, associated with a preferential lane should end at approximately where the Preferential Lane Ends (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-12a/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-12a.svg">R3-12a</a> or <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r3-12c/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R3-12c.svg">R3-12c</a>) sign (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2g/#2g07">Section 2G.07</a>) designating the downstream end of the preferential only lane restriction is installed.</p>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> The spacing of the markings should be based on engineering judgment that considers the operating speed, block lengths, distance from intersections, and other factors that affect clear communication to the road user. <span class="ca">A spacing of 180  feet should be used at the maximum interval for preferential lane markings on on-ramps.</span></p>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> In addition to a regular spacing interval, the preferential lane marking should be placed at strategic locations such as major decision points, direct exit ramp departures from the preferential lane, and along access openings to and from adjacent general-purpose lanes. At decision points, the preferential lane marking should be placed on all applicable lanes and should be visible to approaching traffic for all available departures. At direct exits from preferential lanes where extra emphasis is needed, the use of word markings (such as “EXIT” or “EXIT ONLY”) in the deceleration lane for the direct exit and/or on the direct exit ramp itself just beyond the exit gore should be considered.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> A numeral indicating the vehicle occupancy requirements established for a high-occupancy vehicle lane may be included in sequence after the diamond symbol or HOV word message.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">14 For State highways, refer to Caltrans’ High Occupancy Vehicle (HOV) Guidelines and Ramp Meter Design Manual. Refer to  <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1a/#1a05">Section 1A.05</a> for information regarding these publications. </span></p>
</div>

## 3E.04 Markings for Part-Time Travel on a Shoulder

<div class="secmeta">Section 3E.04 &middot; manual page 979 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/3E.04/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Shoulders are sometimes used to add capacity to a roadway in peak hour conditions to provide for transit or HOV priority or to provide higher throughput when open to all traffic.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> A shoulder that has been opened to travel on a permanent, rather than a part-time basis is considered to be a travel lane and is signed and marked in accordance with other provisions of this Manual.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> When part-time travel on a shoulder is open to all traffic, pavement word and symbol markings shall not be used in the shoulder.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> When a shoulder is assigned part-time to a particular class or classes of vehicles, the shoulder shall be marked with one or more pavement word markings that identify the special use of the shoulder such as BUS ONLY, TRANSIT ONLY, HOV, or instead of the HOV pavement word marking, white lines formed in a diamond-shaped symbol (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3e/#3e03">Section 3E.03</a>). A pavement word or symbol marking shall be provided in the shoulder immediately after exit and entry ramps (see <a href="#fig-3e-5">Figure 3E-5</a>) or immediately departing an intersection at the full-width shoulder (see <a href="#fig-3e-6">Figure 3E-6</a>). Appropriate regulatory signing (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2g/#2g03">Section 2G.03</a>) shall be installed with the pavement word or symbol markings.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> The channelizing line emanating from the entrance ramp shall be a wide dotted line through the intersecting alignment of the shoulder to the theoretical gore (see Drawings A and B in <a href="#fig-3e-5">Figure 3E-5</a>). At exit ramps, the channelizing line proceeding from the theoretical gore across the intersecting alignment of the shoulder shall be a wide dotted line (see <a href="#fig-3e-5">Figure 3E-5</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> If used, the extension of the channelizing line at entrance ramps proceeding from the theoretical gore across the opening of the on-ramp alignment shall be a wide dotted line (see Drawing C in <a href="#fig-3e-5">Figure 3E-5</a>) where it is demonstrated that traffic entering from an on-ramp stops or yields to traffic on the shoulder of the highway mainline.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> An additional outside solid edge line shall be provided on the shoulder in accordance with Sections 3B.09 and 3B.10.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Changes in edge line pattern or direction should occur at appropriate regulatory signs.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> At locations where traffic is allowed to enter, exit, or merge with the shoulder, a dotted edge line may be used either in a continuous manner or angled to the pavement edge (see <a href="#fig-3e-6">Figure 3E-6</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> Red-colored pavement (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3h/#3h07">Section 3H.07</a>) may be used on shoulders that allow only transit vehicles.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> If used, red-colored pavement shall be discontinued on the shoulder through the influence area of the ramp (see <a href="#fig-3e-5">Figure 3E-5</a>).</p>
</div>
