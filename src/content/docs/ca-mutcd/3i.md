## 3I.01 Channelizing Devices

<div class="secmeta">Section 3I.01 &middot; manual page 1018 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/3I.01/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Channelizing devices (see Sections 6K.01 through 6K.07 and <a href="#fig-6k-1">Figure 6K-1</a>) such as cones, tubular markers, vertical panels, lane separators, drums, and barricades may be used for general traffic control purposes such as adding emphasis to reversible lane delineation, channelizing lines, islands, pedestrian facilities, or bicycle facilities. Channelizing devices may also be used along a center line to preclude turns or along lane lines to preclude lane changing, as determined by engineering judgment.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01a</span> <span class="ca">In California, cones and drums are used for temporary traffic control, not as permanent channelizing devices. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Although they are not considered to be traffic control devices, raised islands (see Chapter 3J) are also sometimes used to channelize traffic.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Except for color, the design of channelizing devices, including, but not limited to, retroreflectivity, minimum dimensions, and mounting height, shall comply with the provisions of Chapter 6K.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Except as provided in Paragraph 5 of this Section, the color of channelizing devices used outside of temporary traffic control zones shall be the same color as the pavement marking that they supplement, or for which they are substituted, in accordance with <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3a/#3a03">Section 3A.03</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> The color of channelizing devices used to emphasize pavement marking patterns outside of temporary traffic control zones may be orange provided that the application of the orange-colored channelizing device is not permanent.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Emergency incidents and planned special events are the most common temporary traffic control zones that would justify orange channelizing devices to emphasize standard pavement marking colors. These events do not necessitate police officers or other authorized personnel to obtain and deploy channelizing devices that match the color of the existing pavement marking.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> For nighttime use, channelizing devices shall be retroreflective (as described in Part 6) or internally illuminated. On channelizing devices used outside of temporary traffic control zones, retroreflective sheeting or bands shall be white if the devices separate traffic flows in the same direction and shall be yellow if the devices separate traffic flows in the opposite direction or are placed along the left-hand edge line of a one-way roadway or ramp.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> <span class="ca">Channelizers are flexible retroreflective devices for installation within the roadway to discourage road users from crossing a line </span> <span class="ca">or area of the roadway. Unlike delineators, which indicate the roadway alignment, channelizers are intended to provide additional </span> <span class="ca">guidance and/or restriction to traffic by supplementing pavement markings and delineation. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> <span class="ca">Channelizers may be used for additional emphasis to discourage median crossings at traffic islands and at lane separations. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> <span class="ca">The design of a channelizer shall be as shown in <a href="#fig-3i-101ca">Figure 3I-101(CA)</a>. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> <span class="ca">The retroreflective unit used on channelizers shall be a minimum of 3 x 12 inches. The 3 x 24 inch minimum </span> <span class="ca">retroreflective unit shall be visible at 1000 feet at night under illumination of legal high beam headlights, by persons with </span> <span class="ca">vision of or corrected to 20/20. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> <span class="ca">The post shall be flexible with a 2 ¼ inch minimum width, except that the portion containing the retroreflective unit </span> <span class="ca">shall be a minimum width of 3 inches. The post shall be a minimum height of 36 inches above the pavement.</span></p>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> <span class="ca">Channelizer posts used for temporary traffic control shall be orange with white reflectors. Refer to <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/6h/#6h101ca">Section 6H.101(CA)</a>. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> <span class="ca">At locations where speeds are 40 mph or less a minimum post height of 28 inch may be used. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">15</span> <span class="ca">Since channelizers require closer spacing, their post size requirements differ from those of delineators. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">16</span> <span class="ca">There are two basic types of channelizers: one attaches to the pavement and the other attaches to an anchoring device </span> <span class="ca">imbedded in the pavement. Both the base and anchor systems are designed to permit replacement of the channelizer post. Refer to </span> <span class="ca"><a href="#fig-3i-101ca">Figure 3I-101(CA)</a>. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">17</span> <span class="ca">Channelizers should be placed a minimum of 2 feet from the traffic line, away from traffic, to allow for future maintenance of the </span> <span class="ca">line. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">18</span> <span class="ca">Space limitations may dictate exceptions to this criterion. At certain locations, placement directly on the traffic line may be </span> <span class="ca">required. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">19</span> <span class="ca">Spacing of the channelizers depends on the type of facility where they are to be used, the speed and volume of traffic, and the </span> <span class="ca">alignment to be channelized. Spacing which results in a visual fence/barrier effect is a key factor in channelizer installation. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">20</span> <span class="ca">The maximum post spacing should be 100 feet on carpool lanes where channelizers are used primarily to delineate the </span> <span class="ca">separation between the carpool lane and the main facility. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">21</span> <span class="ca">In locations where a relatively high number of violations occur, the post spacing should be 25 feet. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">22</span> <span class="ca">Where barrier violations are relatively minimal, a post spacing of 50 feet may be adequate. However, spacing in excess of 50 </span> <span class="ca">feet is of negligible value as a deterrent to intentional barrier violations. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">23</span> <span class="ca">Post spacing closer than 25 feet may be considered on lower speed roads, urban streets and at specific locations such as traffic </span> <span class="ca">islands. </span></p>
</div>

## 3I.02 Tubular Markers

<div class="secmeta">Section 3I.02 &middot; manual page 1019 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/3I.02/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Tubular markers for permanent installations shall be a minimum of 28 inches in height and shall be a minimum of 2 inches wide facing road users.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Tubular markers should be affixed to the pavement or other surface either directly or by means of an attachment system that is affixed to the pavement or other surface. Tubular markers should normally be spaced no greater than N as cited in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b14">Section 3B.14</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Other spacing may be used based on engineering judgment. Support:</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Tubular markers are sometimes used to provide additional emphasis or improve lane discipline in advance of an unsignalized crosswalk (see <a href="#fig-3i-1">Figure 3I-1</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> When tubular markers are used to supplement a <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r1-6/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R1-6.svg">R1-6</a> series sign (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2b/#2b20">Section 2B.20</a>) that is either on the center line, lane line, or median island, they should not be used on the same pavement marking line where the <a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r1-6/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R1-6.svg">R1-6</a> series sign is installed.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/6k/#6k04">Section 6K.04</a> contains information for temporary installations of tubular markers.</p>
</div>
