## 2H.01 Scope

<div class="secmeta">Section 2H.01 &middot; manual page 712 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/2H.01/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> General Information signs provide road users with navigational or orientation, geographic, or other information useful for traffic operational purposes. They include such items as State lines, city limits, time zones, stream names, elevations, landmarks, and similar geographic features. Chapter 2M contains recreational and cultural interest area symbol signs that are sometimes used in combination with General Information Signs. <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1d/#1d09">Section 1D.09</a> contains information on unnecessary traffic control devices. <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2a/#2a20">Section 2A.20</a> contains information on the excessive use of signs and sign clutter.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> A General Information (I3-5 through I4-2) symbol sign (see <a href="#fig-2h-1">Figure 2H-1</a>) may be used to provide direction to a transportation (I3 series signs) or other (I4 series signs) facility. The symbol sign may be supplemented by an educational plaque where necessary. The name of the facility may be used, if needed, to distinguish between similar facilities in the same area.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> The Advance Turn (M5 series) or Directional Arrow (M6 series) auxiliary plaques (see <a href="#fig-2h-1">Figure 2H-1</a>) with white arrows on green backgrounds may be used with General Information symbol signs to create a General Information Directional Assembly.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03a</span> <span class="ca">Refer to <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e17">Section 2E.17</a> when a General Information symbol is incorporated into the legend of a guide sign. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> The Recycling Center (I4-2) symbol sign may be used to direct road users to recycling centers.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> The Recycling Center symbol sign should not be used on freeways and expressways.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> The Passengers Only Ferry Terminal (I3-10) symbol sign may be used with the FERRY (I3-10P) plaque (see <a href="#fig-2h-1">Figure 2H-1</a>) mounted below it in a directional assembly to direct road users to passenger-only ferry terminals.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> General Information signs should not be installed within a series of guide signs, or at other equally critical locations, unless there are specific reasons for orienting the road user or identifying control points for activities that are clearly in the public interest. On all such signs, the designs should be simple and dignified, devoid of any tendency toward advertising, such as complex graphics or unnecessary messages, and in general compliance with other guide signing.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Promotional descriptive messages that are not relevant to navigation and orientation, such as “Scenic” or “Historic,” shall not be included in the legends of General Information signs, except as otherwise provided in this Chapter or in cases in which these terms are part of an official name, such as for a Scenic Byway or Historic District.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Except for State Welcome signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2h/#2h07">Section 2H.07</a>), Acknowledgment signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2h/#2h13">Section 2H.13</a>), and Alternative Fuels Corridor signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2h/#2h14">Section 2H.14</a>), General Information signs shall have white legends and borders on green rectangular-shaped backgrounds.</p>
</div>

## 2H.03 Airport Signs

<div class="secmeta">Section 2H.03 &middot; manual page 713 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/2H.03/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">00</span> <span class="ca">Refer to FHWA’s List of Known Errors for error in Paragraph 1 heading. Refer to <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1a/#1a04">Section 1A.04</a> for more details.</span></p>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Guide signs for commercial service airports and general aviation airports may be provided from the nearest Interstate, other freeway, or conventional highway intersection directly to the airport, normally not to exceed 15 miles. The Airport (I3-5) symbol sign (see <a href="#fig-2h-1">Figure 2H-1</a>) along with a supplemental plaque may be used to indicate the specific name of the airport. An Airport symbol sign, with or without a supplemental name plaque or the word AIRPORT, and an arrow may be used as a trailblazer.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Airport pictographs or other graphical representation of the specific airport shall not be used with or in place of the specific airport name on guide signs.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> If airport guide signs are used, adequate trailblazer signs should be used to provide motorist direction to the airport.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Location and placement of all airport guide signs depends upon the availability of longitudinal spacing on highways.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> <a href="#fig-2d-39">Figure 2D-39</a> shows an example of the guide signing that is typically used for a large commercial airport.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> <span class="ca">The Airport (I3-5) symbol sign may be used for large or small commercial airports and airports which do not accommodate large </span> <span class="ca">commercial jet aircraft. </span></p>
</div>

## 2H.04 Traffic Signal Speed Sign (I1-1)

<div class="secmeta">Section 2H.04 &middot; manual page 713 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/2H.04/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> The Traffic Signal Speed (I1-1) sign (see <a href="#fig-2h-1">Figure 2H-1</a>) displaying the legend SIGNALS SET FOR XX MPH may be used to indicate a section of street or highway on which the traffic control signals are coordinated into a progressive system timed for a specified speed at all hours during which they are operated in a coordinated mode.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> If different system progression speeds are set for different times of the day, a changeable message element may be used for the numerals of the Traffic Signal Speed sign. If the system is operated in coordinated mode only during certain times, a blank-out version of the Traffic Signal Speed sign may be used to display the entire message only during those times.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> An electronic-display changeable section of the Traffic Signal Speed sign shall be a white legend on a black opaque or green background.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p>If used, the Traffic Signal Speed sign should be mounted as near as practical to each intersection where the timed speed changes, and at intervals of several blocks throughout any section where the timed speed remains constant.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> <span class="ca">The Traffic Signal Speed (I1-1) sign is not used in California because its displayed speed reflects an internal signal timing </span> <span class="ca">parameter rather than information intended for motorists.</span></p>
</div>

## 2H.05 Jurisdictional Boundary Signs (I2-1)

<div class="secmeta">Section 2H.05 &middot; manual page 713 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/2H.05/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> The Jurisdictional Boundary (I2-1) sign may be used to mark the location of the jurisdictional boundary of a State, county, or municipality or the limits of an unincorporated municipal-level community, <span class="ca">federally recognized</span> Tribal Nation, or governmental district where legal jurisdiction, road maintenance responsibility, or emergency response obligation changes.</p>
<p><span class="pn">01a</span> <span class="ca">The WHERE WE HONOR VETERANS (G10-4P(CA)) sign may be used below the Jurisdictional Boundary Sign (I2-1) in </span> <span class="ca">reference to a county line. Refer to SHC § 1978.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> If used, the Jurisdictional Boundary sign should be located at or as near as practicable to the jurisdictional boundary without interfering with higher-priority traffic control devices. Notices of statutes or local ordinances should be located separately using regulatory signs (see Chapter 2B).</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> If used for an unincorporated community, the community should be one that is readily identifiable on official maps and be consistent with postal mailing addresses.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> In accordance with <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2h/#2h01">Section 2H.01</a>, the Jurisdictional Boundary sign shall be rectangular in shape and shall have a white legend on a green background. The sign shall display only the name of the State, county, municipality, <span class="ca">federally recognized</span> Tribal Nation, or other identifiable community, and an appropriate legend such as ENTERING, STATE LINE, County, or the municipal classification.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Names of elected officials or promotional messages, such as notable accomplishments or claims, shall not be displayed on a Jurisdictional Boundary sign or added as a supplemental sign or plaque.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> A pictograph representing the jurisdiction may be displayed on the Jurisdictional Boundary sign.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> If a pictograph is displayed on the Jurisdictional Boundary sign, it shall be the official seal of the jurisdiction and shall comply with the provisions of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2a/#2a04">Section 2A.04</a>. The pictograph shall be placed to the left of the legend. The height of the pictograph shall not exceed 2 times the height of the initial upper-case letter of the principal legend.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Signs should not be used to identify the boundaries of special-purpose governmental districts, such as school districts, sanitary districts, or improvement districts, as such signs are generally promotional in nature and do not provide navigational or orientation assistance in conjunction with official maps that are available to the general public.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2h/#2h07">Section 2H.07</a> contains information on State Welcome signs. <span class="ca">Unincorporated Community and City Limit (G9-2(CA) and G9-5(CA)) Signs</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> <span class="ca">The Unincorporated Community (G9-2(CA)) and City Limit (G9-5(CA)) signs shall be used to mark the limits of cities </span> <span class="ca">and to identify unincorporated towns. Refer to SHC § 101.1. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> <span class="ca">The G9-2(CA) signs should be placed on the right, as close as practical to the outer town limits of unincorporated towns, facing </span> <span class="ca">traffic entering the named town. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> <span class="ca">The G9-5(CA) sign should be placed on the right, as close as practical to the outer city limits of incorporated cities, facing traffic </span> <span class="ca">entering the named city. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> <span class="ca">The population may be obtained from: </span> <span class="ca">A.</span><span class="ca">Federal census </span> <span class="ca">B.</span><span class="ca">California Dept. of Finance </span> <span class="ca">C.</span><span class="ca">County Board of Supervisors </span> <span class="ca">D.</span><span class="ca">County Planning Commission </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> <span class="ca">The elevation shown may be that of the courthouse, post office, railroad station, or benchmark in the central district of the city. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">15</span> <span class="ca">On state highways Caltrans may place the state’s 9-1-1 emergency telephone number on county, city, and town limits signs. </span> <span class="ca">Refer to § 101.1 of the SHC.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">16</span> <span class="ca">Caltrans, under certain conditions, shall replace any city limit signs. Refer to §§ 101.2 and 101.4 of the SHC. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">17</span> <span class="ca">If a city or community desires to install a distinctive type city limits or "Welcome" sign on conventional highways at its city limits in </span> <span class="ca">place of the standard G9-5(CA) sign, the following criteria should be followed: </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="ca">A.</span><span class="ca">The signs shall be installed by local authorities at no expense to the State, and an approved encroachment permit </span> <span class="ca">will be obtained prior to installation. They shall be maintained by the permittee to the satisfaction of the permitter. </span> <span class="ca">B.</span><span class="ca">Such signs shall be installed in accordance with current Caltrans practices. </span> <span class="ca">C.</span><span class="ca">Signs shall be of reasonable size and proportional to other guide signs in the area. </span> <span class="ca">D.</span><span class="ca">Signs shall be positioned so they do not obstruct the view of official traffic control devices. </span> <span class="ca">E.</span><span class="ca">No moving or flashing displays or advertising of any kind will be permitted. </span> <span class="ca">F.</span><span class="ca">No sign shall encroach over the highway. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="ca">G.</span><span class="ca">Political jurisdiction logos may be displayed on the city limit signs, but the predominant characteristics of the sign will be </span> <span class="ca">white legend on a green rectangular shaped background. Distinctive type city limit signs not conforming to the above may </span> <span class="ca">remain in place until normal replacement is required. </span></p>
</div>

## 2H.06 Geographical Feature Signs (I2-2)

<div class="secmeta">Section 2H.06 &middot; manual page 715 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/2H.06/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> The Geographical Feature (I2-2) sign may be used to mark the locations of land features such as river or stream crossings, and summits, that are identifiable on maps or serve as landmarks in providing navigational orientation or reference to the road user.</p>
<p><span class="pn">01a</span> <span class="ca">The Geographical Feature (I2-2) sign may be used to identify bridges or structures across rivers and creeks and provide motorist </span> <span class="ca">orientation that is not otherwise included in the primary signing. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">01b</span> <span class="ca">The I2-2 sign should be used on freeways to identify major river crossings.</span></p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> If used, the Geographical Feature sign should display only the name of the geographical feature. Additional information that is unnecessary for navigational or orientation purposes, such as watershed or tributary names, should not be displayed on the sign. <span class="ca">Elevation (G16(CA) and G17(CA)) Signs </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> <span class="ca">The Mountain Pass Elevation (G16(CA)) sign may be used at the summit to inform the public of a mountain pass name and </span> <span class="ca">elevation. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> <span class="ca">The G16(CA) sign should be placed facing traffic in each direction on the right. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> <span class="ca">The Elevation (G17(CA)) sign may be used to inform motorists of changes in elevation. Feet will be shown in multiples of 1,000 </span> <span class="ca">feet above sea level, and multiples of 100 feet below sea level. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> <span class="ca">The G17(CA) sign should be placed facing traffic in each direction on the right. </span></p>
</div>

## 2H.07 State Welcome Signs

<div class="secmeta">Section 2H.07 &middot; manual page 715 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/2H.07/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> The design, placement, and function of State Welcome signs that are used to identify State lines differ from Jurisdictional Boundary (I2-1) signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2h/#2h05">Section 2H.05</a>). Because of these differences, it is necessary to distinguish State Welcome signs from State line Jurisdictional Boundary signs.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> A State Welcome sign may be located at or in the vicinity of the State boundary except as prohibited in Paragraph 4 of this Section.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> State Welcome signs may display the State seal or the State flag, the officially-adopted State motto or slogan, and the name of the Governor, in addition to the State name. State Welcome signs may use legend and background colors that provide adequate visual contrast rather than the standard sign colors.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> State Welcome signs shall be located separate from other signs where they will not interfere with or detract from other traffic control devices.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> State Welcome signs shall not display changeable or other electronic-display messages (see Chapter 2L). State Welcome signs shall not display messages that emulate promotional advertising of any type. State Welcome signs shall not incorporate Acknowledgment signs or messages (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2h/#2h13">Section 2H.13</a>), or business identification sign panels or logos (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2j/#2j03">Section 2J.03</a>) into their legends or assemblies. In accordance with <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2a/#2a04">Section 2A.04</a> of this Manual, telephone numbers, Internet addresses, and e-mail addresses, including domain names and uniform resource locators (URLs), and scanning graphics for the purpose of obtaining information shall not be displayed in the legends of State Welcome signs or on their supports.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> State Welcome signs should be located farther from the edge of the roadway than other traffic control devices.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> The maximum size of a State Welcome sign should be consistent with the prevailing size of other guide signs based on the roadway type. <span class="ca">Welcome to California (G10B(CA)) Sign </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> <span class="ca">The Welcome to California (G10B(CA)) sign should be used to indicate the California State line. The sign should be placed on </span> <span class="ca">the right near the State boundary facing traffic entering the State. </span></p>
</div>

## 2H.08 Future Interstate Corridor Signs (I2-4 and I2-4a)

<div class="secmeta">Section 2H.08 &middot; manual page 716 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/2H.08/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> The Future Interstate Corridor (I2-4 and I2-4a) signs (see <a href="#fig-2h-2">Figure 2H-2</a>) may be used sparingly along an existing route that will be reconstructed as an Interstate route or along an existing route adjacent to a corridor through which an Interstate route will be constructed, in accordance with the Policy and Conditions stated in 23 CFR 470, Appendix C.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Where the route number has been approved by the FHWA, either the I2-4 or I2-4a sign may be used.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> The I2-4a sign shall not be used where the route number has not been approved by the FHWA.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Future Interstate Corridor signs shall not be located where they could interfere with or detract from other traffic control devices. If used, Future Interstate Corridor signs shall be installed as independent, post-mounted sign assemblies.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Future Interstate Corridor signs shall not imply that an existing route has already been designated and marked as an Interstate route. Signs indicating that an existing route is designated as a future Interstate route or corridor shall not provide directional or distance information. Route Sign assemblies (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d29">Section 2D.29</a>) of any type shall not be used to sign a route as a future Interstate or other route. The Interstate route marker, or likeness thereof, shall not be displayed on the Future Interstate Corridor signs.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Future Interstate Corridor signs should be limited to strategic locations, such as at the beginning of the designated route or corridor, or beyond interchanges connecting from existing Interstate highways.</p>
</div>

## 2H.09 Project Information Sign (I2-5)

<div class="secmeta">Section 2H.09 &middot; manual page 716 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/2H.09/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">00</span> <span class="ca">For Construction Project Funding Identification Signs, please refer to section 6I.101(CA). </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> The Project Information (I2-5) sign (see <a href="#fig-2h-3">Figure 2H-3</a>) provides limited information to road users about a highway construction project on which work is imminently forthcoming or ongoing.</p>

<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>

</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>

<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>

When installing Project Information signs prior to the commencement of work, the jurisdiction shall have a policy on when the Project Information signs are to be installed. Project Information signs shall be removed at the conclusion of work on the project, even if the final inspection or project closeout has not yet occurred.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> The number of Project Information signs shall be limited to one per direction of travel on the roadway on which the project is based. The location of the Project Information sign shall not interfere with the temporary traffic control zone devices.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> The Project Information sign shall have a white legend on a green background and shall not display Internet addresses, e-mail addresses, or telephone numbers (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2a/#2a04">Section 2A.04</a>).</p>

## 2H.11 Reference Location Signs (D10-1 through D10-3) and Intermediate Reference Location Signs (D10-1a through D10-3a)

<div class="secmeta">Section 2H.11 &middot; manual page 717 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/2H.11/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> There are two types of reference location signs: A.Reference Location (D10-1 through D10-3) signs (see <a href="#fig-2h-5">Figure 2H-5</a>) show an integer distance point along a highway, and B.Intermediate Reference Location (D10-1a through D10-3a) signs (see <a href="#fig-2h-6">Figure 2H-6</a>) show the same information as Reference Location signs, but they also show a tenth-of-a-mile decimal so that they can be installed between integer distance points along a highway.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Except when Enhanced Reference Location signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2h/#2h12">Section 2H.12</a>) are used instead, Reference Location (D10-1 through D10-3) signs shall be placed on all expressway facilities that are located on a route where there is reference location sign continuity and on all freeway facilities to assist road users in estimating their progress, to provide a means for identifying the location of emergency incidents and traffic crashes, and to aid in highway maintenance and servicing.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Reference Location (D10-1 through D10-3) signs may be installed along any section of a highway route or ramp to assist road users in estimating their progress, to provide a means for identifying the location of emergency incidents and traffic crashes, and to aid in highway maintenance and servicing.<span class="ca"> on the highway.</span></p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> To augment the Reference Location sign system, Intermediate Reference Location (D10-1a through D10-3a) signs, which show the tenth of a mile with a decimal point, may be installed at one tenth of a mile, two tenths of a mile, or one-half mile intervals.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> When Intermediate Reference Location (D10-1a through D10-3a) signs are used to augment the reference location sign system, the reference location sign at the integer mile point shall display a decimal point and a zero numeral.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Reference Location and Intermediate Reference Location signs shall have a minimum mounting height of 4 feet, measured vertically from the bottom of the sign to the elevation of the near edge of the roadway, and shall not be governed by the mounting height requirements prescribed in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2a/#2a15">Section 2A.15</a>.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> The distance numbering shall be continuous for each route within a State, except where overlaps occur (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e22">Section 2E.22</a>). Where routes overlap, reference location sign continuity shall be established for only one of the routes. If one of the overlapping routes is an Interstate route, that route shall be selected for continuity of distance numbering.</p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> The route selected for continuity of distance numbering shall also have continuity in interchange exit numbering (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e22">Section 2E.22</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> On a route without continuity of distance numbering, the first reference location sign beyond the overlap should indicate the total distance traveled on the route (including on the portion that did not have continuity of distance numbering) so that road users will have a means of correlating their travel distance between reference location signs with that shown on their odometer.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> For divided highways, the distance measurement shall be made on the northbound and eastbound roadways. The reference location signs for southbound or westbound roadways shall be set at locations directly opposite the reference location signs for the northbound or eastbound roadways.</p>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> Zero distance shall begin at the south and west State lines, or at the south and west terminus points where routes begin within a State.</p>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> Except as provided in Paragraph 13 of this Section, reference location signs shall be installed on the right- hand side of the roadway.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> Where conditions limit or restrict the use of reference location signs on the right-hand side of the roadway, they may be installed in the median. On two-lane conventional roadways, reference location signs may be installed on one side of the roadway only and may be installed back-to-back. Reference location signs may be placed up to 30 feet from the edge of the pavement.</p>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> If a reference location sign cannot be installed in the correct location, it may be moved in either direction as much as 50 feet.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">15</span> If a reference location sign cannot be placed within 50 feet of the correct location, it should be omitted.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">16</span> <span class="ca">In California, reference posts shall be mileage based. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">17</span> <span class="ca">The placement and location of reference posts on State highways shall conform to the database maintained by </span> <span class="ca">Caltrans’ Division of Traffic Operations for reference posts. This database is different from the TASAS Highway database. </span></p>
</div>

## 2H.13 Acknowledgment Signs and Plaques (I20 Series)

<div class="secmeta">Section 2H.13 &middot; manual page 719 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/2H.13/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Acknowledgment signs and plaques (see <a href="#fig-2h-8">Figure 2H-8</a>) are a way of recognizing a company, business, or volunteer group that provides or sponsors a highway-related service. Acknowledgment signs include sponsorship signs for adopt- a-highway litter removal programs, maintenance of a parkway or interchange, and other highway maintenance or beautification sponsorship programs.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> A State or local highway agency that elects to have a sponsorship acknowledgement program should develop a policy on Acknowledgment signs and plaques. The policy should require that eligible sponsoring organizations comply with State laws prohibiting discrimination based on race, religion, color, age, sex, national origin, and other applicable laws.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> The State or local acknowledgment sign policy shall include all of the provisions regarding placement and design of Acknowledgment signs and plaques that are contained in this Section.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Because regulatory, warning, and guide signs have a higher priority, Acknowledgment signs shall only be installed where adequate spacing is available between the Acknowledgment sign and other higher priority signs. Acknowledgment signs shall not be installed in a position where they would obscure the road users’ view of other traffic control devices.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Acknowledgment signs shall not be installed at any of the following locations: A.On the front or back of, adjacent to, or around any other traffic control device, including traffic signs, highway traffic signals, and changeable message signs; B.On the front or back of, adjacent to, or around the supports or structures of other traffic control devices, or bridge piers; or C.At key decision points where a road user’s attention is more appropriately focused on other traffic control devices, roadway geometry, or traffic conditions, including exit and entrance ramps, merging or weaving areas, lane terminations, intersections, grade crossings, toll plazas, temporary traffic control zones, and areas of limited sight distance.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Acknowledgment signs and plaques shall have a white legend and border on a blue background. Acknowledgment signs shall be independent post-mounted roadside installations only and shall not be mounted overhead.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> An Acknowledgment sign may be used to acknowledge the sponsor of a rest area or welcome center.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Acknowledgment signs for a rest area, when located on the highway mainline, shall be limited to one sign per direction of travel from which the rest area is accessible, shall be located at least 500 feet from other traffic control devices, and shall not display names or representations of specific products or services provided by the sponsor within the rest area. Acknowledgment signs for rest areas shall display the legend REST AREA as the program activity, such as REST AREA SPONSORED BY. In accordance with Paragraph 5 of this Section, the Rest Area and Welcome Center Acknowledgment (I20-4 and I20-4a) signs shall not be combined in the same sign assembly with or substitute for the Rest Area General Service guide signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2i/#2i05">Section 2I.05</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> An additional Acknowledgment sign may be used within the rest area provided that it is not visible from the highway mainline or ramps to and from the rest area.</p>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> If a State has officially adopted and is actively promoting a program to encourage the use of safety rest areas through the use of a program name, then that program name may be displayed in smaller lettering below the legend REST AREA on the Rest Area Acknowledgment sign.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> Program names or slogans, as described in Paragraph 14 of this Section, shall not be displayed on the Rest Area General Service guide signs or other types of traffic signs.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> The minimum spacing between Acknowledgment signs and any other traffic control signs, except parking regulation signs, should be: A.150 feet on roadways with speed limits of less than 30 mph, B.200 feet on roadways with speed limits of 30 to 45 mph, and C.500 feet on roadways with speed limits greater than 45 mph.</p>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> If the placement of a newly-installed higher-priority traffic control device, such as a higher-priority sign, a highway traffic signal, or a temporary traffic control device, conflicts with an existing Acknowledgment sign, the Acknowledgment sign should be relocated, covered, or removed.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> State or local highway agencies may use their own pictograph (see definition in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1c/#1c02">Section 1C.02</a>) and/or a brief jurisdiction-wide program name, such as “Adopt-A-Highway” or “Litter Removal,” as part of any portion of the Acknowledgment sign, provided that the signs comply with the provisions for shape, sign and legend size, color, and lettering style in this Chapter and in Chapter 2A.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">15</span> Acknowledgment signs should clearly indicate the type of highway services provided by the sponsor.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">16</span> In addition to the general provisions for signs described in Chapter 2A and the sign design principles covered in the “Standard Highway Signs” publication (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1a/#1a05">Section 1A.05</a>), Acknowledgment sign and plaque designs developed by State or local highway agencies shall comply with the following provisions: A.Neither the sign or plaque design nor the sponsor acknowledgment name or logo shall contain any contact information, directions, slogans (other than a brief jurisdiction-wide program name, if used), telephone numbers, e-mail or Internet addresses, including domain names and uniform resource locators (URLs), metadata tags (“hash-tags”), or quick-response (QR) codes, bar codes, or similar scanning graphics (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2a/#2a04">Section 2A.04</a>); B.Except for the sponsor acknowledgment logo, all of the lettering shall be in upper-case letters of the Standard Alphabets as provided in the “Standard Highway Signs” publication (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1a/#1a05">Section 1A.05</a>); C.If a logo, instead of a word legend, is used to represent the sponsor, the logo shall be the primary logo that identifies the sponsoring entity. Secondary or alternate logos, slogans, products, mascots, spokespersons, or other items associated with the sponsoring entity’s commercial advertising or marketing shall not be displayed on Acknowledgment signs or plaques; D.In order to keep the main focus on the highway-related service and not on the sponsor acknowledgment name or logo, the area reserved for the sponsor acknowledgment name or logo shall not be located at the top of the sign or plaque, shall be a maximum of 8 square feet in area, and shall not exceed ⅓ of the total area of the sign; E.The entire sign display area of an Acknowledgment sign assembly shall not exceed 24 square feet; F.The sign or plaque shall not contain any messages, lights, symbols, or logos that resemble any official traffic control devices; G.The sign or plaque shall not contain any external or internal illumination, light-emitting diodes, luminous tubing, fiber optics, luminescent panels, or other flashing, moving, or animated features; H.The sign or plaque shall not distract from official traffic control messages such as regulatory, warning, or guidance messages; I.The area of the plaque shall not exceed the lesser of ⅓ the area of the General Service sign below which it is mounted or 24 square feet; J.The plaque size shall be based on the standard sizes as specified in <a href="#fig-2h-1">Table 2H-1</a>. If the size of the General Service sign is oversized for its application (greater than the size specified for the corresponding roadway application in <a href="#fig-2h-1">Table 2H-1</a>), or if the size of the General Service sign increases due to modification of the sign legend, a corresponding increase in the size of the plaque shall not be allowed; and K.The sign or plaque shall not display promotional or contact information about the agency’s sponsorship program, including if the sign or plaque does not currently display a sponsor.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">17</span> If a specific outlet of a business with multiple locations in the same area is the sponsoring entity, such as a franchisee, the area reserved for the sponsor acknowledgment name or logo may include the name of the municipality or neighborhood in which the sponsoring entity is located.</p>
<p><span class="pn">18</span> An Acknowledgment plaque may be mounted below the following General Service signs to acknowledge the sponsor of a corridor-based or region-based highway-related service: A.Radio-Weather Information (D12-1) sign (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2i/#2i09">Section 2I.09</a>); B.Radio-Traffic Information (D12-1a) sign (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2i/#2i09">Section 2I.09</a>); C.TRAVEL INFO CALL 511 (D12-5 and D12-5a) signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2i/#2i12">Section 2I.12</a>); and D.Roadside Assistance (D12-6) sign (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2i/#2i13">Section 2I.13</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">19</span> An Acknowledgment plaque shall not be mounted in conjunction with any other sign or traffic control device. An Acknowledgment plaque shall not be used alone or without one of the General Service signs specified in Paragraph 18 of this Section.</p>
<p><span class="pn">20</span> The general restrictions on the type of content allowed for display on Acknowledgment signs (see Paragraph 16 of this Section) shall apply to the legends of Acknowledgment plaques.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">21</span> <span class="ca">The requirements in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2h/#2h13">Section 2H.13</a> do not apply to State highways. </span></p>
</div>

## 2H.14 Alternative Fuels Corridor Sign (D9-19)

<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> The Alternative Fuels Corridor (D9-19) sign (see <a href="#fig-2h-9">Figure 2H-9</a>) may be used to inform motorists of an alternative fuels corridor highway segment that has been designated by the Secretary of Transportation as “Corridor Ready.”</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Alternative Fuels Corridor signs shall only be used to designate alternative fuels corridor highway segments that have been designated by the Federal Highway Administration as “Corridor Ready.” The appropriate General Service signs or plaques identifying the alternative fuels available in the corridor shall be included with the Alternative Fuels Corridor sign in a sign assembly. The alternative fuel services for an alternative fuels corridor shall be limited to electric vehicle charging, compressed natural gas, liquid natural gas, liquified petroleum, and hydrogen.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> The General Service (D9-11a, D9-11b, D9-11d, D9-11e, and D9-11f) symbol signs for use with an Alternative Fuels Corridor sign are shown in <a href="#fig-2i-1">Figure 2I-1</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Alternative Fuels Corridor signs shall only be post-mounted on the side of the road and shall not be mounted overhead.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> State or agency variations of the Alternative Fuels Corridor sign shall not be allowed. Acknowledgments of sponsors shall not be allowed in Alternative Fuels Corridor sign assemblies.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Except as provided in Paragraph 7 of this Section, Alternative Fuels Corridor signs shall be limited to one sign at or near the beginning of the alternative fuels corridor in each direction of travel.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> For long corridors, such as segments connecting control cities or major urban areas, additional signs may be located beyond major intersections or major interchanges following the typical post-interchange sign sequence.</p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> The beginning of an alternative fuels corridor may be indicated with a BEGIN (M4-14P) plaque (see <a href="#fig-2h-9">Figure 2H-9</a>) with a white legend and border on a blue background mounted above the alternative fuels corridor sign in the sign assembly.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">08a</span> <span class="ca">Refer to FHWA’s List of Known Errors for error in Paragraph 8 text. Refer to <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1a/#1a04">Section 1A.04</a> for more details. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> The end of an alternative fuels corridor may be indicated with an END (M4-6P) plaque (see <a href="#fig-2h-9">Figure 2H-9</a>) with a white legend and border on a blue background mounted above the Alternative Fuels Corridor sign in the sign assembly.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> The General Service signs shall not be used in the sign assembly indicating the end of a corridor.</p>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> When the availability of one or more of the alternative fuel facilities discontinues in an alternative fuels corridor, the LAST IN CORRIDOR (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w16-19p/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W16-19P.svg">W16-19P</a>) plaque (see <a href="#fig-2h-9">Figure 2H-9</a>) shall be included on the last General Service directional assembly on the approach to the interchange or intersection.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> When the availability of one or more of the alternative fuel facilities discontinues in an alternative fuels corridor, an Alternative Fuels Corridor sign with accompanying General Service signs indicating the types of fuels still available in the corridor may be provided beyond the intersection or interchange where the last discontinued fuel facilities were available.</p>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> When the distance between electric vehicle (EV) charging services in an alternative fuels corridor is greater than 50 miles, the Next EV Charging (D9-17a) sign (see <a href="#fig-2h-9">Figure 2H-9</a>) may be located after the EV charging directional assembly, but before the EV charging service exit or turn, to inform road users of the extended distance to the next EV charging service.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> The Alternative Fuels Corridor (D9-19) sign shall not be used as a directional sign in a directional assembly, or be combined with other signs, except as provided in this Section.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">15</span> Up to three General Service symbol signs arranged horizontally displaying the alternative fuels available in the designated corridor may be installed below the Alternative Fuels Corridor sign (see <a href="#fig-2h-10">Figure 2H-10</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">16</span> The size of the General Service symbol signs for the alternative fuels available shall not exceed 18 x 18 inches when mounted with the 24 x 24-inch Alternative Fuels Corridor sign and 24 x 24 inches when mounted with the 36 x 36-inch Alternative Fuels Corridor sign.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">17</span> When the number of eligible alternative fuels available in the corridor exceeds three, a separate plaque with the two-letter or three-letter designations (D9-19aP or D9-19bP) of each of the fuels available (see <a href="#fig-2h-9">Figure 2H-9</a>) should be used in place of the General Service symbol signs.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">18</span> When the Alternative Fuels Corridor sign is used in a designated corridor on a freeway or expressway, the applicable General Service signs shall be installed on the approach to an interchange in the corridor from which the designated fuel services are available. If the services are not visible from the ramp of a single-exit interchange, the service signing shall be repeated at the intersection of the exit ramp and the crossroad (see <a href="#fig-2h-10">Figure 2H-10</a>). Where the alternative fuel facility is not located along the crossroad, additional General Service directional assemblies shall be installed in advance of each subsequent turn to reach the facility (see Figure 2H- 11).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">19</span> Because regulatory, warning, and guide signs are necessary for safe and efficient movement of traffic, they have a higher priority in placement location over Alternative Fuels Corridor signs.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">20</span> Alternative Fuels Corridor sign assemblies shall be limited to those locations where adequate spacing is available between the Alternative Fuels Corridor sign and other signs. Alternative Fuels Corridor signs shall not be installed in a location where they might distract driver’s attention from other traffic control devices or the roadway in a complex roadway environment. If the placement of a newly-installed, higher-priority traffic control device conflicts with an existing Alternative Fuels Corridor sign, the Alternative Fuels Corridor sign shall be relocated, covered, or removed.</p>
<p><span class="pn">21</span> Alternative Fuels Corridor signs shall not be installed on routes other than those officially designated as alternative fuels corridors, even if to provide directional information to such corridors.</p>
</div>
