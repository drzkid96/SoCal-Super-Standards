## 2E.01 Scope of Freeway and Expressway Guide Sign Standards

<div class="secmeta">Section 2E.01 &middot; manual page 494 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/2E.01/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> The provisions of this Chapter provide a uniform and effective system of signing for high-volume, high-speed motor vehicle traffic on freeways and expressways. The requirements and specifications for expressway signing exceed those for conventional roads (see Chapter 2D), but are less than those for freeway signing. Since there are many geometric design variables to be found in existing roads, a signing concept commensurate with prevailing conditions is the primary consideration. <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1c/#1c02">Section 1C.02</a> includes definitions of freeway and expressway.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Guide signs for freeways and expressways are primarily identified by the name of the sign rather than by an assigned sign designation. Guidelines for the design of guide signs for freeways and expressways are provided in the “Standard Highway Signs” publication <span class="ca">and in Caltrans’ California Sign Specifications</span> (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1a/#1a05">Section 1A.05</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> The provisions of this Chapter shall apply to any highway that meets the definition of freeway or expressway facilities.</p>
</div>

## 2E.02 Freeway and Expressway Signing Principles

<div class="secmeta">Section 2E.02 &middot; manual page 494 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/2E.02/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> The development of a signing system for freeways and expressways is approached on the premise that the signing is primarily for the benefit and direction of road users who are unfamiliar with the route or area. The signing furnishes road users with clear instructions for orderly progress to their destinations. Sign installations are an integral part of the facility and, as such, are best planned concurrently with the development of highway location and geometric design. For optimal results, plans for signing are analyzed during the earliest stages of preliminary design, and details are correlated as final design is developed. The excessive signing found on many major highways usually is the result of using a multitude of signs that are too small and that are poorly designed and placed to accomplish the intended purpose.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Freeway and expressway signing is to be considered and developed as a planned system of installations. An engineering study is sometimes necessary for proper solution of the problems of many individual locations, but, in addition, consideration of an entire route is necessary.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Road users should be guided with consistent signing on the approaches to interchanges, when they drive from one State to another, and when driving through rural or urban areas. Because geographical, geometric, and operating factors regularly create significant differences between urban and rural conditions, the signing should take these conditions into account.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Guide signs on freeways and expressways should serve distinct functions as follows: A.Give directions to destinations, or to streets or highway routes, at intersections or interchanges; B.Furnish advance notice of the approach to intersections or interchanges; C.Direct road users into appropriate lanes in advance of diverging or merging movements; D.Identify routes and directions on those routes; E.Show distances to destinations; F.Indicate access to general motorist services, rest, scenic, and recreational areas; and G.Provide other information of navigational value to the road user.</p>
</div>

## 2E.04 Characteristics of Urban Signing

<div class="secmeta">Section 2E.04 &middot; manual page 495 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/2E.04/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Urban conditions are characterized not so much by city limits or other arbitrary boundaries as by the following features: A.Mainline roadways with more than two lanes in each direction; B.High traffic volumes on the through roadways; C.High volumes of traffic entering and leaving interchanges; D.Interchanges that are closely spaced; E.Roadway and interchange lighting; F.Three or more interchanges serving the major city; G.A loop, circumferential, or spur route serving a sizable portion of the urban population; and H.Visual clutter from roadside development.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Operating conditions and road geometrics on urban freeways and expressways usually make special sign treatments desirable, including: A.Use of Interchange Sequence signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e24">Section 2E.24</a>); B.Use of sign spreading to the maximum extent possible (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e43">Section 2E.43</a>); C.Elimination of General Service or Specific Service signing (see Chapters 2I and 2J); D.Reduction to a minimum of post-interchange signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e47">Section 2E.47</a>); E.Display of advance signs at distances closer to the interchange, with appropriate adjustments in the legend (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e23">Section 2E.23</a>); F.Use of overhead signs on roadway structures and independent sign supports (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e19">Section 2E.19</a>); G.Use of Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide signs in advance of interchanges with option lanes (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e40">Section 2E.40</a>), or Diagrammatic Advance guide signs in advance of interchanges with complex geometric configurations of ramp departures (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e41">Section 2E.41</a>); and H.Frequent use of street names as the principal message in guide signs.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Lower speeds, which are often characteristic of urban operations, do not justify lower signing standards. Typical traffic patterns are more complex for the road user to negotiate, and large, easy-to-read legends are, therefore, just as necessary as on rural highways.</p>
</div>

<span id="2e05" class="anchor"></span>

## 2E.05 Characteristics of Rural Signing

<div class="secmeta">Section 2E.05 &middot; manual page 495 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/2E.05/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Rural areas ordinarily have greater distances between interchanges, which permits adequate spacing for the sequences of signs on the approach to and departure from each interchange. However, the absence of traffic in adjoining lanes and on entering or exiting ramps often adds monotony or inattention to rural driving. This increases the importance of signs that call for decisions or actions.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Where there are long distances between interchanges and the alignment is relatively unchanging, signs should be positioned for their best effect on road users. The tendency to group all signing in the immediate vicinity of rural interchanges should be avoided by considering the entire route in the development of signing plans. Extra effort should be given to the placement of signs at natural target locations to command the attention of the road user, particularly when the message requires an action by the road user.</p>
</div>

## 2E.07 Designation of Destinations

<div class="secmeta">Section 2E.07 &middot; manual page 496 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/2E.07/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> The direction of a freeway and the major destinations or control cities along it shall be clearly identified through the use of appropriate destination legends (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d35">Section 2D.35</a>). Successive freeway guide signs shall provide continuity in destination names and consistency with available map information. At any decision point, a given destination shall be indicated by way of only one route (see <a href="#fig-2e-1">Figure 2E-1</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Control city legends should be used in the following situations along a freeway: A.At interchanges between freeways; B.At separation points of overlapping freeway routes; C.On directional signs on intersecting routes, to guide traffic entering the freeway; D.On Pull-Through signs; and E.On the bottom line of post-interchange distance signs.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Continuity of destination names is also useful on expressways serving long-distance or intrastate travel.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> The determination of major destinations or control cities is important to the quality of service provided by the freeway. Control cities on freeway guide signs are selected by the States and are contained in the “Guidelines for the Selection of Supplemental Guide Signs for Traffic Generators Adjacent to Freeways, 4th Edition/Guide Signs, Part II: Guidelines for Airport Guide Signing/Guide Signs, Part III: List of Control Cities for Use in Guide Signs on Interstate Highways,” published by and available from the American Association of State and Highway Transportation Officials.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04a</span> <span class="ca">Refer to FHWA’s List of Known Errors for error in Paragraph 4 text. Refer to <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1a/#1a04">Section 1A.04</a> for more details. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> <span class="ca">Each Caltrans District should establish its list of control cities in coordination with neighboring districts and adjacent states to </span> <span class="ca">ensure consistent and uniform signage for through traffic on State highways. The same control cities should be used along a given </span> <span class="ca">route for each direction of travel. </span></p>
</div>

## 2E.08 General

<div class="secmeta">Section 2E.08 &middot; manual page 497 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/2E.08/">short link</a></div>

<p><span class="pn">01</span> Effective signs are legible to road users approaching them, and are readable and comprehensible in the viewing time provided to permit proper responses. </div>

</div>

<p><span class="pn">02</span> Color requirements for route signs and trailblazers; for signs with blank-out or changeable messages; for signs for services, rest areas, park and recreational areas; and for certain miscellaneous signs are provided in the individual Sections dealing with the particular sign or sign group.</p>

## 2E.10 Retroreflection or Illumination

<div class="secmeta">Section 2E.10 &middot; manual page 497 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/2E.10/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Letters, numerals, symbols, arrows, and borders of all guide signs shall be retroreflective. The background of all guide signs that are not independently illuminated shall be retroreflective.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Where there is no serious interference from extraneous light sources, retroreflective post-mounted signs usually provide adequate nighttime visibility.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> On freeways and expressways where much driving at night is done with low-beam headlights, the amount of headlight illumination incident to an overhead sign display is relatively small.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Overhead sign installations should be illuminated (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2a/#2a21">Section 2A.21</a>) unless an engineering study shows that retroreflection alone will perform effectively. The type of illumination chosen should provide effective and reasonably uniform illumination of the sign face and message.</p>
</div>

## 2E.11 Interchange Classification

<div class="secmeta">Section 2E.11 &middot; manual page 497 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/2E.11/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> For signing purposes, interchanges are classified as major, intermediate, and minor. Minimum letter and numeral sizes based on interchange classification are contained in Tables 2E-2 and 2E-4. Descriptions of these classifications are as follows: A.Major interchanges are subdivided into two categories: (a) interchanges with other expressways or freeways, or (b) interchanges with high-volume multi-lane highways, principal urban arterials, or major rural routes where the volume of interchanging traffic is heavy or includes many road users unfamiliar with the area. B.Intermediate interchanges are those with urban and rural routes not in the category of major or minor interchanges. C.Minor interchanges include those where traffic is local and very light, such as interchanges with land service access roads. Where the sum of exit volumes is estimated to be lower than 100 vehicles per day in the design year, the interchange is classified as minor.</p>
</div>

## 2E.12 Size of Signs and Letters

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2a/#2a07">Section 2A.07</a> contains information regarding the applicability of the various columns in <a href="#fig-2e-1">Table 2E-1</a>.</p>

</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> The nominal loop height of the lower-case letters shall be ¾ of the height of the initial upper- case letter (see Paragraph 3 of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d05">Section 2D.05</a> for additional information on the specification of letter heights). Other word legends such as cardinal directions, action messages, and special characters shall be composed of all upper-case letters with a minimum letter height of 8 inches. Minimum numeral and letter sizes for freeway guide signs according to interchange classification, type of sign, and component of sign legend shall be as shown in Tables 2E-4 and 2E-5. <p><span class="pn">06</span> All names of places, streets, and highways on freeway and expressway guide signs shall be composed of lower-case letters with initial upper-case letters. The letters and the numerals used shall be FHWA Standard Alphabet Series E (modified) as provided in the “Standard Highway Signs” publication (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1a/#1a05">Section 1A.05</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Lettering size on freeway and expressway signs shall be the same for both rural and urban conditions.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Sign size is determined primarily in terms of the length of the message and the size of the lettering necessary for proper legibility. Letter style and height, and arrow design have been standardized for freeway and expressway signs to assure uniform and effective application.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Designs for upper-case and lower-case FHWA Standard Alphabets, together with tables of recommended letter spacing, are shown in the “Standard Highway Signs” publication (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1a/#1a05">Section 1A.05</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>

<p><span class="pn">11</span> Other sign letter size requirements not specifically identified elsewhere in this Manual should be guided by these specifications. Abbreviations should be kept to a minimum, except as provided in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e16">Section 2E.16</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> A sign mounted over a particular roadway lane to which it applies might have to be limited in horizontal dimension to the width of the lane, so that another sign can be placed over an adjacent lane. The necessity to maintain proper vertical clearance might also place a further limitation on the size of the overhead sign and the legend that can be accommodated.</p>

</div>

## 2E.15 Amount of Legend on Guide Signs

<div class="secmeta">Section 2E.15 &middot; manual page 499 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/2E.15/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> No more than two destination names or street names should be displayed on any Interchange Advance Guide sign or Exit Direction sign. A city name and street name on the same sign should be avoided. Where two or three signs are placed on the same supports, destinations or street names should be limited to one per sign, or to a total of three in the display. Where a community is served by multiple interchanges, the city name is typically displayed on either a Community Interchanges Identification sign (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e52">Section 2E.52</a>) or a Next Exits sign (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e53">Section 2E.53</a>). Each interchange is then identified by its intersecting roadway name on the Interchange Advance guide and Exit Direction signs rather than by the city name.</p>

<div class="secmeta">Section 2E.18 &middot; manual page 499 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/2E.18/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Arrows used on interchange guide signs shall be of the types shown in <a href="#fig-2d-3">Figure 2D-3</a> and shall comply with the provisions of this Section and <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d08">Section 2D.08</a>.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Except on Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e40">Section 2E.40</a>) and on Exit Direction signs for lane drops (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e28">Section 2E.28</a>), and except as provided in Paragraph 5 of this Section, directional arrows on all overhead and post-mounted Exit Direction signs shall point diagonally upward. Directional arrows on post-mounted Exit Direction signs shall be located at the bottom portion of the sign and centered under the legend.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> On overhead Exit Direction signs that are located fully over the tapered portion of the exit ramp at the theoretical gore, and where a directional arrow to the side of the legend farthest from the roadway might create an unusually wide sign that limits the road user’s view of the arrow, the directional arrow may be placed at the bottom portion of the sign, centered under the legend.</p>

Down arrows shall not be used unless an arrow can be located over and pointed to the approximate center of each lane that can be used to reach the destination displayed on the sign.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> If down arrows are used, having more than one down arrow pointing to the same lane on a single overhead sign (or on multiple signs on the same overhead sign structure) shall not be permitted.</p>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>

This is accomplished by placing the TRUCKS (R70A(CA)) and OK (R70B(CA)) sign panels adjacent to a down arrow. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Directional and down arrows for use on guide signs are shown in <a href="#fig-2d-3">Figure 2D-3</a>. ## 2E.20 Lateral Offset

<div class="secmeta">Section 2E.20 &middot; manual page 501 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/2E.20/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Except where shielded by a rigid traffic barrier, the minimum lateral offset outside the usable roadway shoulder for post-mounted freeway and expressway signs or for overhead sign supports, either to the right-hand or left-hand side of the roadway, shall be 6 feet. This minimum clearance shall also apply outside of a curb. If located within the clear zone, the signs shall be mounted on crashworthy (see definition in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1c/#1c02">Section 1C.02</a>) supports or shielded by appropriate crashworthy barriers.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Where practicable, a sign should not be less than 10 <span class="ca">12 </span>feet from the edge of the nearest traffic lane. Large guide signs especially should be farther removed, preferably 30 feet or more from the nearest traffic lane.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Where an expressway median is 12 feet or less in width, consideration should be given to spanning both roadways without a center support.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Where an overhead sign support cannot be placed sufficiently far away from the line of traffic, it should either be designed to minimize the impact forces, or be adequately shielded by a traffic barrier of suitable design.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Butterfly-type sign supports and other overhead non-crashworthy sign supports shall not be installed in gores or other unshielded locations within the clear zone.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Lesser clearances, but not generally less than 6 feet, may be used on connecting roadways or ramps at interchanges.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> <span class="ca">Refer to <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2a/#2a16">Section 2A.16</a> for additional information about lateral offset. </span></p>

## 2E.22 Interchange Exit Numbering

<div class="secmeta">Section 2E.22 &middot; manual page 502 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/2E.22/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Interchange exit numbering shall use the reference location sign exit numbering method. The consecutive exit numbering method shall not be used. The exit numbers shall correspond to the posted Reference Location or Enhanced Reference Location signs.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Reference location sign exit numbering assists road users in determining their destination distances and travel mileage, assists road users in reporting their location in the event of an incident or breakdown, assists responders in responding to incidents, and assists highway agencies because the exit numbering sequence does not have to be changed if new interchanges are added to a route.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Interchange exit numbering provides valuable orientation for the road user on a freeway or expressway. The feasibility of numbering interchanges or exits on an expressway will depend largely on the extent to which grade separations are provided. Where there is appreciable continuity of interchange facilities, interrupted only by an occasional intersection at grade, the numbering will be helpful to the expressway user.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Interchange exit numbering shall be used in signing each freeway interchange exit. Interchange exit numbers shall be displayed with each Interchange Advance Guide sign, Exit Direction sign, and Exit Gore sign. The exit number shall be displayed on a separate plaque on top <span class="ca">(above and abutting)</span>of the Interchange Advance Guide or Exit Direction sign. The Exit Number (E1-5P series) plaque (see <a href="#fig-2e-9">Figure 2E-9</a>) shall include the word EXIT(S) and the appropriate exit number(s) in a single-line format.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04a</span> <span class="ca">Rest areas, vista points, weigh stations, HOV facility exits, and HOV-to-HOV system connector ramps are not </span> <span class="ca">considered interchange exits and shall not be assigned or signed with exit numbers. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Suffix letters shall only be used to supplement exit numbers where there is more than one exit associated with the reference mile points of the freeway. Suffix letters shall not be used for an exit ramp for the purpose of identifying a downstream ramp split providing access to multiple highways or different directions on the same highway. The suffix letter shall also be included on the Exit Number plaque and shall be separated from the exit number by a space having a width of between ½ and ¾ of the height of the suffix letter. The suffix letters assigned shall be in ascending alphabetical order starting with the letter A for ramps in the direction of travel with increasing exit numbers, and in descending alphabetical order ending in the letter A in the opposite direction of travel. Exit numbers shall not include the cardinal direction initials corresponding to the directions of the cross route. The minimum numeral and letter sizes shall be as given in Tables 2E-2 through 2E-5. If used, the exit numbering system for expressways shall comply with the provisions prescribed for freeways.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Where suffix letters are used for exit numbering, an exit of the same number without a suffix letter shall not be used on the same route in the same direction. For example, if an exit is designated as EXIT 256 A, then there shall not be an exit designated as EXIT 256 on the same route in the same direction.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> To the extent practical, exit numbering should be determined based upon the location of the crossroad with respect to reference location signs as given in the following examples: A.If a crossroad intersects the mainline approximately at or after Mile 15 and before Mile 16, the interchange should be designated as EXIT 15 (see Drawings A and B in <a href="#fig-2e-3">Figure 2E-3</a>). B.If the interchange crossroad is split into two roadways by direction where one direction of the crossroad is downstream of Mile 18 and the other direction is upstream of Mile 18, the interchange exit number should be EXIT 18 (see Drawings A and B in <a href="#fig-2e-3">Figure 2E-3</a>). C.If there are three closely-spaced interchanges, such as less than 1 mile apart, starting before Mile 16 and ending near or at Mile 17, the interchanges should be designated as EXIT 15, EXIT 16, and EXIT 17. D.If there are multiple interchanges so closely spaced together that it is impracticable to designate the exit numbers by the freeway mainline reference mile numbers, suffix letters should be used as provided in this Section (see Drawings C and D in <a href="#fig-2e-3">Figure 2E-3</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Exit numbers may also be used with Supplemental guide signs in compliance with the provisions of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e51">Section 2E.51</a>, and Motorist Service signs in compliance with the provisions of Chapters 2I and 2J.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Where exit suffix letters are used and the number of exits is not equal in both directions of travel, the exit suffix lettering for each direction shall be based on the number of exits in that direction. For example, if in the northbound direction of a freeway there are three exits for Mile 25 and two exits in the southbound direction, the exit numbers northbound shall be EXIT 25 A, EXIT 25 B, and EXIT 25 C; and the exit numbers southbound shall be EXIT 25 B followed by EXIT 25 A (see Drawing D in <a href="#fig-2e-3">Figure 2E-3</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> Except as provided in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e36">Section 2E.36</a> for Collector-Distributor Roadways or as otherwise provided for in this Chapter, exit numbers and suffix letters shall only be used to designate individual exit departure points directly from the freeway mainline. Exit numbers and suffix letters shall not be used for designating ramp splits into two ramps after leaving the mainline.</p>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> The Exit Number (E1-5P) plaque shall be positioned above the top right-hand edge <span class="ca">(above and abutting)</span> of the sign for an exit to the right (see <a href="#fig-2e-9">Figure 2E-9</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> Because road users might not expect an exit to the left and might have difficulty in maneuvering to the left, a Left Exit Number (E1-5bP <span class="ca">E1-5fP to E1-5kP</span>) plaque (see <a href="#fig-2e-9">Figure 2E-9</a>) shall be added abovethe top left-hand edge <span class="ca">(above and abutting)</span> of the sign for all numbered left-hand exits (see Figures 2E-18 and 2E-34). The word LEFT on the Left Exit Number plaque shall be a black legend on a yellow rectangular sign panel and shall be centered above the word EXIT.</p>

<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>

<p><span class="pn">16</span> For circumferential routes, interchange exit numbering shall be in a clockwise direction. The numbering shall begin with the first interchange west of the south end of an imaginary north-south line bisecting the circumferential route, at a radial freeway or other Interstate route, or some other conspicuous landmark in the circumferential route near a south polar location (see <a href="#fig-2e-5">Figure 2E-5</a>).</p>

<p><span class="pn">18</span> Spur route interchanges shall be numbered in ascending order starting at the interchange where the spur leaves the mainline route (see <a href="#fig-2e-7">Figure 2E-7</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">19</span> If a circumferential, loop, or spur route crosses State boundaries, the numbering sequence shall be coordinated by the States to provide continuous interchange exit numbering.</p>
<p><span class="pn">20</span> Where numbered routes overlap, continuity of interchange exit numbering shall be established for only one of the routes (see <a href="#fig-2e-8">Figure 2E-8</a>). ## 2E.23 Interchange Advance Guide Signs (E1-1 through E1-3)

<div class="secmeta">Section 2E.23 &middot; manual page 504 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/2E.23/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p>An Interchange Advance guide sign (see <a href="#fig-2e-9">Figure 2E-9</a><span class="ca"> and <a href="#fig-2e-9ca">Figure 2E-9(CA)</a></span>) gives notice well in advance of the exit point of the principal destinations served by the next interchange and the distance to that interchange.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Except as provided in Paragraph 16 of this Section, and in Paragraph 18 of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e25">Section 2E.25</a>, at least one Interchange Advance guide sign shall be used for all interchange classifications.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> At major and intermediate interchanges (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e11">Section 2E.11</a>), at least two Interchange Advance guide signs should be used, placed at ½ mile and at 1 mile in advance of the exit. A third Interchange Advance guide sign should be placed at 2 miles in advance of the exit if spacing permits.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> At minor interchanges, the Interchange Advance guide sign should be located ½ to 1 mile from the exit gore.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Sections 2E.29 through 2E.44 contain additional provisions regarding the number, location, and mounting of Interchange Advance guide signs for certain interchange configurations.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Except as provided in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e28">Section 2E.28</a>, the legend on Interchange Advance guide signs shall contain the distance message. For each direction of travel, the legend on the Interchange Advance guide signs shall be the same as the legend on the Exit Direction sign, except that the last line shall be the distance message. The distance message shall read XX MILE(S) where exit numbers are used. Where exit numbers are not used, the distance message shall read EXIT XX MILE(S) for an interchange with one exit ramp, and EXITS XX MILE(S) for an interchange with two or more exit ramps.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Where an Interchange Advance guide sign is located more than 1,000 feet to ½ mile but not more than 1 mile from the exit, the distance displayed should be to the nearest ¼ mile. Where the distance to be displayed on an Interchange Advance guide sign is 1,000 feet or less, the distance should be displayed in feet, rather than miles, to the nearest 100 feet.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">07a</span> <span class="ca">Refer to FHWA’s List of Known Errors for error in Paragraph 7 text. Refer to <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1a/#1a04">Section 1A.04</a> for more details. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>

<p><span class="pn">09</span> For numbered exits, the exit number used with the Interchange Advance guides signs shall be displayed using an Exit Number plaque above and abutting the Interchange Advance guide sign.</p>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> For numbered exits to the right, an Exit Number (E1-5P through E1-5eP) plaque (see <a href="#fig-2e-9">Figure 2E-9</a>) shall be added tothe top right-hand edge <span class="ca">(above and abutting)</span> of the sign.</p>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> For numbered exits to the left, a Left Exit Number (E1-5fP through E1-5kP) plaque (see <a href="#fig-2e-9">Figure 2E-9</a>) shall be added abovethe top left-hand edge <span class="ca">(above and abutting)</span> of the sign (see Figures 2E-18 and 2E-34).</p>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> For unnumbered exits to the left, a LEFT (E1-5mP) plaque (see <a href="#fig-2e-9">Figure 2E-9</a>) shall be added to the top left- hand edge of the sign, abutting the sign.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e22">Section 2E.22</a> contains additional information regarding exit numbering.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> Interchange Advance guide signs for multi-lane exits having an optional exit lane that also carries the through route at major interchanges (see Figures 2E-36,<span class="ca"> 2E-36(CA),</span> 2E-37, and 2E-42) and for splits with an option lane (see Figures 2E-38, and 2E-39) shall be Overhead Arrow-per-Lane signs designed in accordance with Sections 2E.39 and 2E.40.</p>

<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>

Duplicate Interchange Advance guide signs or Interchange Sequence Series signs may be placed in the median on the opposite side of the roadway and are not included in the minimum requirements of interchange signing.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>

## 2E.25 Exit Direction Signs (E4 Series)

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p>The Exit Direction sign (see <a href="#fig-2e-12">Figure 2E-12</a><span class="ca"> and 2E-12(CA)</span>) repeats the route and destination information that was displayed on the Interchange Advance guide sign(s) for the next exit, and thereby assures road users of the destination served and indicates whether they exit to the right or left for that destination.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Exit Direction signs shall be used at major and intermediate interchanges. Populations or other similar information shall not be displayed on Exit Direction signs.</p>

<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Sections 2E.28, 2E.30, 2E.31, 2E.33 through 2E.35, 2E.38, and 2E.40 through 2E.42 illustrate the use, location, and mounting of Exit Direction signs for certain interchange configurations. The placement location of the Exit Direction sign at the interchange depends on the type of mounting, post-mounted or overhead, and whether there is a deceleration lane (see <a href="#fig-2e-13">Figure 2E-13</a>).</p>

<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>

When mounted overhead, the Exit Direction sign should be installed over the exiting lane in the vicinity of the theoretical gore. ## 2E.26 Exit Gore Signs and Plaque (E5-1 Series)

<div class="secmeta">Section 2E.26 &middot; manual page 508 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/2E.26/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> The Exit Gore sign (see <a href="#fig-2e-15">Figure 2E-15</a>) in the gore indicates the exiting point or the place of departure from the main roadway. Consistent application of this sign at each exit is important to provide adequate visibility of the departure of the exit roadway from the main roadway.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> The gore shall be defined as the area located between the main roadway and the ramp just beyond where the ramp branches from the main roadway. An Exit Gore sign shall be located in the gore for each ramp that departs from the main roadway of a freeway or expressway, or departs from a collector-distributor roadway, and shall display the word EXIT (E5-1) if interchange exit numbering is not used or EXIT XX (E5-1a or E5-1c) if interchange exit numbering is used, and an appropriate diagonally upward-pointing arrow. If suffix letters are used for exit numbering at a multi-exit interchange, the suffix letter shall also be included on the Exit Gore (E5- 1a or E5-1c) sign or Exit Gore Number (E5-1bP) plaque and shall be separated from the exit number by a space having a width of between ½ and ¾ of the height of the suffix letter. Breakaway or yielding supports shall be used.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> The arrow should be aligned to approximate the angle of departure. Each gore should be treated similarly, whether the interchange has one exit roadway or multiple exits.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> The Narrow Exit Gore (E5-1c) sign (see <a href="#fig-2e-15">Figure 2E-15</a>) may be used in gore areas of limited width where the width of the Exit Gore (E5-1a) sign would not permit sufficient lateral offset (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2a/#2a16">Section 2A.16</a>), such as for ramp departures that are nearly parallel to the main roadway where the Exit Gore sign would be mounted on a narrow island or barrier. Where the E5-1c sign is mounted at a height of 14 feet or more from the roadway, the directional arrow may point diagonally downward.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> The E5-1c sign should not be used in gore areas where an E5-1a sign could be installed with sufficient lateral offset.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Where extra emphasis of an especially low advisory ramp speed is needed, the Confirmation Advisory Speed (W13- 1aP) plaque (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2c/#2c59">Section 2C.59</a>) indicating the advisory speed may be mounted below the Exit Gore sign (see <a href="#fig-2e-15">Figure 2E-15</a>) to supplement, but not to replace, the exit or ramp advisory speed warning signs.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> To improve the visibility of the gore for exiting drivers, a Type 1 object marker (see Chapter 2C) may be installed 4 feet above the ground line on each sign support below the Exit Gore sign.</p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> An Exit Gore Number (E5-1bP) plaque (see <a href="#fig-2e-15">Figure 2E-15</a>) may be installed above an existing Exit Gore (E5-1) sign when an unnumbered exit is converted to a numbered exit until such time as an E5-1 sign is being replaced for other reasons (see Paragraph 9 of this Section).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> An Exit Gore (E5-1a) sign shall be used when the replacement of an existing assembly of an E5- 1 sign and E5-1bP plaque becomes necessary.</p>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> <span class="ca">An Exit Gore (E5-1a) sign shall replace an existing Exit Gore (E5-1) sign when an unnumbered exit is converted to a </span> <span class="ca">numbered exit. </span></p>

<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Pull-Through signs should be used where the geometrics of a given interchange are such that it is not clear to the road user as to which is the through roadway, or where additional route guidance is desired. Pull-Through signs should not be used at exits with option lanes where full-width Overhead Arrow-per-Lane signs are being used.</p>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>

## 2E.28 Signing for Interchange Lane Drops Without an Optional Exit Lane

<div class="secmeta">Section 2E.28 &middot; manual page 509 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/2E.28/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> The provisions of this Section shall only apply to lane drops at exits that do not have an optional exit lane. At exits that have an optional exit lane in addition to the dropped lane, the provisions of Sections 2E.39 through 2E.42 shall apply.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01a</span> <span class="ca">Refer to FHWA’s List of Known Errors for error in Paragraph 1 text. Refer to <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1a/#1a04">Section 1A.04</a> for more details. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Except as provided in Paragraph 15 of this Section, major guide signs for all lane drops at interchanges shall be mounted overhead. An EXIT ONLY sign panel shall be used for all interchange lane drops at which the through route is carried on the main roadway.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">02a</span> <span class="ca">Refer to FHWA’s List of Known Errors for error in Paragraph 2 text. Refer to <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1a/#1a04">Section 1A.04</a> for more details. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Except on Overhead Arrow-per-Lane and Diagrammatic Advance guide signs (see Sections 2E.39 through 2E.41), the EXIT ONLY (down arrow) (E11-1 or E11-1f) sign panel (see <a href="#fig-2e-17">Figure 2E-17</a>) shall be used on all overhead Advance guide signs of lane drops (see Figures 2E-18, 2E-19, and 2E-34). The number of arrows on each sign shall correspond to the number of dropped lanes at the location of each sign. Placement of the down arrow shall comply with the provisions of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e18">Section 2E.18</a>.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> For lane drops, the bottom portion of the overhead Exit Direction sign shall be yellow with a black border and shall include a diagonally upward-pointing black directional arrow (left or right, as appropriate) for each lane dropped at the exit (see Figures 2E-18 and 2E-19). The sign shall be designed and placed so that each arrow is located over the approximate center of each lane being dropped. Except as provided in Paragraph 5 of this Section, the words EXIT and ONLY shall be positioned to the left and right, respectively, of the arrow on the E11-1d sign panel (see <a href="#fig-2e-17">Figure 2E-17</a>) for a single-lane drop. For a two-lane drop, the words EXIT ONLY shall be located between the two arrows on the E11- 1e sign panel (see <a href="#fig-2e-17">Figure 2E-17</a>). The number of arrows on the sign shall correspond to the number of dropped lanes at the location of the sign.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04a</span> <span class="ca">The ONLY (E11-1b) sign panel shall not be used without the EXIT (E11-1a) sign panel. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Where an existing sign structure length or adjacent signs constrain the width or placement of the Interchange Advance guide sign on that structure, the down arrow may be positioned to the right or left of the words EXIT ONLY, instead of between the words, to allow for the positioning of the arrow over the approximate center of the lane. Where the width of the Exit Direction sign extends over the adjacent lane, the directional arrow may be placed to the right of the words EXIT ONLY for an exit to the right, or to the left of the words EXIT ONLY for an exit to the left, to allow for the positioning of the arrow over the dropped lane.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> EXIT ONLY messages of either the combination of E11-1a and E11-1b, or the E11-1c sign panels (see Figure 2E- 17) may be used to retrofit existing signing to warn of a lane drop situation ahead.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> If used to retrofit an existing guide sign, the E11-1a and E11-1b sign panels (see <a href="#fig-2e-17">Figure 2E-17</a>) shall be placed on either side of a white down arrow on an Interchange Advance guide sign and on either side of a white directional arrow on an Exit Direction sign. The E11-1c sign panel (see Figure 2E- 17), if used to retrofit an existing Interchange Advance guide sign, shall be placed between the lower destination message and the white down arrow.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07a</span> <span class="ca">The ONLY (E11-1b) sign panel shall not be used without the EXIT (E11-1a) sign panel. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Except as provided in Paragraph 9 of this Section for an auxiliary lane, Interchange Advance guide signs for lane drops within 1 mile of the interchange should not display the distance message.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Where the dropped lane is an auxiliary lane that is provided between successive entrance and exit ramps of two separate interchanges and the distance between the two ramps is less than 1 mile, the first Interchange Advance guide sign in the sequence downstream from the entrance ramp should display the distance message (see Figures 2E-20 and 2E-21).</p>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> Where the dropped lane carries the through route, signs should be used without the EXIT ONLY sign panel.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> Figures 2E-20 and 2E-21 show examples of guide sign for a dropped auxiliary lane between separate interchanges using post-mounted and overhead guide signs, respectively. <a href="#fig-2e-22">Figure 2E-22</a> shows guides signs used for an auxiliary lane that is ½ mile or longer.</p>
<p><span class="pn">11a</span> <span class="ca">Refer to FHWA’s List of Known Errors for error in Paragraph 11 text. Refer to <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1a/#1a04">Section 1A.04</a> for more details. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> Sections 2E.39 through 2E.42 contain information on the signing of lane drops at exits that also have an option lane.</p>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2b/#2b31">Section 2B.31</a> contains information regarding regulatory signs that can also be used for freeway lane drop situations and <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2c/#2c50">Section 2C.50</a> contains information regarding warning signs that can also be used for freeway lane drop situations.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> In limited cases in which conditions are so constrained that it is impossible to locate an Interchange Advance guide sign either overhead or partly over the dropped lane, precluding positioning of the down arrow as provided in Paragraph 3 of this Section, a sign panel displaying the legend RIGHT (LEFT) LANE ONLY in a black legend on a yellow background should be substituted for the EXIT ONLY panel on that sign. In such cases, the Interchange Advance guide signs should be alternated with RIGHT (LEFT) LANE FOR EXIT ONLY (W9-7) signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2c/#2c50">Section 2C.50</a>).</p>

Where additional destinations are displayed on the main roadway, those destinations should be displayed on Supplemental guide signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e51">Section 2E.51</a>) on the approach to the collector-distributor roadway.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Where exit numbering is used, the exit numbers for exits accessed from the collector-distributor roadway should be displayed on the main roadway guide signs.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> An Exit Gore sign (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e26">Section 2E.26</a>) should be placed in the gore where the collector-distributor roadway departs from the main roadway.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Interchange guide signing along the collector-distributor roadway should comply with the provisions for interchange signing in this Chapter.</p>

## 2E.37 Freeway-to-Freeway Interchanges

<div class="secmeta">Section 2E.37 &middot; manual page 513 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/2E.37/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Freeway-to-freeway interchanges are major decision points where the effect of taking a wrong ramp cannot be easily corrected. Reversing direction on the connecting freeway or reentering to continue on the intended course is usually not possible. Examples of guide signs for freeway-to-freeway interchanges are shown in <a href="#fig-2e-33">Figure 2E-33</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> The sign messages should contain only the route shield, cardinal direction, and the name of the next control city on the route. Arrows should point as indicated in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d08">Section 2D.08</a>, except where Overhead Arrow- per-Lane or Diagrammatic Advance signs are used in accordance with the provisions of Sections 2E.39 through 2E.41.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> An off-route movement is the movement that does not follow the through route. Drivers might not expect the off- route movement to be to the left or an optional lane at a split (see Figures 2E-38 and 2E-39). <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e22">Section 2E.22</a> contains information about the use of the Left Exit Number (E1-5fP through E1-5kP) plaque at splits where the off-route movement is to the left. Sections 2E.39 and 2E.40 contain information about the use of Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide signs for freeway splits with an option lane and for multi-lane freeway-to- freeway exits having an option lane. <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e41">Section 2E.41</a> contains information about the use of a Diagrammatic Advance guide sign for complex geometric configurations at ramp departures.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> The roadway for the off-route shall be signed as an exit. If exit numbering is used, the signs shall comply with the provisions of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e22">Section 2E.22</a>. Distance messages on the Advance guide signs shall comply with the provisions of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e23">Section 2E.23</a>.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Overhead signs shall be used at a distance of 1 mile and at the theoretical gore of each connecting ramp. When Overhead Arrow-per-Lane or Diagrammatic Advance guide signs are used, they shall be located in accordance with the provisions of Sections 2E.40 and 2E.41, respectively.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> The Advance guide signs at the ½-mile and 2-mile locations may also be mounted overhead.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> An Advisory Exit Speed (W13-2) sign should be used where an engineering study shows that it is necessary to display a speed reduction message for ramp signing (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2c/#2c12">Section 2C.12</a>).</p>
</div>

## 2E.40 Design of Overhead Arrow-per-Lane Guide Signs for Option Lanes

<div class="secmeta">Section 2E.40 &middot; manual page 514 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/2E.40/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide signs (see <a href="#fig-2e-35">Figure 2E-35</a>) are used where an option lane is present at freeway and expressway multi-lane exit interchanges and splits. They display an upward-pointing arrow above each lane that conveys the direction(s) of travel that the lane serves at the point of departure. At locations where an option lane is present at a multi-lane exit or split, Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide signs have been shown to be superior to other guide sign designs because they convey positive direction about which destination and direction each approach lane serves, particularly for the option lane, which is otherwise difficult to clearly sign.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide signs as provided in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e39">Section 2E.39</a> shall be used at all new or reconstructed freeway and expressway locations and at freeway and expressway locations where replacement of existing sign support structures is necessitated by reconstruction. The Overhead Arrow- per-Lane guide sign at the exit or split shall be located at or in the immediate vicinity of the point where the exiting lanes begin to diverge from the through lanes or, for a split, at the point where the approach lanes begin to diverge from one another, preserving the relation of the arrows displayed on the sign to their respective lanes. The Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide sign at the exit shall not be located at or near the theoretical gore.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> At existing or non-reconstructed locations where an overhead Exit Direction sign exists at the theoretical gore, and the existing sign support structure is retained, an overhead Exit Direction sign may continue to be used on the existing sign support structure in conjunction with a replacement of the advance signs using the Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide sign design.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> If an existing Exit Direction sign is being retained at an interchange as provided in Paragraph 3 of this Section, an Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide sign shall not be used at the location of the Exit Direction sign at or in the vicinity of the theoretical gore. New installations of Exit Direction and Pull- Through signs shall not be permitted in conjunction with Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide signs on new or reconstructed facilities.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide signs should be located at approximately ½ mile and 1 mile in advance of the exit or split, and at approximately 2 miles in advance of the exit or split where space is available and conditions allow.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide signs used on freeways and expressways shall be designed in accordance with the following criteria: A.Except as provided in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e42">Section 2E.42</a> for partial width Overhead Arrow-per-Lane signs, the sign shall include an upward-pointing (vertical, curved, or bifurcated) arrow for each lane of the approach to the split or exit. B.The shaft of each arrow shall be located over the approximate center of the lane to which it applies. C.Arrows for continuing through lanes shall be vertically upward-pointing (see <a href="#fig-2e-36">Figure 2E-36</a><span class="ca"> and Figure </span> <span class="ca">2E-36(CA)</span>) unless the continuing through lanes are on a significantly curved alignment beyond the theoretical gore (see <a href="#fig-2e-37">Figure 2E-37</a>). D.The arrow for a lane that must exit shall be curved in the direction of the exit and shall be accompanied by black-on-yellow EXIT (E11-1a) and ONLY (E11-1b) sign panels adjacent to the lower end of the arrow shaft. The E11-1a and E11-1b sign panels shall not be used for a split of two overlapping routes where neither of the diverging routes is designated as an exit. Where the through lanes curve and the exit continues on a straight alignment, upward-pointing vertical arrows shall be used for the exiting movement and curved arrows for the through movement (see <a href="#fig-2e-37">Figure 2E-37</a>). E.The arrow for an optional exit lane that also carries the through route shall have a single shaft that bifurcates into a vertically upward-pointing arrow and a curving arrow corresponding to the configuration of the through and exit lanes. F.For splits with an option lane, the arrow for the lane from which either direction of the split can be accessed shall have a single shaft that bifurcates into two upward-pointing curving arrows (see <a href="#fig-2e-38">Figure 2E-38</a>). G.A vertical white line shall be used to separate the route shields and destinations for the two diverging movements from each other. H.The distance to the exit or split shall be displayed below the off-movement destination on the advance signs at the 1-mile and 2-mile locations. I.The number of lanes displayed on a sign shall correspond to the number of lanes at the location of that sign. An advance sign shall not depict lanes that are added downstream of the sign location. J.For numbered exits, the Exit Number (E1-5P) or Left Exit Number (E1-5bP) plaque shall be used at the top <span class="ca">(above and abutting) </span>of the sign in accordance with <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e23">Section 2E.23</a>. For unnumbered exits to the left, a LEFT (E1-5mP) plaque shall be added on the top left-hand edge of and adjacent <span class="ca">(above and abutting)</span> to the sign.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06a</span> <span class="ca">The ONLY (E11-1b) sign panel shall not be used without the EXIT (E11-1a) sign panel. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide signs used on freeways and expressways should be designed in accordance with the following additional criteria: A.No more than one destination should be displayed for each movement, and no more than two destinations should be displayed per sign. B.The arrowhead(s) for the diverging movement should be positioned lower on the sign than the arrowhead(s) for the movement that continues straight ahead, independent of which movement carries the through route. Where the movements are freeway or expressway splits rather than exits, the arrowheads should be positioned at approximately the same height on the sign. C.Route shields, cardinal directions, and destinations should be positioned on the sign such that they are clearly related to the arrowhead(s) for the movement to which they apply. D.The cardinal direction should be placed adjacent to the route shield for exits or splits leading in a single cardinal direction. E.The vertical white line that is used to separate the route shields and destinations for the two diverging movements from each other should not descend below the top of the arrowheads for the through lanes, and should be positioned approximately halfway between the diverging arrowheads for the optional movement lane (see <a href="#fig-2e-35">Figure 2E-35</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide signs shall not be used to depict a downstream split of an exit ramp on a sign located on the mainline.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Specific guidelines for more detailed design of Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide signs are contained in the “Standard Highway Signs” publication (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1a/#1a05">Section 1A.05</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> The arrow heights for Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide signs on freeways and expressways shall be as shown in <a href="#fig-2e-6">Table 2E-6</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> Where extra emphasis of an especially low advisory ramp speed is needed, an EXIT XX MPH (E13- 2) sign panel (see <a href="#fig-2e-14">Figure 2E-14</a>) may be placed below the applicable destination legend to supplement, but not to replace, the exit or ramp advisory speed warning signs.</p>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> Warning Beacons in compliance with the provisions of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e25">Section 2E.25</a> may be used with the E13-2 sign panel.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> An example of guide signing for a narrow gore at a split with an option lane is shown in <a href="#fig-2e-39">Figure 2E-39</a>, and an example of guide signing for a narrow gore at a two-lane exit with an option lane is shown in <a href="#fig-2e-40">Figure 2E-40</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> Where there is 800 feet or more between the beginning of the lane diverge and the theoretical gore, signs indicating the destinations allowed by each lane may be added in the vicinity of the theoretical gore to reinforce positive guidance (see Figures 2E-39 and 2E-40).</p>
</div>

## 2E.41 Design of Freeway and Expressway Diagrammatic Advance Guide Signs

<div class="secmeta">Section 2E.41 &middot; manual page 516 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/2E.41/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> The Diagrammatic Advance guide sign (see <a href="#fig-2e-41">Figure 2E-41</a>) is a guide sign that shows a simplified graphic view of the exit departure arrangement in relationship to the main highway at an interchange. Its purpose is to provide advance notice of complex or unexpected road geometry or ramp departures at an interchange and/or depict successive decision points where additional context might be helpful to interpreting the subsequent primary Interchange Advance guide signs. Unlike Diagrammatic signs that were included in previous editions of this Manual, the Diagrammatic Advance guide sign does not depict which or the number of specific lanes that serve a particular destination or depict lanes added or reduced.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> A Diagrammatic Advance guide sign may be used in advance of the interchange guide sign sequence, or in lieu of an Interchange Advance guide sign located 2 miles in advance of the exit, to supplement conventional or Overhead Arrow- per-Lane guide signs used for a downstream interchange.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Diagrammatic Advance guide signs shall be designed in accordance with the following criteria: A.The graphic legend shall be of a plan view showing a simplified schematic graphic of the relative through and off-ramp movements. B.No symbols or route shields shall be used as a substitute for arrowheads. C.They shall not be installed at the Exit Direction sign location (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e25">Section 2E.25</a>). D.The EXIT ONLY sign panel shall not be used on Diagrammatic Advance guide signs in advance of the interchange. E.For numbered exits, the Exit Number (E1-5P) or Left Exit Number (E1-5bP) plaque shall be used at the top <span class="ca">(above and abutting) </span>of the sign in accordance with <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e22">Section 2E.22</a>. For unnumbered left exits, the LEFT (E1-5aP) plaque shall be used atthe top left edge <span class="ca">(above and abutting)</span> of the sign. F.The graphic shall not depict deceleration or auxiliary lanes. G.Arrow shafts shall not contain lane lines. H.Destination legends for off-movements shall be positioned to the side of the arrow from which the ramp departs.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Diagrammatic Advance guide signs used on freeways and expressways should be designed in accordance with the following additional criteria: A.No more than one destination should be displayed for each movement. B.The arrowhead for the diverging movement should be positioned lower on the sign than the arrowhead for the movement that continues straight ahead, independent of which movement carries the through route (see <a href="#fig-2e-42">Figure 2E-42</a>). Where the movements are freeway or expressway splits rather than exits, the arrowheads should be positioned at approximately the same height on the sign. C.Arrow shaft widths should not vary for different movements. D.Route shields, cardinal directions, and destinations should be positioned on the sign such that they are clearly related to the arrowhead(s), and the arrowhead for the off movement should point toward the route shield or, for unnumbered routes, the upper line of destination legend for the off movement. E.For exits or splits leading in a single direction, the cardinal direction should be placed adjacent to the route shield, and the destination should be placed below the route shield and cardinal direction. F.Where two exits are displayed on a Diagrammatic Advance guide sign, the control destination for the through route should be omitted from the sign. G.The distance legend should be placed below the exit destination legend. For splits where neither direction carries a through route, the distance legend should be centered below the diagrammatic arrow. Where successive exits from the same side of the roadway are displayed, the distance legend should be placed below the destination legend for the first exit, with the distance to the second exit omitted. Where successive exits from opposite sides of the roadway are displayed, the distance to the first exit should be centered below the diagrammatic arrow, with the distance to the second exit omitted.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Diagrammatic Advance guide signs shall not be used at cloverleaf interchanges for the purpose of depicting separate downstream departures from a collector-distributor roadway.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Diagrammatic Advance guide signs located on the main roadway shall not be used to depict a downstream split of an exit ramp.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Specific guidelines for more detailed design of Diagrammatic Advance guide signs are contained in the “Standard Highway Signs” publication (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1a/#1a05">Section 1A.05</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Where extra emphasis of an especially low advisory ramp speed is needed, an EXIT XX MPH (E13- 2) sign panel (see <a href="#fig-2e-14">Figure 2E-14</a>) may be placed below the applicable destination legend to supplement, but not to replace, the exit or ramp advisory speed warning signs.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Warning Beacons in compliance with the provisions of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e25">Section 2E.25</a> may be used with the E13-2 sign panel.</p>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> Diagrammatic Advance guide signs may be used on any class of roadway and may be modified to depict relative movements for intersections on conventional roads.</p>
</div>

## 2E.42 Signing for Intermediate and Minor Interchange Multi-Lane Exits with an Option Lane

<div class="secmeta">Section 2E.42 &middot; manual page 518 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/2E.42/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Intermediate and minor multi-lane exits might have an operational need for the presence of an option lane for only the peak period during which excessive queues might otherwise develop if the option lane were not available. In such cases, the Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide signing described for option lanes in Sections 2E.39 and 2E.40 might not be practical, depending on the level of use of the option lane and the spacing of nearby interchanges, particularly in non- rural areas.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> When full-width Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide signing is not practical, as described in Paragraph 1 of this Section, signing for an intermediate or minor interchange that has a multi-lane exit with an option lane that also carries the through route should use a partial-width form of the Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide sign (see Figures 2E-43 through 2E-45<span class="ca">(CA)</span>). The partial-width Overhead Arrow-per-Lane sign should display arrows only for the option lane and the mandatory exit lane(s) using the same bifurcated arrow type for the option lane and curved arrow type for the exit only lane(s) as are used for the full-width Overhead Arrow-per-Lane sign. The legend displayed for the exit movement should be clearly aligned with the arrows pointing in the direction of the exit and not with the vertical arrow head of the bifurcated arrow depicting the through movement.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> The through route and/or destination shall not be displayed on the partial-width Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide sign.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Partial-width Overhead Arrow-per-Lane signs shall be located in compliance with the provisions of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e40">Section 2E.40</a> for full-width Overhead Arrow-per-Lane signs (see Figures 2E-44 and 2E- 45).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> At an intermediate or minor interchange that has a multi-lane exit with an option lane that also carries the through route, where full-width Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide signing is not practical, conventional signing as provided in Paragraphs 7 through 9 of this Section may be used (see Figures 2E-46 and 2E-47).</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> When either full-width or partial-width Overhead Arrow-per-Lane signing is used at existing or non- reconstructed locations where an overhead Exit Direction sign exists at the theoretical gore, and the existing sign support structure is retained, an overhead Exit Direction sign may continue to be used on the existing sign support structure in conjunction with a replacement of the advance signs using the partial-width Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide sign design (see <a href="#fig-2e-44">Figure 2E-44</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">06a</span> <span class="ca">Refer to FHWA’s List of Known Errors for error in Paragraph 6 text. Refer to <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1a/#1a04">Section 1A.04</a> for more details. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> When conventional signing is used, the option lane should not be signed on the Interchange Advance guide signs. For such exits that involve the addition of an auxiliary lane that is not present at the Interchange Advance guide sign locations, but do not involve a lane drop (see <a href="#fig-2e-47">Figure 2E-47</a>), a sequence of post-mounted or overhead-mounted Interchange Advance guide signs should be used, located in accordance with the interchange classification (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e11">Section 2E.11</a>). The Exit Direction sign should be located at the theoretical gore and should display a diagonally upward- pointing directional arrow above each lane that departs from the mainline alignment. The Exit Direction sign should not contain the EXIT ONLY legend.</p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> For such interchanges that also have a lane drop (see <a href="#fig-2e-46">Figure 2E-46</a>), the Interchange Advance guide and Exit Direction signs should follow the provisions of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e28">Section 2E.28</a>. The Exit Direction sign should be located at the theoretical gore and should contain the EXIT ONLY (E11-1e) sign panel.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Where the modified Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide signs are not used, the presence of the option lane should be conveyed by the use of post-mounted lane-use (R3-8 series) signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2b/#2b30">Section 2B.30</a>). When used, the R3-8 signs should be of an appropriate size for their application to optimize their conspicuity. The signs should be located in succession with the Interchange Advance guide signs, where the option and exit lanes have developed (see <a href="#fig-2e-46">Figure 2E-46</a>). In cases where the exiting lane or lanes have not developed and the option lane is created by the addition of an auxiliary lane that exits, the R3-8 signs should be located only adjacent to where the lanes have been fully developed and not in advance of the lane or along its transition (see <a href="#fig-2e-47">Figure 2E-47</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">09a</span> <span class="ca">Refer to FHWA’s List of Known Errors for error in Paragraph 9 text. Refer to <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1a/#1a04">Section 1A.04</a> for more details.</span></p>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> The use of a down arrow on overhead freeway or expressway guide signs has been shown to be misinterpreted by road users as an indication of a dedicated lane.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> Interchange Advance guide signs that are mounted overhead shall not display a down arrow over an option lane.</p>

## 2E.43 Number of Signs at an Overhead Installation and Sign Spreading

<div class="secmeta">Section 2E.43 &middot; manual page 519 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/2E.43/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> If overhead signs are warranted, as set forth in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2a/#2a13">Section 2A.13</a>, the number of signs at these locations should be limited to only those essential in communicating pertinent destination information to the road user. Exit Direction signs for a single exit and the Interchange Advance Guide signs should have only one sign with one or two destinations. Regulatory signs, such as speed limits, should not be used in conjunction with overhead guide sign installations. Because road users have limited time to read and comprehend sign messages, there should not be more than three guide signs displayed at any one location either on the overhead structure or its support.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01a</span> <span class="ca">Refer to FHWA’s List of Known Errors for error in Paragraph 1 text. Refer to <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1a/#1a04">Section 1A.04</a> for more details. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> At overhead locations, more than one sign may be installed to advise of a multiple exit condition at an interchange. If the roadway ramp or crossroad has complex or unusual geometrics, additional signs with confirming messages may be provided to properly guide the road user.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Sign spreading is a concept where major overhead signs are spaced so that road users are not overloaded with a group of signs at a single location. <a href="#fig-2e-48">Figure 2E-48</a> illustrates an example of sign spreading.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Where overhead signing is used, sign spreading should be used at all single-exit interchanges and to the extent possible at multi-exit interchanges. Sign spreading should be accomplished by use of the following: A.The Exit Direction sign should be the only guide sign used in the vicinity of the gore (other than the Exit Gore sign). It should be located overhead near the theoretical gore and generally on an overhead sign support structure. B.The Interchange Advance guide sign to indicate the next interchange exit should be placed near the crossroad location. If the crossroad goes over the mainline, the Interchange Advance guide sign should be placed on the overcrossing structure or on a separate structure immediately in front of the overcrossing structure.</p>
</div>

## 2E.45 Guide Signing in Tunnels and Similar Structures

<div class="secmeta">Section 2E.45 &middot; manual page 519 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/2E.45/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> The application of the provisions for freeway and expressway guide signs in tunnels and other similar structures can present unique challenges not encountered elsewhere due to the extended and continuous distances of constrained vertical and horizontal clearances in which to place signs. The effect of these constraints is particularly evident when there are interchange exit ramps inside the tunnel that require guide signing. As a result, it might not always be possible to use the typical layouts for guide signs inside a tunnel. In addition, interchange guide signs might need to be limited to one destination only, with other destinations displayed separately on Supplemental guide signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e51">Section 2E.51</a>). Acceptable methods to modify the layout of a sign to fit the space available in a tunnel are provided in Paragraph 2 of this Section.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Overhead-mounted guide signs in tunnels, or in other similar structures with extended constrained vertical and horizontal clearances, may be modified in accordance with the following when needed to accommodate limited vertical clearance available for signs: A.Some sign legend elements may be arranged side by side, such as by placing route shields to the left of the destination instead of above. B.The Exit Number plaque (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e22">Section 2E.22</a>) may be placed at the right-hand edge of the sign for right exits or at the left-hand edge of the sign for left exits instead of at the top edge of the sign. The legend of the Exit Number plaque may use a reduced letter height of not less than 6 inches for the word EXIT(S) and not less than 12 inches for numerals and suffixes. C.Destination and roadway names may be displayed in reduced letter heights of not less than 10.67 inches, when determined acceptable based on consideration of reduced speed and other relevant factors, while maintaining adequate space between the legend and edges of the sign to ensure legibility and quick recognition. D.Unusually long destination and roadway names that cannot be adequately shortened or otherwise acceptably abbreviated may be displayed using Series D letters in lieu of Series E(modified).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">02a</span> <span class="ca">Refer to FHWA’s List of Known Errors for error in Paragraph 2, Item C text. Refer to <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1a/#1a04">Section 1A.04</a> for more details. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Applicability of the provisions of Paragraph 2 of this Section shall be limited to those signs within the limits of the tunnel or other similar structure and shall not be extended to the approaches to or departures from the tunnel.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Unlike typical guide signs that are exposed to rain, guide signs in tunnels accumulate grime and residue quickly. This accumulation can reduce visual contrast between legend and background and reduce the retroreflectivity of the sign sheeting. Therefore, guide signs in tunnels generally need more maintenance.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Overhead signs in tunnels should have external or internal sign illumination to ensure adequate visibility between scheduled maintenance and cleanings.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> One or more Interchange Sequence signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e24">Section 2E.24</a>) should be used on the approach to the tunnel entrance to display the distances to the next interchanges that have exit ramps inside the tunnel or immediately following the end of the tunnel.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Supplementary pavement markings, such as word, arrow, and/or route shield markings, should be considered inside the tunnel in addition to the basic lane and edge line markings.</p>
</div>

## 2E.50 Distance and Travel Time Sign (E7-5) and Comparative Travel Time Sign (E7-6)

<div class="secmeta">Section 2E.50 &middot; manual page 522 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/2E.50/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Some locations might benefit from a travel time message displayed with the distance, or comparative travel times displayed for alternative routes to a common destination. These locations are often in advance of an urbanized area where interchanges become more closely spaced and/or in advance of a circumferential or other alternative route(s) where the road user can decide to divert depending on the destination. Nonetheless, these signs are typically located in advance of a decision point where the road user can divert to an alternate route to avoid recurring congestion.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e49">Section 2E.49</a> contains information on Post-Interchange Travel Time signs.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2g/#2g19">Section 2G.19</a> contains information on Comparative Travel Time signs for parallel lanes within the same highway route, such as for general-purpose lanes and managed lanes.</p>

<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> The Distance and Travel Time (E7-5) sign (see <a href="#fig-2e-52">Figure 2E-52</a>) shall display a major destination, landmark, or junction; a distance message; and a travel time message, each on a separate line. The distance units shall be displayed in the distance message. The travel time shall be displayed in a changeable message element and the abbreviation MINS shall follow the changeable message element. The Distance and Travel Time sign shall not display distance and time to more than one destination or junction.</p>

Each alternative route and associated travel time message shall be on a separate line. The travel time shall be displayed in a changeable message element and the abbreviation MINS shall follow the changeable message element.</p>

</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Where used, the Distance and Travel Time sign should be located between interchanges and away from the sequence of interchange guide signs or other major signs.</p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Where used, the Comparative Travel Time sign should be located in advance of the sequence of interchange guide signs to provide adequate time for the road user to decide whether to reroute.</p>

## 2E.51 Supplemental Guide Signs (E3 Series)

<div class="secmeta">Section 2E.51 &middot; manual page 523 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/2E.51/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Supplemental guide signs (see <a href="#fig-2e-53">Figure 2E-53</a>) can be used to provide information regarding destinations accessible from an interchange, other than places displayed on the standard interchange signing. However, such Supplemental guide signing can reduce the effectiveness of other more important guide signing because of the possibility of overloading the road user’s capacity to receive visual messages and make appropriate decisions. “The AASHTO Guidelines for the Selection of Supplemental Guide Signs for Traffic Generators Adjacent to Freeways” is incorporated by reference in this Section.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Use of Supplemental guide signs should be limited to situations where there is a demonstrated need to sign for more destinations from an interchange than those that are displayed on the Interchange Advance guide and Exit Direction signs.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> A Supplemental guide sign should not be installed unless a destination meets the criteria established by the State or agency policy. States and other agencies should adopt an appropriate policy for installing Supplemental guide signs using the “AASHTO Guidelines for the Selection of Supplemental Guide Signs for Traffic Generators Adjacent to Freeways.” In developing policies for such signing, such items as population, amount of traffic generated, distance from the route, and the significance of the destination, should be taken into account.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> No more than one Supplemental guide sign should be used on each interchange approach.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> A Supplemental guide sign should display no more than two destinations and no more than three lines of destination names. Destination names should be followed by the interchange number (and suffix), or if interchanges are not numbered, by the legend NEXT RIGHT or SECOND RIGHT or both, as appropriate. The Supplemental guide sign should be installed as an independent guide sign assembly.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Where two or more Interchange Advance guide signs are used, the Supplemental guide sign should be installed approximately midway between two of the Interchange Advance guide signs. If only one Interchange Advance guide sign is used, the Supplemental guide sign should follow it by at least 800 feet. If the interchanges are numbered, the interchange number should be used for the action message.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> A Supplemental guide sign should not be installed in the same location with or where it will detract from guide signs for a different interchange.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">07a</span> <span class="ca">Refer to <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d36">Section 2D.36</a> and <a href="#fig-2d-101ca">Table 2D-101(CA)</a> for additional guidance and specific criteria regarding Supplemental guide signs. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> No more than two supplemental traffic generator destinations shall be signed from a single interchange approach and four from a single interchange along the main roadway (see Paragraphs 4 and 5 of this Section regarding the number of Supplemental guide signs at an interchange and the number of destinations displayed on a Supplemental guide sign).</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Supplemental guide signs shall not be placed at the same location as Interchange Advance guide, Exit Direction, or other signs related to an exit or interchange.</p>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> Guide signs for park-and-ride facilities shall be considered as Supplemental guide signs (see <a href="#fig-2e-54">Figure 2E-54</a><span class="ca"> and </span> <span class="ca"><a href="#fig-2e-54ca">Figure 2E-54(CA)</a></span>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> Guide signs for recreational or cultural interest destinations (see Chapter 2M) shall be considered as Supplemental guide signs, except where the interchange provides direct access to such a destination and the destination is instead displayed on the Interchange Advance guide and Exit Direction signs.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> The pictograph of a transit provider (see definition in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1c/#1c02">Section 1C.02</a>) may be displayed on the Park – Ride Supplemental guide sign or on a Supplemental guide sign for a transit facility.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> The use of a transit pictograph and/or the carpool symbol on the PARK – RIDE Supplemental guide sign should comply with the provisions of Paragraph 5 of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d48">Section 2D.48</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> When a transit pictograph is displayed on the PARK – RIDE Supplemental guide sign, it shall be located on the same line as the carpool symbol, if used, above the word legend.</p>
<p><span class="pn">15</span> The maximum dimension (height or width) of a pictograph on a sign shall not exceed two times the upper- case letter height of the destination or PARK – RIDE legend.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">16</span> <span class="ca">The PARK - RIDE (G95eP(CA)) plaque (refer to <a href="#fig-2e-54ca">Figure 2E-54(CA)</a>) may be installed below the Advance Guide (G83(CA) Series) </span> <span class="ca">signs on freeways and expressways to direct motorists to ridesharing parking lots. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">17</span> <span class="ca">The PARK - RIDE Supplemental Guide (G95A-1(CA)) sign (refer to <a href="#fig-2e-54ca">Figure 2E-54(CA)</a>) may be installed separately on freeways </span> <span class="ca">and expressways when it is not possible to use the G95eP(CA) plaque. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>

If only transit parking is available, the local transit symbol or logo should be used in lieu of the carpool symbol.</span></p>

## 2E.52 Community Interchanges Identification Signs (E9-4 and E9-5)

<div class="secmeta">Section 2E.52 &middot; manual page 524 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/2E.52/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>

</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>

</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> If interchanges are not conveniently identifiable or if there are more than three interchanges to be identified, the Next Exits sign (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e53">Section 2E.53</a>) may be used.</p>

## 2E.54 Weigh Station Signing

<div class="secmeta">Section 2E.54 &middot; manual page 525 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/2E.54/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Independent facilities or areas have been added along many highways where certain commercial vehicles are directed to stop to be weighed or inspected. These areas are sometimes permanent, such as in a roadside area, or temporary mobile facilities deployed along the roadway.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> The general concept for signing permanent Weigh Stations is similar to Rest Area signing (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2i/#2i05">Section 2I.05</a>) because in both cases traffic using either area remains within the highway right-of-way.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> The standard sequence of signs for a Weigh Station on an expressway or freeway shall include four basic signs (see <a href="#fig-2e-59">Figure 2E-59</a>): A.An Advance Weigh Station Distance (D8-1) sign with the distance 1 MILE displayed, B.An Advance Weigh Station Distance (D8-1) sign with the distance ½ MILE displayed, or a Weigh Station Advance Direction (D8-2) sign, C.A Weigh Station Entrance Direction (D8-3) sign, and D.A Weigh Station Gore sign (with the same legend as the Entrance Direction (D8-3) sign).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> When spacing of 1 mile and ½ mile are not practical for the D8-1 signs, the 1 MILE and ½ MILE distances on the D8-1 signs may be adjusted to match the spacing determined by engineering judgment.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Where State law requires trucks of a certain weight to enter the weigh station, a Weigh Station (R13-1) regulatory sign (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2b/#2b65">Section 2B.65</a>) may be added to the sign sequence as shown in <a href="#fig-2e-59">Figure 2E-59</a>.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Where only commercial vehicle inspections are conducted in the inspection area and vehicles are not weighed, the WEIGH STATION legend of the D8 series signs may be replaced with the alternate legend, COMMERCIAL VEHICLE INSPECTION AREA.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> When the WEIGH STATION legend of the D8 series signs is replaced with COMMERCIAL VEHICLE INSPECTION AREA legend as provided for in Paragraph 6 of this Section, the WEIGH STATION legend of the R13-1 sign shall be replaced with the alternate legend INSPECTION AREA.</p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> A changeable legend display that displays either OPEN or CLOSED shall be included in the signing sequence to indicate when trucks are required to enter the weigh station.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> The required changeable legend display OPEN or CLOSED describe in Paragraph 8 of this Section should be displayed within and at the bottom of the Weigh Station Advance Direction (D8-2) sign or the Advance Weigh Station Distance (D8-1) sign, or on a supplemental plaque or sign panel.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> A plaque with the legend OPEN WHEN FLASHING may be added to one of the Advance Weigh Station Distance signs along with associated flashing beacons, in place of the changeable legend OPEN or CLOSED sign, to indicate when commercial vehicles are required to enter the weigh station.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> Weigh Station Area sign layouts for freeway and expressway applications are shown in the “Standard Highway Signs” publication (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1a/#1a05">Section 1A.05</a>). An example of weigh station signing for use on freeways and expressways is shown in <a href="#fig-2e-59">Figure 2E-59</a>.</p>
</div>

## 2E.55 Route Signs and Trailblazer Assemblies

<div class="secmeta">Section 2E.55 &middot; manual page 526 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/2E.55/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Route signs (see <a href="#fig-2e-60">Figure 2E-60</a><span class="ca">2D-4 and <a href="#fig-2d-4ca">Figure 2D-4(CA)</a></span>) should be incorporated as cut-out shields or other distinctive shapes on large directional guide signs. Where the Interstate shield is displayed in an assembly or on the face of a guide sign with U.S. or State Route signs, the Interstate numeral should be at least equal in size to the numerals on the other Route signs. The use of independent Route signs should be limited primarily to route confirmation assemblies.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Route signs and auxiliary plaques showing junctions and turns should be used for guidance on approach roads, for route confirmation just beyond entrances and exits, and for reassurance along the freeway or expressway. When used along the freeway or expressway, the Route signs should be enlarged to a 36 x 36-inch minimum size for route numbers with one or two digits and to a 45 x 36-inch minimum size for route numbers with three digits as shown in the “Standard Highway Signs” publication (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1a/#1a05">Section 1A.05</a>). When independently-mounted Route signs are used in place of Pull-Through signs (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2e/#2e27">Section 2E.27</a>), they should be located just beyond the exit.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> The standard Trailblazer Assembly (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d34">Section 2D.34</a>) may be used on roads leading to the freeway or expressway. Component messages of the Trailblazer Assembly may be incorporated into a single sign in accordance with the provisions of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d12">Section 2D.12</a>. Independently-mounted Route signs may be used instead of Pull-Through signs as confirmation information.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2d/#2d58">Section 2D.58</a> contains information regarding the design of signs for Auto Tour Routes.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> The commonly-used name or trailblazer route sign for a toll highway (see Chapter 2F) may be displayed on non-toll sections of the Interstate Highway System at: A.The last exit before entering a toll section of the Interstate Highway System; B.The interchange or connection with a toll highway, whether or not the toll highway is a part of the Interstate Highway System; and C.Other locations within a reasonable approach distance of toll highways when the name or trailblazer symbol for the toll highway would provide better guidance to road users unfamiliar with the area than would place names and route numbers.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> The toll highway name or route sign may be included as a part of the guide sign installations on intersecting highways and approach roads to indicate the interchange with a toll section of an Interstate route. Where needed for the proper direction of traffic, a trailblazer for a toll highway that is part of the Interstate Highway System may be displayed with the Interstate Trailblazer Assembly.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Chapter 2F contains additional information regarding signing for toll highways.</p>
</div>

## 2E.57 Signs for Route Diversion by Vehicle Class

<div class="secmeta">Section 2E.57 &middot; manual page 527 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/2E.57/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> On some highways, a physical condition or highway feature might limit certain types or classes of vehicles from proceeding along that route through the site of that condition beyond which those vehicles are otherwise allowed. Examples include, but are not limited to, a restriction on taller legal-height vehicles through a tunnel with a low clearance; a restriction of hazardous materials through a tunnel or over a bridge; and a restriction on wider vehicles, such as large trucks, over a viaduct with narrow lanes. In such cases, the restricted vehicles might be diverted along another route to reach a destination beyond the location of the limiting condition.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Where certain vehicles are prohibited at a downstream location along a route and those vehicles must divert to reach a through destination beyond that location, regulatory, warning, and/or guide signs advising those vehicle operators of the diversion should be installed in advance of the decision point to leave the through route for the diversion route.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> The interchange and pull-through guide signs for the last point at which restricted vehicles must exit may be modified to incorporate regulatory and/or warning panels with word legends to display the regulations and/or warning messages relative to the vehicle class restriction.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Standard post-mounted regulatory and warning signs, such as the No Hazardous Materials (R14-3) or Advance Low Clearance (W12-2) signs, may be used as provided elsewhere in this Manual at independent locations to supplement the regulatory and warning signs and panels referenced in Paragraphs 2 and 3 of this Section.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> An example of signing for a route diversion by vehicle class is shown in <a href="#fig-2e-62">Figure 2E-62</a>.</p>
</div>
