## 3J.01 General

<div class="secmeta">Section 3J.01 &middot; manual page 1023 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/3J.01/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> This Chapter addresses the marking and delineation of islands (see definition in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1c/#1c02">Section 1C.02</a>) and sidewalk extensions designated by pavement markings. Definitions, types, sizes, and other criteria for the design of islands are set forth in “A Policy on Geometric Design of Highways and Streets,” 2018 Edition, AASHTO.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3c/#3c12">Section 3C.12</a> contains information on pedestrian islands and medians.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Sections 3H.04 and 3H.05 contain information on colored pavement that can be used within islands.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> An island may be designated by curbs, pavement edges, pavement markings, channelizing devices, or other devices.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Raised channelization with sloping (mountable) curbed medians are used instead of channelization accomplished through the  use of pavement markings (flush), for the following operating conditions:  A. Left- and right-turn lane treatments at intersections on all roadways with operating speeds of less than 40 mph. B. Right-turn treatments on roadways with operating speeds equal to or greater than 40 mph.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> On State highways, criteria for the design of islands are set forth in Caltrans’ Highway Design Manual. Refer to <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1a/#1a05">Section 1A.05</a> for information regarding this publication.</p>
</div>

## 3J.02 Approach-End Treatment

<div class="secmeta">Section 3J.02 &middot; manual page 1023 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/3J.02/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> An approach-end treatment to an island consists of longitudinal pavement markings and/or channelizing devices upstream of the island followed by a divergence of those pavement markings and/or channelizing devices concluding with a transition to other pavement markings that demarcate or outline the island (see <a href="#fig-3j-1">Figure 3J-1</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b13">Section 3B.13</a> contains information on pavement markings that function as approach-end treatments for obstructions.</p>
</div>
<div class="mfig-inline single"><figure class="mfig"><span id="fig-3j-1"></span><a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ca-mutcd/p1027.webp"><img loading="lazy" src="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ca-mutcd/p1027.webp" alt="Figure 3J-1. Example of Markings for an Approach-End Treatment to an Island Approach-end treatment (varies) Legend Direction of travel"></a><figcaption>Figure 3J-1. Example of Markings for an Approach-End Treatment to an Island Approach-end treatment (varies) Legend Direction of travel <span class="src-page">p 1027</span></figcaption></figure></div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> The ends of islands first approached by traffic should be marked with an approach-end treatment, curb markings (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3j/#3j04">Section 3J.04</a>), or both to guide vehicles into desired paths of travel along the island edge.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> When raised bars or buttons that project more than 1 inch above the pavement surface are used to create a rumble section in the neutral area, the raised bars or buttons should be marked with white or yellow retroreflective materials, as determined by the direction or directions of travel they separate.</p>
</div>

## 3J.03 Islands Designated by Pavement Markings

<div class="secmeta">Section 3J.03 &middot; manual page 1023 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/3J.03/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Except as provided in Paragraph 2 of this Section, islands formed by pavement markings only shall be established using channelizing lines, and shall be white when separating traffic flows in the same general direction or yellow when separating opposing directions of traffic.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> If a continuous flush median island separating travel in opposite directions is used, two sets of double solid yellow lines shall be used to form the island (see <a href="#fig-3b-5">Figure 3B-5</a>). Other markings in the median island area, such as diagonal lines (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b25">Section 3B.25</a>), shall also be yellow, except crosswalk markings which shall be white (see Chapter 3C).</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> If used, chevron or diagonal markings (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b25">Section 3B.25</a>) within the island shall be the same color as the channelizing line.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Both chevron and diagonal markings of the same color may be used within the same island based on engineering judgment.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> The area within the flush island delineated by pavement markings may use colored pavement in accordance with the provisions of Chapter 3H.</p>
</div>

## 3J.04 Curb Markings for Raised Islands

<div class="secmeta">Section 3J.04 &middot; manual page 1024 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/3J.04/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Where curbs are marked for delineation or visibility purposes, the colors shall comply with the general principles of markings (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3a/#3a03">Section 3A.03</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Retroreflective solid yellow curb markings should be placed on the approach ends of raised medians and curbs of islands that are located in the line of traffic flow where the curb serves to channel traffic to the right of the obstruction (see <a href="#fig-3j-3">Figure 3J-3</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Retroreflective solid white curb markings should be used when traffic is permitted to pass on either side of the island (see <a href="#fig-3j-3">Figure 3J-3</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> The retroreflective area should be of sufficient length to denote the general alignment of the edge of the island along which vehicles travel, including the approach end, when viewed from the approach to the island.</p>
</div>
<div class="mfig-inline single"><figure class="mfig"><span id="fig-3j-3"></span><a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ca-mutcd/p1029.webp"><img loading="lazy" src="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ca-mutcd/p1029.webp" alt="Figure 3J-3. Examples of Curb Markings for Raised Islands"></a><figcaption>Figure 3J-3. Examples of Curb Markings for Raised Islands <span class="src-page">p 1029</span></figcaption></figure></div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Where the curbs of the islands become parallel to the direction of traffic flow or where the island is illuminated or marked with delineators, curb markings may be discontinued based on engineering judgment or study.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Curb markings at openings in a continuous median island may be omitted based on engineering judgment or study.</p>
</div>

## 3J.05 Pavement Markings for Raised Islands

<div class="secmeta">Section 3J.05 &middot; manual page 1024 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/3J.05/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Pavement markings for raised islands include the approach-end treatment (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3j/#3j02">Section 3J.02</a>), channelizing lines, edge lines, and chevron or diagonal markings.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Solid yellow edge lines (see Sections 3B.09 and 3B.10) may be used adjacent to raised islands separating travel in opposite directions (see Drawing A in <a href="#fig-3j-3">Figure 3J-3</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Except as provided in Paragraphs 4 and 6 of this Section, raised islands separating traffic flows in the same general direction shall be outlined with white channelizing lines (see Drawing A in <a href="#fig-3j-4">Figure 3J-4</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="mfig-inline single"><figure class="mfig"><span id="fig-3j-4"></span><a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ca-mutcd/p1030.webp"><img loading="lazy" src="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ca-mutcd/p1030.webp" alt="Figure 3J-4. Examples of Pavement Markings for Raised Islands"></a><figcaption>Figure 3J-4. Examples of Pavement Markings for Raised Islands <span class="src-page">p 1030</span></figcaption></figure></div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Pavement markings for smaller raised islands may be omitted based on engineering judgment.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Smaller raised islands without marked channelizing lines, edge lines, or chevron or diagonal markings should use curb markings (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3j/#3j04">Section 3J.04</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Where traffic passes on the right of a raised island separating traffic flows in the same general direction, a yellow edge line should be used adjacent to raised islands of discernible size or length instead of continuing the white channelizing line from the approach-end treatment (see Drawing B in <a href="#fig-3j-4">Figure 3J-4</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Yellow edge lines adjacent to raised islands that separate traffic flows in the same general direction can be advantageous as a countermeasure for wrong-way entry or travel if the yellow edge line is of discernible length.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Chevron markings may be used in neutral areas formed by diverging channelizing lines at raised islands separating traffic flows in the same general direction.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Diagonal markings of an appropriate color may be used in buffer areas between the channelizing line and the raised island (see <a href="#fig-3j-5">Figure 3J-5</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="mfig-inline single"><figure class="mfig"><span id="fig-3j-5"></span><a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ca-mutcd/p1032.webp"><img loading="lazy" src="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ca-mutcd/p1032.webp" alt="Figure 3J-5. Example of Optional Chevron and Diagonal Pavement Markings at a Raised Island Legend Direction of travel"></a><figcaption>Figure 3J-5. Example of Optional Chevron and Diagonal Pavement Markings at a Raised Island Legend Direction of travel <span class="src-page">p 1032</span></figcaption></figure></div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> Double solid 4 inch wide yellow lines shall be used to delineate the edge of a median island where the median is an all- paved, at-grade section of the highway. The island formed by double yellow lines shall be at least 2 foot in width, as shown  in <a href="#fig-3a-107ca">Figure 3A-107(CA)</a>.</p>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> When used, other markings in the median island area shall be yellow.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> This treatment is not intended for freeways or other highways with a positive barrier in the median. Single solid yellow left edge  line and markers as shown in <a href="#fig-3a-105ca">Figure 3A-105(CA)</a> are standard.</p>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> The use of channelizing lines are shown in <a href="#fig-3a-112ca">Figure 3A-112(CA)</a> and no-passing markings are shown in Figures 3A-104(CA) and  3B-15.</p>
</div>

## 3J.06 Island Delineation

<div class="secmeta">Section 3J.06 &middot; manual page 1025 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/3J.06/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Delineators installed on islands shall be the same colors as the related channelizing or edge lines except that, when only facing wrong-way traffic, they shall be red (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3g/#3g03">Section 3G.03</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Each roadway through an intersection shall be considered separately in positioning delineators to assure maximum effectiveness.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Retroreflective or internally illuminated raised pavement markers of the appropriate color may be placed on the pavement in front of the curb and/or on the top of curbed approach ends of raised medians and curbs of islands, as a supplement to or as a substitute for retroreflective curb markings.</p>
</div>

## 3J.07 Sidewalk Extensions Designated by Pavement Markings

<div class="secmeta">Section 3J.07 &middot; manual page 1025 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/3J.07/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Sidewalk extensions reclaim a portion of the roadway, sometimes including a portion of parking lanes, shoulders, and/or the traveled way, and repurpose that area for non-vehicular uses. They extend the sidewalk or other pedestrian space, shorten pedestrian crossing distances, alter the roadway geometry for speed management or channelizing, or serve other purposes.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Sidewalk extensions, sometimes referred to as curb extensions, neckdowns, or bulb-outs, typically are created by physical infrastructure including concrete or asphalt to form a physical narrowing of the roadway with the finished surface at the same level as the adjoining sidewalk.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Sidewalk extensions can also be designated by pavement markings for temporary or semi-permanent applications in which the finished surface is at the same level as the vehicular travel pavement. Where an adjoining curb and raised sidewalk are present, this type of application results in a multi-level sidewalk due to the difference in elevation between the adjoining pedestrian surfaces.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Sidewalk extensions designated by pavement markings differ from other paved areas designated by pavement markings that are intended to be traversable by a vehicle for authorized or emergency purposes.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Sidewalk extensions designated by pavement markings shall be established using double solid lines connecting to the outside physical curb or, in the absence of a curb, to the edge of the roadway. The color of the double solid line shall comply with the provisions of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3a/#3a03">Section 3A.03</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> The paved area between the double solid line forming the sidewalk extension designated by pavement markings and the sidewalk or other roadside area is not part of the roadway. Sidewalk extensions designated by pavement markings formed by double solid lines are distinct from areas such as shoulders or gore areas where travel is discouraged by the presence of a single line, or flush medians where travel is prohibited by a double solid line. Sidewalk extensions designated by pavement markings with double solid lines designate areas outside the roadway where vehicle traversal is prohibited.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Areas formed by a single wide line are sometimes used to alter the roadway geometry for speed management or channelizing, or to serve other purposes, where pedestrians are not expected (see Drawing B in <a href="#fig-3j-6">Figure 3J-6</a>). These areas are not considered a sidewalk extension, and provisions to delineate areas where vehicle traversal is discouraged include channelizing lines (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b08">Section 3B.08</a>), edge lines (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b09">Section 3B.09</a>), and diagonal markings (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b25">Section 3B.25</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="mfig-inline single"><figure class="mfig"><span id="fig-3j-6"></span><a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ca-mutcd/p1033.webp"><img loading="lazy" src="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ca-mutcd/p1033.webp" alt="Figure 3J-6. Examples of Sidewalk Extensions Designated by Pavement Markings and Channelization"></a><figcaption>Figure 3J-6. Examples of Sidewalk Extensions Designated by Pavement Markings and Channelization <span class="src-page">p 1033</span></figcaption></figure></div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Channelizing devices such as tubular markers (see Chapter 3I) should be used to provide conspicuity for, and to prevent vehicles from traversing, the area of the sidewalk extension designated by pavement markings. They should be located adjacent to the double solid line outside the traveled way.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> When selecting other methods of physical separation, the visual contrast from adjoining pavement and maximum separation distances are considerations so they are visible to pedestrians having limited vision and detectable by pedestrians who travel with a long cane.</p>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> Sight lines and the visibility of road users within the sidewalk extension area are considerations when selecting methods of physical separation.</p>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> The swept path of turning design or other prevailing vehicle types is a consideration, especially if a larger vehicle is expected to traverse a portion of the sidewalk extension while turning where pedestrians might be present.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> Crosswalk markings shall not be extended through sidewalk extensions designated by pavement markings.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> Accessibility provisions at sidewalk extensions designated by pavement markings are outside the scope of this Manual. State and local organizations providing support services to pedestrians with vision disabilities can provide advice to the traffic engineer on site-specific accessibility decisions. In addition, orientation and mobility specialists or similar staff can provide advice to inform such decisions. The U.S. Access Board (www.access-board.gov) provides technical assistance for making pedestrian facilities accessible to persons with disabilities.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> Traffic control devices that are critical to the specific conditions at the sidewalk extension, such as STOP or YIELD signs or Pedestrian Crossing signs, should be located within the sidewalk extension designated by pavement markings. Their lateral offset (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/2a/#2a16">Section 2A.16</a>) should be measured from the center of the double solid line designating a sidewalk extension rather than from the physical curb line behind the sidewalk extension area so that the signs are more visible to approaching traffic and not occluded by any physical features placed within the sidewalk extension area.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">15</span> The location of accessible pedestrian signals (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/4k/#4k02">Section 4K.02</a>) is a consideration when providing a sidewalk extension designated by pavement markings.</p>
<p><span class="pn">16</span> Aesthetic surface treatments (see Chapter 3H) are sometimes used in sidewalk extensions designated by pavement markings to emphasize that the area is outside of the traveled way.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">17</span> In accordance with the provisions of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3h/#3h03">Section 3H.03</a>, aesthetic surface treatments, if used within a sidewalk extension designated by pavement markings, shall be non-retroreflective.</p>
</div>
