## 8E.01 Purpose

<div class="secmeta">Section 8E.01 &middot; manual page 1565 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/8E.01/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Traffic control for pathway and sidewalk grade crossings includes all signs, signals, markings, other warning devices, and their supports at pathway and sidewalk grade crossings and along pathway and sidewalk approaches to grade crossings. The function of this traffic control is to promote safety and provide effective operation of both rail and pathway or sidewalk traffic at pathway or sidewalk grade crossings.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Other physical treatments that are described in this Chapter that are also applicable to pathways and sidewalks at grade crossings, such as detectable warnings, swing gates, and fencing, provide increased safety for pathway and sidewalk users.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Crosswalk markings at intersections where pedestrians cross LRT tracks in mixed-use alignments are covered by the provisions of <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3c/">Chapter 3C</a> rather than by the provisions of this Chapter.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> <a href="#fig-8e-1">Figure 8E-1</a> illustrates the difference between a pathway grade crossing and a sidewalk grade crossing. A pathway is frequently placed in its own right-of-way on an alignment that is independent of any roadway. If a pathway is built parallel to a roadway, it is physically separated from the roadway by an open space or barrier such that the traffic control devices for the roadway grade crossing do not exert an influence over or provide adequate warning to pathway users. A sidewalk runs parallel to a roadway within the highway right-of-way and is close enough to the edge of the roadway’s traveled way that the traffic control devices for the roadway grade crossing can frequently exert an influence over or provide adequate warning to sidewalk users. Pathways are typically used by both pedestrians and bicyclists, whereas sidewalks are typically used only by pedestrians.</p>
</div>

## 8E.02 Use of Standard Devices, Systems, and Practices

<div class="secmeta">Section 8E.02 &middot; manual page 1565 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/8E.02/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> The pathway or sidewalk user’s ability to detect the presence of approaching rail traffic should be considered in determining the type and placement of traffic control devices at pathway or sidewalk grade crossings.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> The traffic control devices, including the appropriate traffic control system to be used, and other physical treatments at a pathway or sidewalk grade crossing should be determined <span class="ca">evaluated</span> by a Diagnostic Team that includes the agency with jurisdiction over the pathway or sidewalk.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> At skewed grade crossings, the adjustment, re-alignment, or relocation of existing sidewalk grade crossings should be considered when determining the placement of traffic control devices for roadway users.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> The safety of pathway and sidewalk users is enhanced when pathways and sidewalks are designed such that they do not cross the tracks at a narrow angle. The casters of wheelchairs and the wheels of bicycles could fall into and might be constrained in the flangeway gap at a skewed crossing. The flangeway gap is typically 2.5 inches wide at LRT grade crossings and 3 inches wide at railroad grade crossings.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> It is desirable that pathways and sidewalks be designed such that they maintain a relatively consistent horizontal alignment and profile from the nearest rail to the detectable warning (if present) or from the nearest rail to the stop line (if present) on each approach to the crossing. Providing a pedestrian refuge area in advance of the stop line or the detectable warning surface so that pedestrians have a place to wait while rail traffic approaches and occupies the crossing can be beneficial to pedestrian safety.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> When designing new sidewalk grade crossings, placing the sidewalk outside of the area occupied by grade crossing traffic control devices for vehicular traffic is desirable (see <a href="#fig-8e-2">Figure 8E-2</a>). This includes making sure that the counterweights and support arms for the automatic gates for vehicular traffic do not obstruct the sidewalk when the gate is fully lowered.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Additional information regarding the design of pathways and sidewalks is contained in the U.S. Department of Justice 2010 ADA Standards for Accessible Design, September 15, 2010, 28 CFR 35 and 36, Americans with Disabilities Act of 1990.</p>
</div>

## 8E.03 Pathway and Sidewalk Grade Crossing Signs and Markings

<div class="secmeta">Section 8E.03 &middot; manual page 1566 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/8E.03/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Pathway and sidewalk grade crossing signs shall be standard in shape, legend, and color.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> The minimum sizes of sidewalk grade crossing signs that are intended to be viewed only by sidewalk users and of pathway grade crossing signs shall be as shown in the shared-use path column in <a href="#fig-9a-1">Table 9A-1</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> No portion of a traffic control device or its support should protrude into the pathway or sidewalk grade crossing. Sidewalk and pathway grade crossing traffic control devices should be located such that all physical features of the device, including the support hardware, conform to clearance requirements provided by the railroad company and/or transit agency, and the regulatory agency with statutory authority (if applicable)<span class="ca">CPUC</span>.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> The minimum mounting height for post-mounted signs adjacent to pathways and sidewalks should be 4 feet, measured vertically from the bottom of the sign to the elevation of the near edge of the pathway or sidewalk surface (see <a href="#fig-9a-1">Figure 9A-1</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> If overhead traffic control devices are placed above pathways, the clearance from the bottom of the device to the pathway surface directly under the sign or device should be at least 8 feet.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> If overhead traffic control devices are placed above pathways that are used by equestrians, the clearance from the bottom of the device to the pathway surface directly under the sign or device should be at least 10 feet.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> If overhead traffic control devices are placed above sidewalks, the clearance from the bottom of the device to the sidewalk surface directly under the sign or device shall be at least 7 feet.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Traffic control devices mounted adjacent to pathways at a height of less than 8 feet measured vertically from the bottom of the device to the elevation of the near edge of the pathway surface should have a minimum lateral offset of 2 feet from the near edge of the device to the near edge of the pathway (see <a href="#fig-9a-1">Figure 9A-1</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> If pathway users include those who travel faster than pedestrians, such as bicyclists or skaters, warning signs should be installed in advance of the pathway grade crossing (see <a href="#fig-8e-3">Figure 8E-3</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> The Skewed Crossing (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w10-12/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W10-12.svg">W10-12</a>) sign (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/8b/#8b22">Section 8B.22</a>) may be used at a skewed pathway or sidewalk grade crossing to warn pathway or sidewalk users that the tracks are not perpendicular to the pathway or sidewalk.</p>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> The LOOK (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r15-8/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R15-8.svg">R15-8</a>) sign (see <a href="#fig-8b-1">Figure 8B-1</a>) may be used at a pathway or sidewalk grade crossing to inform pathway or sidewalk users to look in both directions prior to crossing the track(s).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> If a LOOK (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r15-8/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R15-8.svg">R15-8</a>) sign is used at a pathway or sidewalk grade crossing, it should be mounted on a separate post that is farther from the pathway or sidewalk than the Crossbuck sign or Crossbuck Assembly.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> The LOOK (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r15-8/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R15-8.svg">R15-8</a>) sign is used to provide additional warning for pedestrians and bicyclists.</p>
</div>

## 8E.04 Stop Lines, Edge Lines, and Detectable Warnings

<div class="secmeta">Section 8E.04 &middot; manual page 1566 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/8E.04/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> A stop line should be provided at a pathway grade crossing if the surface where the marking is to be applied is capable of retaining the application of the marking.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> A stop line may be provided at a sidewalk grade crossing if the surface where the marking is to be applied is capable of retaining the application of the marking.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">02a</span> <span class="ca">At a sidewalk grade crossing, detectable warning is typically used without a stop line.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> If used at pathway or sidewalk grade crossings, the stop line should be a transverse line that extends across the full width of the pathway or sidewalk at the point where a pathway or sidewalk user is to stop. If no detectable warning is provided, the stop line should be placed at least 2 feet in advance of the automatic gate, counterweight, flashing-light signals, or Crossbuck Assembly (if any of these are present), and at least 12 feet from the nearest rail.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Edge lines (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3b/#3b09">Section 3B.09</a>) to delineate the designated user route may be used on the approach to and across the tracks at a pathway grade crossing, a sidewalk grade crossing, or a station crossing if the surface where the marking is to be applied is capable of retaining the application of the marking.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Edge line delineation can be beneficial where the distance across the tracks is long, commonly because of a skewed grade crossing or because of multiple tracks, or where the pathway or sidewalk surface is immediately adjacent to a traveled way.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Information regarding the design of detectable warning surfaces is contained in the U.S. Department of Justice 2010 ADA Standards for Accessible Design, September 15, 2010, 28 CFR 35 and 36, Americans with Disabilities Act of 1990.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Detectable warnings (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/3c/">Chapter 3C</a>) shall be used at pathway grade crossings where pedestrian travel is permitted and at sidewalk grade crossings and shall extend across the full width of the pathway or sidewalk.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> The dimension of the detectable warning in the direction of pedestrian travel should be at least 2 feet.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Detectable warnings should be placed immediately beyond the pathway or sidewalk stop line (if a stop line is present) or should be incorporated into and made a part of the stop line. The downstream edge of the detectable warning should be located at least 2 feet upstream from the automatic gate, counterweight, flashing-light signals, or Crossbuck Assembly (if any of these are present)<span class="ca">,</span><span class="ca">which would be on the side of the device further from the tracks</span>, and at least 12 feet from the nearest rail (see Figures 8E-2 and 8E-3).</p>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> If the distance between the nearest rail of two adjacent tracks at a sidewalk or pathway grade crossing is 30 feet or more, additional detectable warnings should be used to designate the limits of the pedestrian refuge area (see <a href="#fig-8e-4">Figure 8E-4</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> At pathway-LRT and sidewalk-LRT grade crossings, the downstream edge of the detectable warning may be located less than 12 feet from the nearest rail.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> The downstream edge of the detectable warning at pathway-LRT and sidewalk-LRT grade crossings should be located at least 2 feet upstream from the automatic gate, counterweight, flashing-light signals, or Crossbuck assembly (if any of these are present), at least 6 feet from the nearest rail<span class="ca"> as measured perpendicular to the rail </span>and in accordance with the requirements of the railroad company and/or transit agency, and regulatory agency with statutory authority (if applicable) <span class="ca">CPUC</span>.</p>
</div>

## 8E.05 Passive Traffic Control Devices– Crossbuck Assemblies

<div class="secmeta">Section 8E.05 &middot; manual page 1567 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/8E.05/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Where the nearest edge of a passive pathway or sidewalk grade crossing is located more than 25 feet from the center of the nearest traffic control warning device at the grade crossing, a Crossbuck Assembly (see Figure 8E- 5) shall be installed on each approach to the pathway or sidewalk grade crossing. The distance shall be measured perpendicular to the traveled way from the center of the support post of a Crossbuck Assembly at a passive grade crossing or from the center of the mast of an active traffic control warning device at an active grade crossing to the nearest edge of the pathway or sidewalk surface where it crosses the track(s) (see <a href="#fig-8e-2">Figure 8E-2</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> A Crossbuck Assembly may be installed on the approaches to a pathway or sidewalk grade crossing where the nearest edge of the pathway or sidewalk is located 25 feet or less from the center of the nearest traffic control warning device at a grade crossing.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> The Crossbuck Assembly may be omitted at station crossings.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> The retroreflective strip on the back of the support may be omitted on the Crossbuck support at a pathway or sidewalk grade crossing.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> The minimum height, measured vertically from the bottom of the YIELD or STOP sign to the elevation of the near edge of the pathway or sidewalk, of Crossbuck Assemblies installed on pathways or sidewalks shall be 4 feet where the lateral offset to the nearest edge of the sign is 2 feet or more and shall be 7 feet where the lateral offset to the nearest edge of the sign is less than 2 feet (see <a href="#fig-8e-5">Figure 8E-5</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> The minimum lateral offset, measured horizontally from the nearest edge of the pathway or sidewalk to the nearest edge of the Crossbuck Assembly signs, shall be 0 feet for sidewalks and 2 feet for pathways.</p>
</div>

## 8E.06 Channelizing Devices used with Sidewalk and Pathway Traffic Control Devices

<div class="secmeta">Section 8E.06 &middot; manual page 1568 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/8E.06/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> The pathway or sidewalk user’s ability to detect the presence of approaching rail traffic needs to be considered in determining the type and placement of channelizing devices such as swing gates, fencing, and pedestrian barriers.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Where automatic gates and swing gates are used, it is desirable to design the pathway or sidewalk in a manner that channelizes or directs users to the entrance to and exit from the pathway or sidewalk grade crossing.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02a</span> <span class="ca">Where automatic pedestrian gates are installed, swing gates are typically installed along an emergency escape route in  conjunction with each automatic pedestrian gate.</span></p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Swing gates (see Figures 8E-6, 8E-9, and 8E-10) are designed to open away from the track(s) so that pathway or sidewalk users can quickly push the swing gate open when moving away from the track(s), and to automatically return to the closed position after each use.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> It is important to use retroreflective material, appropriate object markers (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/9c/#9c09">Section 9C.09</a>), and/or signs on swing gates, maze fencing, or pedestrian barriers that are placed at pathway or sidewalk grade crossings. Illumination of such areas can also be beneficial.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> When used in conjunction with automatic gates at a pathway or sidewalk grade crossing, swing gates are typically equipped with a latching device that permits the swing gate <span class="ca">designed</span>to be opened only from the track side of the swing gate. Push bars, kick plates, or similar devices are also appropriate for use on a swing gate.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Latching devices that are used on swing gates need to be designed in a manner such that they are operable by all users of the pathway or sidewalk.</p>
</div>
<div class="mfig-inline single"><figure class="mfig"><span id="fig-8e-9"></span><a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ca-mutcd/p1580.webp"><img loading="lazy" src="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ca-mutcd/p1580.webp" alt="Figure 8E-9. Example of Active Traffic Control Systems with Automatic Pedestrian Gates and Swing Gates at a Sidewalk Grade Crossing"></a><figcaption>Figure 8E-9. Example of Active Traffic Control Systems with Automatic Pedestrian Gates and Swing Gates at a Sidewalk Grade Crossing <span class="src-page">p 1580</span></figcaption></figure></div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> A swing gate should be equipped with a PUSH TO EXIT (I13-2) sign on the track side of the swing gate, and a DO NOT ENTER (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r5-1/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R5-1.svg">R5-1</a>) sign on the side of the swing gate facing away from the tracks (see <a href="#fig-8e-10">Figure 8E-10</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">07a</span> <span class="ca">Where swing gates are placed along an emergency escape route near a roadway, signs mounted on the swing gate are typically  oriented and sized to be visible to pedestrians rather than motorists.</span></p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> The U.S. Department of Justice 2010 ADA Standards for Accessible Design, September 15, 2010, 28 CFR 35 and 36, Americans with Disabilities Act of 1990 contains information regarding the design of swing gates and related hardware.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Where fencing (see Figures 8E-6 and 8E-9) is installed to direct pathway or sidewalk users to the grade crossing, it is desirable that this fencing be connected to any continuous existing or new fencing or channelization that has been installed parallel to the track(s) to discourage pedestrians from circumventing the grade crossing.</p>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> Pedestrian barriers or fencing, sometimes referred to as a “maze fencing,” direct pathway or sidewalk users to face approaching rail traffic before entering the trackway.</p>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> Where used, maze fencing or pedestrian barriers need to be designed to permit the passage of wheelchairs and power-assisted mobility devices, and if bicycles are permitted, to permit the passage of dismounted bicyclists with tandem bicycles, cargo bicycles, or bicycles with trailers.</p>
</div>

## 8E.07 Active Traffic Control Systems

<div class="secmeta">Section 8E.07 &middot; manual page 1568 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/8E.07/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Except as provided in Paragraph 5 of this Section, at pathway-LRT and sidewalk-LRT grade crossings where LRT operating speeds on a semi-exclusive alignment exceed 25 mph, active traffic control systems shall be used.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Except as provided in Paragraph 5 of this Section, at pathway-LRT and sidewalk-LRT grade crossings where LRT operating speeds on a semi-exclusive alignment exceed 40 <span class="ca">35</span>mph, active traffic control systems, including automatic gates, shall be used. <span class="ca">Refer to CPUC General Order 143.</span></p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> If used at a pathway or sidewalk grade crossing, an active traffic control system (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/8d/#8d01">Section 8D.01</a>) shall include flashing-light signals (see <a href="#fig-8e-7">Figure 8E-7</a>) on each approach to the crossing.</p>
</div>
<div class="mfig-inline single"><figure class="mfig"><span id="fig-8e-7"></span><a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ca-mutcd/p1578.webp"><img loading="lazy" src="/SoCal-Super-Standards/figs/ca-mutcd/p1578.webp" alt="Figure 8E-7. Example of a Flashing-Light Signal Assembly at a Pathway or Sidewalk Grade Crossing 24 inches 30 inches"></a><figcaption>Figure 8E-7. Example of a Flashing-Light Signal Assembly at a Pathway or Sidewalk Grade Crossing 24 inches 30 inches <span class="src-page">p 1578</span></figcaption></figure></div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> If used at a pathway or sidewalk grade crossing, an active traffic control system (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/8d/#8d01">Section 8D.01</a>) should <span class="ca">shall </span>include an audible device such as a bell that is operated in conjunction with the flashing-light signals.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Flashing-light signals, bells, and other audible warning devices may be omitted at pathway or sidewalk grade crossings that are located within 25 feet of an active warning device at a grade crossing that is equipped with those devices.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Additional pairs of flashing-light signals, bells, or other audible warning devices may be installed on the active traffic control devices at a grade crossing for pathway or sidewalk users approaching the grade crossing from the back side of those devices.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Where railroad or LRT tracks in a semi-exclusive alignment are parallel and immediately adjacent to a roadway and if adequate space exists, a pedestrian refuge area or island should be provided between the tracks and the roadway to permit pedestrians to stand clear of the tracks while waiting to cross the roadway and to stand clear of the roadway while waiting to cross the tracks. If a pedestrian refuge area or island is provided at a signalized crossing of the roadway, additional pedestrian features (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/4i/">Chapter 4I</a>), such as signal heads, signing, and detectors, should be installed in the refuge area or on the island.</p>
</div>

## 8E.08 Active Traffic Control Devices – Signals

<div class="secmeta">Section 8E.08 &middot; manual page 1569 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/8E.08/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Pedestrian signal heads are typically used at highway-highway intersections where pedestrians have an expectation that other roadway users will sometimes be legally required to yield the right-of-way to them. At grade crossings where rail traffic does not stop, pedestrians will not have the right-of-way yielded to them. Therefore, pedestrian signal heads are not an appropriate traffic control device to use at a pathway or sidewalk grade crossing where rail traffic does not stop. Instead, the universal application of horizontally-aligned, alternately-flashing red lights is the uniform active traffic control device for all grade crossings where rail traffic does not stop including pathway and sidewalk grade crossings.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Except as provided in Paragraph 3 of this Section, pedestrian signal heads as described in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/4i/">Chapter 4I</a> comprised of Upraised Hand and Walking Person symbols shall not be used at a pathway or sidewalk grade crossing.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Pedestrian signal heads may be used at a pathway or sidewalk grade crossing where the movement of LRT vehicles is controlled by a traffic control signal or by special LRT signals (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/8d/#8d15">Section 8D.15</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> If used at a pathway or sidewalk grade crossing, flashing-light signals shall be aligned horizontally and the light units shall have a diameter of at least 4 <span class="ca">12</span> inches. For 4-inch <span class="ca">12-inch </span>diameter light units, the light centers shall be spaced approximately 16 <span class="ca">30</span> inches apart and, if used, the flashing light unit backgrounds shall be at least 8 <span class="ca">24</span> inches in diameter.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">04a</span> <span class="ca">Refer to FHWA’s List of Known Errors for error in Paragraph 4 text. Refer to <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1a/#1a04">Section 1A.04</a> for more details.</span></p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Each red signal unit in the flashing-light signal shall flash alternately. The number of flashes per minute for each lamp shall be 35 minimum and 65 maximum. Each lamp shall be illuminated for approximately the same length of time. The total time of illumination of each pair of lamps shall be the entire operating time.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> The minimum mounting height of the flashing-light signals shall be 4 feet, measured vertically from the bottom edge of the lights to the elevation of the near edge of the pathway or sidewalk surface.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> At station, pathway, or sidewalk grade crossings with multiple tracks, traffic control devices may be installed between the tracks in compliance with any railroad clearance requirements.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> The mounting height for flashing-light signals that are installed between the tracks at multiple-track crossings shall be a minimum of 1 foot, measured vertically from the bottom edge of the lights to the elevation of the near edge of the pathway surface.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> If a Diagnostic Team finds that a flashing-light signal with a Crossbuck sign and an audible device is still not resulting in appropriate pedestrian behavior, consideration should be given to also installing an automatic pedestrian gate (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/8e/#8e09">Section 8E.09</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> Flashing-light signals (see <a href="#fig-8e-7">Figure 8E-7</a>) with a Crossbuck (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r15-1/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R15-1.svg">R15-1</a>) sign and an audible device should be installed along semi-exclusive LRT alignments at station, pathway, or sidewalk grade crossings where the Diagnostic Team has determined that the sight distance is not sufficient for pathway or sidewalk users to complete their crossing prior to the arrival of LRT traffic at the crossing.</p>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> If the Diagnostic Team determines that flashing-light signals with a Crossbuck sign and an audible device would not provide sufficient notice of approaching LRT traffic, consideration should be given to also installing an automatic pedestrian gate (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/8e/#8e09">Section 8E.09</a>) with appropriate channelization or fencing.</p>
</div>

<span id="8e09" class="anchor"></span>

## 8E.09 Active Traffic Control Devices – Automatic Pedestrian Gates

<div class="secmeta">Section 8E.09 &middot; manual page 1570 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/8E.09/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Automatic pedestrian gates (see Figures 8E-6, 8E-8, 8E-9, 8E-11, and 8E-12) may be used at pathway or sidewalk grade crossings.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01a</span> <span class="ca">If used at a pathway or sidewalk grade crossing, an automatic pedestrian gate typically includes an audible device such as a  bell, a Crossbuck (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r15-1/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R15-1.svg">R15-1</a>) sign, flashing-light signals, and, if applicable, the Pedestrian Grade Crossing Sign (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r15-1dca/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/ca/r15-1dca.webp">R15-1D(CA)</a>). Refer to  CPUC General Order 75, as amended.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> A pathway or sidewalk grade crossing across tracks where trains are permitted to travel at speeds of 80 mph or higher shall be equipped with a system of automatic pedestrian gates and an escape area with swing gates and fencing installed in the vicinity of the crossing to direct users to the pathway or sidewalk grade crossing (see <a href="#fig-8e-6">Figure 8E-6</a>) unless the Diagnostic Team <span class="ca">recommends and CPUC</span>determines that other safety treatments for the crossing would be more appropriate.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Where automatic pedestrian gates are installed across a pathway or sidewalk at a grade crossing, or where a sidewalk is located between the edge of a roadway and the support for an automatic gate arm that extends across the sidewalk and into the roadway, an emergency escape route (see Figures 8E-9 and 8E-10) should be provided to allow pedestrians to egress away from the track area when the automatic pedestrian gates are activated.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Except as provided in Paragraph 6 of this Section,automatic pedestrian gate arms shall be provided with at least one red light as shown in Figures 8E-6, 8E-8, 8E-9, 8E-11, and 8E-12. This light shall be continuously illuminated whenever the warning system is active.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> If any red lights in addition to the continuously-illuminated red light that is required in Paragraph 4 of this Section are provided on the automatic pedestrian gate arm, they shall be installed in pairs and shall be flashed alternately in unison with the other flashing-light units at the crossing.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> The red light on an automatic pedestrian gate arm may be omitted if the pathway or sidewalk grade crossing is located within 25 feet of the traveled way at a highway-rail or highway-LRT grade crossing that is equipped with active warning devices (see <a href="#fig-8e-11">Figure 8E-11</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> If used at a pathway or sidewalk grade crossing, the height of the automatic pedestrian gate arm when in the down position should be a minimum of 3 feet and a maximum of 4 feet above the pathway or sidewalk.</p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> If used at a pathway or sidewalk grade crossing, the gate configuration, which might include a combination of automatic pedestrian gates and swing gates, should provide for full-width coverage of the pathway or sidewalk on each approach to the crossing.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Where a sidewalk is located between the edge of a roadway and the support for an automatic gate arm that extends across the sidewalk and into the roadway, the location, placement, and height prescribed for vehicular gates shall be used (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/8d/#8d03">Section 8D.03</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> Except as provided in Paragraph 11 of this Section, if a separate automatic pedestrian gate is used for a sidewalk at a highway-rail or highway-LRT grade crossing, instead of a supplemental or auxiliary gate arm installed as a part of the same mechanism as the vehicular gate, a separate mechanism (see Figure 8E- 11) should be provided for the separate automatic pedestrian gate so that if a pedestrian manually raises the pedestrian gate arm, it will have no effect on the vehicular gate.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> A supplemental or auxiliary pedestrian gate arm installed as a part of the same mechanism as the vehicular gate may be used if the operating mechanism is designed to prevent the vehicular gate from being raised as a result of a pedestrian manually raising the pedestrian gate arm.</p>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> A horizontal hanging bar (see <a href="#fig-8e-12">Figure 8E-12</a>) may be attached to an automatic pedestrian gate at a pathway or sidewalk grade crossing to inform pedestrians with vision disabilities that the automatic pedestrian gate is in the down position and to reduce the likelihood that pedestrians will violate a lowered crossing gate.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">12a</span> <span class="ca">Striping of the horizontal hanging bar typically matches the pattern of the gate arm.</span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> If a horizontal hanging bar is attached to an automatic pedestrian gate, the height of the horizontal hanging bar when in the down position should be a maximum of 26 inches above the pathway or sidewalk.</p>
</div>

<span id="8e10" class="anchor"></span>

## 8E.10 Active Traffic Control Devices – Multiple-Track Pathway or Sidewalk Grade Crossings

<div class="secmeta">Section 8E.10 &middot; manual page 1571 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/8E.10/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Where railroad or LRT tracks are immediately adjacent to other tracks, the traffic control devices that control pedestrian movements should be designed to avoid having pedestrians wait between sets of tracks.</p>
</div>
