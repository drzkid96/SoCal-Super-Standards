## 6N.01 Work Duration

<div class="secmeta">Section 6N.01 &middot; manual page 1326 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/6N.01/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Work duration is a major factor in determining the number and types of devices used in TTC zones. The duration of a TTC zone is defined relative to the length of time a work operation occupies a spot location.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> The five categories of work duration and their time at a location shall be defined as follows: A.Long-term stationary is work that occupies a location more than 3 days. B.Intermediate-term stationary is work that occupies a location more than one daylight period up to 3 days, or nighttime work lasting more than 1 hour. C.Short-term stationary is daytime work that occupies a location for more than 1 hour within a single daylight period. D.Short duration is work that occupies a location up to 1 hour. E.Mobile is work that moves intermittently or continuously.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> At long-term stationary TTC zones, there is ample time to install and realize benefits from the full range of TTC procedures and devices that are available for use. Larger channelizing devices, temporary roadways, and temporary traffic barriers are frequently used.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Since long-term operations extend into nighttime, retroreflective and/or illuminated devices shall be used in long-term stationary TTC zones.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> In intermediate-term stationary TTC zones, it might not be feasible or practical to use procedures or devices that would be desirable for long-term stationary TTC zones, such as altered pavement markings, temporary traffic barriers, and temporary roadways. The increased time to place and remove these devices in some cases could significantly lengthen the project, thus increasing exposure time.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Since intermediate-term operations extend into nighttime, retroreflective and/or illuminated devices shall be used in intermediate-term stationary TTC zones.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Most maintenance and utility operations are short-term stationary work.</p>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> As compared to stationary operations, mobile and short-duration operations are activities that might involve different treatments. Devices having greater mobility might be necessary such as signs mounted on trucks. Devices that are larger, more imposing, or more visible can be used effectively and economically. The mobility of the TTC zone is important.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> Safety in short-duration or mobile operations should not be compromised by using fewer devices simply because the operation will frequently change its location.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> During short-duration work, it often takes longer to set up and remove the TTC zone than to perform the work. Workers face hazards in setting up and taking down the TTC zone. Also, since the work time is short, delays affecting road users are significantly increased when additional devices are installed and removed.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> Considering these factors, simplified control procedures may be warranted for short-duration work. A reduction in the number of devices may be offset by the use of other more dominant devices such as high- intensity rotating, flashing, oscillating, or strobe lights on work vehicles.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> Mobile operations often involve frequent short stops for activities such as litter cleanup, pothole patching, or utility operations, and are similar to short-duration operations.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> Flags and/or channelizing devices may additionally be used and moved periodically to keep them near the mobile work area.</p>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> Flaggers may be used for mobile operations that often involve frequent short stops.</p>
<p><span class="pn">15</span> Mobile operations also include work activities where workers and equipment move along the road without stopping, usually at slow speeds. The advance warning area moves with the work area.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">16</span> When mobile operations are being performed, a shadow vehicle equipped with an arrow board or a sign should follow the work vehicle, especially when vehicular traffic speeds or volumes are high. Where feasible, warning signs should be placed along the roadway and moved periodically as work progresses.</p>
<p><span class="pn">17</span> To avoid high-volume conditions, consideration should be given to scheduling mobile operations work during off- peak hours.</p>
<p><span class="pn">18</span> If there are mobile operations on a high-speed travel lane of a multi-lane divided highway, arrow boards <span class="ca">and/or  Portable Changeable Message Signs</span> should be used.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">19</span> Mobile operations shall have appropriate devices on the equipment (that is, high-intensity rotating, flashing, oscillating, or strobe lights, signs, or special lighting), or shall use a separate vehicle with appropriate warning devices. Although vehicle hazard warning lights are permitted to be used to supplement high-intensity rotating, flashing, oscillating, or strobe lights, they shall not be used instead of these devices.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">20</span> For mobile operations that move at speeds of less than 3 mph, mobile signs or stationary signing that is periodically retrieved and repositioned in the advance warning area may be used.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">21</span> A rolling roadblock is a method of TTC used to slow or stop traffic as a means of temporarily removing traffic from a roadway segment downstream of the road block. The rolling roadblock closes all lanes of traffic by using pacing vehicles to create a gap so that construction activities can be performed. Rolling roadblocks are used where long-term road closures using TTC devices are not needed. A rolling roadblock consists of one blocking/pacing vehicle per lane of traffic, a clearing vehicle, and an advance warning vehicle. The rolling roadblock is normally performed by law enforcement officers during off-peak hours.</p>
</div>

## 6N.02 Location of Work

<div class="secmeta">Section 6N.02 &middot; manual page 1327 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/6N.02/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Chapter 6C and Sections 6M.04 and 6N.04 contain additional information regarding the steps to follow when pedestrian or bicycle facilities are affected by the worksite.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> The choice of TTC needed for a TTC zone depends upon where the work is located. As a general rule, the closer the work is to road users (including bicyclists and pedestrians), the greater the number of TTC devices that are needed. Procedures are described later in this Chapter for establishing TTC zones in the following locations: A. Outside the shoulder, B. On the shoulder with no encroachment, C. On the shoulder with minor encroachment, D. Within the median, and E. Within the traveled way.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> When the work space is within the traveled way, except for short-duration and mobile operations, advance warning shall provide a general message that work is taking place and shall supply information about highway conditions. TTC devices shall clearly delineate the path roadway users are to follow through the TTC zone.</p>
</div>

## 6N.03 Modifications To Fulfill Special Needs

<div class="secmeta">Section 6N.03 &middot; manual page 1327 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/6N.03/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> The typical applications in Chapter 6P illustrate commonly encountered situations in which TTC devices are employed.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Other devices may be added to supplement the devices provided in the typical applications, and device spacing may be adjusted to provide additional reaction time. When conditions are less complex than those depicted in the typical applications, fewer devices may be needed.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> When conditions are more complex, typical applications should be modified by giving particular attention to the provisions set forth in Chapter 6A and by incorporating appropriate devices and practices from the following list: A.Additional devices: 1.Signs 2.Arrow boards 3.More channelizing devices at closer spacing (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/6m/#6m04">Section 6M.04</a> for information regarding detectable edging for pedestrians) 4.Temporary raised pavement markers 5.High-level warning devices 6.Portable changeable message signs 7.Temporary traffic control signals (including accessible pedestrian signals where not otherwise required) 8.Temporary traffic barriers 9.Crash cushions 10.Screens 11.Rumble strips 12.More delineation B.Upgrading of devices: 1.A full complement of standard pavement markings 2.Brighter and/or wider pavement markings 3.Larger and/or brighter signs 4.Channelizing devices with greater conspicuity 5.Temporary traffic barriers in place of channelizing devices C.Improved geometrics at detours or crossovers D.Increased distances: 1.Longer advance warning area 2.Longer tapers E.Lighting: 1.Temporary roadway lighting 2.Steady-burn lights used with channelizing devices 3.Flashing lights for isolated hazards 4.Illuminated signs 5.Floodlights F.Pedestrian routes and temporary facilities G.Bicycle diversions and temporary facilities</p>
</div>

## 6N.04 Work Affecting Pedestrian and Bicycle Facilities

<div class="secmeta">Section 6N.04 &middot; manual page 1328 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/6N.04/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> It is not uncommon, particularly in urban areas, that road work and the associated TTC will affect existing pedestrian or bicycle facilities. It is essential that the needs of all road users, including pedestrians with disabilities, are considered in TTC zones.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> In addition to specific provisions identified in Sections 6N.05 through 6N.13, there are a number of provisions that might be applicable for all of the types of activities identified in this Chapter.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Where pedestrian or bicyclist usage is high, the typical applications should be modified by giving particular attention to the provisions set forth in Chapter 6C, this Chapter, Sections 6K.02 and 6M.04, and in other Sections of Part 6 related to accessibility and detectability provisions in TTC zones.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Pedestrians should be separated from the worksite by appropriate devices that maintain the accessibility and detectability for pedestrians with disabilities.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Bicyclists and pedestrians should not be exposed to unprotected excavations, open utility access, overhanging equipment, or other such conditions.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Except for - and mobile operations, when a highway shoulder is occupied, a SHOULDER WORK (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w21-5/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W21-5.svg">W21-5</a>) sign<span class="ca">,</span><span class="ca">a  SHOULDER CLOSED C30A(CA) sign, or other similar signs</span> should be placed in advance of the activity area. When work is performed on a paved shoulder 8 feet or more in width, channelizing devices should be placed on a taper having a length that conforms to the requirements of a shoulder taper. Signs should be placed such that they do not narrow any existing pedestrian passages to less than 48 inches.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">06a Refer to FHWA’s List of Known Errors for error in Paragraph 6 text. Refer to <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1a/#1a04">Section 1A.04</a> for more details. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">06b When existing accommodations for bicycle travel are disrupted or closed in a long-term duration project (refer to <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/6n/#6n01">Section 6N.01</a>),  information and devices contained in Figures 6P-19(CA), 6P-47 through 6P-51(CA), and 6P-101(CA) as appropriate per situation  encountered, should be used in order to replicate existing conditions for the needs and control of bicyclists through a TTC zone. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">06c Except for short durations and mobile operations (refer to <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/6n/#6n01">Section 6N.01</a>), when a highway shoulder is occupied and bicyclists  would be sharing a lane with vehicular traffic, as a result of the TTC zone, a combination of Bicycle crossing (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w11-1/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W11-1.svg">W11-1</a>) and IN ROAD  (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w16-1p/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W16-1P.svg">W16-1P</a>) plaque should be placed in advance of the activity area. When work is performed on a paved shoulder 8 feet or more in  width, channelizing devices should be placed on a taper having a length that conforms to the requirements of a shoulder taper. Signs  should be placed such that they do not block the bicyclist’s path of travel and they do not narrow any existing pedestrian passages to  less than 48 inches. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Pedestrian detours should be avoided since pedestrians rarely observe them and the cost of providing accessibility and detectability might outweigh the cost of maintaining a continuous route. Whenever possible, work should be done in a manner that does not create a need to detour pedestrians from existing routes or crossings.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> Where pedestrian routes are closed, alternate pedestrian routes shall be provided.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> When existing pedestrian facilities are disrupted, closed, or relocated in a TTC zone, the temporary facilities shall be detectable and shall include accessibility features consistent with the features present in the existing pedestrian facility.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">09b If establishing or maintaining an alternate pedestrian route is not feasible during the project, an alternate means of providing for  pedestrians may be used, such as adding free bus service around the project or assigning a person the responsibility to assist  pedestrians with disabilities through the project limits. Refer to <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/6c/#6c02">Section 6C.02</a> for details. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> The continuity of a bikeway should be maintained through the TTC zone if practical.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">11</span> The continuity of a bikeway through the TTC zone is particularly important where bicyclists have been traveling on a shoulder, bicycle lane, or shared-use path adjacent to a general-purpose lane (having a speed limit greater than or equal to 35 miles per hour) and there would be a significant safety concern if bicyclists were to share that general-purpose lane through the TTC zone.</p>
<p><span class="pn">12</span> On roadways which are not bikeways but where bicyclists (when present) typically share lanes with motor vehicle traffic, the TTC plan and Typical Applications for general traffic will usually be adequate for bicyclists as well.</p>
<p><span class="pn">13</span> In order to maintain room for bicycle lanes through the TTC zone on a multi-lane roadway, one or more travel lanes could be closed.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">14</span> If a bikeway detour is unavoidable, it should be as short and direct as practical.</p>
<p><span class="pn">15</span> On-road bicyclists should not be directed onto a path or sidewalk intended for pedestrian use except where such a path or sidewalk is a shared-use path, or where no practical alternative is available (such as might be the case on a bridge in the course of a rehabilitation project).</p>
<p><span class="pn">16</span> If a portion of a bikeway is to be closed due to construction activities and the detoured bikeway follows a complex path not in the original bikeway corridor, then a full detour plan should be developed and implemented. The TTC for the detour of the bikeway should include all necessary advance warning (W21 series) signs, detour (W4-9 series) signs, and any other TTC devices necessary to guide bicyclists along the detour route.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">17</span> Figures 6P-47 through 6P-51 provide examples and contain additional information for accommodating bicycles through or around typical TTC zones.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">18</span> If an on-street bikeway had a wide travel lane or lanes in which bicyclists traveled side by side with motor vehicles prior to construction, and construction activities reduce the lane width(s) to less than 14 feet through the TTC zone, then the BICYCLISTS ALLOWED USE OF FULL LANE (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r9-20/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R9-20.svg">R9-20</a>) sign may be used.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">19</span> The minimum TTC sign and plaque sizes for shared-use paths shall conform to those shown in <a href="#fig-9a-1">Table 9A-1</a>. The minimum TTC sign and plaque sizes for on-street bikeways shall conform to Chapters 6G, 6H, and 6I.</p>
</div>

## 6N.05 Work Outside of the Shoulder

<div class="secmeta">Section 6N.05 &middot; manual page 1330 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/6N.05/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> When work is being performed beyond the shoulders, but within the right-of-way, little or no TTC might be needed. TTC generally is not needed where work is confined to an area 15 feet or more from the edge of the traveled way. However, TTC is appropriate where distracting situations exist, such as vehicles parked on the shoulder, vehicles accessing the worksite via the highway, and equipment traveling on or crossing the roadway to perform the work operations (for example, mowing). A typical application for work beyond the shoulder is shown in <a href="#fig-6p-1">Figure 6P-1</a>.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Where the situations described in Paragraph 1 of this Section exist, a single warning sign, such as ROAD WORK AHEAD (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w20-1/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W20-1.svg">W20-1</a>) <span class="ca">or Workers (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w21-1/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W21-1.svg">W21-1</a>) sign</span>, should be used. If the equipment travels on the roadway, the equipment should be equipped with appropriate flags, high-intensity rotating, flashing, oscillating, or strobe lights, and/or a SLOW MOVING VEHICLE (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w21-4/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W21-4.svg">W21-4</a>) sign.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> If work vehicles are on the shoulder, a SHOULDER WORK (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w21-5/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W21-5.svg">W21-5</a>) sign should be used.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> A general warning sign like ROAD MACHINERY AHEAD (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w21-3/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W21-3.svg">W21-3</a>) should be used if workers and equipment must occasionally move onto the shoulder.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> For mowing operations, the sign MOWING AHEAD (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w21-8/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W21-8.svg">W21-8</a>) may be used.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Where the activity is spread out over a distance of more than 2 miles, the SHOULDER WORK (W21- 5) sign may be repeated every 1 mile.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> A supplementary plaque with the message NEXT XX MILES (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w7-3ap/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W7-3aP.svg">W7-3aP</a>) may be used.</p>
</div>

## 6N.06 Work on the Shoulder with No Encroachment

<div class="secmeta">Section 6N.06 &middot; manual page 1330 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/6N.06/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> The provisions of this Section apply to short-term through long-term stationary operations.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> When paved shoulders having a width of 8 feet or more are closed, at least one advance warning sign shall be used. In addition, channelizing devices shall be used to close the shoulder in advance to delineate the beginning of the work space and direct motor vehicle traffic to remain within the traveled way.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> When paved shoulders having a width of 8 feet or more are closed on freeways and expressways, road users should be warned about potential disabled vehicles that cannot get off the traveled way. An initial general warning sign, such as ROAD WORK AHEAD (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w20-1/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W20-1.svg">W20-1</a>), should be used, followed by a RIGHT or LEFT SHOULDER CLOSED (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w21-5a/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W21-5a.svg">W21-5a</a>) sign. Where the downstream end of the shoulder closure extends beyond the distance that can be perceived by road users, a supplementary plaque bearing the message NEXT XX FEET (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w16-4p/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W16-4P.svg">W16-4P</a>) or MILES (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w7-3ap/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W7-3aP.svg">W7-3aP</a>) should be placed below the SHOULDER CLOSED (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w21-5a/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W21-5a.svg">W21-5a</a>) sign. On multi-lane, divided highways, signs advising of shoulder work or the condition of the shoulder should be placed only on the side of the affected shoulder.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> When an improved shoulder is closed on a high-speed roadway, it should be treated as a closure of a portion of the road system because road users expect to be able to use it in emergencies. Road users should be given ample advance warning that shoulders are closed for use as refuge areas throughout a specified length of the approaching TTC zone. The sign(s) should read SHOULDER CLOSED (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w21-5a/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W21-5a.svg">W21-5a</a>) with distances indicated. The work space on the shoulder should be closed off by a taper or channelizing devices with a length of ⅓ L using the formulas in Tables 6B-3<span class="ca">, 6B-3(CA)</span> and 6B-4.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> When the shoulder is not occupied but work has adversely affected its condition, the LOW SHOULDER (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w8-9/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W8-9.svg">W8-9</a>) or SOFT SHOULDER (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w8-4/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W8-4.svg">W8-4</a>) sign should be used, as appropriate.</p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Where the condition extends over a distance in excess of 1 mile, the sign should be repeated at 1-mile intervals.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>

## 6N.12 Work Within the Traveled Way at an Intersection

<div class="secmeta">Section 6N.12 &middot; manual page 1334 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/6N.12/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Chapter 6C and Sections 6M.04 and 6N.04 contain additional information regarding the steps to follow when pedestrian or bicycle facilities are affected by the worksite.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> The typical applications for intersections are classified according to the location of the work space with respect to the intersection area (as defined by the extension of the curb or edge lines). The three classifications are near side, far side, and in-the-intersection. Work spaces often extend into more than one portion of the intersection. For example, work in one quadrant often creates a near-side work space on one street and a far-side work space on the cross street. In such instances, an appropriate TTC plan is obtained by combining features shown in two or more of the intersection and pedestrian typical applications.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> TTC zones in the vicinity of intersections might block movements and interfere with normal road user flows. Such conflicts frequently occur at more complex signalized intersections having such features as traffic signal heads over particular lanes, lanes allocated to specific movements, multiple signal phases, signal detectors for actuated control, and accessible pedestrian signals and detectors.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> The effect of the work upon signal operation should be considered, and temporary corrective actions should be taken, if necessary, such as revising signal phasing and/or timing to provide adequate capacity, maintaining or adjusting signal detectors, and relocating signal heads to provide adequate visibility as described in Part 4.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>

<p><span class="pn">01</span> Special conditions encountered where vehicular traffic must be moved through or around TTC zones on high-speed, high-volume roadways can pose challenges to the TTC. Although the general principles outlined in other Sections of this Manual are applicable to all types of highways, high-speed, access-controlled highways need special planning and attention in order to accommodate vehicular traffic while also protecting road users and workers. The traffic volumes, vehicle mix (buses, trucks, cars, and bicycles, if permitted), and speed of vehicles on these facilities require that careful TTC procedures be implemented, for example, to induce critical merging maneuvers well in advance of work spaces and in a manner that creates minimum turbulence and delay in the vehicular traffic stream.</p>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>

## 6N.15 Crossovers

<div class="secmeta">Section 6N.15 &middot; manual page 1336 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/6N.15/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> The following are considered good guiding principles for the design of crossovers: A. Tapers for lane drops should be separated from the crossovers, as shown in <a href="#fig-6p-39">Figure 6P-39</a>. B. Crossovers should be designed for speeds no lower than 10 mph below the posted speed, the off-peak 85th- percentile speed prior to the work starting, or the anticipated operating speed of the roadway, unless unusual site conditions require that a lower design speed be used. C. A good array of channelizing devices, delineators, and full-length, properly placed pavement markings should be used to provide drivers with a clearly defined travel path. D. The design of the crossover should accommodate all vehicular traffic, including trucks and buses.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Temporary traffic barriers and the excessive use of TTC devices cannot compensate for poor geometric and roadway cross-section design of crossovers.</p>
</div>

## 6N.16 Interchanges

<div class="secmeta">Section 6N.16 &middot; manual page 1336 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/6N.16/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Access to interchange ramps on limited-access highways should be maintained even if the work space is in the lane adjacent to the ramps. Access to exit ramps should be clearly marked and delineated with channelizing devices. For long-term projects, conflicting pavement markings should be removed and new ones placed. Early coordination with officials having jurisdiction over the affected cross streets and providing emergency services should occur before ramp closings.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> If access is not possible, ramps may be closed by using signs and Type 3 Barricades. As the work space changes, the access area may be changed, as shown in <a href="#fig-6p-42">Figure 6P-42</a>. A TTC zone in the exit ramp may be handled as shown in <a href="#fig-6p-43">Figure 6P-43</a>.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> When a work space interferes with an entrance ramp, a lane may need to be closed on the freeway (see Figure 6P- 44). A TTC zone in the entrance ramp may require shifting ramp vehicular traffic (see <a href="#fig-6p-44">Figure 6P-44</a>).</p>
<p><span class="pn">04 A temporary traffic control zone in the entrance and exit ramps may be handled as shown in Caltrans’ Standard Plans T10 and  T14. Refer to <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1a/#1a05">Section 1A.05</a> for information regarding this publication. </span></p>
</div>

## 6N.17 Work in the Vicinity of a Grade Crossing

<div class="secmeta">Section 6N.17 &middot; manual page 1336 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/6N.17/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> When grade crossings exist either within or in the vicinity of a TTC zone, lane restrictions, flagging, or other operations shall not create conditions where vehicles can be queued across the tracks. If the queuing of vehicles across the tracks cannot be avoided, a uniformed law enforcement officer or flagger shall be provided at the crossing to prevent vehicles from stopping on the tracks, even if automatic warning devices are in place.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> <a href="#fig-6p-46">Figure 6P-46</a> shows work in the vicinity of a grade crossing.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/8a/#8a13">Section 8A.13</a> contains additional information regarding TTC zones in the vicinity of grade crossings.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Early coordination with the railroad company or transit agency should occur before work starts.</p>
</div>

## 6N.18 Work During Nighttime Hours

<div class="secmeta">Section 6N.18 &middot; manual page 1337 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/6N.18/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/6a/#6a05">Section 6A.05</a> contains additional information regarding considerations for conducting work operations during nighttime hours.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Considering the safety issues inherent to night work, consideration should be given to enhancing traffic controls (see <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/6n/#6n03">Section 6N.03</a>) to provide added visibility and driver guidance, and increased protection for workers.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> In addition to the enhancements listed in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/6n/#6n03">Section 6N.03</a>, consideration should be given to providing additional lights and retroreflective markings to workers, work vehicles, and equipment.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> Where reduced traffic volumes at night make it feasible, the entire roadway may be closed by detouring traffic to alternate facilities, thus removing the traffic risk from the activity area.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> Consideration should be given to stationing uniformed law enforcement officers and lighted patrol cars at night work locations where there is a concern that high speeds or impaired drivers might result in undue risks for workers or other drivers.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> Except in emergencies, temporary lighting shall be provided at all flagger stations used during nighttime work.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">06a Refer to Construction Safety Order in the California Code of Regulations (Title 8, Division 1, Chapter 4, Subchapter 4, Article 11,  § 1599 - Flaggers). See <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1a/#1a05">Section 1A.05</a> for information regarding this publication. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> Desired illumination levels vary depending upon the nature of the task involved. An average horizontal luminance of 5 foot candles can be adequate for general activities. An average horizontal luminance of 10 foot candles can be adequate for <span class="ca">general activities and </span>activities around equipment. Tasks requiring high levels of precision and extreme care can require an average horizontal luminance of 20 foot candles.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov std"><div class="prov-label">Standard</div>
<p><span class="pn">08 Highway construction work lighting shall be as per Construction Safety Order 1523 (California Code of Regulations  Title 8, Division 1, Chapter 4, Subchapter 4, Article 3, § 1523 - Illumination). See <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1a/#1a05">Section 1A.05</a> for information regarding this  publication. </span></p>
</div>

## 6N.19 Late Merge

<div class="secmeta">Section 6N.19 &middot; manual page 1337 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/6N.19/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> The Late Merge is designed to use all available lanes until the merge point is reached at the lane closure taper rather than merging as soon as possible into the open lane. The Late Merge addresses many of the challenges that are associated with traffic operations in advance of lane closures at TTC zones such as queue length, capacity, and driver satisfaction.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> Late Merge systems may consist of static or portable changeable message signs.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Static Late Merge signing should consist of the STAY IN LANE TO MERGE POINT (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/r9-4a/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/R9-4a.svg">R9-4a</a>) sign <span class="ca">to be within the work  zone or advance warning area</span>and the MERGE HERE TAKE TURNS (<a class="sref" href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/w9-2a/" data-svg="/SoCal-Super-Standards/signs/W9-2a.svg">W9-2a</a>) sign (see <a href="#fig-6n-1">Figure 6N-1</a>).</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">03a Refer to FHWA’s List of Known Errors for error in Paragraph 3 text. Refer to <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/1a/#1a04">Section 1A.04</a> for more details. </span></p>
</div>
<div class="prov opt"><div class="prov-label">Option</div>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> The following messages may be used on changeable message signs at an upstream location during the Late Merge application: A. “STAY IN YOUR LANE/MERGE AHEAD” B. “STAY IN YOUR LANE/MERGE AHEAD XX MILES” C. “USE BOTH LANES/TO MERGE POINT” D. “USE BOTH LANES/STOPPED TRAFFIC AHEAD E. “SLOW TRAFFIC AHEAD/USE BOTH LANES”</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> The following messages are typically used on changeable message signs at the merge point during the Late Merge application: A. “TAKE YOUR TURN/MERGE HERE” B. “MERGE HERE/TAKE TURNS”</p>
</div>
