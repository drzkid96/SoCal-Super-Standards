## 7A.01 Introduction

<div class="secmeta">Section 7A.01 &middot; manual page 1467 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/7A.01/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> Part 7 sets forth basic principles and prescribes standards for the design, application, installation, and maintenance of all traffic control devices (including signs, signals, and markings) and other controls (including adult crossing guards) for the special pedestrian conditions in school areas.</p>
</div>

## 7A.02 School Route Plans and School Crossings

<div class="secmeta">Section 7A.02 &middot; manual page 1467 &middot; <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/s/ca/7A.02/">short link</a></div>

<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">01</span> A school route plan for each school serving elementary to high school students should be prepared in order to develop uniformity in the use of school area traffic controls and to serve as the basis for a school traffic control plan for each school.</p>
<p><span class="pn">02</span> The school route plan, developed in a systematic manner by the school, law enforcement, and traffic officials responsible for school pedestrian safety, should consist of a map (see <a href="#fig-7a-1">Figure 7A-1</a>) showing streets, the school, existing traffic controls, established school walk routes, and established school crossings.</p>
<p><span class="pn">03</span> Bicycle use as a mode of transportation, as applicable, should also be considered. if students biking to and from school are not allowed to use the sidewalks along the pedestrian route.</p>
<p><span class="pn">04</span> The type(s) of school area traffic control devices used, either warning or regulatory, should be related to the volume and speed of vehicular traffic, street width, and the number and age of the students using the crossing.</p>
<p><span class="pn">05</span> School area traffic control devices should be included in a school traffic control plan.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">05a The words “School Pedestrians”, “Children”, and “Students” are used interchangeably and could include student bicyclists for the  purpose of determining appropriate crossing protection measures. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">06</span> To establish a safer route to and from school for schoolchildren, the application of planning criterion for school walk routes might make it necessary for children to walk an indirect route to an established school crossing located where there is existing traffic control and to avoid the use of a direct crossing where there is no existing traffic control.</p>
<p><span class="pn">07</span> The frequency of gaps in the traffic stream that are sufficient for student crossing is different at each crossing location. When the delay between the occurrences of adequate gaps becomes excessive, students might become impatient and endanger themselves by attempting to cross the street during an inadequate gap. In these instances, the creation of sufficient gaps needs to be considered to accommodate the crossing demand.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov gui"><div class="prov-label">Guidance</div>
<p><span class="pn">08</span> School walk routes should be planned to take advantage of existing traffic controls.</p>
<p><span class="pn">09</span> The following factors should be considered when determining the feasibility of requiring children to walk a longer distance to a crossing with existing traffic control: A.The availability of adequate sidewalks or other pedestrian walkways to and from the location with existing control, B.The number of students using the crossing, C.The age levels of the students using the crossing, and D.The total extra walking distance.</p>
</div>
<div class="prov sup"><div class="prov-label">Support</div>
<p><span class="pn">10</span> A School Crossing signal warrant is provided in <a href="/SoCal-Super-Standards/ca-mutcd/4c/#4c06">Section 4C.06</a>.</p>
<p><span class="pn">11 Street closures are authorized by local ordinance or resolution on streets crossing or dividing school grounds when necessary  for the protection of persons attending the school. Refer to CVC § 21102. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">12 Engineering studies determine the appropriate measures developed at school crossings. The devices and treatments described  herein are for use in school zones and do not preclude use of other devices and treatments described elsewhere in this document. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">13 Types of school pedestrian measures that can be considered can include:  A.</span><span class="ca">Warning signs and markings.  B.</span><span class="ca">School speed limits.  C.</span><span class="ca">Intersection stop signs.  D.</span><span class="ca">Flashing yellow beacons.  E.</span><span class="ca">Traffic signals.  F.</span><span class="ca">Pedestrian Hybrid Beacons.  G.</span><span class="ca">Remove visibility obstructions.  H.</span><span class="ca">School Safety Patrol.  I. Adult Crossing Guard.  J.</span><span class="ca">Pedestrian separation structures.  K.</span><span class="ca">Pedestrian walkways along the roadway.  L.</span><span class="ca">Pedestrian walkways separated from the roadway.  M.</span><span class="ca">Parking controls and curb-use zones. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">14 Refer to CVC § 21373 for school board request for traffic control devices. </span></p>
<p><span class="pn">15 Upon request of the local school district, responsible traffic authorities investigate all locations along the school route and  recommend appropriate traffic control measures. Refer to CVC § 21373. </span></p>
